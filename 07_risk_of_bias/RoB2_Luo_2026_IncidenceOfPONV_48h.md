# Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 (RoB 2) Assessment

## Study & Result Information
- **Study Citation:** Luo L, Fu Y, Li S, Jia J. Perioperative transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation at pericardium 6 reduces postoperative nausea and vomiting after tympanoplasty patients: a randomized controlled study. *BMC Complementary Medicine and Therapies*. 2026;26:167.
- **Source Document in Google Drive:** `covidence_35_verified.pdf`
- **Trial Registration:** "The study protocol was prospectively registered at [Anonymized]."
- **Population:** 277 adult patients (aged 18–64 years, ASA physical status I–II) scheduled for elective tympanoplasty under general anesthesia (May–July 2024).
- **Intervention:** Perioperative TEAS at bilateral Pericardium 6 (PC6) using Model JNR2 stimulator, initiated at 1 mA (2 Hz) and gradually increased to 2–20 mA until patient experienced tolerable tingling sensation, maintained continuously from induction through end of surgery (n = 138 analyzed).
- **Comparator:** Sham TEAS with identical electrode placement at PC6, but the stimulator was turned off and no electrical current was delivered (0 mA) (n = 139 analyzed).
- **Assessed Outcome:** Cumulative Incidence of Postoperative Nausea and Vomiting (PONV)
- **Assessed Timepoint:** 0 to 48 hours postoperatively
- **Numerical Result:**
  - Nausea (0–48 h): TEAS 24 / 138 (17.39%) vs. Sham-TEAS 53 / 139 (38.13%), $P < 0.01$ (Table 2)
  - Vomiting (0–48 h): TEAS 9 / 138 (6.52%) vs. Sham-TEAS 27 / 139 (19.42%), $P < 0.01$ (Table 2)
  - Complete Response (no PONV, no rescue): TEAS 112 / 138 (81.16%) vs. Sham-TEAS 83 / 139 (59.71%), $P < 0.01$ (Table 2)

### Step 1: Study Design Verification
- **Experimental Design:** Prospective, single-blind, evaluator-blinded, randomized, parallel-group trial (1:1 allocation ratio).
- **Unit of Randomization:** Individual patient.

---

### Step 2: Signaling Questions & Domain Evaluations

#### Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process
- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random?**
  - **Response:** Yes (Y)
  - **Evidence:** "Eligible participants were randomized in a 1:1 ratio into two groups using a computer-generated randomization sequence." (`covidence_35_verified.pdf`, Section "Randomization and Blinding", Page 2, col. 2).
- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**
  - **Response:** Yes (Y)
  - **Evidence:** "Allocation was concealed in sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes, which were opened prior to anesthesia induction." (`covidence_35_verified.pdf`, Page 2, col. 2).
- **1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
  - **Evidence:** Table 1 shows age (45.18 ± 11.93 vs. 46.29 ± 11.86 yr), BMI, ASA status, sex (91 vs. 80 females), Apfel score distribution, sufentanil equivalents, anesthesia duration, and surgery duration all balanced ($P > 0.05$; Page 4).
- **Domain 1 Judgment:** **Low Risk of Bias**

---

#### Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions (Effect of Assignment)
- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial?**
  - **Response:** Probably Yes (PY)
  - **Evidence:** "In the TEAS group, electrical stimulation was initiated at 1 mA (2 Hz) and gradually increased (up to 2–20 mA) until the patient experienced a tolerable tingling sensation in the hand... In the Sham-TEAS group, identical electrode placement and procedures were followed to ensure blinding; however, the stimulator was turned off, and no electrical current was delivered." (`covidence_35_verified.pdf`, Section "TEAS protocol", Page 3, col. 2).
  - **Reasoning:** Because stimulation was titrated prior to anesthetic induction, awake participants in the TEAS arm experienced hand tingling at 2–20 mA, whereas sham participants experienced a completely silent, turned-off device with zero sensation. Awake sensory contrast compromises complete patient blinding.
- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants' assigned intervention during the trial?**
  - **Response:** Probably No (PN)
  - **Evidence:** "while the attending anesthesiologists performing the intervention could not be blinded, patients, postoperative outcome assessors, ward nurses, and statisticians remained blinded to group allocation throughout the study." (`covidence_35_verified.pdf`, Page 2, col. 2).
- **2.3 Were there deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
  - **Evidence:** Standardized anesthetic regimen (propofol, sufentanil, rocuronium, sevoflurane, dexamethasone 8 mg, palonosetron 0.075 mg). Standardized rescue antiemetic (droperidol) for Simplified PONV score $\ge 5$ (Page 3).
- **2.5 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention?**
  - **Response:** Yes (Y)
  - **Evidence:** 277 patients randomized (138 TEAS, 139 Sham); all 277 accounted for in the primary analysis (Fig. 1 and Table 2).
- **Domain 2 Judgment:** **Some Concerns** (due to sensory difference between active tingling and turned-off sham stimulator prior to induction).

---

#### Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data
- **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?**
  - **Response:** Yes (Y)
  - **Evidence:** 277 of 277 randomized patients (100.0%) completed follow-up with complete data (`covidence_35_verified.pdf`, Fig. 1 & Table 2).
- **Domain 3 Judgment:** **Low Risk of Bias**

---

#### Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome
- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
  - **Evidence:** Validated Simplified PONV Impact Scale (Myles and Wengritzky) and standard nursing records of nausea and retching/vomiting (`covidence_35_verified.pdf`, Page 3, col. 2).
- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
  - **Evidence:** "postoperative outcome assessors, ward nurses, and statisticians remained blinded to group allocation throughout the study." (`covidence_35_verified.pdf`, Page 2, col. 2).
- **Domain 4 Judgment:** **Low Risk of Bias**

---

#### Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result
- **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analyzed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan that was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available?**
  - **Response:** No Information (NI)
  - **Evidence:** "The study protocol was prospectively registered at [Anonymized]." (`covidence_35_verified.pdf`, Page 2, col. 2).
  - **Reasoning:** In the published manuscript, the trial registry name and registration number remain redacted/anonymized (`[Anonymized]`), preventing independent public verification of prospective registration date or pre-specified analysis plan details.
- **5.2 Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected from multiple outcome measurements within the outcome domain?**
  - **Response:** Probably No (PN)
  - **Evidence:** Predefined intervals (0–2, 2–6, 6–24, 24–48 h) and cumulative 0–48 h are systematically reported.
- **5.3 Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected from multiple analyses of the data?**
  - **Response:** Probably No (PN)
- **Domain 5 Judgment:** **Some Concerns** (due to anonymized/unverifiable trial registry citation).
