# Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 (RoB 2) Assessment

## Study & Result Information
- **Study Citation:** Tu LD, Li PC, Zhao Y, Feng RZ, Lv JQ. Transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation for postoperative nausea and vomiting in patients undergoing craniotomy: A randomized controlled trial. *Complementary Therapies in Clinical Practice*. 2024;54:101824. (Available online 23 December 2023).
- **Source Document in Google Drive:** `study_244_Tu_2023.pdf`
- **Trial Registration:** Chinese Clinical Trial Registry ChiCTR-TRC-13003026 (Registered in 2013); Study protocol published in *Trials* 2014;15:377.
- **Population:** 120 adult neurosurgical patients (aged 18–70 years, ASA physical status I–II) undergoing elective craniotomy requiring opening of cranium and dura under general anesthesia.
- **Intervention:** Transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) at bilateral Pericardium 6 (P6 / Neiguan) for 30 minutes in the post-anesthesia care unit upon regaining consciousness (SDZ-V nerve and muscle stimulator, Hwato; dilatational wave 2/100 Hz, current adjusted to patient-confirmed *de qi* sensation; n = 57 analyzed).
- **Comparator:** Sham TEAS at bilateral P6 for 30 minutes with identical stimulator, identical surface electrode pads, identical 2/100 Hz visual frequency display, and identical countdown alarm, but with copper wires inside the lead deliberately severed to deliver 0 mA current; patients informed they received sub-sensory stimulation (n = 58 analyzed).
- **Assessed Outcome:** Incidence of Vomiting
- **Assessed Timepoint:** 2 to 6 hours postoperatively (Key active efficacy interval)
- **Numerical Result:** 
  - TEAS group: 8 / 57 (14.0%)
  - Sham TEAS group: 17 / 58 (29.3%)
  - Difference: $\chi^2 = 3.94$, $P = 0.047$ (Table 3)

---

### Step 1: Study Design Verification
- **Experimental Design:** Individually randomized, parallel-group, sham-controlled trial (1:1 allocation ratio).
- **Unit of Randomization:** Individual patient.
- **Trial Integrity Check:** The trial design meets the criteria for standard parallel-group RoB 2 evaluation.

### Step 2: Signaling Questions & Domain Evaluations

#### Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process
- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random?**
  - **Response:** Yes (Y)
  - **Evidence:** "Patients were randomized to either the TEAS or sham TEAS group using a computer-generated random number list at a ratio of 1:1." (`study_244_Tu_2023.pdf`, Section 2.4, Page 2).
  - **Reasoning:** A computer-generated random number schedule is an unpredictable, truly random sequence generation method.
- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**
  - **Response:** Yes (Y)
  - **Evidence:** "Allocation concealment was achieved by enclosing the assignments in sealed, opaque, sequentially numbered envelopes, which were opened only after confirming eligibility and providing informed consent. Patient allocation was performed by a clinical assistant who was trained with the institutional review board policies." (`study_244_Tu_2023.pdf`, Section 2.4, Page 2).
  - **Reasoning:** Sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes (SNOSE) opened only post-consent by an independent clinical assistant ensure complete allocation concealment.
- **1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
  - **Evidence:** Table 2 demonstrates comparability across age (44.77 ± 15.74 vs. 49.24 ± 14.02 yr, $P = 0.102$), height, weight, sex (54.4% vs. 53.4% female), smoking status, anesthesia duration (246.93 ± 95.46 vs. 262.10 ± 129.88 min), surgery duration (166.49 ± 88.77 vs. 176.64 ± 103.85 min), intraoperative sufentanil dose (28.99 ± 10.78 vs. 28.16 ± 13.23 $\mu$g), and fluid infusion volume (`study_244_Tu_2023.pdf`, Section 3.1 & Table 2, Page 4).
  - **Reasoning:** No statistically significant or prognostically meaningful baseline imbalances were detected.
- **Domain 1 Judgment:** **Low Risk of Bias**

---

#### Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions (Effect of Assignment)
- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial?**
  - **Response:** Probably No (PN)
  - **Evidence:** "In the sham TEAS group, the patients had self-adhesive electrodes placed bilaterally over the P6 acupoints and connected to the SDZ-V nerve and muscle stimulators... the copper wires within the unique wire were deliberately severed to prevent the generation of electrical stimulation when activating the stimulators. However, the stimulators still exhibited the same operational power indicator and emitted the same sound. To ensure that patients were blinded to the interventions, each patient was told that a special acupoint stimulation, which cannot be felt via human sensory perception, was used for the treatment." (`study_244_Tu_2023.pdf`, Section 2.5, Page 2).
  - **Reasoning:** Identical visual appearance, electrode placement, countdown chime, and power indicators combined with explicit sub-sensory framing prevented participant unblinding.
- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants' assigned intervention during the trial?**
  - **Response:** Probably No (PN)
  - **Evidence:** "A separate research nurse who was not involved in patient management recorded the anesthesia time, surgery time... Another blinded observer (nurse) recorded the postoperative data... Outcome assessors, data collectors, and statisticians were also blinded to the group allocation." Ward doctors and nurses deciding rescue medication were blinded (`study_244_Tu_2023.pdf`, Section 2.4 & 2.6, Page 2–3). The therapist administering the unit was unblinded to current delivery, but had no contact with post-PACU care.
  - **Reasoning:** All clinical personnel managing postoperative patient care and co-interventions remained blinded.
- **2.3 Were there deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
  - **Evidence:** Prophylactic antiemetics (ondansetron 8 mg IV) were standardized for all patients 30 min before surgery completion. Rescue antiemetic criteria (metoclopramide 10 mg IM for intractable PONV) and rescue analgesic criteria (tramadol 10 mg IM for VAS > 6) were strictly protocolized (`study_244_Tu_2023.pdf`, Section 2.3, Page 2).
  - **Reasoning:** Co-interventions were protocol-driven and balanced.
- **2.4 Were these deviations balanced between groups and unlikely to have affected the outcome?**
  - **Response:** Not applicable (NA)
- **2.5 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention?**
  - **Response:** Probably Yes (PY)
  - **Evidence:** "In total, 120 patients were enrolled in the present study, and 5 patients (3 in the TEAS group and 2 in the sham TEAS group) were excluded because they met the withdrawal criteria. Three of these five patients had persistent coma, one exhibited cognitive impairment, and one demonstrated intracranial hypertension-induced vomiting (Fig. 1). Finally, data from 115 patients were analyzed." (`study_244_Tu_2023.pdf`, Section 3.1 & Fig. 1, Page 3–4).
  - **Reasoning:** Although 5 randomized participants (4.2%) were excluded post-randomization without intention-to-treat imputation, the exclusions were strictly based on pre-specified medical/neurological withdrawal criteria, balanced across groups (3 vs. 2), and represented <5% attrition.
- **Domain 2 Judgment:** **Low Risk of Bias**

---

#### Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data
- **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?**
  - **Response:** Yes (Y)
  - **Evidence:** 115 of 120 randomized patients (95.8%) completed full 24-hour follow-up with complete recorded outcome data (`study_244_Tu_2023.pdf`, Section 3.1 & Table 3, Page 4).
  - **Reasoning:** Total data completeness exceeds 95%.
- **3.2 Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?**
  - **Response:** Yes (Y)
  - **Evidence:** Documented reasons for post-randomization exclusion were primary neurosurgical/central complications: coma (n = 3), cognitive impairment (n = 1), and intracranial hypertension (n = 1), balanced 3 vs. 2 (`study_244_Tu_2023.pdf`, Section 3.1, Page 4).
  - **Reasoning:** Missingness arose from acute clinical complications unrelated to intervention tolerability or non-response.
- **3.3 Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?**
  - **Response:** Probably No (PN)
- **Domain 3 Judgment:** **Low Risk of Bias**

---

#### Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome
- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
  - **Evidence:** "Another blinded observer (nurse) recorded the postoperative data, which included any episodes of vomiting (based on vomiting action or vomiting in the mouth) at 0–2 h, 2–6 h, and 6–24 h postoperatively." (`study_244_Tu_2023.pdf`, Section 2.6, Page 3).
  - **Reasoning:** Direct observation and nursing documentation of overt vomiting/retching episodes is standard and validated.
- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
  - **Evidence:** Identical observation intervals (0–2 h, 2–6 h, 6–24 h) were enforced across all wards.
- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
  - **Evidence:** "Another blinded observer (nurse) recorded the postoperative data... Outcome assessors, data collectors, and statisticians were also blinded to the group allocation." (`study_244_Tu_2023.pdf`, Section 2.4 & 2.6, Page 2–3).
- **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
- **Domain 4 Judgment:** **Low Risk of Bias**

---

#### Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result
- **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analyzed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan that was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available?**
  - **Response:** Yes (Y)
  - **Evidence:** "This randomized blinded controlled trial was registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry in 2013 (registration number: ChiCTR-TRC-13003026). The study protocols were published in trials [13]" (`study_244_Tu_2023.pdf`, Section 2.1, Page 2).
  - **Reasoning:** Prospective registry and a published protocol (*Trials* 2014) confirm prespecified outcome intervals.
- **5.2 Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected from multiple outcome measurements within the outcome domain?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
  - **Evidence:** All pre-specified measurement intervals (0–2 h, 2–6 h, 6–24 h) and cumulative 24-h totals are presented in full in Table 3.
- **5.3 Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected from multiple analyses of the data?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
  - **Evidence:** Analysis followed standard pre-specified chi-squared tests.
- **Domain 5 Judgment:** **Low Risk of Bias**
