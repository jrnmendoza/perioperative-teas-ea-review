Discussion 
According

Chinese medicine, the Zu- 
sanli acupoints are highly effective in relieving lower 
abdominal pain. In human volunteer studies, when 
TENS was applied at the Zusanli and Hegu acupoints, 
it produced marked increases (367%) in immunoreac- 
tive metenkephalin-arg-phe 
(ir-MEAP) 
in the lumbar 
cerebrospinal fluid (10). The integration of Chinese 
acupuncture-based 
techniques 
with 
conventional 
Western practices (e.g., TENS and PCA therapies) may 
make it possible to improve the control of acute post- 
operative pain (11). This study clearly demonstrates 
the benefits of using a nonpharmacologic complimen- 
tary (or alternative) therapy in combination with PCA 
for controlling postoperative pain.

study (9), we demonstrated that

point

intensity (9 -12 mA) of TENS produced a 50% decrease 
in the postoperative opioid analgesic requirement and 
also reduced opioid-related side effects. Because many 
factors can influence the effect of this nonpharmaco-
