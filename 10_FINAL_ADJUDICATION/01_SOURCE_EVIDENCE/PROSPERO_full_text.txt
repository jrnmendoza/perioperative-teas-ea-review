Protocol characteristics associated with clinically meaningful 24-
hour opioid sparing from perioperative transcutaneous electrical
acupoint stimulation and electroacupuncture: a systematic review
and meta-regression of randomized controlled trials
John Ryan Nual Mendoza, Siv Katrin Peters, Carina Sjöberg, Pether Jildenstål
REVIEW TITLE AND BASIC DETAILS
Review title
Protocol characteristics associated with clinically meaningful 24-hour opioid sparing from perioperative
transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation and electroacupuncture: a systematic review and meta-regression of
randomized controlled trials
Review type
None
Condition or domain being studied
Postoperative Pain; Acute Pain; Postoperative Analgesia; Opioids; General Anesthesia; Acupuncture;
Electroacupuncture; Acupuncture-like Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation; Enhanced Recovery After
Surgery
Adults undergoing operative surgery under general anaesthesia. The review evaluates two electrically stimulated
acupuncture modalities: (1) transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS), delivered non-invasively through
surface electrodes over recognised body acupuncture points; and (2) needle-based electroacupuncture (EA), in
which electrical current is delivered between acupuncture needles inserted at recognised body acupuncture points.
The primary clinical outcome is cumulative systemic opioid consumption during the ﬁrst 24 postoperative hours,
converted to intravenous morphine milligram equivalents (IV MME). Pain at 24 hours is a critical co-outcome used
to determine whether opioid reduction occurs without clinically important worsening of analgesia.
Rationale for the review
Postoperative pain is commonly treated with systemic opioids, which may cause nausea and vomiting, sedation,
respiratory depression, ileus, delayed mobilisation, and persistent use. Electrical acupuncture techniques are
attractive adjuncts because they can be incorporated into multimodal perioperative care and may reduce opioid
exposure while maintaining analgesia.
Recent systematic reviews have already addressed whether TEAS, electroacupuncture, or broader acupuncture-
related interventions improve postoperative pain and opioid consumption. However, the literature remains highly
heterogeneous in treatment timing, number of sessions, cumulative stimulation duration, frequency, intensity,
comparator design, surgery type, and background analgesia. Conventional pooled efﬁcacy estimates do not tell
clinicians which reproducible protocol characteristics are associated with a clinically meaningful opioid-sparing
effect.
TEAS and EA share electrical stimulation but differ in invasiveness, delivery, sensory experience, blinding, and
implementation. Pooling them into one overall estimate may obscure clinically important differences. This review
will therefore estimate effects separately for TEAS and EA and will use sham-controlled comparisons as the
principal evidence base. Usual-care or no-stimulation comparisons will be synthesised separately as supportive
evidence.
The review is designed primarily to investigate protocol-level effect modiﬁcation. Prespeciﬁed moderators are
intervention timing, single versus repeated treatment, cumulative stimulation duration before the 24-hour endpoint,
electrical frequency, and stimulation intensity. Surgical category, regional or neuraxial analgesia, comparator
design, and risk of bias will be treated mainly as adjustment or contextual variables rather than as the principal
research focus.
Review objectives
The primary objective is to determine which protocol characteristics of perioperative TEAS or EA are associated
with greater reduction in cumulative systemic opioid consumption during the ﬁrst 24 hours after surgery, without
clinically important worsening of postoperative pain, in adults undergoing surgery under general anaesthesia.
The review will:
1. Estimate, separately, the sham-controlled effect of TEAS and the sham-controlled effect of EA on cumulative 0-
24-hour postoperative opioid consumption expressed as IV MME.
2. Estimate the corresponding effects on pain at rest and pain during movement, approximately 24 hours.
3. Examine, separately within TEAS and EA where data permit, associations between treatment effect and
intervention timing, number of treatment sessions, cumulative stimulation duration, electrical frequency, and
stimulation intensity.
4. Determine whether the magnitude of opioid reduction reaches a prespeciﬁed threshold for clinical importance
without clinically important worsening of pain.
5. Provide separate supportive syntheses for TEAS and EA compared with usual care, no stimulation, or other non-
sham controls.
6. Assess postoperative opioid consumption beyond 24 hours, intraoperative opioid requirements, pain at additional
time points, postoperative nausea and vomiting, rescue analgesia, quality of recovery, functional and
gastrointestinal recovery, length of stay, and treatment-related adverse events.
7. Assess risk of bias using Cochrane RoB 2 and certainty of evidence using GRADE.

ELIGIBILITY CRITERIA
Population
Included
• Adults aged 18 years or older undergoing an operative surgical procedure under general anaesthesia.
• General anaesthesia alone or general anaesthesia combined with regional or neuraxial anaesthesia or analgesia.
• Any surgical specialty and elective or urgent surgery, provided postoperative opioid consumption or another
eligible outcome is reported.
• Mixed-age studies when adult data can be extracted separately or when at least 90% of participants are aged 18
years or older.
Excluded
• Studies involving only children or adolescents.
• Procedures conducted exclusively under local, regional, or neuraxial anaesthesia without general anaesthesia.
• Non-surgical procedures, dental procedures without general anaesthesia, chronic pain populations without an
acute perioperative context, and interventions delivered only after recovery from the acute postoperative period.
Intervention(s) or exposure(s)
Included
Acupuncture; Electroacupuncture; Acupuncture-like Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation; Perioperative
Care; Non-Pharmacological Interventions
1. Transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS): non-invasive electrical stimulation delivered through
surface electrodes positioned over recognised or explicitly deﬁned body acupuncture points, without needle
penetration.
2. Electroacupuncture (EA): electrical stimulation delivered through one or more pairs of acupuncture needles
inserted at recognised or explicitly deﬁned body acupuncture points.
Eligible terminology may include transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation, transcutaneous electric acupoint
stimulation, transcutaneous acupoint electrical stimulation, electrical acupoint stimulation, electroacupoint
stimulation, acupuncture-like TENS delivered at acupuncture points, electroacupuncture, electric acupuncture, or
electrically stimulated acupuncture when the intervention clearly meets the deﬁnitions above.
At least one eligible stimulation session must occur before the end of the ﬁrst 24 postoperative hours. Interventions
may be preoperative, intraoperative, postoperative, or delivered across more than one perioperative phase. TEAS
or EA may be used alone or as an adjunct to balanced usual multimodal perioperative care.
Trials remain eligible when one or more protocol characteristics are incompletely reported; however, they will
contribute only to analyses for which the relevant intervention information is sufﬁciently available. Protocol
characteristics will be extracted according to STRICTA-compatible domains, including modality, timing, number of
sessions, session duration, cumulative stimulation duration before 24 hours, acupoints, frequency, waveform, pulse
width, intensity strategy, current amplitude, laterality, device, practitioner, and treatment ﬁdelity.
Excluded
• Manual acupuncture without electrical stimulation.
• Dry needling, trigger-point needling, acupuncture-point injection, laser acupuncture, moxibustion, cupping,
massage, or herbal interventions.
• Ordinary TENS or percutaneous electrical nerve stimulation delivered only at non-acupuncture locations.
• Auricular acupuncture, battleﬁeld acupuncture, auricular acupressure, electroauriculotherapy, press-tack or
intradermal needle interventions, wrist-ankle acupuncture, and buccal acupuncture.
• Electrical stimulation used only for neuromuscular monitoring, nerve localisation, or physiological measurement.
• Mixed interventions in which the independent effect of TEAS or EA cannot be separated.
• Studies in which TEAS and EA are combined within the same intervention arm and their individual contribution
cannot be separated.
Comparator(s) or control(s)
Included
PICO tags selected: Sham Acupuncture; Placebo; Usual Care
The principal analyses will use sham-controlled comparisons:
1. TEAS versus inactive or credible sham TEAS, including identical electrode placement with no current, sub-
sensory or minimally active current, or non-acupoint sham electrical stimulation.
2. EA versus sham EA, including non-penetrating sham needles, superﬁcial or non-acupoint needling, needles
connected to an inactive device, or another credible sham procedure designed to preserve participant blinding.
Usual perioperative care, standard multimodal analgesia, no TEAS or EA, waiting-list control, or attention control
will be eligible but analysed separately as supportive comparisons. Active electrical comparators, such as non-
acupoint TENS, may be included and reported separately when the contrast is clinically interpretable.
Anaesthetic techniques, regional or neuraxial analgesia, non-opioid analgesics, antiemetics, and postoperative
care pathways must be balanced between groups or sufﬁciently described to assess their potential inﬂuence.
Excluded
• Comparators containing an active acupuncture-point intervention that prevents estimation of the independent
effect of TEAS or EA.
• Groups receiving substantially different perioperative or analgesic co-interventions without adequate adjustment.
• Comparators that are insufﬁciently described to classify as sham, usual care, no treatment, or active comparator.
Study design
Only randomized study types will be included.
Included
• Individually randomized parallel-group controlled trials.
• Cluster-randomized trials when appropriate design-adjusted results are available or can be calculated.
• Crossover trials only when an eligible perioperative treatment period can be validly separated and carryover is
implausible.
Excluded
• Quasi-randomized studies, non-randomized controlled studies, observational studies, case series, case reports,
qualitative studies, reviews, and protocols without results.
• Conference abstracts without sufﬁcient information for eligibility, risk-of-bias assessment, and outcome extraction.
Such records may be listed as awaiting classiﬁcation and investigators may be contacted.
Context
Inpatient, day-surgery, ambulatory surgical, and other perioperative hospital settings in any country or healthcare
system will be eligible. Surgical specialties may include orthopaedic, abdominal, gynaecological, urological,
thoracic, cardiac, neurological, breast, head and neck, and other operative ﬁelds. No restrictions will be applied
based on sex, ethnicity, socioeconomic status, or geographic region.
Only studies for which the full-text report is published in English will be eligible for inclusion. No language restriction
will be applied at the database-search stage; reports published in languages other than English that are identiﬁed
by the unrestricted searches will be excluded during study selection on the basis of publication language.

Link to search strategy
A full search strategy is available in the full protocol as described in the Availability of full protocol section
Selection process
Studies will be screened independently by at least two people (or person/machine combination) with a process to
resolve differences.
Other relevant information about searching and screening
Database-speciﬁc TEAS search strategies will combine terminology for transcutaneous electrical stimulation at
acupuncture points with surgical or peri-operative concepts and validated randomized-trial terms.
General anaesthesia will be veriﬁed during study selection rather than required as a mandatory search concept
because exploratory testing showed that a general-anaesthesia search block failed to retrieve known eligible TEAS
reports whose anaesthetic technique was incompletely represented in titles, abstracts, or indexing.
Search results will be exported in their original formats, retained unchanged, and deduplicated using documented
automated and manual procedures.
No publication-date or language restrictions will be applied to the electronic database searches. This is intended to
maximise retrieval and avoid language-related loss of potentially relevant records at the search stage. However,
eligibility for the systematic review will be restricted to studies with a full-text report published in English. Reports
published only in languages other than English will therefore be excluded during study selection and the reason for
exclusion will be recorded as publication language.
Full-text exclusion reasons will be recorded using a prespeciﬁed hierarchy, including publication language when the
full-text report is not published in English. Excluded full-text reports with reasons will be provided as supplementary
material. Searches will be updated before manuscript submission when required.

Data extraction from published articles and reports
Data will be extracted independently by at least two people (or person/machine combination) with a process to

Authors will be asked to provide any required data not available in published reports.
Study risk of bias or quality assessment
Risk of bias will be assessed using: Cochrane RoB-2
Data will be assessed independently by at least two people (or person/machine combination) with a process to
resolve differences.
Additional information will be sought from study investigators if required information is unclear or unavailable in the
study publications/reports.
Reporting bias assessment
Risk of bias due to missing results will be assessed by comparing trial registrations, protocols, and publications.
Funnel plots and small-study-effect tests will be considered when at least 10 sufﬁciently similar studies are
available. Selective non-reporting will inform sensitivity analyses and GRADE assessments.
Certainty assessment
Certainty of evidence will be assessed using GRADE for each critical and important outcome. Randomized
evidence will start at high certainty and may be downgraded for risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness,
imprecision, or publication bias. Judgements will be explained in Summary-of-Findings tables.
OUTCOMES TO BE ANALYSED
Main outcomes
1. Cumulative systemic postoperative opioid consumption from the end of surgery to 24 hours, converted to IV
MME using prespeciﬁed conversion rules. Programmed but undelivered patient-controlled analgesia doses will not
be counted as consumed opioid. The principal effect measure will be mean difference in IV MME.
2. Postoperative pain at approximately 24 hours, assessed separately at rest and during movement where
reported. Pain scores will be converted to a 0-10 scale when valid; otherwise standardised mean differences will be
used in supportive analyses.
Clinical interpretation: a pooled opioid-sparing effect will be considered clinically meaningful when the point
estimate indicates a reduction of at least 10 mg IV MME during 0-24 hours and the corresponding pain evidence
does not permit clinically important worsening. Clinically important pain worsening is prespeciﬁed as an increase of
1.0 point or more on a 0-10 scale; the upper 95% conﬁdence limit will be examined against this margin. Sensitivity
interpretations will use an 8 mg IV MME threshold and, when calculable, a 30% relative opioid reduction.
Additional outcomes
• Cumulative postoperative opioid consumption during 0-48 hours, 0-72 hours, and the longest reported acute
postoperative interval.
• Intraoperative opioid consumption, converted to an appropriate common opioid-equivalent metric when valid.
• Pain at rest and during movement at approximately 6, 24, 48, and 72 hours and at the longest reported acute time
point.
• Postoperative nausea, vomiting, or composite PONV during 0-24 hours, 0-48 hours, and the longest reported
interval.
• Need for rescue analgesia and time to ﬁrst rescue analgesia.
• Quality of recovery measured using validated instruments such as QoR-15, QoR-40, or QoR-9.
• Patient satisfaction, gastrointestinal recovery, functional recovery, time to mobilisation, and length of hospital stay.
• Persistent postoperative opioid use beyond 30 days, when reported.
• Serious and non-serious adverse events plausibly related to TEAS or EA, including skin reactions, bleeding,
infection, electrical discomfort, needle-related adverse effects, and device-related events.
PLANNED DATA SYNTHESIS
Strategy for data synthesis
TEAS and electroacupuncture (EA) will not be combined in a pooled estimate. Analyses will be stratiﬁed by
modality. Primary comparisons will be TEAS versus credible sham TEAS and EA versus sham EA. Supportive
comparisons will evaluate TEAS and EA against usual care, no stimulation, or attention controls. Active
comparators will be analysed separately.
When at least two comparable studies are available within a modality–comparator stratum, random-effects meta-
analysis will be performed. Continuous outcomes will be pooled using inverse-variance methods and expressed as
mean differences or standardised mean differences, as appropriate. Dichotomous outcomes will be pooled as risk
ratios. Between-study variance will be estimated using restricted maximum likelihood, with Hartung–Knapp
conﬁdence intervals where appropriate.
For multi-arm trials, compatible intervention groups may be combined when justiﬁed. Otherwise, shared control
groups will be divided or correlations addressed using multivariate methods to avoid double counting. Missing
standard deviations may be derived from standard errors, conﬁdence intervals, p values, or other statistics. Median
and quantile data will be converted only when assumptions are reasonable and examined in sensitivity analyses.
Statistical heterogeneity will be assessed using forest plots, Cochran's Q, tau-squared, I-squared, and prediction
intervals. Clinical heterogeneity will be evaluated before pooling.
Prespeciﬁed protocol moderators are intervention timing; single versus repeated treatment before the 24-hour
outcome; cumulative active stimulation duration before 24 hours, analysed per 30-minute increment; electrical
frequency; and stimulation intensity. Timing will be classiﬁed as preoperative only, intraoperative-inclusive,
postoperative only, or spanning multiple perioperative phases. Frequency will be grouped as low, intermediate,
high, or alternating/mixed. Intensity will be classiﬁed as sub-sensory or ﬁxed low current, sensory-level, motor-
twitch threshold, maximum tolerated, or insufﬁciently reported. Actual current amplitude will be analysed only when
reporting is comparable.
Meta-regression will be conducted separately for TEAS and EA, without causal claims of superiority. Surgery type,
regional or neuraxial analgesia, comparator design, background multimodal analgesia, geographic setting,
publication year, and risk of bias will inform adjustment, sensitivity analysis, or interpretation.
Meta-regression will require sufﬁcient study numbers and moderator variation. Normally, at least 10 independent
estimates per predictor and four studies per categorical level will be required. Univariable models will be prioritised.
Multivariable models will be considered only with at least 20 studies and 10 studies per predictor. Collinearity will be
assessed. Results will report coefﬁcients, 95% conﬁdence intervals, residual heterogeneity, and variance
explained, and will be interpreted as study-level associations vulnerable to confounding and ecological bias.
If meta-regression is unjustiﬁed, protocol characteristics will be synthesised descriptively using structured tables
and effect-direction plots. Subgroup analyses will examine sham design, surgical category, regional or neuraxial
analgesia, background analgesia balance, and risk of bias.
Sensitivity analyses will restrict or exclude studies based on sham credibility, high risk of bias, imputed or converted
data, opioid-conversion uncertainty, unbalanced co-interventions, non-peer-reviewed reports, inﬂuential studies,
and unresolved cohort overlap. Alternative variance estimators and conﬁdence-interval methods will be tested.
Clinical thresholds of 8 mg IV MME and 30% relative opioid reduction will be examined. No network meta-analysis
is planned because transitivity and head-to-head evidence are expected to be insufﬁcient.
