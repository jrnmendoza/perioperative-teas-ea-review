Abstract
Background  A history of severe nausea and vomiting during pregnancy (SNVP) is a risk factor for postoperative nausea 
and vomiting (PONV). This study aimed to explore potentially effective treatment strategies and potential genetic factors 
underlying SNVP risk-related PONV.
Methods  A total of 140 female patients undergoing breast cancer surgery were assigned to either the study group (70 with 
SNVP) or the control group (70 with mild to moderate nausea and vomiting during pregnancy (MNVP)). Patients in each 
group were randomly assigned to two different treatment subgroups and received either ondansetron plus dexamethasone 
(OD) or OD + TEAS (ODT) (transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation, TEAS). Blood samples were collected from 
patients before induction (D0) and 24 h (D1) after surgery for growth differentiation factor 15 (GDF-15) evaluation. The 
primary outcome was the incidence of PONV within 36 h. The secondary outcome was the serum GDF-15 level.
Results  The incidence of PONV in the SNVP group was significantly higher than that in the MNVP group within 24 h 
(P < 0.005). In the SNVP group, ODT-treated patients had less PONV than those in the OD-treated group during the 6–12 h 
(P = 0.033) and 12–24 h (P = 0.008) intervals, while within 6 h, there were fewer vomiting cases in the ODT-treated group 
(SNVP-ODT vs. SNVP-OD, 7/33 vs. 19/35, P = 0.005). The preoperative GDF-15 serum levels in patients with SNVP were 
significantly higher (P = 0.004). Moreover, higher preoperative GDF-15 serum levels correlated with a higher incidence of 
PONV (P = 0.043).
Conclusions  TEAS showed significant effect on PONV treatment in patients with SNVP. A higher serum GDF-15 level was 
associated with a history of SNVP, as well as a higher risk of PONV.
Keywords  Breast cancer surgery · Transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation · Nausea and vomiting during 
pregnancy · Postoperative nausea and vomiting · Growth differentiation factor 15
Introduction
Patients undergoing breast cancer surgery under general anes-
thesia experience a common adverse phenomenon called post-
operative nausea and vomiting (PONV) [1]. PONV can lead 
to prolonged hospital stay, wound dehiscence, and delayed 
nutrition, and is associated with clinical and economic conse-
quences [2, 3]. It is widely known that the main risk factors for 
PONV are female sex, non-smoking status, history of motion 
sickness or PONV, and the use of postoperative opioids [4]. 
Patients with breast cancer usually face at least two risk fac-
tors: female sex and non-smoking, which is a high-risk popu-
lation for PONV. However, according to the results of one of

pregnancy (SNVP) is another risk factor for PONV in breast 
cancer patients. Although ondansetron combined with dexa-
methasone is an effective routine prophylactic plan, it worked 
poorly in patients with SNVP, with approximately 50% expe-
riencing PONV and more than 70% of the PONV patients 
being severe cases [5]. Based on this background, we aimed 
to explore potential effective treatment strategies and possible 
causes of PONV in those SNVP-risk patients. Additionally, 
the association between SNVP and PONV was reconfirmed.
Transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) 
is a form of acupuncture that applies electrical stimulation 
at specific acupoints through cutaneous electrodes. TEAS is 
a non-pharmacological and non-invasive treatment method 
that has been used in perioperative anesthesia management 
to improve patient recovery, including PONV prevention 
[6–8] and postoperative pain reduction [9]. In this study, 
we evaluated whether TEAS, along with ondansetron and 
dexamethasone, could decrease the risk of PONV in patients 
with SNVP. According to the theory of traditional Chinese 
medicine, bilateral Hegu (LI4) and Neiguan (PC6) were 
chosen as acupuncture points, which have been frequently 
recommended for PONV prevention [10–12].
PONV is a multifactorial condition. Hereditary factors 
might play a crucial role in predisposing patients with SNVP 
risk to PONV. Although hereditary factors associated with 
the development of PONV or nausea and vomiting during 
pregnancy (NVP) have been frequently discussed [13, 14], 
there was no identical genetic element has been reported 
to be associated with either PONV or NVP. In 2018, Fejzo 
et al. [15] published a genome-wide association study that 
identified growth differentiation factor 15 (GDF-15) as a 
possible genetic factor influencing SNVP. Another inter-
esting study by Fejzo et al. [16], which was published in 
Nature, affirmed that GDF15 is linked to maternal risk of 
nausea and vomiting during pregnancy. GDF15 triggers ano-
rexia through the induction of nausea and/or emetic neuro-
circuitry [17]. GDF15 signaling has also been implicated in 
the development of NVP [18]. Accordingly, we hypothesized 
that GDF-15 might be an identical genetic element associ-
ated with both PONV and NVP, predisposing patients with 
SNVP to the risk of PONV. In summary, this study recon-
firmed the association between SNVP and PONV, explored 
treatment strategies of SNVP-risk patients, and evaluated 
serum levels to determine whether GDF-15 is a genetic fac-
tor that increases the development of PONV in SNVP-risk 
patients.
Methods
This prospective trial was performed between Febru-
ary and August 2022, and prospectively registered in 
Chictr.org.cn registry system on 28 November 2021 
(ChiCTR2100053752). Ethical approval for this study 
(approval number: 21/391-3062) was provided by the Ethi-
cal Committee of National Cancer Center/Cancer Hospi-
tal, Beijing, China on 9 September 2021. This work has 
been reported in line with the Consolidated Standards of 
Reporting Trials (CONSORT) Guidelines (www.​conso​rt-​
state​ment.​org) [19].
Study participants
Seventy sequential female patients with a history of 
SNVP, who were planning to undergo breast cancer sur-
gery, were enrolled in this prospective study. At the same 
time, patients with mild-moderate nausea and vomiting 
during pregnancy (MNVP) (who were operated on by the 
same surgeons) were chosen as controls (SNVP group vs. 
MNVP group). Written informed consent was obtained 
from all enrolled patients. The inclusion criteria were as 
follows: (1) age 18–60 years, (2) history of at least one 
full-term pregnancy, (3) American Society of Anesthe-
siologists physical status I or II, and (4) unilateral breast 
cancer confirmed by histopathology or cytopathology 
before surgery. Exclusion criteria included a history of 
motion sickness, diabetes, gastrointestinal diseases, pep-
tic ulcers, endocrinological disorders, liver diseases, use 
of corticosteroids or antiemetic medications within 24 h 
before surgery, a history of allergy to any medication 
used in the study protocol, active drug abuse, pregnancy 
or breastfeeding, and uncertainty of the severity of NVP.
An investigator collected the patients’ history of SNVP 
before surgery. For the classification of the severity of 
NVP in participants, we referred to the method used by 
Zhang [20] in a study that found familial aggregation 
of SNVP. This study defined the degree of NVP based 
on the methods used to determine the severity of NVP 
in the participants’ family members. Patients were asked 
to report the severity of NVP according to the following 
definitions [20]: (1) No NVP-no nausea or vomiting. (2) 
Very little NVP-felt nauseous and/or vomited for less than 
seven days. (3) Typical NVP-nausea and vomiting last-
ing more than seven days; able to sustain a normal daily 
routine; without weight loss; no need to consult a health 
professional. (4) More severe NVP-persistent nausea and 
vomiting that interfered with normal daily routine may 
have consulted a health professional and may have lost 
a few pounds. (5) Hyperemesis Gravidarum-persistent 
nausea and vomiting with weight loss that significantly 
interfered with daily routine; medical treatments, such as 
medications or nutritional therapy, were needed to prevent 
weight loss and/or nausea/vomiting. In this study, patients 
with items 1–3 were regarded as having mild-moderate 
NVP (MNVP), while 4–5 were defined as having SNVP.

Randomization
Seventy patients from each group were then randomly 
allocated into the ondansetron plus dexamethasone 
(Ondan + Dex, OD) or Ondan + Dex + TEAS (ODT) treat-
ment subgroups (SNVP-OD group vs. SNVP-ODT group, 
MNVP-OD group vs. MNVP-ODT group). Prior to study 
initiation, 140 sequentially numbered envelopes containing 
the allocations were prepared. A physician independent of 
the study randomly inserted 70 treatment strategies for each 
group into the envelope. A random allocation sequence was 
generated by using computer-generated random numbers. 
The researchers opened an envelope to determine the treat-
ment strategy before the induction of general anesthesia. 
This was a double-blinded study. The investigator who 
administered the interventions was not blinded. Outcomes 
were assessed by trained investigators who were blinded 
to the patients’ allocation. Patients and other clinical staff, 
including anesthesiologists, surgeons, and ward staff, were 
unaware of this allocation.
Intervention
Standard general anesthesia was induced with propofol 
1–2 mg ­kg−1, sufentanil 0.3–0.6 μg ­kg−1, and cis-atracurium 
0.2–0.4 mg ­kg−1. Additionally, 5 mg of dexamethasone was 
used as antiemetic prophylaxis in all enrolled patients [21]. 
After inserting a laryngeal mask airway, the patients were 
mechanically ventilated to maintain the end-tidal carbon 
dioxide concentration at 35–45 mmHg, with a fresh gas 
flow of 2 L/min 60% oxygen. 1.5–2.5% sevoflurane was 
constantly inhaled for maintenance of anesthesia. Sufen-
tanil 0.1 μg ­kg−1 was added intraoperatively, as required. 
At the end of the surgery, 100 mg flurbiprofen axetil and 
4 mg ondansetron were administered to all patients. After 
the operation, all patients were extubated and transferred to 
the post-anesthesia care unit.
Before anesthetic induction, a HANS-200A Acupoint 
Stimulator (Nanjing Jisheng Company, China) was used to 
stimulate the bilateral Neiguan (PC6) and Hegu (LI4) acu-
points for 30 min. The LI4 acupoint is positioned between 
the first and second metacarpal bones at the highest point of 
prominence when the thumb and index finger are adducted 
[22]. With the forearm supine, the PC6 acupoint is located 
between the flexor carpi radialis muscle and palmaris longus 
tendon, 2 cm proximal to the midpoint of the volar trans-
verse carpal crease [22]. The frequency of the TEAS device 
was set at 2/100 Hz. In the TEAS-treated group, an intensity 
of 10 mA was administered. Meanwhile, Participants in the 
control group received sham stimulation at the same points 
using sham electrode wires, cutting the output wires and 
resulting in no current output being delivered to the patient 
despite being connected. The researchers also informed each 
patient that they might not have felt a tingling sensation 
when the intervention was applied. Additionally, to make 
the simulation as realistic as possible, the stimulator with 
the dummy electrode wires had the same operating power 
indicator and sound as the normal electrode wires, indicat-
ing that the stimulator was operating effectively even though 
there was actually no current output.
Outcomes
The primary outcome was the incidence of PONV within 
36 h. Episodes of vomiting and severity of nausea were 
recorded at 2, 6, 12, 24, and 36 h after the operation. The 
degree of nausea was measured using a visual analog scale 
(VAS; 0 = no nausea to 10 = most severe nausea) [23]. A 
score of < 5 was considered mild nausea and ≥ 5 was con-
sidered severe nausea. Vomiting episodes ≥ 2 were consid-
ered severe and those < 2 were considered mild. For severe 
PONV, 4 mg ondansetron was administered intravenously. 
Postoperative pain was assessed simultaneously using VAS 
(0 = no pain to 10 = most severe pain). If the pain VAS score 
was ≥ 3, 100 mg flurbiprofen axetil was administered.
The secondary outcome was the serum GDF-15 level. 
GDF15’s short half-life makes it possible to track changes 
in serum levels within hours[24]. Most PONV occurs within 
24 h after the operation. Therefore, we chose to detect the 
level of GDF-15 at the 24 h postoperative point to analyze 
the association between GDF-15 levels and the incidence of 
PONV. Blood samples were collected from the patients and 
stored in heparinized tubes before induction (D0) and 24 h 
(D1) after surgery in the ward. The samples were centrifuged 
for 20 min after collection, and the extracted plasma was 
frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C until analysis. 
Plasma GDF-15 concentrations were measured using ELISA 
(Human GDF-15 ELISA Kit, Multisciences China) with a 
linear range from 15 to 1000 pg ­ml−1.
Statistical analysis
Sample sizes were calculated based on our preliminary 
experiment. We enrolled 11 and 13 patients in the SNVP-
OD and SNVP-ODT groups, respectively. The incidence 
rates of PONV in the two groups were 54.5% and 23.1%, 
respectively. Therefore, it was determined that in order to 
detect a significant difference with a power of 0.8 at α = 0.05, 
there would need to be at least 34 patients in each group. 35 
patients in each treatment subgroup were recruited to ensure 
a sufficiently large sample size. The SPSS 23.0 for windows 
(SPSS, Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) was used for data analysis. 
Normally distributed continuous data are expressed as mean 
(SD) and were analyzed by analysis of variance (ANOVA), 
independent-sample t-test, or paired t-test. Non-normally 
distributed variables are presented as medians (interquartile

The incidence of PONV in different groups
The incidence of PONV in the SNVP group was signifi-
cantly higher than that in the MNVP group at 2, 6, 12, 
and 24 h, with no significant differences at 36 h after 
the operation (Table 2). Overall, the incidence of PONV 
was lower in the MNVP group than in the SNVP group 
(Table 2). Severe PONV (nausea score ≥ 5 and/or vomit-
ing episode ≥ 2) occurred more often in the SNVP group 
than in the MNVP group (25/68 vs. 3/70, P < 0.001). 
Among the severe cases, in the SNVP group, 14 occurred 
within 6 h after the operation, while 7 within 2 h, 3 within 
12 h, and 1 within 24 h (in the MNVP group, 1 within 
2 h, 1 within 6 h, and 1 within 12 h). In the subgroup 
analysis, ODT-treated patients in the SNVP group 
(SNVP-ODT group) had less PONV than the patients 
in the SNVP-OD group during the 6–12 h (P = 0.033) 
and 12–24 h (P = 0.008) intervals after the operation 
(Table 3), whereas less PONV occurred in the patients 
in the MNVP-ODT group than in the MNVP-OD group 
during the 0–2 h (P = 0.022) and 2–6 h (P = 0.04) intervals 
after the operation (Table 3). Although no significant dif-
ference in PONV incidence was observed during 0–6 h 
intervals between the OD- and ODT-treated groups in 
the SNVP group, there were fewer vomiting cases in the 
SNVP-ODT group than in the SNVP-OD group (SNVP-
ODT vs. SNVP-OD, 7/33 vs. 19/35, P = 0.005).
Association of GDF‑15 with the history of SNVP 
and the incidence of PONV
The postoperative mean serum levels of GDF-15 on Day 
1 were significantly higher than those on day 0 in all the 
patients (Fig. 2, P < 0.001). The preoperative serum concen-
trations of GDF-15 in patients receiving total mastectomy 
plus axillary dissection were significantly higher than those 
in patients receiving other surgical procedures (total mastec-
tomy plus axillary dissection vs. other surgical procedures, 
153.14 ± 63.60 vs. 106.40 ± 44.76, P < 0.001), suggesting 
that higher GDF-15 levels might predict more aggressive 
surgical procedures. Moreover, it was found that the preop-
erative serum concentrations of GDF-15 in patients receiv-
ing neo-adjuvant chemotherapy were significantly higher 
than in those who did not receive the therapy (with neo-
adjuvant chemotherapy vs. without neo-adjuvant chemo-
therapy, 148.39 ± 70.06 vs. 116.08 ± 48.89, P = 0.002). Fur-
thermore, the preoperative GDF-15 serum levels in patients 
with SNVP were significantly higher than those in patients 
with MNVP (Fig. 3a, P = 0.004). At the same time, a higher 
preoperative GDF-15 serum level was also associated with 
a higher incidence of PONV (Fig. 3b, P = 0.043). The base-
line GDF-15 serum levels in patients with SNVP-OD and 
SNVP-ODT, or with MNVP-OD and MNVP-ODT were all 
similar (Fig. 3c, P > 0.05).
Discussion

that a history of SNVP in patients led to a higher incidence 
of severe PONV. In-depth exploration of treatment methods 
revealed that TEAS significantly improved the therapeutic 
effect within 24 h after the operation in patients with SNVP 
risk, in which the incidence of PONV was significantly 
reduced during 6–24 h, and the vomiting symptom was 
effectively controlled during 0–6 h by the ODT. A slightly 
positive effect was observed in the MNVP group. Analysis

This study tested the GDF-15 serum concentrations to 
verify whether it is a genetic factor predisposing patients 
with a history of SNVP to PONV. GDF-15 is a genetic 
factor that correlates with the development of emesis [17], 
nausea and vomiting during pregnancy [25], and chemo-
therapy-induced nausea and anorexia [26]. Serum levels 
of mature GDF15 dramatically increase during pregnancy 
and in many diseases, including cardiovascular disease, 
cancer, tissue damage, and liver and kidney diseases [27]. 
In this study, GDF-15 levels were significantly elevated 
after surgical procedures, which corroborated the increase 
in GDF-15 levels during tissue damage. GDF-15 is asso-
ciated with poor prognosis in cancer patients [28, 29]. 
In contrast, this study found that higher GDF-15 levels 
are associated with more aggressive surgical procedures.

during pregnancy [16]. However, whether GDF-15 levels

This study has several limitations. First, plasma GDF-
15 concentrations were only measured on D0 and D1, and 
shorter or more extended periods might lead to the discov-
ery of more information. Second, owing to unpredictable 
sentinel lymph node involvement, the number of different 
surgical procedures was not balanced between the different 
treatment subgroups. The number of patients who underwent 
total mastectomy and axillary dissection was significantly 
higher in the SNVP-OD group than in the SNVP-ODT 
group. At the same time, the number of cases receiving seg-
mental mastectomy and axillary dissection was not well bal-
anced in the MNVP-ODT and MNVP-OD groups. Although 
the mean surgical time and opioid consumption of the differ-
ent treatment subgroups were similar, it would be better if 
the number of different surgical types was balanced.
In conclusion, our study confirmed that women with a 
history of SNVP had a significantly higher incidence of 
PONV and severe PONV after breast cancer surgery than 
women without SNVP. TEAS is an effective treatment for 
PONV in patients with SNVP. A higher circulating level of 
GDF-15 was associated with SNVP history and a higher 
risk of PONV, establishing GDF-15 as a genetic factor that 
might predispose patients with a history of SNVP to PONV.
