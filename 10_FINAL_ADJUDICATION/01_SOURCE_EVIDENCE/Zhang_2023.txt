Abstract
Background Facilitating the recurrence of spontaneous voiding is considered to be a way to prevent urinary retention
after surgery, which is of great importance in cholecystectomy. This study aimed to assess the effect of transcuta-
neous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) on spontaneous voiding recovery after laparoscopic cholecystectom.
Methods Participants who underwent elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy were randomly assigned to either the
TEAS group or the sham group. Active TEAS or sham TEAS at speciﬁc acupuncture points was conducted intra-
operatively and postoperatively. The primary outcome was the recovery speed of spontaneous voiding ability after
surgery and secondary outcomes included postoperative urinary retention (POUR), voiding dysfunction, pain, anxiety
and depression, and early recovery after surgery.
Results A total of 1,948 participants were recruited and randomized to TEAS (n = 975) or sham (n = 973) between
August 2018 and June 2020. TEAS shortens the time delay of the ﬁrst spontaneous voiding after laparoscopic
cholecystectomy (5.6 h [IQR, 3.7–8.1 h] in the TEAS group vs 7.0 h [IQR, 4.7–9.7 h] in the sham group)
(p \ 0.001). The TEAS group experienced less POUR (p = 0.020), less voiding difﬁculty (p \ 0.001), less anxiety
and depression (p \ 0.001), reduced pain (p = 0.007), and earlier ambulation (p = 0.01) than the sham group.
Conclusions Our results showed that TEAS is an effective approach to accelerate the recovery of spontaneous
voiding and reduce POUR which facilitates recovery for patients after laparoscopic cholecystectomy.
Introduction
Gallstone disease is one of the most common and costly
gastroenterological disorders. It is estimated that nearly
700,000 cholecystectomy surgeries are performed annually
in the USA [1] and the incidence is increasing in devel-
oping countries [2, 3]. Urinary retention is one of the major
complications after laparoscopic cholecystectomy, affect-
ing 4.7–15.8% patients, and it is associated with prolonged
hospital stay, increased costs, and patient anxiety and dis-
comfort [4–6].
Elective
laparoscopic
cholecystectomy
without
catheterization has become popular in recent years because
of its minimal invasiveness [7]. However, it may impair
spontaneous voiding ability due to diminished awareness of
bladder sensation after surgical procedures [8]. Delayed

Acupuncture has gained popularity as a non-pharma-

function. Recent clinical trials have shown that acupunc-
ture and related methods can reduce the incidence of
postoperative urinary retention (POUR) after orthopedic,
obstetric, gynecological, and gastrointestinal surgeries

(TEAS) applies transcutaneous electrical stimulation to
acupuncture points instead of needles and has demon-
strated beneﬁts in regulating voiding in animal studies
[15, 16]. However, these studies had limitations, including
a small sample size, inadequate allocation concealment,
etc.
Therefore, we conducted a large-scale, single-center,
double-blind randomized trial to assess the efﬁcacy of
TEAS for spontaneous voiding recovery after laparoscopic
cholecystectomy.
Methods
Study design
This prospective, double-blind trial was conducted at
Nankai Hospital, a tertiary hospital trusted by China
National Natural Science Foundation between August 21,
2018, and June 17, 2020. This study was approved by the
local institutional review board, and written informed
consent was obtained from all subjects participating in the
trial. The trial was registered prior to patient enrollment at
clinicaltrials.gov
(NCT03631160)
and
conducted
in
accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.
Patients
Patients were included if they were 40–75 years old, the
age at which gallbladder stones are most likely to
occur[17]. Exclusion criteria were preventive or intraop-
erative indwelling catheter, urinary tract obstruction, or
infections within 3 months (such as moderate or severe
benign prostatic hyperplasia, stress urinary incontinence,
urinary tract infections, etc.), critical condition (preopera-
tive American Society of Anesthesiologists, [ASA] grade
C IV), severe renal impairment (need to undergo renal
replacement therapy), severe liver and kidney dysfunction
(Child–Pugh grade C), contraindications to TEAS (such as
skin allergy, infection, pruritus, or wearing a pacemaker),
and participating in other clinical investigators within
3 months prior to enrollment in this study. Besides,
patients with previous acupuncture treatment experience
were excluded for they had a higher probability of
acupuncture insensitivity.
Randomization and blinding
Eligible participants were randomly assigned to the TEAS
or sham group in a 1:1 ratio using block randomization.
The randomized numbers were enclosed in sequentially
numbered, sealed, opaque envelopes and revealed by the
acupuncturist in charge prior to the start of surgery. The
participants, outcome assessors, staff responsible for the
postoperative care and statisticians were blinded to the
treatment allocation.
Interventions
A full-time acupuncturist who is a registered TCM prac-
titioner with eight years of clinical experience provided
TEAS and sham treatments independently. In the inter-
vention, she remained silent regarding the allocation and
effects of TEAS. However, she informed every patient
before performing the intervention that they may or may
not feel a tingling sensation around the calf and lower back
during TEAS treatment. The participants in the TEAS
group received stimulation at four locations: Ciliao (BL32,
located at the third sacral foramen), Sanyinjiao (SP6,
located 3 cun proximal to the medial malleolus), Zhongji
(CV3, located 4 cun below the belly button), and Guanyuan
(CV4, located 3 cun below the belly button) (Fig. 1). In the
TEAS group, a low-frequency pulse electroacupuncture
stimulator (Hwato brand model no. SDZ—IV, Suzhou
Medical Appliances Co., Ltd, Suzhou, China) was attached
to four acupuncture points. TEAS was started 30 min
before the induction of anesthesia and lasted until 45 min
after the surgery. The stimulator applied a 2-Hz sparse
wave, and the intensity was gradually increased until the
strongest needle-like tingling sensation that patients could
tolerate was reached [18]. Participants in the sham group
received a sham stimulus at the acupoints by cutting off the
output wires and no current output was delivered to the
patient, even though it was turned on. This approach is
based on a clinical study of acupuncture treatment and
shown to be free of de qi reactions in patients [19]. In order
to make the simulation as realistic as possible, when
switched on, the apparatus with sham electrode lines had
the same working power indicator and sound as those with
normal electrode lines, suggesting to participants that the
apparatus is functional even though it actually had no
current output.

Outcomes
The primary outcome was the recovery of spontaneous
voiding ability after surgery, evaluated by the time between
the surgery end point and the ﬁrst spontaneous voiding time
point. The above was recorded in a perioperative bladder
diary, a tool commonly recommended for investigating
patients with lower LUTS, by the accompanying person [21].
The secondary outcomes included POUR, voiding dif-
ﬁculty, oliguria, nocturia, postoperative pain, sleep quality,
anxiety and depression, vomiting, early ambulation, length
of hospital stay, and hospital costs. POUR was diagnosed
based on clinical symptoms, ultrasound assessment, and the
requirement for bladder catheterization [22]. All treatment-
related and treatment-unrelated adverse events were doc-
umented throughout the trial.
Statistical analysis
Sample size calculation was based on data from our pilot
trial done in 2016 (n = 50) in which the delay of recovery
of
spontaneous
voiding
ability
after
surgery
was
6.0 ± 3.0 h in the TEAS group and 6.5 ± 3.3 h in the
sham group, respectively. The minimum sample size
required was 823 patients for each group, at 80% power
and one-sided test with a 2.5% signiﬁcance level. To
account for dropout (assuming a dropout rate of approxi-
mately 15%), we sought to enroll 970 patients in each
group. Statistical analysis was done using PASS software,

and mean with standard deviation for normally distributed
data. Comparisons of the parameters between the TEAS
group and the sham group were conducted using the t-test or

Discussion
In this study, we found that TEAS was an effective treat-
ment for accelerating the recovery of spontaneous voiding
after
laparoscopic
cholecystectomy.
It
is
effective
regardless of sex, age, duration of surgery or PCIA.
Moreover, TEAS is more effective in mem than in women,
as well as in patients with PCIA than those without PCIA.
The study also proved that TEAS is effective in relieving
pain and anxiety and reducing POUR and voiding difﬁculty
after laparoscopic cholecystectomy.
Spontaneous urination is considered as one of the most
important criteria to assess bladder voiding capacity
[12, 23]. Moreover, restoring the ability to urinate after
surgery is crucial to the prevention and treatment of
POUR[24]. Coyle et al. also pointed out that delayed return
of normal bladder function can lead to POUR [25]. In
addition, the recovery of spontaneous urination within 6 h
after surgery was used as a postoperative indicator to guide
postoperative catheterization and discharge at the Univer-
sity of Texas Southwestern Medical Center [26]. In our
study, the recovery time of postoperative spontaneous
urination in patients after TEAS was 5.6 h, which was 1.
3 h faster than in the control group and shorter than the
threshold of 6 h for catheter insertion mentioned above. A
recent study exploring interventions for patients with uri-
nary difﬁculties after anorectal surgery showed that the
median time to spontaneous voiding was 8 h without
intervention and 6 h after moxibustion treatment (an acu-
point treatment that shares its theoretical background with
acupuncture) which was consistent with our study [14].
Several etiologies of postoperative urination disorders
exist, including anesthetic disruption of bladder innerva-
tion, bladder overdistention, and bladder outlet obstruction
[27]. General anesthetics, including sedative-hypnotic and
volatile agents, are thought to inhibit autonomic control of
urinary function at the level of the pontine micturition
center, and voluntary control of the cortex [22]. Urinary
retention has adopted many deﬁnitions in the literature, and
it can best be described as the inability to spontaneously
and adequately empty the bladder [22]. However, the
diagnosis of POUR is often arbitrary, and its reported
incidence widely varies due to the lack of deﬁning criteria.

TEAS has been widely used in clinical practice as a
noninvasive, economical, and easy treatment for pain,
gastrointestinal disorders, and reproductive medicine [29].
In the present study, the BL32, CV3, and CV4 points were
selected as the primary local acupoints and the SP6 points
as the distal acupoints. BL-32 is located in the third sacral
foramen and plays a role in bladder control by modulating
the nervous system [30]. CV3 and CV4 are located near the
bladder and have been shown to partially reduce inﬂam-
mation and edema and regulate vesical pressure by
inhibiting hyperpolarization-activated cyclic nucleotide-
gated channel protein expression [13, 31]. SP6 is associated
with pain management and improved voiding dysfunction
when used with BL32, CV3, and CV4; however, the
underlying mechanism remains unclear [12, 23, 32].

Strengths and limitations
The strengths of this study include the large sample size.
The study also used a sham stimulus to avoid subjective
bias in the control group, which made the results more
reliable. However, there are several important limitations
to this study. First, it was a single-center study, which may
have limited generalizability. However, the fact that this
study involved 51 surgeons, 25 anesthesiologists, and a
relatively large number of patients can mitigate these
shortcomings to some extent. Second, despite performed
meticulous blinding, there may be a potential placebo
effect. Third, we only applied 2-Hz sparse wave signals to
the stimulator; other frequencies may be tested and com-
pared in the next step.
In conclusion, this study suggests that perioperative
TEAS
signiﬁcantly
improves
spontaneous
urination
recovery in patients after laparoscopic cholecystectomy,
which may be beneﬁcial for better recovery after surgery.
Further studies, such as those conducted in the current trial,
are needed to explore the underling mechanisms of TEAS.
