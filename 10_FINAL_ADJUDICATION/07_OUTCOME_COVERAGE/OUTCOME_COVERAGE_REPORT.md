# Remaining outcome coverage: harms and patient-reported recovery

20 September 2026. Coverage addendum to the v38 analysis, not a replacement analytical release.

## Conclusion

The review must **not** say that patient-reported recovery evidence is absent or that Yu 2020 is its only source. Searching the available full texts of all 70 included reports, followed by examination of the relevant methods, results, tables and selected PDF pages, identified:

- **16 reports using validated QoR instruments:** eight QoR-40, seven QoR-15, and one QoR-9.
- **Nine reports assessing satisfaction or willingness to repeat treatment**, with variable reporting completeness.
- **Two additional health-related quality-of-life reports:** SF-12 and EORTC-QLQ-C30.
- **Two additional acceptability reports**, kept distinct from satisfaction with analgesia.
- **32 safety-relevant report entries**, including explicit zero statements, nonzero reactions, limited perioperative side-effect statements, postoperative bleeding withdrawals and one all-cause mortality result. This is not 32 uniformly ascertained intervention-safety studies.
- **No eligible result located for persistent postoperative opioid use beyond 30 days.** This is a reporting gap, not zero persistent users.

These are report counts, not independent-trial counts or mutually exclusive categories. The 70-report/69-family accounting is unchanged. Excluded reports were not re-screened.

## What was checked

The registered protocol explicitly includes validated QoR instruments, satisfaction, functional recovery, mobilisation, length of stay, persistent opioid use **beyond 30 days**, and serious/non-serious events plausibly related to TEAS or EA. See `01_SOURCE_EVIDENCE/PROSPERO_full_text.txt`, “Additional outcomes” and “Strategy for data synthesis”.

The audit used the canonical 70-report registry to select source texts. Searches covered instrument names, satisfaction/acceptability, local treatment reactions, general adverse-event statements, and long-term opioid terminology. Matched statements were distinguished from background claims, references, eligibility criteria, and descriptions of other studies. Source-page anchors and PDF/text hashes accompany every positive evidence record. Three ambiguous tables were also rendered and visually inspected under the PDF workflow: Huang 2024 Table 4, He 2026 hepatectomy Table 3, and Jiang 2026 Table 5.

This was a search-assisted, AI-conducted coverage assessment, not an independent second extraction or a claim that all supplements have been obtained. “Not located” is bounded by the available report text. Sleep, anxiety, cognition, pain and clinician-assessed function are not automatically interchangeable with validated global QoR or satisfaction. This audit does not certify exhaustive coverage of every ancillary sleep/anxiety/function scale.

### Compatible quantitative bodies identified

At least three fixed-time bodies have numerical mean/SD data available for a new, result-specific synthesis:

1. **QoR-40 approximately 24 h, TEAS versus sham:** Yao 2015 and Yu 2020, combined analysed N=131. Chen 2015 additionally provides medians/IQRs, not direct mean/SD data.
2. **QoR-40 24 h, TEAS versus usual care:** Liang 2021 and Pan 2023, combined analysed N=175. Their corrected comparator class must be retained.
3. **QoR-15 approximately 24 h, TEAS versus sham:** Hou 2023, Wu 2025, Xing 2022 and Zhou 2025, combined analysed N=327. For Xing, use NTG versus NG, both with TAP block; the general-anesthesia-only arm would confound TEAS with the block. Hou's D2 denotes postoperative day 1.

These are **candidate input populations, not pooled-effect results**. Clinical comparability, exact-result RoB, model construction and GRADE have not been added by this coverage audit. The registered rule is to pool at least two comparable studies within the relevant modality/comparator stratum; therefore a manuscript should not claim “no synthesis possible because only one QoR study exists.” Completing these analyses is a real remaining task, not a request for reviewer approval.

POD2, POD3, POD7 and discharge outcomes must remain separate. No new claim of clinically meaningful QoR improvement is made here. In particular, a statistically favourable recovery result cannot establish the registered joint opioid/pain criterion.

### Why some QoR results are descriptive or held

- **Chen 2015:** median/IQR global QoR-40, with possible ceiling/skew; no direct mean/SD.
- **Gao 2022:** domain findings and a referenced Supplementary Table 1. The needed supplement/global variance was not located in this audit. PONV versus non-PONV subgroup comparisons cannot replace randomized arm comparisons.
- **Grech 2016:** female subgroup and graph-only mean/SE; no guessed values or denominators.
- **He 2026 hepatectomy:** parentheses are explicitly labelled *range*, not SD or IQR.
- **Lu 2022:** median/min/max with wide and asymmetric bounded-score ranges. The 24 h median is lower in TEAS; do not describe all recovery studies as favourable.
- **Huang 2025:** EA/usual-care median/min/max at discharge, not at 24 h.
- **Zheng 2025:** conflicting 48 h contrasts and percentage labels, no reconciled arm dispersion. Retain a specific QoR source-conflict hold rather than borrowing another outcome's SD.
- **Zhou 2025:** the POD3 printed P value does not agree with its nearly identical means/SDs. Flag it; do not replace calculated tests with the printed P value.
- **Zhu 2022:** exact QoR timing not specified. Do not assign 24 h merely because pain was measured then.

### Satisfaction and acceptability

The nine reports are Chen 2015, Gu 2019, Liu 2026 ESD, Lu 2021, Ntritsou 2014, Sim 2002, Sun 2017, Szmit 2021 and Yao 2015. They include 10-point scores, a 0–100 score, ordered categories, a 6-point Likert scale, “extremely satisfied” on a 5-point scale, and willingness to repeat EA. Several give only narrative or overall percentages.

Report these descriptively by instrument and time; a single pooled “satisfaction” RR is not presently justified. In particular:

- Gu 2019's narrative percentages conflict with its category counts. Preserve the table counts and flag the discrepancy.
- Liu 2026 ESD labels values as mean/SD in the table but calls them “mean ranks” in prose; timing is also unclear.
- Lu 2021's scale endpoints were not located in the main report.
- Yao's threshold of at least 8/10 is not the same endpoint as Sun's 5/5.
- Ntritsou's “all satisfied” wording does not establish identical ordinal distributions.
- Sim's 65% willingness to repeat and Szmit's “nearly 90%” do not supply exact comparative counts.

Huang 2024's acceptance/discomfort VAS and Yang 2024's narrative treatment acceptability are recorded separately. They do not repair missing satisfaction distributions in other trials.

## Harms

An overall statement of “no adverse events” is false. Examples of directly relevant reporting include:

- **Guo 2023:** electrode dermatitis, 1 versus 1; electrical discomfort, 2 versus 0. Its all-cause 6 versus 9 AE counts include unrelated events and must not be labelled TEAS-related.
- **Liu 2026 burn:** one skin redness/itching withdrawal in each randomized arm. The denominator for these withdrawals is **44 per arm**, not the 43 efficacy completers.
- **Zhang 2023:** treatment-related redness (2 versus 1) and itching (0 versus 1) are tabulated separately from voiding-related events. Event types must not be summed into unique patients without overlap information.
- **Lu 2021 and Szmit 2021:** electrode discomfort or mild skin irritation occurred.
- **Huang 2024:** two contralateral-EA and one ipsilateral-EA needle/tolerance incidents; table headings and one AE percentage imply different denominators.
- **Zhu 2022:** three combined-treatment patients had needling-site pain/itching; two declined the second treatment.
- **Yang 2020:** hematoma, sleeplessness and transient sharp pain are mentioned without counts. **Yang 2024:** one discontinuation for discomfort.
- **Jin 2023:** bruising/hematoma counts are available, but the existing eligibility hold remains; do not add it silently to a quantitative eligible-evidence body.

Some other reports explicitly describe no local or serious treatment-related events. These statements are retained with their definitions, windows and populations; reports without such statements are **not** assigned zero events. PONV, dizziness, respiratory depression and surgical complications are not automatically needle/device harms.

**He 2026 hepatectomy reports two deaths in TEAS and none in control at six months.** The article does not establish intervention causation. This must neither be presented as TEAS-caused mortality nor erased by a blanket claim of no serious outcomes. A nonsignificant between-group P value does not demonstrate safety.

Liu 2026 ESD's flowchart also records postoperative bleeding withdrawals in two TEAS and one sham participant. These are all-cause postoperative complications with no established intervention attribution, not zero events among the smaller efficacy-completer population.

No pooled safety RR was computed. Definitions, windows, ascertainment and denominators differ; some reports give event types without counts and many lack explicit treatment-harm reporting. Double-zero studies contribute descriptive safety information but do not by themselves identify a comparative RR. A later targeted synthesis could assess a sufficiently consistent local skin-reaction endpoint, with result-specific bias assessment and explicit population rules. It should not manufacture one all-harms composite from these records.

## Persistent opioid use

No eligible result beyond 30 days was located. Three-, six- or twelve-month **pain** outcomes in Hou, Lu or Guo do not measure opioid use. Gao's 30-day complications are not beyond-30-day opioid use. References to persistent opioid use in the burn report are background literature. Seevaunnamtum's twelve-month study period is not evidence of twelve-month patient opioid follow-up.

Manuscript wording: “No included report supplied an eligible result for persistent postoperative opioid use beyond 30 days in the available source material. This review therefore cannot determine whether perioperative TEAS or EA affects persistent opioid use.”

## Manuscript-ready coverage wording

“Patient-reported recovery outcomes were available but incompletely represented in the existing quantitative synthesis. Sixteen reports used QoR-15, QoR-40 or QoR-9; satisfaction and longer-term quality-of-life measures were also reported. Differences in instruments, timing, statistics and reporting completeness required separate handling. Several fixed-time TEAS comparisons contain compatible numerical data and require additional outcome-specific synthesis. No eligible persistent-opioid-use result beyond 30 days was located.”

“Treatment-related harms were inconsistently reported. Local skin reactions, electrical discomfort, needle-related incidents and treatment intolerance were described, including withdrawals. Explicit reports of no events were distinguished from absent reporting. The available evidence does not support a blanket conclusion of no harms, and no overall comparative safety estimate was calculated.”
