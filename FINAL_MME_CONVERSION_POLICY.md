# Final IV morphine-equivalent policy

**RESOLVED — SENSITIVITY FRAMEWORK.** Target unit is mg intravenous morphine, not oral morphine equivalents. Every valid linear factor multiplies both mean and SD. Never combine mg/kg with absolute mg, or dose rates with cumulative dose, by multiplying group mean weight or duration. Joint individual-level covariance is unavailable. Programmed PCA doses and button presses are not delivered dose. Rescue components cannot be added to a total SD without covariance or an explicitly declared sensitivity model.

IV morphine: factor1. IV hydromorphone: factor5 mg IV morphine/mg. IV fentanyl: factor0.1 mg IV morphine/µg (100 per mg). These are conventional equianalgesic research standardizations, not dosing instructions. Route and original unit are checked per record.

For IV sufentanil, select **0.5 mg IV morphine/µg as the central analytical factor**, with0.25 and1.0 mandatory sensitivities. This is the geometric midpoint of a published broad parenteral equivalence range, chosen symmetrically on a ratio scale before final fits; it is not a validated acute-PCA constant. The [British Columbia equianalgesic table](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/health/practitioner-pro/bc-guidelines/palliative2_-_pain_equianalgesic.pdf) equates10mg parenteral morphine with10–40µg sufentanil in chronic dosing. Its context limits certainty for acute postoperative exposure. The [Pfizer sufentanil label](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?format=PDF&id=4634) describes context-dependent relative potency versus fentanyl in anaesthesia; it does not validate a universal postoperative PCA factor. None of the strict principal contributors requires a sufentanil conversion; the factor therefore affects only explicitly expanded/uncertain-exposure analyses.

He2026's published morphine-equivalent number is retained at factor1 only in a labeled **assuming author MME equals IVMME** sensitivity. Do not reconvert the mixed pump from its programmed contents: delivered component masses and source conversion basis are not established. Similar author-equivalent metrics are held or kept native when the IV/weight/time basis is uncertain.

Tramadol, pethidine, alfentanil, remifentanil, butorphanol, dezocine, oxycodone and bucinnazine are not assigned unsupported universal postoperative IVMME factors. Native drug analyses are preferable where matching routes/units/windows exist. In particular, no remifentanil-to-morphine factor is used for intraoperative dose; µg remifentanil MD is a distinct additional estimand. Oztas reports separate IV tramadol and IM pethidine rescue: each can be described, but their summed systemic variance cannot be reconstructed without assumptions. Song's nominal butorphanol calculation from presses is not promoted to delivered consumption.

The conversion register lists all opioids encountered, including unavailable/unused conversions. Canonical result rows retain raw units, applied factor, route uncertainty and decision, so an unconverted row is never silently treated as IVMME.
