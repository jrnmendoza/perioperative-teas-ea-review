## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Supported by the Clinical Research Award of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University (No. XJTU1AF-CRF-2016-003, No. XJTU1AF-CRF-2017-012); the National Nature Science Foundation of China (No. 81971290, No. 81771485); and the Young Scholar Research Grant of the Chinese Anesthesiologist Association (No. 21800008) [cite: 15].
* **Country:** China [cite: 15].
* **Journal:** Surgery [cite: 15].
* **DOI:** 10.1016/j.surg.2021.08.007 [cite: 15].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT03086304) [cite: 15].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Multicenter, parallel-group, randomized controlled trial (sham-controlled) [cite: 15].
* **Randomization method:** SPSS software was used for block randomization in a 1:1 ratio with a block size of 4 [cite: 15].
* **Blinding:** Double-blind; all participants and study personnel (including researchers who collected data, anesthesiologists, and surgeons) were blinded to the group allocation [cite: 15].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** TEAS group and Sham group [cite: 15].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 610 enrolled and randomized (TEAS: n=303; Sham: n=307) [cite: 15]. All 610 patients were included in the intention-to-treat analysis [cite: 15].
* **Age:** TEAS group: 62.0 (53.0-69.0) years; Sham group: 63.0 (54.0-70.0) years (median [IQR]) [cite: 15].
* **Sex distribution:** TEAS group: 176 (58.1%) Men; Sham group: 156 (50.8%) Men [cite: 15].
* **BMI:** TEAS group: 22.5 (20.4-24.7) kg/m²; Sham group: 22.7 (20.7-24.4) kg/m² (median [IQR]) [cite: 15].
* **ASA classification:** ASA score I: 24 (7.9%) in TEAS, 22 (7.2%) in Sham; ASA score II: 243 (80.2%) in TEAS, 240 (78.2%) in Sham [cite: 15].
* **Comorbidities:** Included coronary heart disease, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, diabetes, hypertension, preoperative hypokalemia, and abdominal surgery history [cite: 15]. Patients were excluded if they had a history of intestinal surgery, intraoperative enterostomy, or were pregnant/breast-feeding [cite: 15].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Colorectal cancer surgery [cite: 15].
* **Specific types:** Colectomy (right hemicolectomy, transverse colectomy, left hemicolectomy, sigmoidectomy) and Proctectomy [cite: 15]. 
* **Surgical approach:** Laparoscopic surgery was performed in 199 (65.7%) TEAS patients and 187 (60.9%) Sham patients [cite: 15].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** General anesthesia combined with transverse abdominis plane block (0.375% ropivacaine 20 mL) [cite: 15].
* **Induction:** Etomidate (0.2 mg/kg), sufentanil (0.3 μg/kg), and rocuronium bromide (1 mg/kg) [cite: 15].
* **Maintenance:** Remifentanil (0.1 μg/kg/h), cisphenyl sulfatracurium (0.1 mg/kg/h), dexmedetomidine (initial 1 μg/kg for 10 min, maintenance 0.4 μg/kg/h), and sevoflurane and/or propofol to maintain a bispectral index of 40-60 [cite: 15].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) [cite: 15].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 303 [cite: 15].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Bilateral Zusanli (ST36), Shangjuxu (ST37), and Sanyinjiao (SP6) acupoints at both legs [cite: 15].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** 2 to 10 Hz [cite: 15].
* **Treatment intensity:** Adjusted to the maximum intensity tolerated by the patient (intensities in PACU ranged from 13.0 mA to 15.0 mA, which was 2 to 5 mA stronger than in the ward) [cite: 15].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** First time was in the postoperative recovery unit (PACU) when patients reached a Steward recovery score of ≥4; the remaining 3 times were once each day between 9 AM and 11 AM on the first to third postoperative days [cite: 15].
* **Duration of each treatment:** 30 minutes [cite: 15].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** Total 4 times [cite: 15].
* **Total treatment period:** From PACU up to the third postoperative day [cite: 15].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** Not specifically listed beyond standard postoperative analgesia (received by 98% in TEAS and 99% in Sham) [cite: 15].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Sham TEAS [cite: 15].
* **Number of participants in control group:** 307 [cite: 15].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** Received identical settings and manipulation, except the wire between the electrode pads and TEAS stimulator was severed to prevent current delivery [cite: 15].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** Standardized postoperative analgesia was utilized by 297 (98.0%) TEAS patients and 304 (99.0%) Sham patients [cite: 15]. The exact postoperative opioid/PCA regimens were not detailed in the text [cite: 15]. 
* **Dosage parameters / Duration:** Not explicitly reported for postoperative analgesia [cite: 15].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Abdominal pain score [cite: 15].
* **Pain assessment time points:** At the postoperative 72 hours [cite: 15].
* **Mean/median pain score for each group:** TEAS group = 1.4 ± 1.4; Sham group = 1.3 ± 1.5 [cite: 15].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** Not significantly different (P = .555) [cite: 15].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used / Opioid consumption:** Postoperative opioid consumption was not quantified in the study [cite: 15]. Intraoperative consumption of remifentanil (median 1500.0 μg in both groups) and sufentanil (median 30.0 μg in both groups) showed no significant differences (P = .744 and P = .643, respectively) [cite: 15].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Rescue analgesia requirement:** Not reported [cite: 15].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **Gastrointestinal recovery (Primary Outcome):** 
    * Postoperative paralytic ileus (POI) incidence (no flatus for >72 hours) was 32.3% (98/303) in the TEAS group and 41.0% (126/307) in the Sham group (P = .026) [cite: 15]. 
    * First-time flatus: TEAS = 63.4 ± 29.0 h; Sham = 76.3 ± 37.9 h (P < .001) [cite: 15].
    * First-time defecation: TEAS = 106.7 ± 65.6 h; Sham = 121.5 ± 65.6 h (P = .005) [cite: 15].
    * First-time resume normal diet: TEAS = 373.9 ± 224.1 h; Sham = 425.5 ± 235.1 h (P = .006) [cite: 15].
    * First-time bowel sounds: TEAS = 41.4 ± 22.6 h; Sham = 46.0 ± 24.1 h (P = .016) [cite: 15].
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting:** Nausea score (TEAS = 1.0 ± 1.6; Sham = 1.1 ± 1.7, P = .624) and Number of vomiting episodes (TEAS = 0.0 ± 0.5; Sham = 0.0 ± 0.1, P = .155) showed no significant differences [cite: 15].
* **Length of stay:** The total length of hospital stay between the TEAS and sham groups was not statistically significantly different (referenced in Supplementary Table S4, not explicitly in the main text) [cite: 15].
* **Other postoperative complications:** 30-day postoperative complications were not statistically significantly different [cite: 15].
* **Other relevant physiological/biochemical outcomes (Biomarkers at 72 hours):**
    * Acetylcholine (Ach): TEAS = 124.5 [80.4, 171.7] pg/mL vs Sham = 80.4 [64.7, 124.5] pg/mL (P = .007) [cite: 15].
    * Interleukin-10 (IL-10): TEAS = 157.0 [97.4, 241.3] pg/mL vs Sham = 83.6 [49.0, 127.3] pg/mL (P < .001) [cite: 15].
    * Interleukin-6 (IL-6): TEAS = 15.8 [11.6, 25.5] pg/mL vs Sham = 30.0 [19.8, 44.0] pg/mL (P < .001) [cite: 15].
    * Inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS): TEAS = 7.2 [3.4, 12.1] IU/mL vs Sham = 12.3 [7.7, 17.4] IU/mL (P = .002) [cite: 15].
