# Data Extraction: s40122-023-00493-2.pdf (Ge et al., 2023)

## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Supported by Project of National Clinical Research Base of Traditional Chinese Medicine in Jiangsu Province, China (JD2022SZ18), Xuzhou Science and Technology Innovation Project (KC19023, KC22181) and Key Subject Project of Jiangsu Province: Surgery of Traditional Chinese Medicine (js2251) [cite: 45].
* **Country:** China (Nanjing, Xuzhou) [cite: 45].
* **Journal:** Pain Therapy [cite: 45].
* **DOI:** 10.1007/s40122-023-00493-2 [cite: 45].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2100054971) [cite: 45]. Approved by the ethics committee of Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing University of Chinese Medicine (no. 2021NL-199-02) and Xuzhou Traditional Chinese Medicine Hospital Affiliated to Nanjing University of Chinese Medicine (no. 09/2020) [cite: 45].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Multi-center, prospective, double-blind, randomized controlled trial [cite: 45].
* **Randomization method:** Assigned by a table of computer-generated random numbers [cite: 45]. Group assignments were sealed in sequentially numbered opaque envelopes [cite: 45].
* **Blinding:** Double-blind; patients, attending surgeons, operating room nurses, data collectors and individuals who performed the final statistical analysis were blinded to group assignment [cite: 45].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** TEAS group and Sham group [cite: 45].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 98 assessed; 88 randomized; 83 completed and were analyzed (TEAS group: n=40; Sham group: n=43) [cite: 45].
* **Age:** TEAS group: 58.20 ± 7.90 years; Sham group: 60.44 ± 9.64 years (mean ± SD) [cite: 45].
* **Sex distribution:** Not explicitly reported in the text or tables [cite: 45].
* **BMI:** TEAS group: 23.87 ± 2.85 kg/m²; Sham group: 23.73 ± 2.37 kg/m² [cite: 45].
* **ASA classification:** ASA grade I to III [cite: 45]. TEAS group: I=0, II=34 (85.0%), III=6 (15.0%); Sham group: I=0, II=29 (67.4%), III=14 (32.6%) [cite: 45].
* **Comorbidities:** Included patients undergoing elective laparoscopic GI tumor surgery [cite: 45]. Excluded patients with PSQI ≥ 7 or AIS ≥ 6 before surgery, sleep apnea/snoring, pacemaker, severe heart/liver/kidney disease, fever/infection, or communication barriers [cite: 45].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Laparoscopic gastrointestinal tumor surgery (laparoscopic radical gastrectomy for gastric cancer or laparoscopic radical resection for colorectal cancer) [cite: 45].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** Intravenous-inhalation compound anesthesia (General Anesthesia) [cite: 45].
* **Induction:** Intravenous bolus injection of midazolam 0.04 mg/kg, etomidate 0.3 mg/kg, sufentanil 0.4 μg/kg and vecuronium 0.08 mg/kg [cite: 45].
* **Maintenance:** Continuous inhalation of 1%-2% sevoflurane and continuous infusions of propofol (5 mg/kg·h) and remifentanil (0.2 μg/kg·min) [cite: 45]. BIS monitored to maintain a range of 40-60 [cite: 45].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) [cite: 45].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 40 analyzed [cite: 45].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Bilateral Shenmen (HT7), Neiguan (PC6) and Zusanli (ST36) [cite: 45].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** Dense-disperse frequency of 2/10 Hz [cite: 45].
* **Treatment intensity:** 6-15 mA (adjusted to maintain a slight twitching of the regional muscle according to individual maximum tolerance) [cite: 45].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** (1) the night before surgery, (2) the night of the surgery and (3) the first night after surgery [cite: 45].
* **Duration of each treatment:** 30 min [cite: 45].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** 3 sessions in total [cite: 45].
* **Total treatment period:** Perioperative (preoperative through first postoperative night) [cite: 45].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** 20 ml 0.25% ropivacaine was injected prior to closing the incision [cite: 45].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Sham TEAS [cite: 45].
* **Number of participants in control group:** 43 analyzed [cite: 45].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** Connected to the apparatus, provided with the same lamplight as real TEAS, but no electronic stimulation was applied [cite: 45].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** Ropivacaine injected at incision; rescue analgesia cumulative doses recorded [cite: 45].
* **Dosage parameters:** 20 ml 0.25% ropivacaine [cite: 45]. Additional doses of rescue analgesia tracked [cite: 45].
* **Duration:** Pain assessed up to 72 h postoperatively [cite: 45].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Visual analog scale (VAS) [cite: 45].
* **Pain at rest / movement:** Assessed at rest and during activity [cite: 45].
* **Pain assessment time points:** 24 h, 48 h and 72 h after surgery [cite: 45].
* **Mean/median pain score for each group:** Visualized in Figure 4 [cite: 45].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** The TEAS group had significantly lower VAS scores during activity than the Sham group at 24, 48 and 72 h after surgery (P < 0.05) [cite: 45]. There was no significant difference in VAS scores at rest between the two groups 72 h after surgery (P > 0.05) [cite: 45].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Sufentanil (induction) and Remifentanil (maintenance) [cite: 45].
* **Route of administration:** Intravenous [cite: 45].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention/control groups:** Explicit dosages not detailed beyond standard protocol [cite: 45].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** Cumulative number of rescue analgesia (number, mean ± SD): TEAS group = 0.53 ± 0.55; Sham group = 0.98 ± 0.96 [cite: 45].
* **Between-group difference:** The TEAS group had a significantly lower cumulative number of additional doses of rescue analgesia compared to the Sham group (P = 0.011) [cite: 45].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **Sleep Quality (Primary Outcome):** 
    * PSQI and AIS scores were significantly lower in the TEAS group than in the Sham group at D1 and D3 (P < 0.05 for all) [cite: 45].
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting:** Incidence of PONV 72 h after surgery: TEAS group = 6 (15.0%); Sham group = 19 (44.2%) [cite: 45]. The incidence of PONV was significantly higher in the Sham group (P = 0.004) [cite: 45].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** Incidence of abdominal distension 72 h after surgery: TEAS group = 11 (27.5%); Sham group = 24 (55.8%) (P = 0.009) [cite: 45]. Incidence of dizziness: TEAS = 10 (25.0%); Sham = 16 (37.2%) (P = 0.231) [cite: 45].
* **Length of stay:** Postoperative hospitalizations (days): TEAS group = 8.60 ± 3.86; Sham group = 8.65 ± 3.47 (P = 0.949) [cite: 45].
* **Other relevant physiological/biochemical outcomes (Biomarkers):** 
    * The TEAS group had significantly lower levels of IL-6 at D1, D3 and D5 compared to the Sham group (P < 0.05) [cite: 45].
    * The TEAS group had higher concentrations of Nrf2, GPX3, SOD and cortisol compared to the Sham group after D1 (P < 0.05) [cite: 45].
