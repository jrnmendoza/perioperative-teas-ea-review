## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Supported by Guangdong Provincial Department of Science and Technology Project, No. 2017ZC0181 [cite: 23].
* **Country:** China (Guangzhou, Guangdong Province) [cite: 23].
* **Journal:** World Journal of Clinical Oncology (World J Clin Oncol) [cite: 23].
* **DOI:** 10.5306/wjco.v17.12.114431 [cite: 23].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** Not reported; protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Guangdong Provincial Hospital of Chinese Medicine (Approval No. BF2018-006-01) [cite: 23].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Randomized controlled trial (described as a Case Control Study in the header, but methods describe random assignment to intervention/control groups) [cite: 23].
* **Randomization method:** Randomization was performed by block [cite: 23]. Blocks of random length were used through the computer [cite: 23].
* **Blinding:** Patients and postoperative outcome assessors were blinded to group allocation [cite: 23]. Assessors and data analysts remained blinded throughout the study [cite: 23]. Acupuncturists could not be blinded due to the nature of the intervention [cite: 23].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** Electroacupuncture (EA) group and Control group [cite: 23].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 130 patients were included in the statistics (EA group: n=65; Control group: n=65) [cite: 23].
* **Age:** EA group: 52 (43.5-61.5) years; Control group: 52 (43.5-61) years (median [interquartile range]) [cite: 23].
* **Sex distribution:** The study enrolled patients with breast cancer, which inherently implies a predominantly or exclusively female population, though explicit sex counts are not tabulated [cite: 23].
* **BMI:** Inclusion criteria specified Body mass index 18-30 kg/m² [cite: 23].
* **ASA classification:** American Society of Anesthesiologists class I-III [cite: 23].
* **Comorbidities:** Patients with combined diseases were 36 (55.40%) in the EA group and 29 (44.60%) in the Control group [cite: 23]. Patients were excluded if they had unstable angina, myocardial infarction within 3 months, NYHA class ≥ 3, blood pressure ≥ 180/100 mmHg, history of peptic ulcer/bleeding/perforation, severe liver or kidney function abnormalities, or known allergies to nonsteroidal drugs [cite: 23].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Elective breast cancer surgery [cite: 23].
* **Specific procedures:** Included Partial resection + sentinel lymph node biopsy, Partial resection + axillary dissection, Total mastectomy + sentinel lymph node biopsy, and Total mastectomy + axillary dissection [cite: 23].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** Uniform general anesthesia [cite: 23].
* **Induction:** Induced with propofol (2 mg/kg) and sufentanil (0.5 μg/kg) [cite: 23]. Vecuronium (0.1 mg/kg) was administered when muscle relaxants were needed [cite: 23].
* **Maintenance:** Drugs were delivered at maintenance doses requested by the surgeons according to patients' vital signs and to maintain a depth of anesthesia with a bispectral index of 50 ± 5 [cite: 23]. None of the patients received inhalational anesthesia [cite: 23].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Electroacupuncture (EA) [cite: 23].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 65 analyzed [cite: 23].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Zusanli and Sanyinjiao acupoints [cite: 23].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** 2/10 Hz sparse-dense wave stimulation [cite: 23].
* **Treatment intensity:** Based on the tolerance and degree of soreness of individual patients, adjusted within a standardized and predefined safe range [cite: 23].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** From 30 minutes before anesthesia induction until the end of surgery (when suturing was complete) [cite: 23].
* **Duration of each treatment:** Maintained throughout surgery (median anesthesia time was 240 minutes) plus 30 minutes pre-induction [cite: 23].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** A single intraoperative session [cite: 23].
* **Total treatment period:** Single day (intraoperative) [cite: 23].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** Intravenous injection of 4.48 mg tropisetron and 50 mg flurbiprofen axetil (FA) when suturing was complete [cite: 23].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Sham acupuncture [cite: 23].
* **Number of participants in control group:** 65 analyzed [cite: 23].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** Needles were connected to the electrical stimulator and used to create a shallow puncture 0.5 cm away from the Zusanli and Sanyinjiao acupoints, with no needle manipulation [cite: 23]. An inactive stimulator was used to mimic the appearance of EA [cite: 23]. Patients also received the same FA and tropisetron postoperatively [cite: 23].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** Flurbiprofen axetil (FA) [cite: 23].
* **Dosage parameters:** 50 mg FA via intravenous injection [cite: 23].
* **Duration:** Given once when suturing was complete [cite: 23].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Visual Analog Scale (VAS) scores and the incidence of moderate-to-severe pain [cite: 23].
* **Pain at rest / movement:** Measured as pain during exercise (ipsilateral upper-arm movement or walking) and pain at rest [cite: 23].
* **Pain assessment time points:** 24 hours, 48 hours, and 72 hours postoperatively [cite: 23].
* **Mean/median pain score for each group:**
    * 24h pain during exercise: EA = 2.862 ± 0.233; Control = 3.954 ± 0.233 [cite: 23].
    * 24h pain at rest: EA = 1.789 ± 0.174; Control = 2.692 ± 0.174 [cite: 23].
    * 48h pain during exercise: EA = 1.785 ± 0.18; Control = 2.108 ± 0.18 [cite: 23].
    * 48h pain at rest: EA = 0.631 ± 0.119; Control = 1.169 ± 0.119 [cite: 23].
    * 72h pain during exercise: EA = 1.115 ± 0.12; Control = 1.215 ± 0.12 [cite: 23].
    * 72h pain at rest: EA = 0.415 ± 0.086; Control = 0.631 ± 0.08 [cite: 23].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** The EA group reduced VAS scores during movement and at rest 24 hours after surgery (P < 0.01) [cite: 23]. There was no significant difference in VAS scores between the two groups at 48 hours and 72 hours after surgery (P > 0.05) [cite: 23].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Sufentanil was used for induction [cite: 23]. Rescue analgesics such as morphine (1-3 mg IV) or paracetamol (1000 mg IV) were permitted based on pain severity [cite: 23].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention/control groups:** Rescue opioid consumption was not recorded or compared between groups [cite: 23]. This limited the ability to quantify the incremental analgesic effect of EA [cite: 23].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** Not recorded or compared between groups [cite: 23].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting:** The incidence of postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) was significantly reduced in the EA group (20.0%) compared to the control group (33.8%) (P < 0.05) [cite: 23]. The EA group also had significantly less appetite loss (41.5%) compared to the control group (55.4%) (P < 0.05) [cite: 23]. Tropisetron (4.48 mg) was administered intravenously to all patients at the end of surgery [cite: 23].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** Basic activities of daily living (BADL) scores at 24 hours post-surgery were assessed using the modified Barthel Index [cite: 23]. The scores for grooming, dressing, toileting, and transferring were significantly better in the EA group than in the control group (P < 0.05) [cite: 23]. The total BADL score was significantly higher in the EA group (85 [75-90]) than in the control group (80 [65-87.5]) (P < 0.01) [cite: 23].
* **Length of stay:** Not reported [cite: 23].
* **Other postoperative complications:** The incidence of dizziness, diarrhoea, and constipation showed no significant differences between the two groups [cite: 23].
