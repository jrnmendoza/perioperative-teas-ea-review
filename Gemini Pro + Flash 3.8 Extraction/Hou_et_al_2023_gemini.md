# Data Extraction: 007_hou_2023.pdf

## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Supported by the Natural Science Foundation of Anhui Province of China [1908085MH251] and Fundamental Research Funds for the Central Universities [WK9110000059] [cite: 27].
* **Country:** China (Hefei, Anhui) [cite: 27].
* **Journal:** Heliyon [cite: 27].
* **DOI:** 10.1016/j.heliyon.2023.e14423 [cite: 27].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2000029891) [cite: 27].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Randomized, controlled clinical trial [cite: 27].
* **Randomization method:** Randomized using a randomization number generated by an independent researcher using IBM®SPSS 25.0 [cite: 27].
* **Blinding:** No participant, surgeon, anesthesiologist, data collector, or outcome assessor knew the allocation of participants (Double-blind design contextually) [cite: 27]. Only the acupuncturist performing the intervention knew which intervention the assigned number represented [cite: 27].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** TEAS group and Control group [cite: 27].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 74 LKDs recruited, 72 completed and analyzed (TEAS: n=36; Control: n=36) [cite: 27].
* **Age:** TEAS group: 54.0 ± 5.8 years; Control group: 54.6 ± 8.0 years [cite: 27].
* **Sex distribution:** TEAS group: 13 Male / 23 Female; Control group: 9 Male / 27 Female [cite: 27].
* **BMI:** TEAS group: 24.1 ± 2.0 kg/m²; Control group: 24.0 ± 3.2 kg/m² [cite: 27].
* **ASA classification:** ASA physical status I-II [cite: 27].
* **Comorbidities:** Patients with significant medical co-morbidity (ASA III-IV) were excluded [cite: 27]. Patients were relatively healthy Living Kidney Donors (LKDs) [cite: 27].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Nephrectomy [cite: 27].
* **Surgical approach:** Laparotomy [cite: 27].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** General anesthesia combined with ultrasound-guided bilateral transversus abdominis plane (TAP) block [cite: 27].
* **Induction:** IV sufentanil 0.3-0.5 μg/kg and propofol 0.2-0.3 mg/kg [cite: 27].
* **Maintenance:** Continuous target-controlled infusion (TCI) of propofol (Cp 2.0-3.0 μg/ml) and remifentanil (Cp 2.0-3.0 ng/ml), plus 1% sevoflurane to maintain BIS between 40 and 60 [cite: 27].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) [cite: 27].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 36 analyzed [cite: 27].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Yingtang (EX-HN3), bilateral Taichong (LR3), and Neiguan (PC6) [cite: 27].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** Dense-disperse mode, alternated 2Hz/100 Hz frequencies [cite: 27].
* **Treatment intensity:** Adjusted according to individual tolerance, generally between 6 and 15 mA [cite: 27].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** One day before surgery (D0), before the induction of anesthesia (D1), and one day after surgery (D2) [cite: 27].
* **Duration of each treatment:** 30 minutes [cite: 27].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** Total of three treatments over a three-day period [cite: 27].
* **Total treatment period:** From the day before surgery up to one day after surgery [cite: 27].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** Postoperative patient-controlled intravenous analgesia (PCA) [cite: 27].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Inactive stimulator [cite: 27].
* **Number of participants in control group:** 36 analyzed [cite: 27].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** Participants had the same electrode placements, but the stimulator was disabled without any transmission of electricity [cite: 27].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** Patient-controlled intravenous analgesia pump [sufentanil (1 μg/ml), azasetron (0.4 mg/ml), and droperidol (0.01 mg/ml)] [cite: 27].
* **Dosage parameters:** Flow rate (2 ml/h); bolus (4 ml); lock time (30 min) [cite: 27].
* **Duration:** Connected in the PACU for 48 h postoperatively [cite: 27].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Visual Analogue Scale for Pain (VAS-P) [cite: 27].
* **Pain assessment time points:** One day after surgery (D2), three days after surgery (D3), at the time of discharge (D4), and at 3-month after surgery (M3) [cite: 27].
* **Median pain score for each group (median [IQR]):**
    * D2: TEAS = 30.0 (30.0, 47.5); Control = 55.0 (40.0, 60.0) [cite: 27].
    * D3: TEAS = 30.0 (30.0, 30.0); Control = 40.0 (30.0, 50.0) [cite: 27].
    * D4: TEAS = 30.0 (20.0, 30.0); Control = 30.0 (20.0, 30.0) [cite: 27].
    * M3: TEAS = 20.0 (20.0, 30.0); Control = 30.0 (20.0, 40.0) [cite: 27].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** LKDs who received TEAS reported significantly less postoperative pain than those in the control group at D2 (P = 0.001) and D3 (P = 0.014), but there was no significant difference at D4 (P = 0.145) [cite: 27].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Sufentanil and remifentanil [cite: 27].
* **Route of administration:** Intravenous via TCI (intraoperative) and PCA (postoperative) [cite: 27].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention group:** Intraoperative remifentanil = 950 (750, 1238) μg (median [IQR]) [cite: 27].
* **Opioid consumption for control group:** Intraoperative remifentanil = 1000 (881, 1144) μg (median [IQR]) [cite: 27].
* **Measurement time point(s):** Intraoperative period [cite: 27].
* **Between-group difference:** There was no significant difference in intraoperative consumption of remifentanil (P = 0.572) [cite: 27]. Postoperative opioid consumption was not quantified in the text [cite: 27].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** Rescued analgesics were used by 15 (41.7%) LKDs in the TEAS group compared to 28 (77.8%) in the Control group (P = 0.002) [cite: 27]. At D3, if VAS-P ≥ 30, 50 mg of IV flurbiprofen was used as rescue analgesic [cite: 27].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting:** LKDs in the TEAS group experienced fewer episodes of PONV than those in the control group (4 [11.1%] vs 12 [33.3%], P = 0.023) [cite: 27]. Droperidol (1 mg) and flurbiprofen (100 mg) were given 15 min before the end of surgery to prevent PONV and pain [cite: 27].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** 
    * Quality of Recovery-15 (QoR-15) scores were significantly higher in the TEAS group at D2 (P = 0.009) and D3 (P = 0.001) but not at D4 (P = 0.402) [cite: 27]. 
    * LKDs in the TEAS group had significantly better sleep quality (lower ISI scores) at D2 (P = 0.025) and D3 (P = 0.030) compared to the control group [cite: 27]. 
    * Anxiety (HADS-A scores) was significantly lower in the TEAS group at D1 (P < 0.001), D2 (P = 0.009), and D3 (P < 0.001) [cite: 27].
    * Time to first bowel movement was significantly shorter in the TEAS group (1.7 ± 0.6 days vs 2.2 ± 0.7 days, P = 0.002) [cite: 27].
* **Length of stay:** All LKDs were discharged at 12 days postoperatively according to hospital policy, so there were no differences in the duration of hospitalization [cite: 27].
* **Other postoperative complications:** Chronic pain: At 3-month follow-up (M3), fewer LKDs in the TEAS group still experienced pain (VAS-P ≥ 30) compared to the control group (44.4% vs 69.4%, P = 0.032) [cite: 27]. 
* **Other relevant physiological/biochemical outcomes (Biomarkers):** Plasma 5-HT and MT levels were significantly higher in the TEAS group at D1 and D2 (all P < 0.001) [cite: 27]. Plasma IL-1β and TNF-α were significantly suppressed in the TEAS group compared to the control group (IL-1β: P = 0.011 at D1, P < 0.001 at D2; TNF-α: P < 0.001 at D2) [cite: 27].
