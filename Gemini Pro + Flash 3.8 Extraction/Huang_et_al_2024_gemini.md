## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Funded by the Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine (grant no. Y201908) [cite: 31].
* **Country:** China [cite: 31].
* **Journal:** Acupuncture in Medicine [cite: 31].
* **DOI:** 10.1177/09645284231211601 [cite: 31].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR1800020297) [cite: 31].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Parallel, single-blind randomized controlled trial [cite: 31].
* **Randomization method:** Random numbers were generated using SPSS and sealed in opaque envelopes [cite: 31]. Patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1:1 ratio [cite: 31].
* **Blinding:** Single-blind [cite: 31]. Assessors and statistical analysts remained blind to group allocation [cite: 31]. Patients were blinded using a sterile rubber pad, blunt needles that did not penetrate the skin, and an internal metal wire cut to ensure no current passed [cite: 31]. Acupuncturists were not blinded [cite: 31].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** Contralateral EA group, Ipsilateral EA group, and Sham EA group [cite: 31].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 114 patients were randomized (n=38 per group) [cite: 31]. Intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis was performed, including all 38 per group (108 actually completed) [cite: 31].
* **Age:** Contralateral EA: 67.29 ± 4.78 years; Ipsilateral EA: 68.82 ± 5.36 years; Sham EA: 67.58 ± 4.95 years [cite: 31].
* **Sex distribution:** Contralateral EA: 5 Male / 33 Female; Ipsilateral EA: 2 Male / 36 Female; Sham EA: 7 Male / 31 Female [cite: 31].
* **BMI:** Contralateral EA: 24.43 ± 3.60 kg/m²; Ipsilateral EA: 24.12 ± 3.69 kg/m²; Sham EA: 23.34 ± 2.92 kg/m² [cite: 31].
* **ASA classification:** Not reported [cite: 31].
* **Comorbidities:** Patients were diagnosed with knee osteoarthritis (KOA) [cite: 31]. Patients were excluded if they had severe arrhythmias, heart failure, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, epilepsy, or mental disorders [cite: 31].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Unilateral total knee arthroplasty (TKA) [cite: 31].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** General anesthesia [cite: 31].
* **Induction:** Fentanyl (4 μg/kg), propofol (2 mg/kg), and cisatracurium (0.15 mg/kg) [cite: 31].
* **Maintenance:** Not specifically detailed apart from standard general anesthesia care [cite: 31].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Electroacupuncture (EA) [cite: 31].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** Contralateral EA: 38 analyzed; Ipsilateral EA: 38 analyzed [cite: 31].
* **Acupuncture points used:** ST32 (Futu), ST36 (Zusanli), SP9 (Yinlingquan), and GB34 (Yanglingquan) [cite: 31]. The Contralateral EA group received EA on the healthy side and sham EA on the ipsilateral side [cite: 31]. The Ipsilateral EA group received EA on the operated side and sham EA on the healthy side [cite: 31].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** Continuous wave of 2 Hz frequency, 0.25 ms pulse width [cite: 31].
* **Treatment intensity:** 1 mA intensity [cite: 31]. Needles were inserted to a depth of 25-30 mm until a muscle twitch response was elicited [cite: 31].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** Administered postoperatively [cite: 31].
* **Duration of each treatment:** 30 minutes per session [cite: 31].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** Once daily on postoperative days 1-3 [cite: 31].
* **Total treatment period:** 3 days postoperatively [cite: 31].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** Postoperative patient-controlled analgesia (PCA) pump [cite: 31].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Sham EA [cite: 31].
* **Number of participants in control group:** 38 analyzed [cite: 31].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** Received sham EA on both the operated and the healthy sides [cite: 31]. Four blunt needles touched the skin through a sterile rubber pad without skin penetration [cite: 31]. Needle handles were rotated to simulate real acupuncture [cite: 31]. The internal metal wire of the EA instrument was cut so no current passed, though the display matched the true EA units [cite: 31].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** Patient-controlled analgesia (PCA) pump [cite: 31].
* **Dosage parameters:** The text states a continuous infusion rate of 0.25 g/kg/h via the PCA pump (Note: The exact drug used in the PCA is not explicitly named in the paragraph) [cite: 31].
* **Duration:** Not explicitly specified when PCA was discontinued [cite: 31].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Visual analog scale (VAS) score [cite: 31].
* **Pain at rest / movement:** Assessed when patients were walking or moving their joints [cite: 31].
* **Pain assessment time points:** Postoperative days 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, and 10 [cite: 31].
* **Mean/median pain score for each group:**
    * Day 3 VAS: Contralateral EA = 43.82 ± 8.18; Ipsilateral EA = 45.82 ± 10.11; Sham EA = 52.87 ± 7.80 [cite: 31].
    * Day 10 VAS: Contralateral EA = 36.95 ± 6.82; Ipsilateral EA = 36.71 ± 6.56; Sham EA = 43.45 ± 6.61 [cite: 31].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** At 3 days postoperatively (and continuing on days 5, 7, and 10), the VAS scores were significantly lower in both the contralateral EA and ipsilateral EA groups compared with the sham EA group (p < 0.001) [cite: 31]. Scores did not significantly differ between the contralateral and ipsilateral EA groups [cite: 31].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Delivered by PCA pump [cite: 31].
* **Route of administration:** Intravenous via PCA [cite: 31].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention group:** Additional dose released by PCA pump (mL) on postoperative day 2: Contralateral EA = 0.00 [0.00, 2.00] mL; Ipsilateral EA = 2.00 [0.00, 2.00] mL (median [Q1, Q3]) [cite: 31].
* **Opioid consumption for control group:** Additional dose released by PCA pump on postoperative day 2: Sham EA = 2.00 [2.00, 4.00] mL [cite: 31].
* **Measurement time point(s):** Postoperative day 2 (i.e., 24h after the first intervention) [cite: 31].
* **Between-group difference:** The additional dose delivered by the PCA pump was significantly greater in the sham EA group than in the contralateral EA group (p < 0.001) [cite: 31].
* **Percentage/relative reduction:** Not explicitly calculated [cite: 31].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** Not explicitly reported as a proportion [cite: 31].
* **Rescue analgesia details:** Additional analgesics (etoricoxib tablets, 60 mg) were provided according to the patient's needs after discontinuing the PCA pump when pain severity was unbearable (VAS > 60) [cite: 31].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting:** Not reported [cite: 31].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** 
    * Hospital for Special Surgery (HSS) knee scores were significantly better in both true EA groups compared to the sham EA group on postoperative days 3 and 10 (p < 0.01 and p < 0.001, respectively) [cite: 31]. 
    * Active and passive range of motion (AROM/PROM) were significantly better in the true EA groups during the follow-up period [cite: 31].
    * Improvement in swelling around the knee joint was significantly greater in the ipsilateral EA group compared to both other groups on postoperative day 3 [cite: 31].
    * Hamilton anxiety scale (HAMA) scores were significantly lower in both true EA groups compared to the sham EA group on postoperative day 10 [cite: 31].
* **Length of stay:** Not reported [cite: 31].
* **Other postoperative complications:** Three cases of side effects of acupuncture were recorded: a bent needle in one patient and slight fluster in another (Contralateral EA group), and a curved needle in one patient (Ipsilateral EA group) [cite: 31].
