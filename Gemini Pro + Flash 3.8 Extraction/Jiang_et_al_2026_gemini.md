# Data Extraction: study_4_Jiang_2026.pdf

## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Supported by Tianjin Key Medical Discipline Construction Project (Grant No. TJYXZDXK-3-013B) and Fundamental Research Program of Shanxi Province (Grant No. 202403021222405) [cite: 37].
* **Country:** China (Taiyuan, Tianjin) [cite: 37].
* **Journal:** Complementary Therapies in Clinical Practice [cite: 37].
* **DOI:** 10.1016/j.ctcp.2026.102076 [cite: 37].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** ClinicalTrials.gov (registration number NCT03724656) [cite: 37].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Prospective, double-blind, randomized controlled clinical trial [cite: 37].
* **Randomization method:** Randomized at a 1:1 ratio using block randomization [cite: 37]. The randomization sequence was generated by an independent statistician using computer software and accessed through sealed opaque envelopes [cite: 37].
* **Blinding:** Double-blind [cite: 37]. Patients, outcome assessors, and statisticians were blinded to group assignments [cite: 37]. The acupuncturist was aware of allocations but did not disclose them, informing patients only that tingling sensations might occur [cite: 37].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** TEAS group and sham-TEAS group [cite: 37].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 614 eligible patients randomized; 587 completed the study and were analyzed (TEAS: n=294; sham-TEAS: n=293) [cite: 37].
* **Age:** TEAS group: 51 (40-58) years; sham-TEAS group: 50 (39-58) years (median [IQR]) [cite: 37].
* **Sex distribution:** TEAS group: 122 Male / 178 Female; sham-TEAS group: 115 Male / 185 Female [cite: 37].
* **BMI:** TEAS group: 3 (1.0%) Underweight, 119 (40.5%) Normal, 172 (58.5%) Overweight; sham-TEAS group: 4 (1.4%) Underweight, 106 (36.2%) Normal, 183 (62.5%) Overweight [cite: 37].
* **ASA classification:** ASA class I-III [cite: 37]. Distribution (I/II/III): TEAS = 14 (2.4%) / 250 (85.0%) / 34 (11.6%); sham-TEAS = 4 (1.4%) / 257 (87.7%) / 32 (10.9%) [cite: 37].
* **Comorbidities:** Overall comorbidities were present in 72 (24.5%) of the TEAS group and 68 (23.2%) of the sham-TEAS group [cite: 37]. Hypertension was present in 30 (10.2%) TEAS patients and 26 (8.9%) sham-TEAS patients [cite: 37]. Patients were excluded if they had a previous history of abdominal surgery, gastrointestinal motility disorders, severe cardiovascular/hepatic/renal disease, cardiac pacemaker, or infection at acupuncture sites [cite: 37].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Selective laparoscopic non-gastrointestinal surgery [cite: 37].
* **Specific procedures:** Included laparoscopic cholecystectomy (LC), laparoscopic common bile duct exploration (LCBDE-LC), laparoscopic gynecologic surgery, and others [cite: 37].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** General anesthesia [cite: 37].
* **Induction:** 5 mg dexamethasone prior to induction, 0.03 mg/kg midazolam, 1.5-2.5 mg/kg propofol, 0.3-0.4 μg/kg sufentanil, and 0.8 mg/kg rocuronium [cite: 37].
* **Maintenance:** 0.05-0.2 μg/kg/min remifentanil and 4-12 mg/kg/h propofol, with intermittent rocuronium (0.2 mg/kg) every 30 min [cite: 37]. Bispectral index (BIS) was maintained between 40 and 60 [cite: 37].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) [cite: 37].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 294 analyzed [cite: 37].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Bilateral Zusanli (ST36), Neiguan (PC6), and Hegu (LI4) [cite: 37].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** 2/10 Hz dense-disperse frequency [cite: 37].
* **Treatment intensity:** Adjusted to induce mild muscle twitching at the individual's maximum tolerance, achieving the radiating paresthesia sensation known as "de qi" [cite: 37].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** Starting 30 min before anesthesia induction and continuing through the operation [cite: 37].
* **Duration of each treatment:** 30 minutes pre-induction plus the duration of the surgery (median surgical time was 61 [45-89] minutes) [cite: 37].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** Single continuous intraoperative session [cite: 37].
* **Total treatment period:** Single day (intraoperative) [cite: 37].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** Postoperative patient-controlled intravenous analgesia (PCIA); ondansetron administered for PONV prevention [cite: 37].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Sham-TEAS [cite: 37].
* **Number of participants in control group:** 293 analyzed [cite: 37].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** Identical acupoints were targeted, but stimulation was simulated by cutting output wires, ensuring no electrical current delivery [cite: 37].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** Patient-controlled intravenous analgesia (PCIA) utilizing sufentanil and flurbiprofen [cite: 37]. PCIA was used by 286 (97.3%) in the TEAS group and 279 (95.2%) in the sham-TEAS group [cite: 37].
* **Dosage parameters:** Specific PCIA dosage settings were not detailed [cite: 37].
* **Duration:** Administered for 24-48 hours postoperatively if needed [cite: 37].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Numeric rating scale (NRS; 0 = no pain, 10 = worst imaginable pain) [cite: 37].
* **Pain at rest / movement:** Not specifically distinguished [cite: 37].
* **Pain assessment time points:** Evaluated at 6 h, 24 h, and 48 h postoperatively [cite: 37].
* **Median pain score for each group (median [IQR]):** Overall postoperative NRS pain score: TEAS = 3 [2-3]; sham-TEAS = 3 [2-3.5] [cite: 37].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** The TEAS group demonstrated a significantly decreased pain intensity compared to the sham-TEAS group (p = 0.026) [cite: 37].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Sufentanil (induction and PCIA) and Remifentanil (maintenance) [cite: 37].
* **Route of administration:** Intravenous [cite: 37].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention group:** Intraoperative Remifentanil = 841.4 (593.85-1278.0) μg; Intraoperative Sufentanil = 21.0 (18.0-22.8) μg (median [IQR]) [cite: 37].
* **Opioid consumption for control group:** Intraoperative Remifentanil = 795.2 (584.0-1228.2) μg; Intraoperative Sufentanil = 21.0 (18.0-22.5) μg (median [IQR]) [cite: 37].
* **Between-group difference:** There were no significant differences between the groups in the consumption of intraoperative remifentanil (p = 0.284) or sufentanil (p = 0.927) [cite: 37].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** Not explicitly reported, though PCIA utilization rates were reported (TEAS: 97.3%; sham-TEAS: 95.2%, p=0.190) [cite: 37].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **Gastrointestinal recovery (Primary Outcome):**
    * Time to first flatus (median [IQR]): TEAS = 21.5 (19.0-23.5) h; sham-TEAS = 23.5 (21.0-26.0) h (p < 0.001) [cite: 37].
    * Time to first defecation (median [IQR]): TEAS = 41.2 (38.5-45.0) h; sham-TEAS = 46.0 (42.0-52.0) h (p < 0.001) [cite: 37].
    * Incidence of abdominal distention: TEAS = 62 (21.1%); sham-TEAS = 87 (29.7%) (p = 0.017) [cite: 37].
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting:** 24 h PONV incidence was significantly lower in the TEAS group (44 patients, 15.0%) compared to the sham-TEAS group (67 patients, 22.9%) (p = 0.015) [cite: 37]. Intraoperative ondansetron administration was similar between groups (TEAS: 12.9%; sham-TEAS: 11.9%) [cite: 37].
* **Length of stay:** Length of postoperative hospital stay (median [IQR]) was 3.0 (2-4) days in both the TEAS and sham-TEAS groups (p = 0.725) [cite: 37].
* **Hospital cost:** Median hospital cost was 22,646.44 CNY in the TEAS group and 21,957.81 CNY in the sham-TEAS group; the difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.059) [cite: 37].
* **Other postoperative complications:** Severe adverse events were 0 in both groups [cite: 37]. Minimal adverse events like subcutaneous hematoma, rubefaction, and hydroderma were not significantly different between groups [cite: 37].
