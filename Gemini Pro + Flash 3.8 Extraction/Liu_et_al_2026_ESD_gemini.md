## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Supported by Beijing Municipal Administration of Traditional Chinese Medicine (No. BJZYQN-2023-22), and Beijing Tongzhou District Science and Technology Program, China (No. WS2024036) [cite: 55].
* **Country:** China (Beijing) [cite: 55].
* **Journal:** Chinese Journal of Integrative Medicine [cite: 55].
* **DOI:** 10.1007/s11655-025-3844-7 [cite: 55].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** China Clinical Trials Registry (No. ChiCTR2100052837) [cite: 55].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Single-center, prospective, randomized, sham-controlled clinical trial [cite: 55].
* **Randomization method:** Computer-generated random numbers with a block size of 4, stratified by the surgical type (i.e., gastric or esophageal ESD) [cite: 55]. The allocation was sealed in an opaque envelope [cite: 55].
* **Blinding:** The patients, surgeons, and physicians responsible for follow-up were unaware of the grouping [cite: 55]. A trained research assessor who was blinded to the group allocation performed the assessment of outcomes [cite: 55]. The anesthesiologist and acupuncturist in charge of stimulation were aware of the grouping [cite: 55].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** TEAS group and Sham group [cite: 55].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 275 assessed; 129 enrolled and randomized (TEAS: n=64; Sham: n=65); 120 completed and were analyzed (TEAS: n=58; Sham: n=62) [cite: 55].
* **Age:** TEAS group: 65 (54, 69) years; Sham group: 62 (53, 67) years (Median [IQR]) [cite: 55].
* **Sex distribution:** TEAS group: 20 Female (34.5%); Sham group: 18 Female (29.0%) [cite: 55].
* **BMI:** TEAS group: 24.0 (21.5, 26.0) kg/m²; Sham group: 24.0 (22.5, 25.9) kg/m² (Median [IQR]) [cite: 55].
* **ASA classification:** ASA physical status classification I to III [cite: 55]. TEAS: 9 (15.5%) ASA I, 40 (69.0%) ASA II, 9 (15.5%) ASA III; Sham: 14 (22.6%) ASA I, 37 (59.7%) ASA II, 11 (17.7%) ASA III [cite: 55]. Patients with ASA > III were excluded [cite: 55].
* **Comorbidities:** Included hypertension, diabetes, coronary disease, hyperlipidemia, cerebral infarction, smoking history, and history of alcohol intake [cite: 55]. Patients were excluded if they had ongoing use of central analgesic medications, addiction/dependence on opioids, severe CNS diseases, psychiatric disorders, or a cardiac pacemaker [cite: 55].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) for early gastric and esophageal neoplasms [cite: 55]. (TEAS: 29 Gastric, 29 Esophageal; Sham: 33 Gastric, 29 Esophageal) [cite: 55].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** General anesthesia [cite: 55].
* **Induction:** Midazolam (5 mg, 0.03 mg/kg), remifentanil (1 mg, 1-2 μg/kg), etomidate (20 mg, 0.1-0.2 mg/kg), and rocuronium (25 mg, 0.6-0.8 mg/kg) [cite: 55].
* **Maintenance:** Total intravenous infusion of propofol (200 mg, 4-6 mg/(kg·h)), remifentanil (0.05-0.2 μg/(kg·min)), and rocuronium (one-third of the induced dose every 40 min) [cite: 55]. BIS index was maintained between 45-55 [cite: 55].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) [cite: 55].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 58 analyzed [cite: 55].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Bilateral Hegu (LI 4), Neiguan (PC 6), Zusanli (ST 36), and Shangjuxu (ST 37) [cite: 55].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** 2/100 Hz (wave width: 2 Hz, 0.6 ms; 100 Hz, 0.2 ms), alternating frequency for 3 s [cite: 55].
* **Treatment intensity:** Set to the patient's maximum tolerance level [cite: 55].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** From 30 min before ESD until the end of surgery [cite: 55].
* **Duration of each treatment:** Maintained continuously throughout the operation (median duration of surgery in TEAS was 76 (53,112) min) plus 30 min pre-induction [cite: 55].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** One single continuous intraoperative session [cite: 55].
* **Total treatment period:** Single day (intraoperative) [cite: 55].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** 50 mg tramadol was applied for analgesia 30 min before the end of ESD [cite: 55].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Sham stimulation [cite: 55].
* **Number of participants in control group:** 62 analyzed [cite: 55].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** Treated at sham acupoints (2 located 7 cun above and 1 cun outside Shenmen (HT 7); 2 located 9 cun and 12 cun above Kunlun (BL 60)) [cite: 55]. Self-adhesive electrodes were glued to these points, but no electrical stimulation was given from the HANS stimulator [cite: 55].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** Tramadol (50 mg) 30 min before the end of surgery [cite: 55]. Postoperatively, if NRS ≥ 4 or the patient requested analgesia, 1 mg morphine was administered intravenously [cite: 55].
* **Dosage parameters:** 1 mg morphine IV [cite: 55].
* **Duration:** Administered postoperatively as needed (monitored up to 48 hours) [cite: 55].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) (0-10) [cite: 55].
* **Pain at rest / movement:** Not specifically distinguished; evaluated as "postoperative abdominal pain" [cite: 55].
* **Pain assessment time points:** 1, 2, 4, 6, 18, 24, and 48 h postoperatively [cite: 55].
* **Mean/median pain score for each group (Mean ± SD / Median (IQR)):**
    * 1 h: TEAS = 1.3 ± 0.5; Sham = 2.6 ± 2.9 (P = 0.000) [cite: 55].
    * 2 h: TEAS = 1.6 (0.6, 2.0); Sham = 2.1 (0.5, 3.2) (P = 0.013) [cite: 55].
    * 4 h: TEAS = 1.6 ± 1.3; Sham = 2.0 ± 1.5 (P = 0.165) [cite: 55].
    * 6 h: TEAS = 1.5 (0.6, 2.7); Sham = 2.7 (1.1, 4.3) (P = 0.023) [cite: 55].
    * 18 h: TEAS = 1.5 ± 1.2; Sham = 3.2 ± 1.5 (P = 0.000) [cite: 55].
    * 24 h: TEAS = 1.2 ± 1.3; Sham = 2.8 ± 1.9 (P = 0.001) [cite: 55].
    * 48 h: TEAS = 0.5 ± 0.4; Sham = 0.9 ± 0.6 (P = 0.635) [cite: 55].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** The NRS pain score was significantly lowered by TEAS for the first 24 h after ESD (P = 0.001) [cite: 55]. The difference was not significant at 48 h (P = 0.635) [cite: 55]. Incidence of moderate-to-severe pain (NRS ≥ 4) within 24 h was significantly decreased by TEAS (37.9% vs. 64.5%, P = 0.004) [cite: 55].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Remifentanil (intraoperative), Morphine (postoperative rescue) [cite: 55].
* **Route of administration:** Intravenous [cite: 55].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention group:** Intraoperative Remifentanil = 672 (519, 884) μg (Median [IQR]) [cite: 55].
* **Opioid consumption for control group:** Intraoperative Remifentanil = 805 (435, 1088) μg (Median [IQR]) [cite: 55].
* **Measurement time point(s):** Intraoperative period [cite: 55].
* **Between-group difference:** There was no statistically significant difference in intraoperative remifentanil consumption (P = 0.178) [cite: 55].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** Patients needing morphine: TEAS = 19 (32.7%); Sham = 40 (64.5%) (P = 0.000) [cite: 55].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting:** The incidence of PONV was significantly lower in the TEAS group (4 cases, 6.9%) than in the sham group (14 cases, 22.6%) (P = 0.016) [cite: 55]. Note: The abstract mentions P = 0.06 for PONV, but Table 2 states P = 0.016 [cite: 55].
* **Gastrointestinal recovery:** Time to first flatus (TEAS = 11.2 [9.5, 12.8] h; Sham = 14.5 [6.2, 23.0] h), defecation, drinking, and eating showed no statistically significant differences between the two groups (P > 0.05) [cite: 55].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** Patient satisfaction scores were significantly higher in the TEAS group (73.0 ± 28.5) compared with the sham group (48.8 ± 21.2) (P = 0.000) [cite: 55].
* **Length of stay:** Not reported [cite: 55].
* **Other postoperative complications:** Postoperative adverse events were similar between the two groups [cite: 55]. Three patients in the TEAS group and two in the sham group dropped out or were converted due to postoperative bleeding or surgical factors [cite: 55].
