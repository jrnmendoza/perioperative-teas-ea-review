## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Not explicitly stated in the provided text [cite: 55].
* **Country:** Greece [cite: 55].
* **Journal:** Acupuncture in Medicine (Acupunct Med) [cite: 55].
* **DOI:** 10.1136/acupmed-2013-010498 [cite: 55].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** ClinicalTrials.gov (number NCT01526525) [cite: 55]. Approved by the Ethics and Deontology Committee of the Aristotelian University of Thessaloniki Greece and the Scientific Committee of the General Hospital of Thessaloniki Greece 'G. Gennimatas' [cite: 55].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Prospective, randomised, sham-controlled, single-blind trial [cite: 55].
* **Randomization method:** Randomly divided into two groups using a computer-generated randomisation sequence [cite: 55]. The randomisation was concealed by the director of the anaesthesiology department [cite: 55].
* **Blinding:** Single-blind [cite: 55]. The patients were blinded (anaesthetised during the initial application, and second application just after awakening) [cite: 55]. An anaesthetist who was not involved with patients intraoperatively performed the assessments [cite: 55].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** EA group (tramadol + ketamine + EA) and control group (tramadol + ketamine + sham EA) [cite: 55].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 75 recruited; 70 completed the study and were analyzed (35 in each group) [cite: 55].
* **Age:** EA group: 67.3 ± 6.3 years; Control group: 65.5 ± 5.6 years [cite: 55].
* **Sex distribution:** 100% Male (patients undergoing radical prostatectomy) [cite: 55].
* **BMI:** EA group: 28.7 ± 3.8 kg/m²; Control group: 27.9 ± 3.8 kg/m² [cite: 55].
* **ASA classification:** ASA Physical Status 1-3 [cite: 55]. EA group (1/2/3): 5 (14.2%) / 28 (80%) / 2 (5.7%); Control group (1/2/3): 6 (17.1%) / 24 (68.5%) / 5 (14.2%) [cite: 55].
* **Comorbidities:** Hypertension, Heart disease, Diabetes mellitus type II, Pulmonary disease [cite: 55]. Excluded: obesity (BMI >35), chronic use of opioids or chronic pain, postoperative nausea and vomiting, cognitive dysfunction, significant cardiovascular, pulmonary, renal or hepatic disease, epilepsy or a history of seizures, and previous treatment with acupuncture [cite: 55].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Radical retropubic prostatectomy (for prostate malignancy) with a midline extraperitoneal lower abdominal incision [cite: 55].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** General anaesthesia [cite: 55].
* **Induction:** Not explicitly detailed in the provided text [cite: 55].
* **Maintenance:** Not explicitly detailed in the provided text [cite: 55].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Electroacupuncture (EA) [cite: 55].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 35 analyzed [cite: 55].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Bilateral LI4 and ST36 [cite: 55].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** 100 Hz frequency initially; then 4 Hz frequency [cite: 55].
* **Treatment intensity:** 300 μs duration, depth of 20 mm [cite: 55].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** Intraoperative (during closure of the abdominal walls) and postoperative (immediately after extubation) [cite: 55].
* **Duration of each treatment:** 30 min per session [cite: 55].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** Two sessions (one intraoperative at LI4, one post-extubation at ST36 and LI4) [cite: 55].
* **Total treatment period:** Single day (perioperative) [cite: 55].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** 40 mg parecoxib IV 1h before the end of surgery, 20 mg/kg paracetamol IV 30 min before the end, and an IV bolus of 1.5 mg/kg tramadol and 10 mg ketamine 30 min before the end of surgery, followed by an IV infusion of 0.15 mg/kg/h tramadol and ketamine [cite: 55].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Sham acupuncture [cite: 55].
* **Number of participants in control group:** 35 analyzed [cite: 55].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** Needles were placed and secured by adhesive tape, without penetrating the skin, at the same points [cite: 55]. The stimulator light was on, but no electrical current was applied [cite: 55].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** Intravenous infusion of tramadol and ketamine via an adjustable flow disposable pump [cite: 55].
* **Dosage parameters:** Infusion of 0.15 mg/kg/h tramadol and ketamine [cite: 55]. For rescue: Tramadol (50 mg IV) first, then morphine (2 mg IV) if needed [cite: 55].
* **Duration:** Administered and evaluated over 24 h postoperatively [cite: 55].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Numerical Rating Scale (NRS) with 11 levels, Present Pain Intensity Scale (PPI) with 6 levels, Short-Form McGill Scale (SF-MPQ), and electronic pressure algometer (for mechanical pain thresholds) [cite: 55].
* **Pain at rest / movement:** Assessed at rest and during movement (deep breath) [cite: 55].
* **Pain assessment time points:** 45 min, 2 h, 6 h, 12 h, and 24 h after surgery [cite: 55].
* **Mean/median pain score for each group:** Visualized in Figures 2 and 3 [cite: 55].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** Pain scores on the NRS, PPI, and SF-MPQ were significantly lower in the EA group than in the control group (p < 0.001) at all assessments [cite: 55]. Electronic pressure algometer measurements were significantly higher in the EA group than in the control group (p < 0.001) at all assessments (specifically on the right side) [cite: 55].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Tramadol [cite: 55]. (Ketamine and Paracetamol also tracked) [cite: 55].
* **Route of administration:** Intravenous [cite: 55].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention group:** Tramadol sum (mg) = 443.7 ± 67.4 [cite: 55].
* **Opioid consumption for control group:** Tramadol sum (mg) = 461.5 ± 63.2 [cite: 55].
* **Measurement time point(s):** Postoperative period (presumably 24h totals) [cite: 55].
* **Between-group difference:** Tramadol sum (p = 0.257), Ketamine sum (p = 0.136), and Paracetamol sum (p = 0.960) did not show statistically significant differences between the groups [cite: 55].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** A significant decrease was detected in rescue analgesia in the EA group at 45 min (p < 0.001) and in the total amount of analgesia (p < 0.001) [cite: 55]. (Explicit values for rescue doses not tabulated) [cite: 55].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting:** Number of cases with nausea (EA vs Control): 3 vs 9 at 45 min; 4 vs 8 at 2h; 2 vs 5 at 6h; 7 vs 5 at 12h; 4 vs 2 at 24h [cite: 55]. Number of cases with vomiting: 0 vs 1 at 45 min; 0 vs 0 at 2h; 2 vs 0 at 6h; 2 vs 3 at 12h; 2 vs 1 at 24h [cite: 55]. (Differences not noted as statistically significant overall) [cite: 55].
* **Gastrointestinal recovery:** Significant delays in the start of bowel movements (absent bowel movement) were observed in the control group compared to the EA group at 45 min (EA: 6, Control: 20; p < 0.001) and 2 h (EA: 11, Control: 9; p < 0.05) [cite: 55]. Note: 11 vs 9 with p < 0.05 is stated in Table 2, though 11 > 9.
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** Sedation (number of cases): 1 (EA) vs 7 (Control) at 45 min [cite: 55]. Patients expressed satisfaction with the analgesia, especially in the EA group (p < 0.01) [cite: 55]. No differences in STAI Y-6 scores [cite: 55].
* **Length of stay:** Not reported [cite: 55].
* **Other relevant physiological/biochemical outcomes (Biomarkers):** A significant decrease in cortisol levels was observed in the EA group for Cort3 (14.37 ± 9.18) versus Cort1 (17.01 ± 5.42) measurements (p < 0.05) [cite: 55].
