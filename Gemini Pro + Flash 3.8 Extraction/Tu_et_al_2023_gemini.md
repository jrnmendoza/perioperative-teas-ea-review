## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Supported by the National Science Fund for Young Scholars (No: 82304958) and the West China Nursing Discipline Development Special Fund Project of Sichuan University (grant number: HXHL19017) [cite: 41].
* **Country:** China [cite: 41].
* **Journal:** Complementary Therapies in Clinical Practice [cite: 41].
* **DOI:** 10.1016/j.ctcp.2023.101824 [cite: 41].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (registration number: ChiCTR-TRC-13003026) [cite: 41]. Approved by the Ethics Review Board of West China Hospital, Sichuan University (approval number: 2012249) [cite: 41].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Randomized blinded controlled trial [cite: 41].
* **Randomization method:** Computer-generated random number list at a ratio of 1:1 [cite: 41]. Allocation concealment was achieved by enclosing assignments in sealed, opaque, sequentially numbered envelopes [cite: 41].
* **Blinding:** Blinded controlled trial [cite: 41]. Patients, outcome assessors, data collectors, and statisticians were blinded to the group allocation [cite: 41]. To ensure patient blinding, each patient in the sham group was told that a special acupoint stimulation, which cannot be felt via human sensory perception, was used [cite: 41].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** TEAS group and Sham TEAS group [cite: 41].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 143 assessed; 120 randomized; 115 analyzed (TEAS group: n=57; Sham TEAS group: n=58) [cite: 41].
* **Age:** TEAS group: 44.77 ± 15.74 years; Sham TEAS group: 49.24 ± 14.02 years (mean ± SD) [cite: 41].
* **Sex distribution:** TEAS group: 26 Male / 31 Female; Sham TEAS group: 27 Male / 31 Female [cite: 41].
* **BMI:** Weight: TEAS group = 61.42 ± 10.09 kg; Sham TEAS group = 58.19 ± 8.93 kg [cite: 41]. BMI not explicitly reported [cite: 41].
* **ASA classification:** American Society of Anesthesiologists physical status classification of I or II [cite: 41].
* **Comorbidities:** Included patients scheduled for neurosurgery requiring opening of the cranium and dura [cite: 41]. Excluded patients with vomiting 24 h before surgery, pregnant/lactating women, drug/alcohol abusers, recent chemotherapy/radiation, cardiac pacemaker, menstruating, mental disorder, epilepsy, unconsciousness, undergoing ventricle or brainstem surgery, poorly controlled diabetes, or serious systemic disease [cite: 41].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Craniotomy [cite: 41].
* **Specific procedures:** Supratentorial (TEAS: 35; Sham: 41), subtentorial (TEAS: 19; Sham: 16), or both (TEAS: 3; Sham: 1) [cite: 41].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** General anesthesia with endotracheal intubation [cite: 41].
* **Induction:** Not explicitly detailed [cite: 41].
* **Maintenance:** Not explicitly detailed [cite: 41].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) [cite: 41].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 57 analyzed [cite: 41].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Bilateral pericardium 6 (P6; Neiguan) [cite: 41].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** Dilatational wave of 2/100 Hz [cite: 41].
* **Treatment intensity:** Intensity varied until the patients continuously felt a de qi sensation [cite: 41].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** Administered as the patient gained consciousness after being transferred from the operating room to the anesthesia resuscitation room [cite: 41].
* **Duration of each treatment:** 30 min [cite: 41].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** One single postoperative session [cite: 41].
* **Total treatment period:** Single day (postoperative) [cite: 41].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** Prophylactic antiemetic drug (ondansetron injection 8 mg) 30 minutes prior to the end of the operation [cite: 41].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Sham TEAS [cite: 41].
* **Number of participants in control group:** 58 analyzed [cite: 41].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** Self-adhesive electrodes placed bilaterally over P6 [cite: 41]. Special wires were used with the copper wires severed to prevent the generation of electrical stimulation [cite: 41]. The stimulators exhibited the same operational power indicator and emitted the same sound [cite: 41].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** Tramadol [cite: 41].
* **Dosage parameters:** 10 mg tramadol (intramuscular injection) administered if the VAS pain score was >6 [cite: 41].
* **Duration:** Tracked up to 24 h postoperatively [cite: 41].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Visual analog scale (VAS) [cite: 41].
* **Pain at rest / movement:** Not specifically distinguished [cite: 41].
* **Pain assessment time points:** 0-2 h, 2-6 h, and 6-24 h after craniotomy [cite: 41].
* **Median pain score for each group (median (IQR)):**
    * 0-2 h: TEAS = 2 (1-3); Sham = 3 (2-4) (P = 0.091) [cite: 41].
    * 2-6 h: TEAS = 2 (1-3); Sham = 3 (2-3.25) (P = 0.057) [cite: 41].
    * 6-24 h: TEAS = 2 (0.5-3); Sham = 4 (3-4) (P = 0.001) [cite: 41].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** During 0-2 and 2-6 h, the pain scores did not differ significantly between the two groups (P > 0.05) [cite: 41]. During 6-24 h after craniotomy, the pain score was significantly higher in the sham TEAS group than in the TEAS group (P = 0.001) [cite: 41].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Sufentanil [cite: 41].
* **Route of administration:** Not explicitly detailed (likely intravenous) [cite: 41].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention group:** Dose of sufentanil = 28.99 ± 10.78 μg [cite: 41].
* **Opioid consumption for control group:** Dose of sufentanil = 28.16 ± 13.23 μg [cite: 41].
* **Measurement time point(s):** Intraoperative [cite: 41].
* **Between-group difference:** There was no significant difference in the intraoperative sufentanil dosage between the two groups (P = 0.350) [cite: 41].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** Use of tramadol within 6-24 h: TEAS group = 3 (5.3%); Sham TEAS group = 6 (10.3%) [cite: 41].
* **Between-group difference:** There was no significant difference in the tramadol usage rate between the two groups (P = 0.315) [cite: 41].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting (Primary Outcome):** 
    * Vomiting within 0-2 h: TEAS = 4 (7.0%); Sham = 10 (17.2%) (P = 0.095) [cite: 41].
    * Vomiting within 2-6 h: TEAS = 8 (14.0%); Sham = 17 (29.3%) (P = 0.047) [cite: 41].
    * Vomiting within 6-24 h: TEAS = 11 (19.3%); Sham = 15 (25.9%) (P = 0.405) [cite: 41].
    * During 2-6 h, the incidence of vomiting was significantly higher in the sham TEAS group than in the TEAS group [cite: 41].
    * The degree of nausea (scores) was not significantly different between the two groups in any period [cite: 41].
    * Rescue antiemetic use (metoclopramide): Only 1 patient (1.8%) in the TEAS group and 1 patient (1.7%) in the sham group required metoclopramide within 6-24 h [cite: 41].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** Not reported.
* **Length of stay:** Not reported [cite: 41].
* **Other postoperative complications:** AEs (malignant arrhythmia, abnormal blood pressure fluctuations, fainting, serious pain, and local infection) were not observed in any patient during the 0-24-h testing period [cite: 41].
