# Data Extraction: covidence_828_full_article.pdf (Yeh et al., 2011)

## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** None [cite: 43].
* **Country:** Taiwan [cite: 43].
* **Journal:** International Journal of Nursing Studies [cite: 43].
* **DOI:** 10.1016/j.ijnurstu.2010.10.009 [cite: 43].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** Not explicitly reported; ethical approval was obtained from the Institutional Review Board in the study hospital [cite: 43].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Single-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial [cite: 43].
* **Randomization method:** Computer-generated numbers and sequential information on intervention allocation without sealed envelopes [cite: 43].
* **Blinding:** Single-blind; individual subjects and medical staff were blinded to the group assignments [cite: 43]. The AES interventions and data collection were performed by the researchers, so the investigators were not blinded [cite: 43].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** AES group (true acupoints), sham group (sham acupoints), and control group (no AES intervention) [cite: 43].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 99 eligible; 90 signed informed consent, were randomized, and analyzed (AES: n=30; Sham: n=30; Control: n=30) [cite: 43].
* **Age:** AES group: 60.7 ± 12.0 years; Sham group: 57.2 ± 15.6 years; Control group: 63.2 ± 14.6 years (mean ± SD) [cite: 43].
* **Sex distribution:** AES group: 10 Male / 20 Female; Sham group: 9 Male / 21 Female; Control group: 15 Male / 15 Female [cite: 43].
* **BMI:** Not reported. (Note: Body weight and height were reported in Table 1 without units: Weight: AES = 158.4 ± 7.4, Sham = 157.1 ± 9.9, Control = 160.3 ± 9.1; Height: AES = 65.4 ± 10.4, Sham = 63.9 ± 13.6, Control = 64.5 ± 13.3) [cite: 43].
* **ASA classification:** American Society of Anesthesiologists physical status I and II [cite: 43].
* **Comorbidities:** Included patients scheduled for surgery to correct non-traumatic lumbar spine injuries [cite: 43]. Excluded patients using a pacemaker, with a history of arrhythmia or epilepsy, opiate dependence, clinically significant cardiovascular disease, and cutaneous lesions at application sites [cite: 43].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Spinal surgery (correction of non-traumatic lumbar spine injuries) [cite: 43].
* **Specific procedures:** Admitting diagnoses included herniated intervertebral disc (HIVD), spondylolisthesis, and lumbar spinal stenosis [cite: 43].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** General anesthesia [cite: 43].
* **Induction:** Intravenous thiopental (2.1 mg/kg) and fentanyl (4.3 μg/kg) [cite: 43].
* **Maintenance:** Not explicitly detailed [cite: 43].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Acupoint electrical stimulation (AES) [cite: 43].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 30 analyzed [cite: 43].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Bilateral Weizhong (BL40), Yanglingquan (GB34), Shenmen (HT7), and Neiguan (P6) [cite: 43].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** Alternating pulses of a 2 Hz / 100 Hz dense-disperse waveform and 0.25 min pulse duration (as stated in the text) [cite: 43].
* **Treatment intensity:** Current output of 4-7 mA, adjusted depending on individual body weight [cite: 43].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** The first session was administered in the third postoperative hour, and the second session in the fourth postoperative hour [cite: 43].
* **Duration of each treatment:** 20 minutes per session [cite: 43].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** 2 sessions [cite: 43].
* **Total treatment period:** Single day (within the first 4 postoperative hours) [cite: 43].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** Postoperative intravenous patient-controlled analgesia (PCA) connected to the central venous line [cite: 43].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Sham AES and Control (no intervention) [cite: 43].
* **Number of participants in control group:** Sham: 30 analyzed; Control: 30 analyzed [cite: 43].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** 
    * The sham group received AES in the same manner, but electrodes were placed 2 cm away from the true acupoints (not on meridian acupoints) [cite: 43].
    * The control group received no AES intervention [cite: 43].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** Intravenous patient-controlled analgesia (PCA) [cite: 43].
* **Dosage parameters:** Programmed to deliver a patient-triggered 1 mg bolus of morphine with a 10 min lockout interval between doses [cite: 43].
* **Duration:** Tracked during the first 24 hours after surgery [cite: 43].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Visual Analog Scale (VAS) (0-10) and Brief Pain Inventory (BPI) assessing pain intensity and interference (0-10) [cite: 43].
* **Pain at rest / movement:** BPI assessed the "worst" and "average" pain intensity [cite: 43].
* **Pain assessment time points:** Baseline (prior to intervention), 30 min after the first and second AES interventions, and 24 h after surgery [cite: 43].
* **Mean/median pain score for each group (Mean ± SD):**
    * Baseline VAS scores: AES = 4.37 ± 1.78; Sham = 5.43 ± 2.12; Control = 4.91 ± 1.89 (P = 0.09) [cite: 43].
    * Worst pain intensity (24h): AES = 5.4 ± 1.6; Sham = 6.5 ± 1.6; Control = 6.5 ± 1.7 (P = 0.01) [cite: 43].
    * Average pain intensity (24h): AES = 2.1 ± 1.3; Sham = 3.0 ± 1.4; Control = 3.0 ± 1.4 (P = 0.01) [cite: 43].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** 
    * There was a significant difference in pain reduction across time (VAS scores) with pain significantly lower in the AES group than the control (P < 0.007) and sham groups (P = 0.005) [cite: 43]. 
    * The worst and average pain intensities 24h after surgery were significantly lower in the AES group compared to the sham (P = 0.03) and control groups (P = 0.03) [cite: 43].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Morphine [cite: 43].
* **Route of administration:** Intravenous via PCA [cite: 43].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention group:** 24 h opiate dose = 19.3 ± 9.7 mg [cite: 43].
* **Opioid consumption for control groups:** Sham = 21.6 ± 13.1 mg; Control = 28.0 ± 12.1 mg [cite: 43].
* **Measurement time point(s):** First 24 postoperative hours [cite: 43].
* **Between-group difference:** The amount of opiate required during the first 24 hours was significantly different across groups (P = 0.017), with the AES group requiring significantly less than the control group (P = 0.023) [cite: 43].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** Occurrence of PCA button pushed (times): AES = 24.9 ± 28.5; Sham = 47.4 ± 39.7; Control = 76.9 ± 86.1 [cite: 43]. The timing of the first request for PCA morphine did not differ significantly between groups [cite: 43].
* **Between-group difference:** The number of times patients pushed the button to achieve pain relief was significantly lower in the AES group compared to the control group (P = 0.004) [cite: 43].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting:** Not reported [cite: 43].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** Pain interference with daily activities, change of position, talking, and sleep were all significantly lower in the AES group compared to the control group (P < 0.05 for all) [cite: 43]. Pain interference with bodily functions and emotion did not differ significantly [cite: 43].
* **Length of stay:** Not reported [cite: 43].
* **Other postoperative complications:** The AES group had no adverse effects associated with the AES intervention during the study period [cite: 43].
