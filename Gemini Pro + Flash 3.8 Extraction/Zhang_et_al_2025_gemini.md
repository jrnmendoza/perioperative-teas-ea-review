# Data Extraction: 109551.pdf (Zhang et al., 2025)

## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Supported by Zhejiang Traditional Chinese Medicine Administration Project (grant No. 2023ZL086), National Natural Science Foundation of China (grant No. 82104622), Wenzhou Science and Technology Bureau (grant No. Y20210787), and Zhejiang Natural Science Foundation (grant No. LQ24H310013) [cite: 39].
* **Country:** China (Wenzhou) [cite: 39].
* **Journal:** Journal of Pain Research [cite: 39].
* **DOI:** Not explicitly stated with standard prefix in the text, but the URL https://doi.org/10.3147/JPR.S533921 is present in the document footer [cite: 39].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (Registration No.: ChiCTR2400093634) [cite: 39]. Approved by the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University (Approval No.: KY2024-019) [cite: 39].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Randomized controlled trial (RCT) [cite: 39].
* **Randomization method:** Random sequences generated using Stata 15.0 software in a 1:1 ratio [cite: 39]. The random allocation sequences were placed in sealed opaque envelopes [cite: 39].
* **Blinding:** Double-blind; Anesthesiologists, outcome assessors, and patients were blinded to the group allocation [cite: 39]. The TEAS intervention and outcome assessments were conducted by separate personnel at distinct time points to maintain blinding [cite: 39].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** TEAS group and Control group [cite: 39].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 120 assessed for eligibility; 108 randomized; 93 analyzed (TEAS group: n=45; Control group: n=48) [cite: 39].
* **Age:** TEAS group: 51.91 ± 7.37 years; Control group: 50.31 ± 6.46 years (mean ± SD) [cite: 39].
* **Sex distribution:** 100% Female (undergoing laparoscopic hysterectomy) [cite: 39].
* **BMI:** TEAS group: 23.63 ± 2.72 kg/m²; Control group: 24.52 ± 3.09 kg/m² [cite: 39].
* **ASA classification:** ASA I-II [cite: 39].
* **Comorbidities:** Included patients aged 18-65 years scheduled for elective laparoscopic hysterectomy [cite: 39]. Excluded dermatological conditions at electrode placement sites, allergy to TEAS electrodes, conversion to open surgery, comorbid psychiatric illness, pregnancy/lactation, severe cardiopulmonary disease, severe hepatic or renal dysfunction, preoperative opioid abuse, and history of illicit drug use [cite: 39].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Laparoscopic hysterectomy [cite: 39].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** General anesthesia [cite: 39].
* **Induction:** Sufentanil (0.4 μg/kg), propofol (2 mg/kg) and rocuronium (0.6 mg/kg) intravenously [cite: 39].
* **Maintenance:** Continuous infusion of propofol (4-12 mg/kg/h) and remifentanil (0.1-0.2 μg/kg/min), supplemented with sevoflurane (1-2% end-tidal concentration) [cite: 39].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) [cite: 39].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 45 analyzed [cite: 39].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Bilateral Hegu (LI4), Neiguan (PC6), Sanyinjiao (SP6), and Zusanli (ST36) [cite: 39].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** 2/100 Hz dense-disperse wave mode [cite: 39].
* **Treatment intensity:** Set at 1 mA below the patient's maximum tolerance threshold [cite: 39].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** Commenced 30 min prior to anesthesia and continued throughout the surgery [cite: 39].
* **Duration of each treatment:** 30 min preoperative plus the duration of surgery [cite: 39].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** One single continuous session [cite: 39].
* **Total treatment period:** Single day (perioperative) [cite: 39].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** Intravenous palonosetron (0.25 mg) and flurbiprofen axetil (100 mg) administered 30 min before anticipated surgical completion [cite: 39]. Local anesthetic infiltration at the surgical incision site using 10 mL 0.75% ropivacaine hydrochloride [cite: 39]. Postoperative PCIA (sufentanil) [cite: 39].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Sham TEAS [cite: 39].
* **Number of participants in control group:** 48 analyzed [cite: 39].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** Electrodes placed identically for sham stimulation, administered in the 2/100 Hz dense-disperse mode, but 1 mA below the sensory threshold (no sensation) [cite: 39].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** Patient-controlled intravenous analgesia (PCIA) and rescue analgesia [cite: 39].
* **Dosage parameters:** PCIA: 100 μg sufentanil diluted to 100 mL with normal saline. Background infusion of 2 mL/h, bolus dose of 2 mL, lockout interval of 10 min [cite: 39]. Rescue in PACU: intravenous sufentanil if NRS ≥ 4. Rescue in ward: rectal indomethacin suppository (50 mg) if NRS ≥ 4 [cite: 39].
* **Duration:** Administered postoperatively [cite: 39].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Numerical Rating Scale (NRS) (0-10) [cite: 39].
* **Pain at rest / movement:** Differentiated by pain phenotype: Visceral pain, Low back pain (LBP), and Incisional pain [cite: 39].
* **Pain assessment time points:** Postoperative days (PODs) 0 (within first 6 h), 1, and 2 [cite: 39].
* **Mean/median pain score for each group:** Visualized in Figure 3 as median (IQR) [cite: 39].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** 
    * Visceral pain scores were significantly lower in the TEAS group on POD 0 and POD 1 (P = 0.007 and P = 0.009, respectively) [cite: 39].
    * LBP scores were significantly reduced in the TEAS group on POD 0, POD 1, and POD 2 (P = 0.012, P = 0.016, and P = 0.010, respectively) [cite: 39].
    * No differences were observed between the two groups in maximum acute incisional pain score (P > 0.017) [cite: 39].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Sufentanil (via PCIA and rescue) [cite: 39]. Remifentanil used intraoperatively [cite: 39].
* **Route of administration:** Intravenous [cite: 39].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention group:** Total sufentanil consumption on POD 1 = 50.53 ± 4.46 μg [cite: 39].
* **Opioid consumption for control group:** Total sufentanil consumption on POD 1 = 53.79 ± 5.14 μg [cite: 39].
* **Measurement time point(s):** POD 1 [cite: 39].
* **Between-group difference:** Patients in the TEAS group exhibited significantly reduced total sufentanil consumption on POD 1 compared to the control group (P = 0.002) [cite: 39].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** Total PCIA bolus demands (Median (IQR)): TEAS = 0 (0, 2); Control = 2 (1, 4) (P < 0.001) [cite: 39]. Rescue analgesia demands on POD 1 (n (%)): TEAS = 14 (31.11%); Control = 29 (60.42%) (P = 0.005) [cite: 39].
* **Between-group difference:** The TEAS group had significantly fewer total PCIA bolus demands and rescue analgesia demands on POD 1 (P < 0.05) [cite: 39].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting:** PONV incidence: TEAS group = 14 (31.11%); Control group = 25 (52.08%) (P = 0.04) [cite: 39].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** 
    * Time to first postoperative ambulation (h): TEAS = 19.91 ± 5.88; Control = 28.31 ± 19.01 (P = 0.006) [cite: 39].
    * Time to pelvic drain removal (h): TEAS = 19.95 ± 7.89; Control = 29.10 ± 17.51 (P = 0.005) [cite: 39].
    * No significant difference in time to first flatus, oral liquid intake, or solid food tolerance [cite: 39].
* **Length of stay:** Time to postoperative discharge (days): TEAS = 4.07 ± 1.68; Control = 6.06 ± 4.28 (P = 0.004) [cite: 39].
* **Hospitalization costs:** Total hospitalization costs (¥): TEAS = 14,542 ± 158; Control = 15,903 ± 15,245 (P < 0.001) [cite: 39].
* **Other relevant physiological/biochemical outcomes (Biomarkers):** Plasma IL-6 levels were significantly lower in the TEAS group than in the control group on POD 1 (P < 0.001) [cite: 39]. No differences were observed in IL-2, IL-4, or TNF-α [cite: 39].
