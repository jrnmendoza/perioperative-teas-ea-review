## STEP 2 — COMPLETE ALL RoB 2 SIGNALING QUESTIONS

### DOMAIN 1 — Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

#### 1.1 Was the allocation sequence random?
* **Response:** Y (Yes)
* **Evidence:** "A randomization sequence was generated by Statistical Product and Service Solutions (SPSS 25.0) software"[cite: 8].
* **Source:** Guo et al. (2023), Methods section[cite: 8].
* **Reasoning:** Sequence generation was performed using standard statistical software (SPSS), which is an established method for creating a genuinely random sequence.

#### 1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?
* **Response:** NI (No information)
* **Evidence:** "...and the allocation code was performed by an independent technician... Until the 12 months trial was completed, the technician didn’t share the information about divided groups with the statistician"[cite: 8].
* **Source:** Guo et al. (2023), Methods section[cite: 8].
* **Reasoning:** Although an independent technician held the allocation code, the source does not report the physical or logistical mechanism used to conceal allocations from enrolling investigators at the moment of participant recruitment (e.g., sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes or central web-based allocation).

#### 1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?
* **Response:** N (No)
* **Evidence:** "Overall, patients who completed the trial in the TEAS and the sham-TEAS groups were well-matched for baseline characteristics (Table 1). There were no significant differences in age, sex, education level, chronic diseases, and MoCA, or MMSE scores between the two groups at baseline"[cite: 8].
* **Source:** Guo et al. (2023), Results section and Table 1[cite: 8].
* **Reasoning:** Demographic and baseline clinical characteristics were evenly distributed with no notable imbalances suggesting compromised randomization.

#### Domain 1 Judgment
* **Judgment:** Some concerns
* **Rationale:** The allocation sequence was generated randomly and baseline comparability was satisfactory, but the specific procedure for allocation concealment at participant enrollment is not described.

### DOMAIN 2 — Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions (Effect of assignment to intervention)

#### 2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial?
* **Response:** PN (Probably no)
* **Evidence:** "The patients, anesthesiologists, surgeons, and observers were blinded to group assignment"[cite: 8]. "In the sham-TEAS group, the stimulators were also connected, but the power was turned off"[cite: 8].
* **Source:** Guo et al. (2023), Methods section[cite: 8].
* **Reasoning:** Patients were formally blinded, and identical-appearing stimulators were connected in both groups. While the absence of electrical sensation in the sham group is a known limitation of inactive controls, the trial explicitly implemented blinding with identical device setup.

#### 2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants' assigned intervention?
* **Response:** Y (Yes)
* **Evidence:** "This part was performed by an experienced technician who held a Good Clinical Practice Certificate. According to the allocation code of the enrolled patients with the time sequence, they were given TEAS and sham-TEAS interventions, respectively"[cite: 8]. Other staff ("anesthesiologists, surgeons, and observers") were blinded[cite: 8].
* **Source:** Guo et al. (2023), Methods section[cite: 8].
* **Reasoning:** The technician administering the intervention operated according to the allocation code to turn the power on or keep it off, making them aware of group assignment.

#### 2.3 If participants or carers were aware of intervention assignment, were there deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the experimental context?
* **Response:** NI (No information)
* **Evidence:** Insufficient information / not reported in the available sources.
* **Source:** Guo et al. (2023)[cite: 8].
* **Reasoning:** While the delivering technician was unblinded, no deviations, co-interventions, or protocol non-adherences arising from the experimental context are documented.

#### 2.4 Were these deviations likely to have affected the outcome?
* **Response:** NA (Not applicable)
* **Evidence:** Insufficient information / not reported in the available sources.
* **Reasoning:** No context-driven deviations were reported.

#### 2.5 Were these deviations balanced between groups?
* **Response:** NA (Not applicable)
* **Evidence:** Insufficient information / not reported in the available sources.
* **Reasoning:** No context-driven deviations were reported.

#### 2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention?
* **Response:** N (No)
* **Evidence:** "128 of them were enrolled and randomly assigned to the TEAS or sham-TEAS group, and 110 (85.94%) completed the trial, while 18 (14.06%) patients dropped out of the study, so their data were excluded from the analyses..."[cite: 8].
* **Source:** Guo et al. (2023), Results section[cite: 8].
* **Reasoning:** Eighteen randomized participants were excluded from the analysis rather than being evaluated according to an intention-to-treat (ITT) framework.

#### 2.7 If an inappropriate analysis was used, was there potential for a substantial impact on the result?
* **Response:** PN (Probably no)
* **Evidence:** Exclusions were balanced across groups (9 in the TEAS group and 9 in the sham-TEAS group)[cite: 8]. Documented reasons included conversion to open thoracotomy, surgical duration, benign pathology, and death[cite: 8]. Baseline characteristics of dropouts versus completers showed no significant differences ("Supplementary Material 3")[cite: 8].
* **Source:** Guo et al. (2023), Figure 1 and Results section[cite: 8].
* **Reasoning:** Because post-randomization exclusions were identical in number between arms, largely dictated by intraoperative surgical criteria or death, and balanced at baseline, they are unlikely to have substantially biased the estimated effect of assignment to intervention.

#### Domain 2 Judgment
* **Judgment:** Low risk
* **Rationale:** Key clinicians and participants were blinded. Post-randomization exclusions (14.1% overall) were balanced in number and etiology between arms, presenting minimal risk of substantial bias.

### DOMAIN 3 — Risk of bias due to missing outcome data

#### 3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, randomized participants?
* **Response:** PN (Probably no)
* **Evidence:** 
  * Total randomized: 128 (64 TEAS, 64 sham-TEAS)[cite: 8].
  * Analyzed at 6 months: 110 (55 TEAS, 55 sham-TEAS)[cite: 8, 31].
  * Missing outcome data: 18/128 (14.06%) overall (9/64 [14.1%] TEAS; 9/64 [14.1%] Sham-TEAS)[cite: 8].
* **Source:** Guo et al. (2023), Results section and Table 4[cite: 8, 31].
* **Reasoning:** Approximately 14% missing outcome data does not meet the standard of "all, or nearly all" participants.

#### 3.2 Is there evidence that the result was not biased by missing outcome data?
* **Response:** PY (Probably yes)
* **Evidence:** "No significant difference in baseline characteristics was found between the dropped patients and the completed patients (Supplementary Material 3)"[cite: 8].
* **Source:** Guo et al. (2023), Results section[cite: 8].
* **Reasoning:** Dropouts were compared directly to completers and demonstrated balanced baseline characteristics, mitigating concerns of attrition-related selection bias.

#### 3.3 Could missingness in the outcome depend on its true value?
* **Response:** PN (Probably no)
* **Evidence:** Exclusions and dropouts detailed in Figure 1:
  * TEAS group (n = 9): converted to open thoracotomy (2), unilateral pulmonary resection (1), underwent two operations (3), declined follow-up (2), passed away (1)[cite: 8].
  * Sham-TEAS group (n = 9): converted to open thoracotomy (4), operation time < 2 hours (1), benign tumor (1), passed away (3)[cite: 8].
* **Source:** Guo et al. (2023), Figure 1[cite: 8].
* **Reasoning:** Reasons for missingness were related to surgical procedural changes, histological diagnoses, or mortality, rather than participant pain levels at 6 months.

#### 3.4 Is it likely that missingness in the outcome depended on its true value?
* **Response:** PN (Probably no)
* **Evidence:** Flow diagram and dropout breakdown in Figure 1[cite: 8].
* **Source:** Guo et al. (2023), Figure 1[cite: 8].
* **Reasoning:** There is no evidence indicating that patient withdrawal or surgical deviation was driven by underlying postoperative pain.

#### Domain 3 Judgment
* **Judgment:** Low risk
* **Rationale:** Although outcome data were missing for 14.1% of patients, the proportion and reasons were balanced between groups, and missingness was primarily driven by intraoperative surgical events and pathology rather than patient pain scores.

### DOMAIN 4 — Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

#### 4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?
* **Response:** N (No)
* **Evidence:** Pain was assessed as part of the EORTC-QLQ-C30 questionnaire[cite: 8, 31].
* **Source:** Guo et al. (2023), Methods, Table 3, and Table 4[cite: 8, 31].
* **Reasoning:** The EORTC-QLQ-C30 is an established, validated instrument for evaluating health-related quality of life and symptom burdens (including pain) in oncology cohorts.

#### 4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?
* **Response:** N (No)
* **Evidence:** Both groups completed identical EORTC-QLQ-C30 follow-up evaluations at 6 and 12 months[cite: 8, 31].
* **Source:** Guo et al. (2023), Methods and Tables 3–4[cite: 8, 31].
* **Reasoning:** Identical instruments and assessment schedules were applied to both study arms.

#### 4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received?
* **Response:** N (No)
* **Evidence:** "The patients, anesthesiologists, surgeons, and observers were blinded to group assignment"[cite: 8].
* **Source:** Guo et al. (2023), Methods section[cite: 8].
* **Reasoning:** For patient-reported outcomes (such as pain scores on the EORTC-QLQ-C30), participants act as their own assessors. In this study, participants and follow-up observers were blinded to group assignments.

#### 4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?
* **Response:** NA (Not applicable)
* **Evidence:** Patients and outcome assessors were blinded[cite: 8].
* **Reasoning:** With effective blinding maintained, knowledge of intervention cannot bias the evaluation.

#### 4.5 Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?
* **Response:** NA (Not applicable)
* **Evidence:** Patients and outcome assessors were blinded[cite: 8].
* **Reasoning:** Blinding prevents awareness of intervention assignment from influencing the assessment.

#### Domain 4 Judgment
* **Judgment:** Low risk
* **Rationale:** The outcome was collected using a validated patient-reported symptom scale with participants and observers adequately blinded.
