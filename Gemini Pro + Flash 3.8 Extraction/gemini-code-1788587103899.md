### DOMAIN 1 — Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1 Was the allocation sequence random?**
* **Response:** Y
* **Evidence:** "The participants were randomized into either TEAS (n = 37) or control (n = 37) group in a 1:1 ratio based on a randomization number generated by an independent researcher using IBM®SPSS 25.0..."[cite: 7].
* **Reasoning:** A computer-generated sequence using SPSS qualifies as a genuinely random allocation sequence.

**1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**
* **Response:** Y
* **Evidence:** "The randomization number was placed in a sealed envelope and was not revealed until the first intervention."[cite: 7].
* **Reasoning:** The use of sealed envelopes prepared by an independent researcher prevents advance knowledge of assignments before enrollment. 

**1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**
* **Response:** NI
* **Evidence:** Insufficient information / not reported in the available sources.
* **Reasoning:** The provided notebook sources for this specific study do not contain the baseline characteristics table to verify whether imbalances exist. 

**Domain 1 judgment:** LOW RISK
**Domain 1 rationale:** The allocation sequence was randomly generated and adequately concealed using sealed envelopes. Although baseline characteristics are not available in the provided text, the robust randomization and concealment methods generally protect against selection bias, yielding a Low Risk judgment.

### DOMAIN 2 — Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention)

**2.4 Were these deviations likely to have affected the outcome?**
* **Response:** NA
* **Evidence:** Insufficient information / not reported in the available sources.
* **Reasoning:** No known context-driven deviations were identified.

**2.5 Were these deviations balanced between groups?**
* **Response:** NA
* **Evidence:** Insufficient information / not reported in the available sources.
* **Reasoning:** No known context-driven deviations were identified.

**2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention?**
* **Response:** N
* **Evidence:** 37 participants were randomized to each group (total 74), but data are reported for 36 in the Control group and 36 in the TEAS group[cite: 7]. 
* **Reasoning:** The authors appear to have excluded 1 participant per group from the final analysis, meaning a strict intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis of all randomized participants was not performed.

**2.7 If an inappropriate analysis was used, was there potential for a substantial impact on the result?**

* **Reasoning:** The exclusion of a single participant per group (approx. 2.7% missing data) is perfectly balanced and highly unlikely to have a substantial impact on the overall effect estimate.

**Domain 2 judgment:** LOW RISK
**Domain 2 rationale:** Participants and routine clinical care providers were blinded. Although the acupuncturist was unblinded, no deviations from the intended intervention were reported. Exclusions from the analysis were minimal (1 per group) and balanced, posing no substantial risk of bias.
