### DOMAIN 1 — Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1 Was the allocation sequence random?**
* **Response:** Y
* **Evidence:** "Patients were randomly assigned to either the TEAS group (T group) or the control group (C group), using a computer-generated design (with the time of the start of the study, 20,231,212 as the random number seed), in a 1:1 ratio."[cite: 31].
* **Reasoning:** A computer-generated random number seed represents an appropriate random sequence generation method.

**1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**
* **Response:** Y
* **Evidence:** "Allocations were concealed in sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes prepared by independent study monitors."[cite: 31].
* **Reasoning:** Sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes are an adequate method of allocation concealment.

**1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**
* **Response:** N
* **Evidence:** "The baseline patients’ characteristics were comparable between the two groups (P > 0.05), and there was no difference in surgical data between the groups (P > 0.05)."[cite: 31].
* **Reasoning:** No baseline imbalances were observed.

**Domain 1 judgment:** LOW RISK
**Domain 1 rationale:** The allocation sequence was randomly generated, appropriately concealed using opaque sealed envelopes, and yielded balanced baseline characteristics.

### DOMAIN 2 — Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions (effect of assignment to intervention)

**2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial?**
* **Response:** N
* **Evidence:** "All other individuals involved in the study (patients, surgeons, anesthesiologists, data collectors, data analysts) were blinded to the randomization and the stimulation."[cite: 31].
* **Reasoning:** Participants were explicitly reported to be blinded.

**2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants' assigned intervention?**
* **Response:** Y
* **Evidence:** "the TEAS operator, who was not involved in the care of the patient and independent of this study, opened the numbered envelope, followed the arrangement, and conducted TEAS or false stimulation"[cite: 31].
* **Reasoning:** The dedicated TEAS operator was aware of the intervention allocation, although general carers (anesthesiologists, surgeons) were blinded.

**2.3 If participants or carers were aware of intervention assignment, were there deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the experimental context?**
* **Response:** NI
* **Evidence:** Insufficient information / not reported in the available sources.
* **Reasoning:** No deviations arising from the unblinded TEAS operator are reported.

**2.4 Were these deviations likely to have affected the outcome?**
* **Response:** NA
* **Evidence:** Insufficient information / not reported in the available sources.
* **Reasoning:** No known deviations.

**2.5 Were these deviations balanced between groups?**
* **Response:** NA
* **Evidence:** Insufficient information / not reported in the available sources.
* **Reasoning:** No known deviations.

**2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention?**
* **Response:** N
* **Evidence:** "Among the 102 patients, 16 were excluded: 9 patients declined to participate, 5 patients withdrew from the study due to surgical modification, and one patient in each of the two groups withdrew due to redness and itching on the skin... * **Reasoning:** The exclusions were largely due to surgical modifications or minor skin reactions, and the final groups were perfectly balanced (43 vs 43), suggesting minimal potential for substantial impact.

**Domain 2 judgment:** LOW RISK
**Domain 2 rationale:** The strict blinding of patients, anesthesiologists, and surgeons prevents context-driven deviations. While some patients were excluded post-randomization, the reasons were largely surgical and balanced, leading to low risk of bias.
