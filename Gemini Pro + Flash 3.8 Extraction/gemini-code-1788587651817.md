### DOMAIN 4 — Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?**
* **Response:** N
* **Evidence:** Time to first flatus was measured in hours[cite: 16].
* **Reasoning:** Time to first flatus is a standard, appropriate clinical metric for postoperative gastrointestinal recovery.

**4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?**
* **Response:** N
* **Evidence:** The outcome is a standard milestone assessed postoperatively for both groups[cite: 16].

**4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received?**
* **Response:** Y
* **Evidence:** The comparator was "usual care"[cite: 16]. 
* **Reasoning:** Time to first flatus is typically self-reported by the patient to the nursing staff. Because the trial lacked a sham group, the patients (the primary assessors of this event) were aware of their intervention.

**4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**
* **Response:** PY
* **Evidence:** Patients knew they were receiving electroacupuncture aimed at improving recovery[cite: 16].
* **Reasoning:** Flatus is a patient-reported milestone. Knowledge of receiving an active, complementary treatment versus usual care can introduce a placebo effect, making patients hyper-vigilant or biased in reporting the exact timing of their first flatus.

**4.5 Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**
* **Response:** PY
* **Evidence:** The trial lacked blinding entirely[cite: 16].
* **Reasoning:** In an open-label trial with a usual care control, subjective, patient-reported outcomes like time to first flatus are highly susceptible to performance and measurement biases.

**Domain 4 judgment:** HIGH RISK
**Domain 4 rationale:** The outcome (time to first flatus) relies heavily on patient reporting. Because patients were completely unblinded (usual care control), their knowledge of the intervention likely influenced the assessment of this subjective recovery milestone.

* **Response:** NI
* **Evidence:** Insufficient information / not reported in the available sources.
* **Reasoning:** There is no mention of a prospective trial registration or a published protocol in the provided text.

**5.2 Is the numerical result likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible outcome measurements within the outcome domain?**
* **Response:** PN
* **Evidence:** "The time to first flatus was 2 h shorter in the EA group as compared with that of the UC group"[cite: 16].
* **Reasoning:** Time to first flatus is a singular, standard event; it is unlikely there were multiple eligible measurements of this specific event to selectively choose from.

**5.3 Is the numerical result likely to have been selected, on the basis of the results, from multiple eligible analyses of the data?**
* **Response:** PN
* **Evidence:** "T tests or Mann–Whitney U tests were used to compare the group differences on continuous variables as appropriate."[cite: 16].
* **Reasoning:** The analysis used standard unadjusted non-parametric/parametric tests without evidence of selective covariate manipulation.

**Domain 5 judgment:** SOME CONCERNS
**Domain 5 rationale:** While the analysis is standard and there is no obvious selective reporting, the lack of a verified, prospectively registered analysis plan means selective reporting cannot be completely ruled out.
