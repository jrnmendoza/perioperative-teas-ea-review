# 20. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Ao et al. 2021** was a single-center randomized controlled trial in China involving women aged 20-65 years undergoing elective radical mastectomy for breast cancer under general anesthesia. **70 patients were randomized (35 TEAS, 35 sham), and 65 were analyzed (32 TEAS, 33 sham).** TEAS was delivered bilaterally at **LI4, PC6 and ST36** using a **HANS LH-202** device at **2/100 Hz**, with intensity of **5-10 mA in the upper limbs and 10-30 mA in the lower limbs**, for **30 min per session**. Treatment was given **30 min before anesthesia induction, at 4 and 12 h postoperatively, and three times daily on POD1 and POD2**.

The comparator received **electrodes at the same acupoints without electrical stimulation**. Both groups received standardized general anesthesia and postoperative **sufentanil PCA for 48 h**.

TEAS significantly reduced VAS pain at **12 and 24 h**, reduced **48-h PCA solution consumption (102.8 ± 7.4 vs 120.6 ± 9.2 ml; P<0.001)** and reduced effective PCA presses (**6.2 ± 3.7 vs 12.3 ± 4.6; P<0.001**). Based on the stated PCA concentration, the 48-h sufentanil exposure corresponds to approximately **1.028 vs 1.206 µg/kg**, a **14.8% relative reduction**. However, **0-24 h opioid consumption is not reported**, so a 24-h MME endpoint or 24-h MCID classification cannot be determined directly from the paper.

PONV was lower with TEAS (**4/32 [12.5%] vs 12/33 [36.4%], P=0.042**), as was headache. Dizziness and pruritus did not differ significantly. TEAS also produced favorable postoperative changes in IL-2, IL-4, IFN-gamma and the IL-2/IL-4 ratio.
