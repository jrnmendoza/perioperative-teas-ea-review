# 21. Important methodological / extraction issues

1. **Four-arm design:** Do not collapse sham, nonacupoint, and dermatomal stimulation into a single control group.
2. **Active electrical comparator:** Group II controls for electrical stimulation away from recognized acupoints.
3. **Dermatomal active comparator:** Group III achieved an opioid-sparing effect comparable to the ST36 acupoint group.
4. **No exact pain VAS means:** The paper assessed pain VAS but does not provide usable numeric postoperative pain means in the supplied full text.
5. **No trial registration reported.**
6. **Random-sequence generation and allocation concealment are not described.**
7. **Actual number of TENS sessions was not recorded.**
8. **MME is not reported.** A route-specific hydromorphone conversion must come from the review's predefined conversion framework, not from this article.
9. **Study site is not completely explicit:** authors are from two institutions, while the IRB was Cedars-Sinai.
10. **Single-blind rather than double-blind.**

# 23. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Chen et al. 1998** randomized **100 ASA I-II women** undergoing elective abdominal hysterectomy or myomectomy under standardized general anesthesia to four postoperative regimens: **sham ST36 TENS (0 mA), active nonacupoint shoulder TENS, active peri-incisional dermatomal TENS, or active bilateral ST36 acupoint TENS** (n=25 each).

Active stimulation used a **HAN LY257** device in dense-and-disperse **2/100 Hz** mode, alternating 2 and 100 Hz every 3 s, at **9-12 mA**. Treatment began in PACU, lasted **30 min per session**, and was repeated every **2-3 h while awake**, at bedtime, and on waking. The acupoint group used TENS for a mean of **32 ± 8 h**.

All patients received postoperative **IV hydromorphone PCA**. During the first 24 h, hydromorphone consumption was **6.5 ± 3.5 mg in the ST36 acupoint group vs 10.7 ± 5.0 mg in the sham group**, an absolute reduction of **4.2 mg** and a relative reduction of **39.3%**. Acupoint TENS also reduced PCA demand/delivery counts, shortened PCA duration (**31 ± 10 vs 38 ± 9 h**), and reduced nausea and dizziness compared with sham and nonacupoint stimulation.

The active dermatomal group produced a similar opioid-sparing effect, which is important when interpreting whether the benefit is specifically acupoint-mediated. Exact postoperative pain VAS means are not numerically reported, and MME/MCID classification requires the systematic review's predefined IV hydromorphone conversion rule.
