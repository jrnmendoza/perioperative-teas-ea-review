# 20. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Chen et al. 2020** was a single-center randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in China of adults aged 18-64 years with early-stage lung cancer undergoing **VATS pulmonary resection under general anesthesia**. The analyzed sample was **80 patients (40 TEAS, 40 sham TEAS)**, although the flowchart indicates **86 were originally randomized** before six were lost to follow-up.

TEAS was applied bilaterally at **LI4, PC6, SI3 and TE6** using a **HANS-200A** device at **2/100 Hz**. Active stimulation was **10-15 mA for 30 min before induction**, **30 mA continuously intraoperatively**, and **10-15 mA for 30 min at 6, 24 and 48 h postoperatively**. Sham patients received **4 mA pre- and postoperatively** and **no intraoperative stimulation**.

Both groups received standardized general anesthesia and postoperative **sufentanil PCIA**. Cumulative sufentanil consumption was significantly lower with TEAS at all reported time points: **46.85 ± 5.65 vs 60.08 ± 6.91 µg at 6 h**, **72.43 ± 4.78 vs 100.62 ± 10.20 µg at 24 h**, and **118.52 ± 9.77 vs 140.15 ± 7.87 µg at 48 h**, all **P<0.001**. The **24-h absolute reduction was 28.19 µg and the relative reduction was 28.0%**.

VAS pain was also significantly lower with TEAS at **6, 24, and 48 h**, and at **1 month**. At 24 h, VAS was **2.91 ± 0.20 vs 5.97 ± 0.56 (P<0.001)**. Nausea and vomiting were substantially reduced at most early postoperative time points. Exact MME and MCID classification should be derived using the review's prespecified sufentanil conversion rule rather than inferred from the article alone.
