# 19. Major extraction / quality issues requiring attention

1. **High post-randomization attrition:** 10/32 randomized patients were excluded (31.3%).
2. **Original randomized group denominators are not reported**, so attrition by treatment allocation cannot be assessed.
3. **Analysis is not intention-to-treat.**
4. **Anesthetic management was not standardized.**
5. **Small analyzed sample:** 13 intervention and 9 control.
6. **Gender imbalance:** treatment 10/3 M/F vs control 4/5 M/F; authors acknowledge this could explain some observed differences.
7. **Sham was not fully matched:** no needles, only brief 300-Hz stimulation for 5 s, and PC6-TE5 is not listed in the sham electrode sites.
8. **Pain summary definition is unclear.**
9. **PONV was not actually quantified**, despite discussion of opioid side effects.
10. **Fentanyl is reported in µg/kg**, preventing absolute-dose MME conversion without body weight.
11. **Supplementary morphine/fentanyl was permitted but not reported separately.**
12. **Trial registration is not reported.**
13. **No sample-size calculation is provided.**
14. **No dedicated acupuncture adverse-event reporting.**

# 21. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Coura et al. 2011** was a prospective randomized controlled trial in Brazil evaluating **one 30-minute session of electroacupuncture 12-18 h before conventional cardiac surgery**. Thirty-two patients were randomized, but **10 were excluded after randomization**, leaving **13 EA and 9 sham-control patients** for analysis.

Active EA used bilateral **LI4-LI11, LR3-ST36 and PC6-TE5**, with alternating **3 and 15 Hz every 4 seconds**, at the patient's maximum tolerated intensity. Needles were inserted 1-2 cm, with ST36 inserted 2.3 cm and manually elicited de qi before stimulation. The sham group received ECG electrode patches at LI4-LI11 and LR3-ST36, **300 Hz for 5 seconds**, after which the device was switched off but left connected for 30 minutes.

Postoperatively, both groups received fentanyl PCA with **0.4 µg/kg/h basal infusion** and **0.3 µg/kg boluses** with a 15-minute lockout. During the first 24 h, total fentanyl consumption was **13.1 ± 2.2 µg/kg with EA vs 16.3 ± 1.6 µg/kg with sham (P<0.002)**, a **19.6% relative reduction**. PCA bolus fentanyl was **4.1 ± 2.0 vs 6.9 ± 1.7 µg/kg (P<0.003)**, a **40.6% relative reduction**, and effective boluses were **13.9 ± 7.0 vs 24.8 ± 7.0 (P<0.002)**. The reported 24-h summary pain score was also lower with EA (**2.5 ± 1.1 vs 4.0 ± 2.0; P<0.04**).

The study cannot provide a reliable absolute 24-h MME without additional body-weight/absolute-dose data. Major audit concerns are the **31% post-randomization exclusion rate, small analyzed sample, gender imbalance, non-standardized anesthesia, and incompletely matched sham intervention**.
