## 20. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**An 2014** was a single-center randomized trial from Beijing, China, of **88 adults (ASA I-II, 18-60 y)** undergoing supratentorial tumor resection under general anesthesia. Patients were randomized to **intraoperative electro-acupuncture** or **no acupuncture**. EA used **LI4, SJ5, BL63, LR3, ST36, and GB40**, ipsilateral to the craniotomy, with **2/100 Hz dense-dispersed stimulation alternating every 3 s**, from anesthesia induction until surgery end. **81 patients were analyzed (41 EA, 40 control).**

Both groups received postoperative **fentanyl PCIA for 48 h** (10 µg/kg fentanyl + 8 mg ondansetron in 100 mL; no basal infusion; 1 mL bolus; 15-min lockout). EA significantly reduced early postoperative VAS pain and PCIA bolus use during the **first 6 h**, but **total postoperative fentanyl did not differ significantly** (**0.67 ± 0.09 mg vs 0.73 ± 0.12 mg**). Exact **0-24 h fentanyl consumption is not reported**, so a 24-h MME endpoint cannot be reliably extracted from the publication. PONV was numerically lower but not statistically significant. EA reduced dizziness/fullness during the first 24 h and improved appetite at 24 h.
