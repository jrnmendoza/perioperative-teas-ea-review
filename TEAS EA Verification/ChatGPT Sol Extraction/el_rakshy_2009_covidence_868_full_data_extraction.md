# 3. Participant flow and major denominator inconsistencies

## Flow chart (Figure 1)
The published flow chart reports:

| Stage | Acupuncture | Control | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Randomized / allocated | **60** | **47** | **107** |
| Did not receive / discontinued allocated intervention | **2** | **3** | **5** |
| Analyzed | **58** | **44** | **102** |

Reasons shown:
- Acupuncture: **2 refused acupuncture**
- Control: **3 changed operative or anesthetic procedure**

## Results text / Table 1
However, the Results section explicitly states:
- **58 patients were analyzed in the PCA-alone arm**
- **44 patients were analyzed in the PCA + acupuncture arm**

Table 1 also totals:
- PCA alone = **58**
- PCA + acupuncture = **44**

This is the **reverse of the labels in the flow chart**.

## Withdrawal narrative
The text further states:
- **2 patients in the PCA + acupuncture group** withdrew because they did not want acupuncture.
- **3 patients in the PCA-alone group** were withdrawn because of epidural/other analgesia or change in procedure.

Those statements do **not** reconcile with the flow-chart starting group sizes of 60 and 47.

## Abstract
The abstract gives yet another group description: electroacupuncture **n=56** and no additional treatment **n=46**, which totals the 102 analyzed patients but does not match the Results text, Table 1, or flow chart.

## Table 3
The published pain table uses denominators of:
- PCA = **56**
- PCA + acupuncture = **46**

Again, these do not match the stated 58/44 analyzed groups.

### Extraction decision
For outcome extraction, preserve the **outcome-specific denominators printed in each table**. For overall group assignment, the most internally supported narrative/Table 1 statement is **58 PCA-alone and 44 PCA + acupuncture**, but this should remain flagged as a publication-level inconsistency.

# 10. Morphine / analgesic consumption

Table 2 reports the following. Outcome-specific denominators vary because of missing data.

| Outcome | PCA alone | PCA + acupuncture | P-value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| PCA used in recovery, mg | **n=52; 3.6 ± 3.3** | **n=40; 4.0 ± 2.7** | **0.070** |
| Total analgesia in first 4 h, mg | **n=53; 13.2 ± 8.6** | **n=40; 13.4 ± 7.5** | **0.675** |
| Total analgesia in 24 h, mg | **n=53; 36.9 ± 18.0** | **n=42; 35.3 ± 18.0** | **0.961** |

### 24-h opioid-sparing calculation
Assuming the Table 2 24-h value corresponds to the morphine outcome described as the primary endpoint:

- PCA alone: **36.9 ± 18.0 mg**
- PCA + EA: **35.3 ± 18.0 mg**
- Absolute difference: **1.6 mg less** in the EA group - calculated
- Relative reduction: **4.3%** - calculated
- Statistical significance: **none (P=0.961)**

### 4-h result
- PCA alone: **13.2 ± 8.6 mg**
- EA: **13.4 ± 7.5 mg**
- EA was **0.2 mg higher**, not lower - calculated
- **P=0.675**

### MME / MCID
The opioid is morphine, but it was given by the **IV PCA route**. The paper does not report MME and does not specify a route-conversion framework.

- Do **not** automatically equate 1 mg IV morphine to 1 mg oral MME unless that is the review's prespecified conversion rule.
- The raw 24-h between-group difference is only **1.6 mg IV morphine** and is statistically non-significant.
- Final MME and MCID classification should use the systematic review's fixed conversion method.

# 18. Major audit / data-quality issues

1. **Severe internal inconsistency in group sizes and labels.**
   - Flow chart: acupuncture 60 -> 58 analyzed; control 47 -> 44 analyzed.
   - Results text/Table 1: PCA alone 58; PCA + acupuncture 44.
   - Abstract: acupuncture n=56; no-treatment n=46.
   - Table 3 pain denominators: PCA 56; acupuncture 46.

2. **Withdrawal reasons do not reconcile with the flow-chart starting group sizes.**

3. **Sex allocation is internally inconsistent.** Table 1 shows 8 male patients in the acupuncture arm, while the text says 8 male patients were randomized to PCA alone.

4. **Original study dataset became inaccessible**, preventing recovery of baseline age and serial pain, PONV and analgesic-use data.

5. **Outcome-specific denominators vary substantially** in Table 2, indicating missing outcome data.

6. **Pain Table 3 denominators are incompatible with the stated analyzed groups.**

7. **Recovery-time unit is questionable.** Table 2 labels the outcome in hours while ~59 appears much more consistent with minutes.

8. **No allocation-concealment procedure is reported.**

9. **No trial registration is reported.**

10. **No sample-size/power calculation is reported.**

11. **Groups were not homogeneous for sex or operation type**, which the authors identify as a limitation.

12. **The two surgery types were pooled**, and surgery-specific results were no longer available.

13. **Single-frequency 10-Hz EA** may be a protocol-specific limitation; this is an author interpretation, not an extraction correction.

14. **No dedicated acupuncture adverse-event reporting.**

# 20. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**El-Rakshy et al. 2009 (Study #868)** evaluated **10-Hz intraoperative electroacupuncture under general anesthesia** in patients undergoing elective abdominal hysterectomy or laparoscopic cholecystectomy. The article states that **107 patients were randomized and 102 completed**, but the publication contains major inconsistencies regarding the actual group sizes and labels. The Results text and Table 1 support **58 PCA-alone and 44 PCA + acupuncture** patients, while the flow chart labels these numbers in the opposite direction and other sections report 56/46.

For hysterectomy, EA used **GV2, GV4, BL32, BL23, LI4 and PC6**; for laparoscopic cholecystectomy, **LR3, SP6, LI4 and PC6** were used. Stimulation began **after induction of anesthesia**, continued for the **duration of surgery**, and was delivered at **10 Hz** with intensity **7/10** on an IC-4107 electroacupuncture unit. No postoperative EA was given.

All patients received standardized general anesthesia and postoperative **IV morphine PCA (1 mg/mL; 5-min lockout)**. The primary opioid outcome showed **no benefit**: total 24-h analgesia/morphine was **35.3 ± 18.0 mg with EA vs 36.9 ± 18.0 mg with PCA alone (P=0.961)**, only a **4.3% relative reduction**. Four-hour analgesic use was virtually identical. Pain distribution favored EA numerically but was **not significant (P=0.147)**; PONV and sedation were also not significantly different.

This study has substantial extraction-quality concerns because the **original dataset became inaccessible**, group denominators conflict across the publication, baseline variables and serial outcomes were lost, and the two surgery types could not be analyzed separately.
