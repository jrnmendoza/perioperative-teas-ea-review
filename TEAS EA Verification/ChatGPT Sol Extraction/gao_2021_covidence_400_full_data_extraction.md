# 20. Methodological / audit issues

1. **Treatment begins only after surgery.** Randomization occurred after completion of surgery/general anesthesia, so this is a postoperative TEAS trial rather than a pre/intraoperative opioid-sparing trial.
2. **Participant treatment blinding was imperfect.** Active TEAS produced perceptible tingling/de qi; sham produced no current.
3. **Small block size (4).** The authors themselves note this could potentially weaken allocation concealment.
4. **One baseline/perioperative imbalance:** intraoperative plasma use was lower in TEAS and was adjusted for.
5. **No postoperative opioid-dose outcome.** Despite postoperative analgesia being nearly universal, the main publication does not report drug/dose consumption.
6. **Primary outcome is gastrointestinal recovery, not analgesia.**
7. **Abdominal pain score is not a standard clearly defined postoperative surgical-pain endpoint in the main text.**
8. **Supplementary material contains additional 30-day/hospital-stay data not reproduced in the main PDF.**
9. **PDF text extraction distorts some µg symbols as mg**, so exact anesthetic drug units should be checked against the rendered page before entering dose fields.

# 22. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Gao et al. 2021 (Study #400)** was a **10-center randomized sham-controlled trial** in China involving **610 adults undergoing colorectal cancer surgery under general anesthesia**. Patients were randomized after surgery to **postoperative TEAS (n=303)** or **sham TEAS (n=307)**, and all were included in intention-to-treat analysis.

TEAS was applied bilaterally at **ST36, ST37, and SP6** using an **SDZ-V stimulator** at **2-10 Hz**, adjusted to maximum tolerated intensity. Treatment was delivered for **30 min on four occasions**: first in PACU once the patient reached a Steward recovery score of 4, then once each morning on POD1-POD3. Sham patients received identical electrode placement and device handling, but the connecting wire was severed so **no current was delivered**.

The primary outcome was **postoperative paralytic ileus (no flatus >72 h)**. POI occurred in **32.3% with TEAS vs 41.0% with sham (P=.026)**; unadjusted OR was **0.68 (95% CI 0.48-0.96)**. TEAS also shortened first flatus by **12.8 h**, first defecation by **14.9 h**, normal diet recovery by **51.6 h**, and bowel-sound recovery by **4.6 h**.

However, this study does **not report quantitative postoperative opioid consumption**, PCA settings, or MME. The abdominal pain score (**1.4 ± 1.4 vs 1.3 ± 1.5, P=.555**), nausea score, and vomiting frequency did not significantly differ. Therefore, this article is informative for **gastrointestinal recovery/POI**, but it **cannot supply a 24-h opioid-sparing or MME endpoint from the published main text**.
