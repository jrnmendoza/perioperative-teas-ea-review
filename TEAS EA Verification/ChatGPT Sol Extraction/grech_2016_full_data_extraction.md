### Important sex imbalance
The **control group contained women only (9/9)**, while the EA group contained **9 women and 2 men**. Because the authors considered sex to affect pain/analgesic threshold, they performed a **women-only analgesic analysis**.

### Individual-data issue
Table 1 includes patient #11 in the EA group, but the methods also state:

> “Patient #11 was excluded because of pre-existing levels of TNF >1 µg/mL prior to surgery.”

The publication does **not clearly explain whether patient #11 was excluded from the entire clinical analysis or only from specific biomarker analyses**. Table 2 still reports **n=20**, so this should remain an audit flag.

# 9. Postoperative analgesic requirement

## Entire heterogeneous sample
The authors report that when **all patients** were compared, the EA and control groups had **similar analgesic use and pain scores**.

Exact numeric morphine-equivalent means and SEM are **not tabulated**.

## Women-only analysis
Because the control group contained women only and sex was considered relevant to analgesic threshold, the authors compared the **9 control women vs 9 EA women**.

### Key result
**EA-treated women required 60% less postoperative analgesic in PACU than control women**, while pain scores remained similar.

- Outcome unit: **morphine-equivalent mg**
- Exact group means: **not numerically reported**
- Exact SEM: **not numerically reported**
- Statistical figure marker: Figure 1D shows a significant difference
- Authors’ summary: **60% reduction**

## Age subgroup
EA significantly reduced PACU analgesic request in the **younger (<45 y)** subgroup but not the older subgroup.

## Weight subgroup
The text states EA produced a similar analgesic-reduction pattern in heavier and lighter subgroups; Figure 1F should be used for the graphical subgroup interpretation.

# 13. Physiological stress outcomes

Blood was collected at:
1. **Pre** - before EA and surgery
2. **Intra** - after EA, during surgery
3. **Post** - during anesthesia immediately after surgery

## ACTH
- Surgery increased ACTH in controls by approximately **10-fold**.
- EA reduced mean postoperative/PACU ACTH by **>70%**.
- EA reduced postoperative ACTH by **>80% in older and heavier subgroups**.
- Exact numeric means are graphical, not tabulated.

## Cortisol
- Surgery increased cortisol in controls by approximately **3-fold**.
- EA **did not significantly alter cortisol** overall.
- Exact numeric means are graphical.

## Glucose
- Surgery produced progressive postoperative hyperglycemia in controls.
- EA **prevented postoperative hyperglycemia**.
- Effect was more significant in **older and heavier** patients.
- Exact numeric means are graphical.

## Insulin
- Surgery did not meaningfully change serum insulin.
- EA did not produce a clearly reported major insulin difference.

# 14. Immune / inflammatory outcomes

## TNF
- Neither surgery nor EA significantly changed TNF.

## IL-2
- Neither surgery nor EA significantly changed IL-2.

## IL-4
- Neither surgery nor EA significantly changed IL-4.

## IL-6
- Surgery increased IL-6 by approximately **7-fold** postsurgery.
- EA **did not prevent or attenuate the IL-6 increase**.

## IL-10
- Surgery increased IL-10 by approximately **2-fold** postsurgery.
- EA **did not significantly alter this protective response**.

## TGF-β1
- Surgery increased serum TGF-β1 in controls.
- EA induced approximately **3-fold higher postoperative TGF-β1**.
- Enhancement was especially noted in **older and lighter** patients.
- Exact numeric means are graphical rather than tabulated.

# 19. Major extraction / quality issues

1. **Very small pilot sample (n=20).**
2. **Sex imbalance:** control group was entirely female; EA group included two men.
3. **Authors therefore emphasize a women-only analgesic analysis.**
4. **Random-sequence generation and allocation concealment are not described.**
5. **Sham-control procedure is not adequately described in the main article.**
6. **Exact postoperative analgesic means are not tabulated; key analgesic results are graphical.**
7. **Exact 24-h postoperative morphine-equivalent values are unavailable.**
8. **Specific postoperative analgesic drugs and regimen are not described.**
9. **The publication states patient #11 was excluded for high preoperative TNF, yet Table 1 includes patient #11 and Table 2 still reports n=20.**
10. **Most hormonal/cytokine outcomes are graphical rather than tabulated.**
11. **Data are reported as mean ± SEM, not SD.**
12. **No dedicated adverse-event dataset.**
13. **Primary endpoints were physiological/immune outcomes, with analgesic use as a secondary endpoint.**
14. **The 60% analgesic reduction applies specifically to women at PACU, not clearly to the full randomized population or to a 24-h endpoint.**

# 21. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Grech et al. 2016** was a small prospective randomized double-blinded pilot trial in the United States involving **20 patients undergoing thyroid or parathyroid surgery under general anesthesia** (**9 control, 11 EA**). Active EA was delivered bilaterally at **LI4, LI11, and ST36** using a **10-Hz symmetrical biphasic waveform** through 30-gauge needles. Patients received **two 30-minute intraoperative treatments**, the first beginning approximately **15 minutes after anesthetic induction**, with a roughly 30-minute interval between sessions.

The control/sham procedure is insufficiently described in the main article. Postoperative analgesics were converted to **morphine-equivalent milligrams**, but exact PACU and 24-h group means are not numerically tabulated. In the authors’ women-only comparison (**9 control women vs 9 EA women**), EA reduced PACU analgesic use by **60%** without increasing pain scores. When the full mixed-sex groups were compared, analgesic use and pain scores were similar.

EA also reduced postoperative ACTH and prevented postoperative hyperglycemia, while preserving the normal surgical increases in IL-6 and IL-10. The most prominent immune finding was increased postoperative **TGF-β1**. Quality-of-recovery scores during the following 3 days were similar between groups.

For a systematic review requiring an exact **24-h opioid/MME endpoint**, this article is **not directly usable without author data**, because the exact 0-24 h morphine-equivalent mean and variance are not provided.
