# 22. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **Complete-case rather than intention-to-treat analysis:** 3/120 randomized participants were not analyzed.
2. **Allocation concealment is not described.**
3. **Patient blinding was intended, but success was not tested.**
4. **Active TEAS caused de qi sensations while sham delivered no current**, making perceptual blinding potentially vulnerable before induction and postoperatively.
5. **Long-duration exposure differs substantially from many TEAS trials**, with preoperative, intraoperative, immediate postoperative and repeated POD1-POD2 treatment.
6. **PCIA was multimodal**, so “analgesic consumption” in mL is not a pure opioid endpoint.
7. **Exact 24-h PCIA consumption is not numerically reported**, despite 24 h being one of the graph time points.
8. **The article reports significance at 4, 8 and 36 h for PCIA consumption, but not at 16 or 24 h.**
9. **Composite satisfaction statement is internally inconsistent with Table 4 category counts.**
10. The Results text says no significant RSS difference among “three groups,” although the trial has **two groups**; this appears to be a textual error.
11. **No dedicated intervention-safety reporting.**
12. Postoperative rescue medication outside PCIA is not clearly reported.

# 24. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Gu et al. 2019 (Study #1471)** randomized **120 gastric-cancer patients undergoing laparoscopic radical gastrectomy** to long-duration TEAS or sham TEAS. **117 patients were analyzed (58 L-TEAS, 59 C-TEAS).** TEAS was applied bilaterally at **ST36 and PC6**, using a **HANS LH-202** device with **2/100-Hz dense-disperse stimulation at 5-30 mA**, sufficient to evoke de qi. Treatment began **30 min before anesthesia induction**, continued throughout surgery until **30 min postoperatively**, and was then repeated **three times daily for 30 min during the first 2 postoperative days**. Sham patients had identical electrode placement and device settings, but the output wires were broken so no current was delivered.

Both groups received standardized general anesthesia and a 100-mL multimodal PCIA mixture containing **100 µg sufentanil, 200 µg dexmedetomidine, 100 mg flurbiprofen axetil and 0.5 mg palonosetron**, with a **2 mL/h basal infusion**, **0.5 mL bolus**, and **15-min lockout**.

Pain was significantly lower with L-TEAS at **4 h (2.79 ± 0.48 vs 3.41 ± 0.62)**, **8 h (2.65 ± 0.42 vs 3.17 ± 0.49)**, and **24 h (1.98 ± 0.39 vs 2.72 ± 0.73)**. Cumulative PCIA consumption was lower at **4 h (10.02 ± 2.26 vs 13.38 ± 2.98 mL)**, **8 h (20.55 ± 4.59 vs 24.63 ± 3.94 mL)**, and **36 h (72.64 ± 9.74 vs 76.01 ± 10.43 mL)**. Because the pump contained **1 µg/mL sufentanil**, those mL values are numerically equivalent to delivered sufentanil in µg, but the pump was multimodal.

The article does **not provide the exact 24-h PCIA consumption numerically**, so an exact 24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint should not be invented from the graph. PONV was lower (**12.0% vs 32.2%**), and gastrointestinal recovery was faster: first bowel sounds by **6.9 h**, flatus by **7.0 h**, and defecation by **5.8 h**.

The major audit issues are the **lack of an exact 24-h opioid value**, a **multimodal PCIA mixture**, incomplete allocation-concealment information, untested blinding success, and an internally inconsistent satisfaction composite in the published text.
