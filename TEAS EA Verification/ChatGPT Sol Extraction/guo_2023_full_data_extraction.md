# 23. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **Complete-case rather than ITT analysis:** 18/128 randomized patients were excluded from analysis.
2. **14.1% post-randomization attrition/exclusion** over 12 months.
3. **Different reasons for loss by arm**, including 1 TEAS death and 3 sham deaths.
4. **Diabetes is listed as an exclusion criterion but appears in Table 1** in both analyzed groups.
5. **Blinding may be imperfect** because active TEAS began 30 min before induction at a perceptible 10-30 mA, whereas sham delivered no current.
6. **No formal blinding-success assessment** is reported.
7. **Primary outcome is cognitive function**, not opioid consumption.
8. **Intraoperative opioid reduction is available**, but **postoperative actual opioid consumption is not**.
9. **PCIA regimen is described without consumption data.**
10. Acute pain/MMSE/sleep results are primarily shown graphically without exact numeric postoperative means in the main article.
11. The study is **single-center** and restricted to older adults undergoing minimally invasive thoracic surgery.
12. POCD diagnosis used a **within-patient 1-SD MoCA decline**, which should be preserved exactly when comparing with studies using different POCD definitions.

# 25. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Guo et al. 2023** was a prospective randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial in China of **128 adults aged ≥65 years undergoing thoracoscopic pulmonary resection for suspected lung cancer**. Sixty-four patients were randomized to TEAS and 64 to sham; **110 completed 12-month follow-up and were analyzed (55/group)**.

TEAS was delivered at **DU20 and bilateral PC6, LI4, and ST36** using **2/100-Hz disperse-dense stimulation at 10-30 mA**, beginning **30 min before anesthesia induction and continuing until the end of surgery**. Sham patients had identical electrodes and devices but with the **power switched off**.

The primary endpoint was postoperative cognitive dysfunction. POCD was substantially lower with TEAS at **POD1 (20.0% vs 65.4%)**, **POD7 (7.3% vs 43.6%)**, and **POD30 (3.6% vs 40.0%)**, all **P<0.001**. TEAS also reduced intraoperative remifentanil consumption (**0.87 ± 0.30 vs 1.01 ± 0.39 mg; P=0.040**, approximately **13.9% lower**) and produced lower acute pain scores through POD30.

Both groups received **48-h sufentanil PCIA**, but the article does **not report actual postoperative sufentanil consumption**, so a 24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint cannot be extracted. Remedial analgesic frequency was not significantly different.

At 12 months, current chronic pain was lower with TEAS (**7.3% vs 23.6%, P=0.018**), and pain duration >2 months was also less common (**14.5% vs 32.7%, P=0.025**). Adverse events were mild; no serious adverse events were reported.

Important audit concerns are the **complete-case analysis after 18 post-randomization exclusions**, potential **pre-induction blinding limitations**, and the internal inconsistency that **diabetes was an exclusion criterion yet appears in Table 1**.
