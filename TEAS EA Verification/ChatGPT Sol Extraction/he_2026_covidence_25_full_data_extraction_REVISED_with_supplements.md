|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **24 h** | **20.0 ± 2.5 mg MME** | **20.6 ± 4.5 mg MME** | **-0.6 mg (-1.7 to 0.5)** | **0.30** |
| **48 h** | **39.5 ± 4.0 mg MME** | **40.4 ± 7.1 mg MME** | **-0.9 mg (-2.7 to 0.8)** | **0.32** |

## Calculated relative opioid reduction

- **24 h:** (20.6 - 20.0) / 20.6 = **2.9% lower MME** with TEAS.
- **48 h:** (40.4 - 39.5) / 40.4 = **2.2% lower MME** with TEAS.

## Interpretation for the review

- The **24-h opioid endpoint is directly available** and can be entered into the quantitative extraction.
- The between-group difference is only **0.6 mg MME at 24 h**, with the 95% CI crossing zero and **P=0.30**.
- Therefore, TEAS did **not produce a statistically significant or clinically substantial reduction in 24-h opioid consumption**.
- The same conclusion applies at 48 h (**0.9 mg difference; P=0.32**).
- This contrasts with the statistically significant reduction in **PCA attempts/effective deliveries at 24 h**, indicating fewer button presses without a meaningful reduction in total opioid exposure.

## MCID interpretation

If the review's prespecified clinically meaningful opioid-sparing threshold is substantially greater than **0.6 mg MME at 24 h**, this study should be classified as **not meeting the opioid-sparing MCID**. Apply the review's exact prespecified MCID rule for the final coded field.

# 23. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **Primary endpoint negative:** TEAS did not reduce 24-h PONV.
2. **Control-event rate was substantially below anticipated**, reducing statistical power.
3. Authors report a **post-hoc power of only 27.7%** for detecting a 40% relative risk reduction with observed rates.
4. Therefore, authors state the negative result should be considered **inconclusive rather than definitive evidence of no effect**.
5. **Single-session intraoperative TEAS only**, which authors consider potentially insufficient cumulative stimulation.
6. **Strong pharmacologic co-interventions**: dexamethasone + palonosetron for PONV, parecoxib, oxycodone, sufentanil/oxycodone PCA.
7. TEAS reduced **PCA attempt/delivery counts at 24 h**, but **not total opioid MME**, limiting evidence of clinically meaningful opioid sparing.
8. Exact total opioid MME values were reported in **Supplemental Material 2** and are now available: **20.0 ± 2.5 vs 20.6 ± 4.5 mg at 24 h** and **39.5 ± 4.0 vs 40.4 ± 7.1 mg at 48 h**.
9. Secondary outcomes were **exploratory without multiplicity correction**.
10. The study population was predominantly male, although subgroup interaction by sex was not significant.
11. mITT excluded two randomized patients who never initiated intervention/withdrew consent.
12. Four TEAS and two control patients were additionally excluded from PP because of protocol deviations.
13. 48-h PCA attempts/deliveries had **P=0.05**, but the authors treated them as not significantly different because significance was defined as P<0.05.
14. Two deaths occurred in TEAS vs none in control by 6 months, but this difference was not significant and causality is not reported.

# 25. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**He et al. 2026 (Study #25)** was a **three-center randomized double-blind trial** in China involving **161 adults scheduled for elective hepatectomy**. Patients were randomized to TEAS (**81**) or control (**80**); **159 entered the mITT analysis (80 TEAS, 79 control)**.

TEAS was applied at **bilateral PC6** using an **Hwato SDZ-V** device with alternating **2/10-Hz disperse-dense stimulation**, titrated to the maximum tolerable intensity with de qi. Crucially, stimulation began **only after anesthesia induction** and continued until the end of surgery, providing stronger participant blinding than many perioperative TEAS trials. Controls had identical electrodes and device handling but **no current**.

All patients received standardized anesthesia plus **dexamethasone, palonosetron, parecoxib, oxycodone**, and postoperative **sufentanil + oxycodone IV PCA**.

The primary outcome was negative: 24-h PONV occurred in **23.8% with TEAS vs 26.6% with control** (OR **0.86, 95% CI 0.42-1.76; P=0.68**). Pain scores were also not significantly different.

However, by 24 h TEAS patients made fewer **PCA attempts (2.7 ± 3.3 vs 3.5 ± 6.1; P=0.02)** and had fewer **effective deliveries (2.6 ± 3.3 vs 3.5 ± 6.0; P=0.02)**. Despite this, the authors explicitly report that **total opioid consumption in morphine equivalents did not differ at either 24 or 48 h**.

Supplemental Material 2 resolves the quantitative opioid endpoint: total opioid consumption was **20.0 ± 2.5 mg MME with TEAS vs 20.6 ± 4.5 mg with control at 24 h** (MD **-0.6 mg**, 95% CI **-1.7 to 0.5**, **P=0.30**) and **39.5 ± 4.0 vs 40.4 ± 7.1 mg at 48 h** (MD **-0.9 mg**, 95% CI **-2.7 to 0.8**, **P=0.32**). Thus, despite fewer PCA attempts at 24 h, TEAS did **not produce meaningful opioid sparing**.
