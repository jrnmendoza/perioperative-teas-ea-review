# 21. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**He et al. 2026 (Study #41)** included **130 breast-cancer surgery patients**, allocated **65 to electroacupuncture and 65 to sham control**. The Methods describe **computer-generated variable-block randomization**, although the journal labels the paper a **case-control study**, creating a publication-level design inconsistency.

EA was applied at **ST36 and SP6** using a **2/10-Hz sparse-dense waveform**, beginning **30 min before anesthesia induction and continuing until the end of surgery**. The control group received shallow needle insertion **0.5 cm away from the true acupoints** connected to an inactive stimulator. Both groups underwent standardized general anesthesia and received **flurbiprofen axetil 50 mg IV at wound closure**. Rescue **morphine 1-3 mg IV** or **paracetamol 1 g IV** could be given according to pain severity.

At 24 h, EA reduced moderate-to-severe movement-evoked pain from **64.6% to 49.2% (P<0.05)** and lowered VAS pain during movement (**3.954 ± 0.233 vs 2.862 ± 0.233**) and at rest (**2.692 ± 0.174 vs 1.789 ± 0.174**), both **P<0.01**. These differences were no longer significant at 48 or 72 h.

EA also improved the 24-h BADL total score (**85 vs 80, P<0.01**) and reduced **PONV (20.0% vs 33.8%)** and appetite loss. However, **rescue opioid consumption was not recorded or compared**, so this article **cannot provide a quantitative 24-h postoperative opioid or MME endpoint**.

The most important audit problem is **Table 1**, where the published operation-procedure counts are impossible for the stated denominators and should be clarified with the authors before use in subgroup analysis.
