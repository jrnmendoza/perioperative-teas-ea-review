# 25. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **Small single-center pilot RCT** with 72 analyzed participants.
2. **Two randomized participants were excluded from analysis**, so no full ITT analysis.
3. **No formal blinding-success test**, although sham methodology was designed to preserve blinding.
4. TEAS was administered **before surgery, before anesthesia, and on POD1**, but **not intraoperatively**, making the exposure pattern distinct from many perioperative TEAS trials.
5. The **primary endpoint was anxiety**, not opioid consumption or postoperative pain.
6. Postoperative pain was measured at **D2 (“one day after surgery”)**, not an exact clock-time 24-h endpoint.
7. **Actual postoperative sufentanil consumption is not reported**, despite a clearly specified PCIA regimen.
8. Rescue analgesic use was **flurbiprofen**, not opioid rescue.
9. The D2 VAS Hodges-Lehmann result is internally inconsistent: **point estimate 25 with CI 10-20**.
10. The paper states propofol induction as **0.2-0.3 mg/kg**, an unusually low value; preserve as published unless author clarification is obtained.
11. Hospital discharge timing was constrained by institutional policy (~12 days), limiting length-of-stay sensitivity.
12. No dedicated intervention-related adverse-event reporting.

# 27. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Hou et al. 2023 (Study #7)** was a single-center randomized controlled trial in China involving **74 living kidney donors undergoing open nephrectomy**, with **72 analyzed (36 TEAS, 36 control)**. TEAS was delivered for **30 min on three occasions**: one day before surgery, before anesthesia induction on the day of surgery, and one day after surgery. Acupoints were **Yintang (EX-HN3), bilateral LR3, and bilateral PC6**, using **2/100-Hz dense-disperse stimulation at 6-15 mA**. Controls received identical electrode placement with the stimulator disabled.

All participants received standardized general anesthesia plus bilateral TAP block and a **48-h sufentanil PCIA** containing **1 µg/mL sufentanil**, with a **2 mL/h basal infusion**, **4 mL bolus**, and **30-min lockout**.

TEAS did not reduce intraoperative remifentanil use, but postoperative recovery favored TEAS. On D2, pain was **30 vs 55 mm VAS (P=0.001)**; fewer TEAS patients required rescue flurbiprofen (**41.7% vs 77.8%, P=0.002**); PONV was lower (**11.1% vs 33.3%, P=0.023**); and bowel movement occurred earlier (**1.7 vs 2.2 days, P=0.002**). QoR-15 and sleep were also better early after surgery.

At 3 months, fewer TEAS participants had persistent pain (**44.4% vs 69.4%, P=0.032**) or neuropathic pain (**19.4% vs 41.7%, P=0.041**).
