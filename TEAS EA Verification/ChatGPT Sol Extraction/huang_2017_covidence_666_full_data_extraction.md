# 23. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **Four-arm frequency-comparison design:** control, 2/100 Hz, 2 Hz, and 100 Hz must remain separate.
2. The strongest opioid result is **intraoperative**, not postoperative.
3. **Postoperative analgesic doses did not differ significantly** among groups.
4. The paper says postoperative medication was recorded at **6, 12, 24 and 48 h**, but Table 5 provides only **one aggregate dose per group**.
5. Therefore the main publication **does not provide an exact 24-h postoperative sufentanil endpoint**.
6. Actual PCA consumption cannot be reconstructed from settings because **bolus counts are missing**.
7. The Discussion's stated **33.5% intraoperative opioid reduction** does not exactly match the **30.2% reduction calculated from Table 2 means**.
8. The trial registration number is internally inconsistent between the title page and Methods.
9. PONV morbidity of **26%** in the 2-Hz arm is not exactly compatible with n=20, suggesting rounding/reporting complexity; preserve percentage rather than back-calculating an integer.
10. The study is **small and single-center**, as acknowledged by the authors.
11. Six patients were missing 1-month SF-12 data.
12. No dedicated TEAS skin/electrode safety table is presented.
13. Postoperative analgesia was multimodal: sufentanil + flurbiprofen PCIA plus intercostal ropivacaine block and pre-end flurbiprofen/sufentanil.
14. Because 24-h opioid consumption is not separately tabulated, **MME/MCID classification requires author data or another source**.

# 25. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Huang et al. 2017 (Study #666)** randomized **80 patients undergoing VATS lobectomy** to placebo TEAS, **2/100-Hz TEAS, 2-Hz TEAS, or 100-Hz TEAS (20/group)**. TEAS was delivered at **PC6, LI4, LU7 and LI11 on the surgery-side upper limb**, for **30 min before induction, continuously intraoperatively, and for 30 min at 24 and 48 h postoperatively**.

The clearest opioid-sparing effect occurred **intraoperatively**. Total opioid exposure expressed as remifentanil-equivalent was **0.1128 ± 0.034 µg/kg/min with 2/100-Hz TEAS vs 0.1617 ± 0.037 in control**, significantly lower than control, 2-Hz, and 100-Hz groups. The 2/100-Hz group also had shorter extubation time, lower immediate post-extubation NRS pain (**1.3 ± 0.7 vs 2.4 ± 0.5**), and shorter PACU stay.

Postoperative analgesia consisted of **sufentanil 1 µg/mL + flurbiprofen 1.2 mg/mL IV PCA**, basal **2 mL/h**, **3-mL bolus**, and **15-min lockout**. However, although postoperative medication requirements were recorded at 6, 12, 24 and 48 h, the main article provides only one aggregate dose per group and explicitly states that **postoperative sufentanil and flurbiprofen requirements did not differ significantly**.

Therefore this paper **cannot provide an exact 24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint from the published main text**. Its main opioid finding should be coded as an **intraoperative 2/100-Hz TEAS opioid-sparing effect**, not as demonstrated postoperative 24-h opioid sparing.

The 100-Hz group had lower PONV morbidity (**5% vs 35% control, P=0.044**), while the serial nausea/vomiting scores did not significantly differ.
