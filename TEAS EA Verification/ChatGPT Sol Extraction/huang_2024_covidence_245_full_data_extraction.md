# 24. Major methodological / extraction issues

1. **Three-arm design:** contralateral EA, ipsilateral EA, and sham EA must remain separate.
2. The study is **postoperative-only EA**, not preoperative or intraoperative stimulation.
3. The **PCA drug is not identified** in the main article.
4. The printed PCA continuous dose **“0.25 g/kg/h”** is not clinically interpretable without knowing the drug and may represent a source-reporting issue; do not silently correct it.
5. The **additional PCA dose** has no explicit unit and no opioid mass per release.
6. The PCA primary outcome is on **POD2, 24 h after the first EA treatment**, not explicitly 24 h after surgery.
7. Therefore, this publication **cannot provide exact 24-h postoperative MME**.
8. Formal primary significance threshold was **P<0.007**, so some nominal P<0.05 comparisons should not be labeled formally significant.
9. Six patients did not complete treatment, although authors state ITT analysis.
10. Safety and blinding tables use completer denominators, while blinding percentages appear based on n=38/group.
11. Acupuncturists were not blinded; authors acknowledge possible performance bias.
12. Follow-up ended at POD10, so no long-term pain outcome.
13. PONV and opioid-related adverse events are not reported.
14. The protocol/supplement may contain missing PCA details and should be sought if opioid quantification is essential.

# 26. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Huang et al. 2024 (Study #245)** randomized **114 patients undergoing unilateral total knee arthroplasty under general anesthesia** to **contralateral EA, ipsilateral EA, or bilateral sham EA (38/group)**. EA was delivered postoperatively once daily on **POD1-POD3**, for **30 min/session**, at **ST32, ST36, SP9, and GB34** using **2-Hz continuous stimulation, 1 mA intensity, and 0.25-ms pulse width**.

Contralateral EA consisted of true EA on the healthy limb and sham EA on the operated limb; ipsilateral EA used true EA on the operated limb and sham on the healthy limb. Sham EA used blunt nonpenetrating needles on both sides with the EA-unit wire internally cut.

Pain during walking/joint movement was lower in both true-EA groups than sham from approximately POD3 onward, with no meaningful difference between contralateral and ipsilateral EA. On POD2, the additional PCA-release outcome was **0 [0-2] in contralateral EA, 2 [0-2] in ipsilateral EA, and 2 [2-4] in sham**. Contralateral EA differed significantly from sham under the trial's multiplicity-adjusted threshold (**P<0.001**); ipsilateral vs sham had **P=0.013**, which did not meet the prespecified primary threshold of **P<0.007**.

The study also showed improved HSS knee scores and several ROM outcomes with true EA, and only **three minor acupuncture-related events** were reported.

For the opioid-sparing review, however, the main article is insufficient for quantitative opioid synthesis: **the PCA drug/composition, bolus dose, and exact opioid mass are not reported**, and the PCA outcome was measured **24 h after the first postoperative EA treatment rather than explicitly 24 h after surgery**. Therefore, an exact **24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint cannot be extracted from the article alone**.
