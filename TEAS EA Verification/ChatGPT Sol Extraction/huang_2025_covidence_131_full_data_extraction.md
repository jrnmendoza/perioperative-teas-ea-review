### Major blinding concern
The Methods state that **patients were blinded to treatment allocation**, yet:
- EA patients received penetrating needles plus electrical stimulation.
- Control patients received **no sham procedure at all**.

The Discussion itself acknowledges that the **absence of a sham acupuncture control** is a major limitation because placebo effects could influence subjective outcomes. # 25. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **No sham acupuncture control**, despite the Methods calling patients “blinded.”
2. The Discussion explicitly acknowledges that lack of sham may allow **placebo/performance bias** in subjective outcomes.
3. **101 randomized but only 88 analyzed**; 13 post-randomization exclusions (12.9%).
4. Post-randomization exclusions included clinically meaningful events/interventions such as **pulmonary embolism, conversion to laparotomy, enema, and Chinese patent medicine use**.
5. No **intention-to-treat analysis** is reported.
6. The flowchart says 88 were “recruited,” but they were actually the **primary-analysis sample**.
7. The study's **primary outcomes are GI recovery**, not pain or opioid consumption.
8. Nearly all participants used a self-controlled analgesia pump, but **the pump content and opioid dose are completely unreported**.
9. Therefore no quantitative postoperative opioid/MME endpoint can be extracted.
10. The specified rescue analgesic is **ketorolac, not an opioid**.
11. At 24 h, both I-FEED groups are summarized as **3 (2-4)** yet P<0.001; preserve but consider author clarification.
12. The prose says QoR-40 was **lower** with EA, while Table 4 clearly shows **higher scores (196 vs 192)** and higher scores mean better recovery.
13. A pulmonary embolism occurred in a randomized EA patient but that patient was excluded from primary analysis, relevant to safety interpretation.
14. Single-center, female-only gynecologic population limits generalizability.
15. No formal ERAS-compliance measure was recorded, acknowledged by the authors.

# 27. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Huang et al. 2025 (Study #131)** was a single-center prospective randomized controlled trial in China of women undergoing **laparoscopic hysterectomy with or without adnexal surgery under general anesthesia**. **101 patients were randomized (51 EA, 50 standard care), but only 88 were included in the primary analysis (43 EA, 45 control)** after 13 post-randomization exclusions.

EA was entirely **postoperative**, beginning **3-5 h after surgery** and repeated in the **morning and afternoon on POD1**, for **three 30-minute sessions**. Bilateral **ST36, ST37 and ST39** were needled to a depth of **20-40 mm**, with de qi elicited, followed by **2-Hz stimulation at 0.5-2.0 mA** using a G6805-I stimulator.

EA significantly accelerated GI recovery: first flatus occurred at **36.4 ± 8.0 vs 42.2 ± 8.5 h (P<0.001)** and first defecation at **46.0 ± 8.0 vs 51.3 ± 9.4 h (P=0.010)**. Pain VAS was also lower at **24 h (5 vs 6), 48 h (3 vs 4), and 72 h (2 vs 3)**, all **P<0.001**. Discharge QoR-40 was **higher/better with EA (196 vs 192, P<0.001)** despite a contradictory sentence in the Results text saying it was lower.

The specified rescue analgesic was **ketorolac 30 mg IM**; additional analgesic use was **1/43 vs 4/45**, not significant. Although almost all patients used a self-controlled analgesia pump, the article does **not report the pump drug, opioid concentration, settings, or actual opioid consumption**. Therefore this study **cannot provide a quantitative 24-h opioid/MME endpoint from the publication alone**.

The major audit concerns are the **absence of a sham control despite claimed patient blinding**, **12.9% post-randomization exclusion**, lack of ITT analysis, and incomplete reporting of postoperative analgesia.
