### HR interpretation issue
The article describes an HR of **0.55** while also saying TEAS patients had a **45% higher probability of achieving first flatus**. Because the Kaplan-Meier plot is described as a **cumulative no-flatus rate**, an HR below 1 may represent the hazard of remaining without flatus rather than the conventional event hazard. Do **not** reinterpret this HR beyond the authors' reporting without clarification.

# 23. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **614 randomized vs 587 analyzed.** The abstract conflates the analyzed sample with the assigned sample.
2. The reported **“ITT” primary analysis uses n=593, not all 614 randomized patients**, so it is not a conventional full ITT analysis.
3. **Baseline Table 1 contains impossible denominators:** sex counts sum to 300 in both groups; TEAS ASA counts sum to 298 despite n=294.
4. **Fixed block randomization** was acknowledged by the authors as potentially predictable.
5. **Blinding success was not tested.**
6. Active TEAS involved perceptible stimulation/de qi before induction, whereas sham delivered no current, making sensory unblinding possible.
7. The reported first-flatus **HR 0.55** is described as a “45% higher probability” of first flatus; direction depends on the plotted **no-flatus** survival function and should not be reinterpreted without clarification.
8. First-defecation effect CI is printed in reversed order: **-3.8 to -6.0**.
9. Methods imply routine dexamethasone, but Table 2 shows use in only ~80% of participants.
10. Methods describe ondansetron PONV prophylaxis, but Table 2 records it in only ~12%.
11. The trial is primarily a **GI-recovery study**, not an opioid-sparing study.
12. Postoperative PCIA was used in >95% of patients, but **no quantitative postoperative opioid dose is reported**.
13. Therefore exact 24-h MME and opioid MCID classification are impossible from the main paper.
14. Single-center design limits generalizability.
15. Adverse events were low, but the paper does not clearly distinguish all events as stimulation-related vs general postoperative events.

# 25. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Jiang et al. 2026 (Study #4)** was a large single-center randomized double-blind sham-controlled trial in China evaluating **perioperative TEAS in adults aged 18-64 undergoing elective laparoscopic non-gastrointestinal surgery**. The CONSORT diagram shows **614 randomized patients (308 TEAS, 306 sham)**, with **587 included in the final per-protocol analysis (294 TEAS, 293 sham)**.

Active TEAS was applied bilaterally at **ST36, PC6, and LI4** using a **Hua Tuo SDZ-III** device at **2/10-Hz dense-disperse stimulation**, titrated to maximum tolerated intensity with mild muscle twitching/de qi. Treatment started **30 min before anesthesia induction and continued until the end of surgery**. Sham patients had identical electrodes/acupoints but the output wires were cut, so no current was delivered.

TEAS accelerated gastrointestinal recovery: median time to first flatus was **21.5 (19.0-23.5) h vs 23.5 (21.0-26.0) h (P<0.001)** and time to first defecation was **41.2 (38.5-45.0) h vs 46.0 (42.0-52.0) h (P<0.001)**. TEAS also reduced **24-h PONV (15.0% vs 22.9%, P=0.015)** and abdominal distension (**21.1% vs 29.7%, P=0.017**), with a small statistically significant difference in the reported postoperative NRS pain score (**3 [2-3] vs 3 [2-3.5], P=0.026**).

There was **no significant difference in intraoperative remifentanil or sufentanil use**. Although >95% of analyzed patients received postoperative **sufentanil + flurbiprofen PCIA**, the publication does **not report the PCIA concentrations/settings or actual postoperative opioid consumption**. Consequently, this study **cannot provide a valid quantitative 24-h opioid/MME endpoint from the main article alone**.

The most important audit concerns are the **614-randomized/587-analyzed distinction**, the incomplete “ITT” population, **impossible baseline denominators in Table 1**, the first-defecation CI ordering issue, and the absence of quantitative postoperative opioid data.
