# 25. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **49 randomized but only 47 analyzed**, because 2 Group 4 participants could not complete VAS assessment.
2. No **intention-to-treat analysis** is reported.
3. **Small sample**, particularly Group 4 with only **n=10 analyzed**.
4. The study was initially planned for **60 participants** but recruited only 49.
5. **Group-specific baseline characteristics are not reported**.
6. The pooled age range includes a minimum of **14 years**, unusual for hysterectomy and requiring clarification if age eligibility matters.
7. Allocation concealment beyond random-number assignment is **not reported**.
8. The paper claims strong blinding, including the intervention performer, but **the exact mechanism by which the intervention provider remained unaware of active vs sham stimulation is not fully explained**.
9. The active intervention is **needle-free surface stimulation**, not needle EA, and should not be merged with invasive EA without respecting modality classification.
10. Group 1 is a **no-SSP control**, while Group 2 is a **sham-point/no-stimulation comparator**; these comparators should remain separate.
11. The strongest 24-h morphine difference is **low-frequency SSP vs untreated control**, not clearly vs sham.
12. Some analyses use **P<0.10** as evidence of overall differences, which is weaker than the conventional 0.05 threshold.
13. The Discussion statement that total dose delivery “decreased to 53, 43, 53, and 48%” is unclear and does not transparently correspond to Table 8 values.
14. No dedicated adverse-event results are presented despite safety questions being asked.
15. Opioid-related side-effect-sensitive patients were excluded, limiting generalizability for nausea/vomiting outcomes.
16. The exact postoperative timing of the second SSP session is not given in minutes/hours.
17. Long-term postoperative pain was not assessed beyond 24 h.

# 27. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Lee et al. 2011 (Study #49)** randomized **49 women undergoing hysterectomy** to four postoperative analgesia strategies: **no SSP control (n=13), sham SSP near ST36 (n=12), 100-Hz SSP at ST36 (n=12), or low-frequency dense-sparse SSP at ST36 (n=12)**. Two low-frequency patients were excluded because they could not complete the VAS, leaving **47 analyzed**.

The intervention was **needle-free Silver Spike Point electroacupuncture**, not invasive needle EA. Active or sham SSP was delivered for **30 min beginning 30 min before general anesthesia** and again for **30 min after the patient regained consciousness in recovery**. The high-frequency arm used **100 Hz continuous stimulation**; the low-frequency arm used a dense-sparse sequence of **3 Hz for 4 s, 10 Hz for 4 s, and 20 Hz for 4 s**.

General anesthesia used **propofol 2 mg/kg, atracurium 0.5 mg/kg, sevoflurane, 50% nitrous oxide and oxygen**, with the authors stating that **no narcotics** were given intraoperatively. Postoperatively, all patients received **IV morphine PCA**, started after 1 h in recovery, with **0.8-mg boluses and an 8-min lockout**.

The low-frequency SSP arm had the strongest analgesic signal. At **24 h**, pain was **1.21 ± 1.31 vs 3.50 ± 1.27** in untreated control. Total delivered morphine over 24 h was:

- Control: **38.63 ± 11.08 mg**
- Sham: **34.20 ± 6.40 mg**
- 100 Hz: **31.14 ± 8.15 mg**
- Low-frequency: **29.29 ± 8.89 mg**

Thus, low-frequency SSP reduced raw 24-h IV morphine by **9.34 mg (24.2%) vs untreated control**. The article reports a significant post hoc difference between these two groups. Total PCA demand was also substantially lower (**41.60 vs 84.54 requests**).

For your opioid-sparing review, this is therefore one of the more useful studies because it provides an **exact 24-h opioid dose in mg of IV morphine**. Final conversion to MME should use the review's prespecified IV-morphine conversion rule.

The main audit concerns are the **very small sample**, **two post-randomization exclusions in the low-frequency arm**, absence of group-specific baseline data, the unusual reported **minimum age of 14 years**, and incomplete adverse-event reporting.
