# 25. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **306 randomized but only 280 analyzed**, with an explicitly **per-protocol** database.
2. Post-randomization exclusions included ICU transfer and cancelled operations.
3. No full **ITT analysis** is reported.
4. Participant blinding may have been imperfect because active TEAS used maximum-tolerated current while sham delivered **no current**.
5. Acupuncture and anesthesia technicians were **not blinded**.
6. The postoperative sufentanil pump formulation is printed as **250 mg in 250 mL**, which is clinically implausible and likely a unit/typesetting problem.
7. Pump basal rate, bolus dose, lockout, and delivered volume are **not reported**.
8. Therefore a valid 24-h or 48-h opioid/MME endpoint cannot be reconstructed.
9. The article states that **48-h sufentanil dose** was analyzed as a PGD predictor, but does not report the actual group values.
10. Pain reporting is mainly binary **VAS ≥4**, not continuous VAS means.
11. There is no exact standalone **24-h pain measurement**; POD1 measurements are at fixed morning/afternoon clock times.
12. PGD definition is study-specific: absence of bowel sounds >48 h, acknowledged by authors as lacking a universal standard.
13. TEAS-specific adverse-event reporting is incomplete.
14. The study is primarily a **GI recovery trial**, not an opioid-sparing trial.

# 27. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Li et al. 2021 (Study #437)** was a single-center randomized, single-blinded trial in China evaluating **perioperative TEAS in 280 analyzed patients undergoing laparoscopic gastric or colorectal tumor surgery**. The study randomized 306 patients, but used an explicitly **per-protocol analysis** after 26 post-randomization exclusions.

TEAS was applied bilaterally at **LI4, PC6, ST36, and ST37** using a **HANS-200A** with a **2/100-Hz disperse-dense symmetric biphasic waveform**, alternating every 3 s, at maximum tolerated intensity. Stimulation began **30 min before surgery**, continued throughout the operation, and was repeated for **30 min at 09:00 on POD1-POD3 until flatus recovered**. Sham participants had electrodes on non-meridian points but received **no electrical stimulation**.

TEAS significantly accelerated postoperative recovery:
- first bowel motion: **31 vs 42 h**
- first flatus: **44 vs 64 h**
- first ambulation: **44 vs 67 h**
- PGD: **18.6% vs 31.4%**

At 6 h, fewer TEAS patients had **VAS ≥4** (**21.4% vs 35.7%, P=0.008**). PONV was also significantly lower at several early postoperative time points. Postoperative stay was shorter (**6.2 vs 8.1 days**) and hospitalization cost was lower with TEAS.

For the opioid-sparing review, however, the main article is **not quantitatively usable for 24-h opioid/MME synthesis**. It reports a postoperative sufentanil pump, but the formulation is printed as **250 mg sufentanil in 250 mL saline**, an implausible dose that likely reflects a source unit error. The basal rate, bolus, lockout, and actual delivered dose are not reported. Therefore exact **24-h postoperative sufentanil consumption and MME cannot be calculated** without author clarification or supplementary/protocol data.
