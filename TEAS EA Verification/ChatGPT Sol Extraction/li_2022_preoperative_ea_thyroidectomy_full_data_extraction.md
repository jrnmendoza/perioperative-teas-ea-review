# 21. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **One post-randomization EA participant was excluded from analysis**, so the published analysis is 39 vs 40 rather than full ITT.
2. The article states **all study personnel were blinded**, but the acupuncturist delivering active EA versus sham could likely distinguish procedures; intervention-provider blinding is therefore unclear.
3. The sham was carefully designed, but active stimulation itself may still have produced distinguishable sensations.
4. The sample-size pilot statement appears internally suspicious: it reports incision threshold **99.5 ± 13.8 g in sham vs 89.3 ± 14.2 g in EA**, implying a lower threshold in the EA group, opposite the trial hypothesis and observed final result. Group labels or direction may have been reversed in the pilot description.
5. The main outcome was **mechanical hyperalgesia**, not postoperative opioid use.
6. Intraoperative remifentanil was deliberately fixed at a high dose (**0.3 µg/kg/min**) to induce a standardized RPH exposure.
7. No postoperative opioid regimen was reported and no rescue analgesic was required in either group.
8. Therefore the study should not be interpreted as demonstrating clinically meaningful postoperative opioid sparing.
9. Pain benefits were **short-lived**, present at 30 min and 6 h but not at 24 or 48 h.
10. Hyperalgesia differences likewise disappeared by 24 h.
11. Single-center trial with relatively small sample.
12. No mechanistic biomarkers/endogenous opioid measurements were collected.
13. PONV and other postoperative adverse-reaction event counts are not fully tabulated in the main Results despite being mentioned.

# 23. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Li et al. 2022** was a prospective single-center randomized double-blind sham-controlled trial in China evaluating whether **one 30-minute session of electroacupuncture within 24 h before thyroidectomy** could reduce **remifentanil-induced post-infusion hyperalgesia**. Eighty patients were randomized to EA or sham; **79 were analyzed (39 EA, 40 sham)**.

EA was delivered bilaterally at **ST36 and PC6** with an **XS-998B stimulator**, using **20-100-Hz dense-disperse stimulation**. The sham intervention used nonpenetrating bent needles at nearby non-acupoints with a flashing but electrically inactive stimulator.

All patients received a standardized general anesthetic with **remifentanil fixed at 0.3 µg/kg/min**, plus propofol and sufentanil. Intraoperative opioid exposure was virtually identical between groups.

EA significantly preserved mechanical pain thresholds and reduced hyperalgesia around the incision and on the forearm at **30 min and 6 h after surgery**. Pain NRS was also modestly lower at those early time points. By **24 and 48 h**, however, there were **no significant differences** in mechanical threshold, hyperalgesia, or pain.

The postoperative rescue analgesic was **flurbiprofen 50 mg IV**, but **no patient in either group required rescue analgesia from 0-48 h**. No postoperative opioid regimen was reported. Therefore this study is primarily an **antihyperalgesia trial** and does **not provide a meaningful quantitative 24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint** for opioid-sparing meta-analysis.

The main audit issues are the **39/40 complete-case analysis rather than full ITT**, uncertainty about true intervention-provider blinding, and a **sample-size pilot statement whose EA/sham direction appears inconsistent with the final study findings**.
