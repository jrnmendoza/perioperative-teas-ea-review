# 24. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **Age eligibility is internally impossible:** inclusion states **18-64 years**, but mean ages are ~69-71 years.
2. **Abstract says 70 enrolled**, while Methods/flow chart show **80 assessed, 75 randomized, 70 analyzed**.
3. **Intervention duration inconsistency:** Methods/abstract state **30 min**, but flowchart prints **3 min**.
4. Control is **no stimulation**, not a robust sensory sham.
5. The study is described as **double-blind**, but participant masking is incompletely explained given the lack of active sensory sham.
6. **Two post-randomization TEAS losses** were due to blood sample loss/failure of arterial puncture; **three control losses** were due to surgery change.
7. No clear **intention-to-treat analysis** is reported for all randomized participants.
8. Methods print **sufentanil 0.1 mg/kg every 30 min**, incompatible with Table 1 totals of ~20 µg and likely a unit error.
9. Methods print **cisatracurium 2 mg/kg**, also unusually high and likely a publication error.
10. Postoperative “**extra analgesia**” is numerically reported but **unit and drug are not defined**.
11. Therefore the study cannot provide quantitative postoperative opioid consumption or MME.
12. Control T11 pain count/percentage is inconsistent: **8/35** but **19.6%** printed.
13. Biomarker reporting is inconsistent: Methods say **β-endorphin**, Table 5 labels **epinephrine**.
14. TEAS-specific safety outcomes are incompletely reported.
15. Primary outcome is **CRBD**, not postoperative pain or opioid consumption.

# 26. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Liang et al. 2021 (Study #14)** was a randomized, controlled, double-blind trial evaluating **preoperative TEAS for prevention of catheter-related bladder discomfort after TURP**. The flow diagram shows **80 assessed, 75 randomized (37 TEAS, 38 control), and 70 analyzed (35/group)**.

TEAS was delivered noninvasively using a **HANS-200A** stimulator at **2/100 Hz** to **RN7, RN6, RN5, RN4, RN3 and bilateral BL32, BL33, BL34**. The Methods and abstract describe **30 min of stimulation before general anesthesia**; however, the flowchart on page 5 incorrectly or inconsistently labels the intervention as **3 min**.

The main clinical effect was on CRBD. At **24 h**, CRBD occurred in **7/35 (20%) with TEAS vs 31/35 (89%) with control**, with no moderate/severe CRBD in TEAS vs **9/35 (26%)** in control. QoR-40 was also significantly better with TEAS at 24 and 48 h.

For postoperative analgesia, the paper reports **“extra analgesia” of 3.8 ± 1.9 in TEAS vs 5.0 ± 2.9 in control (P=0.045)**, but it does **not define the analgesic drug, dose unit, or observation interval**. Pain incidence and PONV were not significantly different.

Therefore, this study **cannot provide a valid quantitative 24-h opioid or MME endpoint** from the publication alone. Its analgesic finding should be coded only as **reduced postoperative analgesic requirement, unit/drug unresolved**.

The major audit concerns are the impossible **18-64 inclusion age vs mean age ~70 years**, the **70-vs-80 enrollment discrepancy**, the **30-min vs 3-min TEAS discrepancy**, and implausible printed intraoperative sufentanil/cisatracurium doses.
