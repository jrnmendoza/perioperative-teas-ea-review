# 21. Major methodological / audit issues

1. The study is a **pilot cognitive-outcome RCT**, not primarily an opioid-sparing study.
2. Participant blinding is inadequately described; active TEAS was maximum-tolerated while control had **no current**.
3. Allocation concealment is **NR**.
4. The PCA sufentanil formulation is printed as **0.15 µg/kg in 100 mL**, which appears unusually low and requires verification.
5. The paper reports **PCA compressions but not successful bolus deliveries or total opioid mass**.
6. Therefore exact 24-h opioid consumption/MME cannot be reliably reconstructed.
7. 24-h pain and PCA demand **did not differ** between groups.
8. Group-specific intraoperative opioid totals are not reported.
9. POD1 control POCD is described as **11 cases (25%)**, but 11/50 is **22%**.
10. The main statistically significant POCD finding is **cumulative duration**, not incidence at an individual postoperative day.
11. The article uses only **MMSE** to define POCD, acknowledged by the authors as a limitation.
12. Small single-center sample; authors explicitly describe this as a pilot study.
13. No dedicated opioid adverse-event or TEAS safety reporting.
14. No long-term cognitive follow-up beyond POD3.

# 23. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Liu et al. 2021** randomized **100 elderly patients undergoing laparoscopic radical colon cancer surgery** to perioperative TEAS or control (**50/group**). TEAS was delivered at **PC6, LI4, and ST36**, using a **2-100-Hz density wave** at maximum tolerated intensity from **30 min before anesthesia induction until the end of surgery**. Controls had electrodes placed at the same acupoints but received **no electrical current**.

The study primarily evaluated **postoperative cognitive decline**, not opioid sparing. Daily POCD incidence did not differ significantly on POD1, POD2, or POD3, although the **cumulative duration of POCD was significantly lower with TEAS by POD2 and POD3**.

For the opioid-sparing review, all patients received postoperative **sufentanil IV PCA** with a background flow of **2 mL/h**, **0.5-mL bolus**, and **15-min lockout**. At 24 h, the number of PCA compressions was essentially identical (**17.62 ± 2.687 vs 17.38 ± 3.801, P=0.716**). Rest pain (**2.52 vs 2.66**) and cough pain (**3.20 vs 3.16**) were also not significantly different.

The publication does **not provide reliable quantitative 24-h sufentanil consumption**. The PCA formulation is printed as **0.15 µg/kg sufentanil in 100 mL saline**, which requires clarification, and the article does not report successful bolus deliveries or cumulative pump volume. Therefore this study **cannot provide a valid 24-h MME endpoint from the main publication alone**.
