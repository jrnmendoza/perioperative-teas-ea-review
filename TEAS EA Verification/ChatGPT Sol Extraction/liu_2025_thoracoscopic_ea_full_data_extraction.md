# 22. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **80 randomized but only 77 analyzed**; no full ITT analysis.
2. The primary endpoint is **postoperative SVT**, not opioid consumption.
3. The study nevertheless shows a statistically significant **16.9% intraoperative remifentanil reduction**.
4. **Postoperative pain did not differ** between groups.
5. Postoperative PCIA protocol is clearly described, but **actual total sufentanil consumption is not reported**.
6. Therefore an exact quantitative **24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint cannot be extracted**.
7. The control intervention is incompletely specified: non-acupoints were needled, but whether actual electrical stimulation was delivered is **unclear**.
8. Because active EA produced de qi and 8-12 mA stimulation, successful sensory blinding is uncertain unless control current was also delivered.
9. The intervention included a **postoperative EA session at exactly 24 h**, so timing relative to any 24-h pain/opioid assessment should be clarified.
10. The Results prose reverses the apparent order of hypotension counts; Table 2 should be treated as authoritative.
11. The abstract also phrases sleep-time values ambiguously, while Table 4 clearly shows **EA = 209.5 min vs control = 119 min**.
12. Small single-center trial, acknowledged by the authors.
13. No PONV or detailed opioid-related adverse-event outcomes are reported despite opioid-based PCIA.
14. The authors' conclusion centers on arrhythmia and sleep; the opioid result is secondary/exploratory.

# 24. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Liu et al. 2025** randomized **80 adults undergoing single-port thoracoscopic lung cancer surgery** to perioperative electroacupuncture or a non-specific-acupoint control procedure. **77 patients were analyzed (38 EA, 39 control)**.

EA was applied on the surgical side at **PC6 (Neiguan) and SP4 (Gongsun)**, using **2/100-Hz stimulation at approximately 8-12 mA**, titrated to maximum tolerance with de qi. Treatment began **30 min before anesthesia induction**, continued until the end of surgery, and was repeated for **30 min at 24 h postoperatively**.

The primary result favored EA: new-onset postoperative SVT occurred in **4/38 (10.5%) vs 13/39 (33.3%)**, **P=0.02**, and β-blocker use was lower. EA also improved first-night sleep duration and efficiency.

For the opioid-sparing review, the notable result is intraoperative: remifentanil use was **911.1 ± 233.8 µg with EA vs 1095.9 ± 362.2 µg in control**, approximately **16.9% lower (P=0.01)**. Sufentanil exposure did not differ.

Postoperatively, all patients received sufentanil PCIA containing **1.5 µg/mL**, with a **2 mL/h basal infusion, 3-mL bolus, and 20-min lockout**. However, the paper does **not report actual PCA demand/delivery counts or cumulative sufentanil consumption**, so an exact **24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint cannot be calculated**.

Postoperative VAS pain did **not differ between groups**. The main audit issues are the **77/80 complete-case analysis**, incomplete description of whether the control non-acupoints received electrical current, and the absence of quantitative postoperative opioid consumption despite a fully specified PCIA regimen.
