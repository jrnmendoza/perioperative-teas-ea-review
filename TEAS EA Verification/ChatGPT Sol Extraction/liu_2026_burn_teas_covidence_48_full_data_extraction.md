14. Single-center, modest sample size.
15. Follow-up ended at 48 h, so no longer-term opioid/pain outcomes.

# 24. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Liu et al. 2026 (Study #48)** evaluated **30 min of preoperative TEAS as preemptive analgesia in moderate burn patients undergoing wound debridement under general anesthesia**. The CONSORT diagram shows **88 randomized (44/group)** and **86 analyzed (43/group)** after one participant in each arm developed redness/itching from the electrodes and did not receive the intervention.

TEAS was applied bilaterally at **LI4, PC6, ST36, and SP6** with a **HANS-200A**, using **2/100-Hz stimulation at <10 mA**, titrated to maximum tolerated intensity. The control used a placebo HANS stimulator, with both procedures producing numbness.

The primary outcome favored TEAS. In the Results section, intraoperative remifentanil consumption was **0.70 (0.50-1.10) mg with TEAS vs 0.90 (0.65-1.25) mg with control (P=0.039)**, corresponding to an approximately **22.2% lower median dose**. Propofol consumption did not differ.

At 24 h, resting F-VAS pain was significantly lower with TEAS (**P=0.039**), although exact means/SDs are only displayed graphically. Through POD1, TEAS also reduced **nausea-vomiting (16.3% vs 37.2%)** and **constipation (18.6% vs 39.5%)**. Rescue dezocine use was lower numerically (**7.0% vs 18.6%**) but not statistically significant.

For the review's **24-h postoperative opioid-sparing endpoint**, the study is not quantitatively usable from the publication alone. Postoperative PCIA contained **sufentanil 100 µg + butorphanol 4 mg in 100 mL**, but the bolus volume, basal rate, number of successful deliveries, and cumulative 24-h PCIA volume were not reported. Therefore exact **24-h sufentanil/butorphanol exposure and MME cannot be calculated**.

The main audit issues are the **88-vs-86 randomization discrepancy**, retrospective registration, the **SP6 labeling typo**, and inconsistent reported remifentanil and secondary-outcome statistics between the abstract, Results, and Table 2.
