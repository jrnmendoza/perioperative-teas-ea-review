# 23. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **129 randomized but only 120 analyzed**; no full ITT analysis.
2. Post-randomization exclusions included **postoperative bleeding and conversion to laparotomy**, which may be clinically meaningful.
3. Attrition was somewhat higher in TEAS (**9.4%**) than sham (**4.6%**).
4. Active TEAS used maximum-tolerated current while sham delivered **no current**, and formal blinding success was not assessed.
5. The anesthesiologist was **not blinded** to allocation.
6. The paper claims a **morphine-sparing effect**, but reports only **patients needing morphine**, not cumulative dose.
7. Therefore this study cannot provide a continuous 24-h opioid/MME endpoint from the main article.
8. Routine **tramadol 50 mg** was administered before the end of ESD in both groups and should be considered when defining total perioperative opioid exposure.
9. Several NRS confidence intervals in Table 2 are incompatible with their reported P-values.
10. PONV Table 2 RR **0.840** is incompatible with the event counts **4/58 vs 14/62**.
11. PONV P-value differs between abstract (~**0.06**) and Results/Table 2 (**0.016**).
12. Satisfaction mean-difference sign is opposite to the raw group means.
13. Adjusted TEAS OR differs slightly between abstract/prose and forest plot.
14. Single-center design.
15. The study is highly relevant for **binary rescue-opioid use** and postoperative pain, but not yet for continuous opioid-dose meta-analysis.

# 25. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Liu et al. 2026 (Study #69)** was a single-center prospective randomized sham-controlled trial in China involving **129 patients undergoing gastric or esophageal ESD**, of whom **120 were analyzed (58 TEAS, 62 sham)**.

TEAS was applied bilaterally at **LI4, PC6, ST36, and ST37** using a **HANS-200A**, with alternating **2/100-Hz stimulation every 3 s**, pulse widths of **0.6 ms at 2 Hz and 0.2 ms at 100 Hz**, and intensity titrated to maximum tolerance. Treatment began **30 min before ESD and continued until the end of surgery**. Sham patients had electrodes placed at non-acupoints but received **no electrical current**.

The primary outcome favored TEAS: moderate-to-severe pain within 24 h occurred in **22/58 (37.9%) vs 40/62 (64.5%)**, RR **0.588 (95% CI 0.403-0.858), P=0.004**. At 24 h, NRS was **1.2 ± 1.3 vs 2.8 ± 1.9 (P=0.001)**.

TEAS also substantially reduced the proportion of patients requiring rescue IV morphine: **19/58 (32.7%) vs 40/62 (64.5%)**, RR **0.508**, P<0.001. However, the publication reports only **whether a patient needed morphine**, not the number of 1-mg doses or cumulative morphine dose. Therefore this study is directly usable for a **binary rescue-opioid-use outcome**, but **not for a continuous 24-h morphine/MME meta-analysis without author data**.

PONV was also lower (**4/58 vs 14/62**), but the paper contains major inconsistencies in the reported RR and P-value for this endpoint. Several NRS confidence intervals and the satisfaction mean-difference sign are also internally inconsistent and should be audited before final consensus coding.
