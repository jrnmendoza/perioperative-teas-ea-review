# 7. Intervention classification

The article repeatedly calls the intervention **electroacupuncture (EA)**, but its own description and **Figure 1 on page 3** show **surface electrode patches** at PC6, ST36 and SP6, and the text states that a “millimeter needle or electrode patch” could be used.

### Review classification
For this extraction, the study is best classified as **transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) / non-invasive electrical acupoint stimulation**, because:
- the paper's Introduction explicitly defines TEAS as the intervention category;
- Figure 1 depicts transcutaneous electrode patches;
- the control group had electrodes placed at the same acupoints without current.

If the review distinguishes invasive EA from non-invasive TEAS, this study should be audited carefully and **not automatically coded as invasive needle EA based on the title alone**.

# 11. PCIA pharmacologic implications

For an individual patient of weight **W kg**:

- Reservoir sufentanil = **2W µg in 100 mL**
- Concentration = **0.02W µg/mL**
- Basal delivery = **1.5 mL/h**
- Basal sufentanil rate = **0.03W µg/h**
- One 2-mL bolus = **0.04W µg**

These are formula-derived and should **not** be converted into a group mean without actual group body-weight data.

# 26. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Long et al. 2025** was a prospective randomized trial in elderly patients undergoing **hip-fracture surgery under TIVA general anesthesia**. Sixty patients were initially randomized, but only **53 were analyzed (27 active, 26 control)** after withdrawals, cancellation, and loss to follow-up.

Although the article calls the intervention **electroacupuncture**, its Methods and the electrode-placement images on **page 3** are most consistent with **non-invasive TEAS**. Active stimulation was delivered at **GV20, bilateral PC6, ST36 and SP6** using a **Huatuo SDZ-II** at **2/100-Hz density-wave stimulation**, for **30 min before anesthesia** and another **30 min after awakening in PACU**. Controls had electrodes at the same acupoints but **no electrical current**.

The study's primary focus was PND. Published PND incidence was lower with active stimulation on **POD1 (25.0% vs 56.0%)** and **POD3 (14.3% vs 48.0%)**, although the reported percentages do not reconcile with the final group denominators.

For postoperative analgesia, all patients received **sufentanil PCIA containing 2 µg/kg in 100 mL**, with a **1.5-mL/h basal rate, 2-mL bolus, and 15-min lockout**. At 48 h, the active group had fewer PCIA compressions (**7.11 ± 2.14 vs 8.62 ± 1.84**) and lower PCIA solution consumption (**86.22 ± 4.27 vs 89.23 ± 3.67 mL**), both **P=0.008**. However, because the reservoir concentration was weight-based and group body weight was not reported, exact sufentanil mass cannot be reconstructed from these group-level volume data. An exact **24-h opioid/MME endpoint is also unavailable**.
