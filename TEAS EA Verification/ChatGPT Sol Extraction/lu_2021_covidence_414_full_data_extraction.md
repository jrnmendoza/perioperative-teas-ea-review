# 28. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **Strong multicenter design** with six centers and centralized randomization.
2. **576 randomized**, with only 8 total discontinuations/losses; follow-up retention was high.
3. Participant masking was **not feasible**, acknowledged by the authors.
4. Surgeons, anesthesiologists, ward staff, and outcome assessors were blinded.
5. Primary-outcome reporting contains a **major internal discrepancy**:
   - Table/abstract: single group **72/198**
   - Results prose: **79/196**
6. Pairwise primary-outcome P-values differ between the abstract, Results prose, and Table 2.
7. Printed RRs for chronic pain do **not match simple crude RRs from the published raw counts**.
8. Table 1 ASA counts add up to per-protocol completer numbers rather than the randomized denominators printed in the headers.
9. Combined-acupoint TEAS clearly reduced **intraoperative remifentanil** vs sham.
10. Single-acupoint vs sham remifentanil **P=0.04** does not meet the paper's Bonferroni-adjusted pairwise threshold of **0.0125**.
11. Acute 24-h pain and rescue analgesia were **not different** among groups.
12. Exact postoperative sufentanil PCA consumption is **not reported**, so a 24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint cannot be extracted.
13. Combined TEAS substantially reduced **PONV**, driven mainly by reduction in **nausea**, not vomiting.
14. At 3 months, the reported combined-vs-sham pain-severity P=0.03 does not meet the study's stated Bonferroni pairwise threshold.
15. One minor electrode-related discomfort event was reported.

# 30. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Lu et al. 2021 (Study #414)** was a **six-center randomized clinical trial** of preoperative TEAS in **576 women undergoing radical mastectomy**. Participants were randomized to sham (**n=188**), **bilateral PC6 TEAS (n=198)**, or **combined bilateral PC6 + CV17 TEAS (n=190)**.

Active TEAS used a **Hwato SDZ-V** with **2/10-Hz disperse-dense stimulation** for **30 min immediately before anesthesia induction**, titrated to maximum tolerated de qi-like sensations. Sham participants had electrodes attached but received no stimulation. Patients could not be reliably blinded, but surgeons, anesthesiologists, ward staff, and outcome assessors were blinded.

Using **Table 2**, chronic post-mastectomy pain at 6 months occurred in:
- **65/188 (34.6%) sham**
- **72/198 (36.4%) single PC6**
- **42/190 (22.1%) combined PC6+CV17**

The combined-acupoint intervention therefore showed the strongest long-term analgesic signal. However, the article contains important primary-outcome inconsistencies: the Results prose instead gives **79/196 (40.3%)** for the single-acupoint arm, and pairwise P-values/RRs differ between the abstract, prose, and table.

For the opioid-sparing review, intraoperative remifentanil was:
- sham **1.4 ± 0.7 mg**
- single PC6 **1.3 ± 0.6 mg**
- combined PC6+CV17 **1.2 ± 0.5 mg**

The best-supported comparison is **combined vs sham**, with a **0.20-mg lower remifentanil dose (P<0.001)**. The single-vs-sham P=0.04 does not meet the paper's stated Bonferroni-adjusted pairwise threshold.

Postoperatively, patients received **sufentanil PCA**, but the paper does **not report the PCA prescription or cumulative 24-h sufentanil consumption**, so a valid **24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint cannot be calculated** from the main article.

At 24 h, acute pain and rescue parecoxib use were similar among groups. Combined-acupoint TEAS substantially reduced PONV (**18.4% vs 36.2% sham**) and especially nausea, while vomiting was unchanged.

The most important consensus-audit issues are the inconsistent **single-arm chronic-pain denominator/count**, conflicting primary-outcome P-values and RRs, the Table 1 ASA denominator mismatch, and the absence of quantitative postoperative sufentanil consumption.
