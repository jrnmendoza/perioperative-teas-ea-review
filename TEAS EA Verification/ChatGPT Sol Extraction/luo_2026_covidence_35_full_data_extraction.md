# 23. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **Excellent participant retention:** all 277 randomized patients were analyzed.
2. The trial is **single-blind**, not double-blind; the intervention anesthesiologist was aware of allocation.
3. Participant blinding was **not successful**: 77.5% of active and 66.2% of sham patients correctly guessed allocation.
4. Active TEAS caused perceptible tingling while sham used **no current**, making sensory unblinding predictable.
5. The primary endpoint is **PONV**, not opioid consumption.
6. TEAS was tested on top of **dexamethasone + palonosetron**, so the antiemetic effect is additive to dual prophylaxis.
7. The perioperative opioid field **“Sufentanil equivalents (mg)”** has an unresolved definition/unit.
8. There was **no significant perioperative opioid-sparing signal** in that reported variable.
9. Postoperative oxycodone is mentioned but **quantitative 24-h consumption is not reported**.
10. Therefore exact 24-h postoperative opioid/MME cannot be extracted.
11. The published **0.7 mg/kg IV oxycodone** regimen is insufficiently explained and should be verified before using it.
12. Table 4 is internally inconsistent: interval rescue antiemetic use is reported, but the 0-48 h row incorrectly gives **0 in both groups**.
13. The article says no significant difference in total analgesic consumption, but does not provide the quantitative totals.
14. Pain differences at 6, 24 and 48 h are statistically significant but relatively small in absolute magnitude.
15. The study population is limited to ASA I-II adults undergoing tympanoplasty, reducing generalizability.

# 25. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Luo et al. 2026 (Study #35)** randomized **277 adults undergoing tympanoplasty** to active bilateral **PC6 TEAS (n=138)** or sham TEAS (**n=139**), with **no attrition**. Active TEAS used surface electrodes at bilateral PC6, beginning at **1 mA and 2 Hz**, titrated to approximately **2-20 mA** until tolerable hand tingling, and continued from **anesthesia induction until the end of surgery**. Sham patients had identical electrode placement but no electrical current.

All patients received standardized general anesthesia and dual antiemetic prophylaxis with **dexamethasone 8 mg** and **palonosetron 0.075 mg**.

TEAS substantially reduced PONV:
- cumulative nausea: **17.39% vs 38.13%**
- cumulative vomiting: **6.52% vs 19.42%**
- complete response: **81.16% vs 59.71%**

Postoperative pain was also modestly lower with TEAS at:
- **6 h: 1.55 ± 0.85 vs 1.86 ± 0.97**
- **24 h: 1.30 ± 0.92 vs 1.65 ± 0.95**
- **48 h: 0.82 ± 0.75 vs 1.09 ± 0.75**

For the opioid-sparing review, however, the paper is **not quantitatively usable for a 24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint**. Table 1 reports **“Sufentanil equivalents (mg)” 15.86 ± 5.11 vs 15.18 ± 4.95 (P=0.27)**, but the unit and conversion method are not explained. Postoperative pain management is described as IV oxycodone, yet the publication does not provide cumulative 24-h oxycodone consumption.

The most important audit problems are **failed participant blinding**, the unexplained perioperative opioid-equivalent unit, the ambiguous postoperative oxycodone regimen, and the impossible cumulative rescue-antiemetic row in Table 4.
