# 28. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **Partially randomized design:** Group C is observational; only S vs T is randomized.
2. The trial has a **small randomized sample**: 35 randomized, 33 analyzed.
3. Two randomized participants were lost and no full **ITT analysis** is reported.
4. There was **no sham TEAS**, so participants were unblinded and placebo/sensory effects cannot be separated from treatment effects.
5. Allocation concealment is explicitly described as **not feasible**.
6. NRS pain and AIS sleep outcomes are subjective, increasing the importance of the lack of participant blinding.
7. Exact NRS means/SDs are **graph-only**, so continuous pain data should not be meta-analyzed without digitization or author data.
8. The study provides **no quantitative postoperative opioid-consumption endpoint**.
9. It also does not report group-specific intraoperative remifentanil or sufentanil totals.
10. The postoperative regimen was primarily **non-opioid flurbiprofen**, which may explain why opioid outcomes were not emphasized.
11. The TEAS acupoint arrangement is asymmetric: **LI4 bilateral, HT7 left only, PC6 right only**.
12. Group T's modified-radical-mastectomy percentage is inconsistent with its count.
13. The sample-size paragraph says **72 enrolled**, whereas the CONSORT flow indicates 72 screened/recruited and 61 entered the study.
14. Single-center design limits generalizability.
15. Follow-up was short: pain through **72 h**, sleep through **POD2**; chronic postoperative pain was not assessed.

# 30. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Ma et al. 2026** was a **single-center partially randomized controlled trial** evaluating one preoperative TEAS session in breast-cancer patients with preoperative sleep disturbance. Seventy-two patients were screened/recruited, 61 entered the study, and **59 were analyzed**.

Patients with good sleep (**PSQI <6**) formed a **non-randomized reference group (C, n=26)**. Patients with sleep disturbance (**PSQI ≥6**) were randomized to **no TEAS (S: 17 randomized, 16 analyzed)** or **TEAS (T: 18 randomized, 17 analyzed)**. Therefore the valid randomized treatment comparison is **T vs S**.

TEAS was delivered for **30 min before anesthesia induction** with a **HANS-200A** using **2/100-Hz dense-disperse biphasic stimulation at 8-12 mA**. The acupoint pattern was unusual and should be coded precisely: **LI4 bilaterally, HT7 on the left, and PC6 on the right**. Figure 1 on page 4 confirms this placement.

The study found significantly lower postoperative resting NRS pain with TEAS than no TEAS at **4, 24, 48, and 72 h**, and improved postoperative sleep quality on POD1 and POD2. However, exact NRS means/SDs are provided only graphically, not in numerical tables.

TEAS also substantially reduced intraoperative blood-pressure variability:
- sdSBP: **9.31 ± 2.73 vs 18.85 ± 6.74**
- cvSBP: **0.08 ± 0.02 vs 0.16 ± 0.05**
and reduced ephedrine use.

For the opioid-sparing review, the study is **not quantitatively usable for a 24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint**. All patients received intraoperative sufentanil/remifentanil, but group-specific opioid totals are not reported. Postoperative analgesia was **flurbiprofen axetil 100 mg IV once daily for three days**, and the paper does not report quantitative postoperative opioid use.

The major audit issues are the **partially randomized design**, absence of sham/participant blinding, small randomized sample, lack of full ITT analysis, graph-only pain values, and absence of quantitative opioid-consumption data.
