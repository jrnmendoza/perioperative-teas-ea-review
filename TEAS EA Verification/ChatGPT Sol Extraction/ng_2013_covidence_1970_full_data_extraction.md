# 25. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **Strong follow-up:** all 165 randomized participants were analyzed.
2. Randomization occurred **after surgery**, which reduces confounding by intraoperative surgical conversion/complication but means the intervention targets postoperative recovery only.
3. Allocation concealment is questionable because the sequence was placed in **sealed nonopaque envelopes**.
4. EA and sham participants were blinded, but the no-acupuncture group could not be blinded.
5. The sham procedure is relatively rigorous and includes superficial off-point needling and pseudostimulation.
6. EA was **postoperative only**, beginning on POD1; this should not be pooled indiscriminately with preoperative/intraoperative protocols.
7. The opioid-sparing outcome is clinically important and large, but its **time window is not clearly specified**.
8. Pethidine dosing is reported inconsistently as **1 mg/kg on demand** in Methods versus **50-mg injections** in outcome tables.
9. Therefore the reported injection count should not be converted to a 24-h opioid/MME endpoint without clarification.
10. Pain on POD1 was unchanged, while benefits emerged on POD2-POD3.
11. Formal ERAS/fast-track care was not used.
12. The selected population excluded complex colorectal cases, epidural patients, conversions, stomas, and intraoperative complications, limiting generalizability.
13. No acupuncture-related adverse events were reported.
14. The study is primarily a **postoperative ileus / recovery trial**, despite providing useful opioid-sparing data.

# 27. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Ng et al. 2013 (Study #1970)** was a single-center randomized three-arm trial in **165 adults undergoing elective laparoscopic colonic or upper rectal cancer surgery**, with **55 patients each** assigned to electroacupuncture, sham acupuncture, or no acupuncture. There was **no attrition**, and all randomized patients were analyzed by intention to treat. fileciteturn36file0

EA was delivered **postoperatively only**, once daily from **POD1 to POD4 or until first bowel motion**, using invasive needling at **ST36, SP6, LI4, and TE6**, de qi, and **100-Hz electrical stimulation for 20 min**. The sham arm used superficial off-point needling without de qi plus a visually active but electrically inactive stimulator.

EA significantly accelerated bowel recovery:
- first bowel motion: **85.9 ± 36.1 h**
- sham: **107.5 ± 46.2 h**
- no acupuncture: **122.1 ± 53.5 h**

EA also reduced postoperative pain on POD2 and POD3 and substantially reduced postoperative pethidine use. The reported mean number of **50-mg pethidine injections** was:
- EA: **2.7 ± 2.3**
- sham: **5.2 ± 4.7**
- no acupuncture: **5.0 ± 4.5**

If the table's 50-mg-per-injection wording is applied literally, this corresponds to calculated cumulative means of approximately:
- **135 mg EA**
- **260 mg sham**
- **250 mg no acupuncture**

However, the exact **time window is not stated**, and the Methods describe pethidine as **1 mg/kg every 4 h on demand**, creating a dosing inconsistency. Therefore this study provides a strong **postoperative opioid-sparing signal**, but should **not be entered as a 24-h MME endpoint without clarification**.
