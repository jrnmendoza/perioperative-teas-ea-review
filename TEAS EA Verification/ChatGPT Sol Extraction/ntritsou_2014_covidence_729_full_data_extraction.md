# 7. Rescue analgesia protocol

If postoperative analgesia was unacceptable:
- **NRS ≥3**, or
- **PPI ≥2**

then:

1. **Tramadol 50 mg IV** was given first.
2. If NRS did not fall by ≥2 points after 30 min, **morphine 2 mg** was administered.
3. This sequence could be repeated as needed.

# 27. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **75 randomized but only 70 analyzed**; no full ITT analysis.
2. Post-randomization exclusions included nausea, dizziness, vomiting, reoperation, and delirium, which are clinically important outcomes.
3. Participant and postoperative-observer blinding was reasonably attempted using a nonpenetrating same-point sham.
4. Intraoperative anesthetists and the acupuncturist were **not blinded**.
5. Allocation concealment is described only as being handled by the department director; operational details are not given in the main article.
6. The trial used a strong **multimodal analgesic background** of tramadol, ketamine, parecoxib, and paracetamol.
7. EA clearly improved pain and reduced **rescue analgesia**, but the main paper does **not provide exact rescue-dose totals**.
8. The directly reported continuous tramadol total is **not statistically different** between groups.
9. Therefore “rescue analgesia reduced” and “total tramadol reduced” must **not** be treated as equivalent claims.
10. Total tramadol is likely a 24-h cumulative measure, but the table does not explicitly restate the observation window.
11. Morphine rescue use is described in protocol but not quantitatively reported.
12. Exact continuous pain outcomes are graph-based in the main PDF.
13. The satisfaction paragraph appears to contain a likely typographical sign error (**p>0.01** despite describing significance).
14. Five post-randomization exclusions may bias adverse-effect and analgesia estimates.
15. Single-center study with only 35 analyzed participants per arm.

# 29. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Ntritsou et al. 2014 (Study #729)** randomized **75 men undergoing radical retropubic prostatectomy** to perioperative electroacupuncture plus tramadol/ketamine or sham acupuncture plus the same multimodal analgesic regimen. Five participants were excluded after randomization, leaving **35 patients per group** for analysis. fileciteturn37file0

EA was delivered in two stages:
- **100 Hz at bilateral LI4 for 30 min during abdominal-wall closure**
- **4 Hz at bilateral ST36 + LI4 for 30 min immediately after extubation**

The sham arm used nonpenetrating needles at the same points, with the stimulator light on but **no electrical current**.

All patients received multimodal analgesia including parecoxib, paracetamol, a **1.5 mg/kg tramadol bolus**, ketamine, and continued tramadol/ketamine infusion. Rescue treatment was **tramadol 50 mg IV**, followed by **morphine 2 mg IV** if needed.

EA significantly reduced postoperative pain and **rescue analgesia**, especially early after surgery. However, the directly reported total tramadol exposure was:
- **EA: 443.7 ± 67.4 mg**
- **Control: 461.5 ± 63.2 mg**
- **P=0.257**

Thus, the trial shows a clear reduction in **rescue analgesic requirement**, but not a statistically significant reduction in the reported total tramadol sum. Intraoperative remifentanil was also similar (**1003.6 ± 522.7 vs 1041.9 ± 631.9 μg, P=0.783**).

For your opioid-sparing review, this distinction is important: the study is highly relevant, but **do not code the significant rescue-analgesia result as a significant total 24-h tramadol reduction** unless supplementary or author data provide the rescue-dose breakdown.
