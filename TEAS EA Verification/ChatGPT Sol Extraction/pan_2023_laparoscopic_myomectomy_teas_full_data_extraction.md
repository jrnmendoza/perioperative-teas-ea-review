# 27. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **120 randomized but only 105 analyzed**; no full ITT analysis.
2. The abstract incorrectly/ambiguously describes **105 as randomized**, whereas Figure 3 shows 120 randomized.
3. The **group-specific exclusions in Figure 3 do not mathematically reconcile with the final n=52 vs 53**.
4. Patients who used off-protocol analgesics or **PCIA were excluded after randomization**, which is particularly important for an opioid-sparing review and may bias analgesic outcomes.
5. The study is described as **double-blind**, but the control group received **no TEAS and no sham stimulation is described**.
6. Subjective endpoints such as NRS and QoR-40 are therefore vulnerable to performance/placebo bias.
7. The study does provide a valid quantitative **intraoperative remifentanil outcome**, significantly favoring TEAS.
8. The calculated reduction in remifentanil is **13.3%**, whereas the authors describe it as “almost 15%.”
9. Postoperative “analgesic drug” use is reported only as a binary outcome with **no drug identity or dose**.
10. Consequently, exact **24-h postoperative opioid consumption/MME cannot be calculated**.
11. The article prints an unusual/unclear TEAS current-intensity expression and should be queried before consensus coding.
12. The cisatracurium induction dose printed in the Methods appears unusually high relative to the actual Table 2 total dose and may contain a decimal/unit error.
13. An unidentified **20-mg drug** is described during emergence.
14. Two analyzed groups were similar in baseline characteristics, but post-randomization exclusion could still introduce bias.
15. Single-center design.

# 29. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Pan et al. 2023** evaluated perioperative **TEAS in women undergoing laparoscopic myomectomy**. The flow diagram shows **120 randomized (60/group)**, but **105 were analyzed (52 TEAS, 53 control)** after post-randomization exclusions. The abstract's statement that 105 patients were randomized is therefore inconsistent with the study flow.

TEAS used:
- **bilateral LI4 + PC6**, beginning **30 min before surgery and continuing through the end of anesthesia**
- followed by **bilateral ST36 + SP6 for 30 min in PACU**
- frequency **2/100 Hz**.

The strongest opioid-related result is intraoperative remifentanil:
- **TEAS: 740.1 ± 276.9 µg**
- **Control: 854.0 ± 287.5 µg**
- **P=0.04**
- approximately **13.3% lower** from the published means.

Postoperative movement NRS was also lower with TEAS:
- **1 h: 2 (1,4) vs 3 (2,4), P=0.04**
- **24 h: 2 (2,3) vs 3 (3,4), P<0.001**

However, the study does **not provide a usable continuous 24-h postoperative opioid endpoint**. It only reports that **19/52 (37%) vs 23/53 (43%)** received unspecified analgesic drugs within 24 h. Drug identity and doses are not given, and patients who used PCIA were excluded after randomization. Therefore a **24-h postoperative MME cannot be calculated** from the main article.

Other notable effects favoring TEAS included lower intraoperative hypotension, less vasoactive-drug use, lower 24-h vomiting, faster first flatus and solid-food tolerance, and a higher **QoR-40 total score (182.7 ± 6.9 vs 176.4 ± 7.1)**.

The major consensus-audit concerns are the **120-vs-105 randomization discrepancy, internally inconsistent attrition arithmetic, post-randomization exclusion of analgesic/PCIA users, unsupported “double-blind” description, unclear TEAS current wording, and absence of quantitative postoperative opioid consumption**.
