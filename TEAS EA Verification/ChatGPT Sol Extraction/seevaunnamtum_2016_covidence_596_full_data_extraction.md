# 19. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **All 64 randomized patients were analyzed**, which is a major strength.
2. Randomization was computer-generated, but **allocation concealment is not described**.
3. The study is described as double-blind; this is reasonably plausible for participants because EA began after induction and needles were removed before awakening.
4. There was **no sham-acupuncture control**, so the control arm did not reproduce procedural contact/needle placement.
5. The anesthetist/acupuncturist was unblinded, although postoperative assessors were blinded.
6. The paper provides a particularly strong and clean **exact 24-h IV morphine endpoint**.
7. PCA programming is fully described and **no basal infusion** was used.
8. Rescue postoperative IV morphine was included in the total-dose data, making the 24-h endpoint clinically comprehensive.
9. The reported 37% reduction in morphine is not merely a proxy: it is a **direct drug-consumption endpoint**.
10. Intraoperative morphine was standardized at **0.1 mg/kg in both groups**.
11. Smoking status was not collected, limiting full baseline PONV-risk characterization.
12. Separate vomiting counts and quantitative rescue-antiemetic totals are not clearly presented in the main Results.
13. De qi could not be assessed because the intervention was delivered under general anesthesia.
14. The paper states the device was set to intensity “level 1,” but gives **no exact current amplitude in mA**.
15. Single-center sample is modest (32/group).

# 21. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Seevaunnamtum et al. 2016 (Study #596)** was a prospective randomized double-blind trial in **64 women undergoing total abdominal hysterectomy**, with **32 patients in each arm and no attrition**.

EA was delivered **intraoperatively only**, beginning after induction of general anesthesia and continuing until the end of surgery. The intervention used invasive acupuncture at **bilateral PC6 and LI4**, with a **Hwato SDZ-IV** stimulator at **2 Hz continuous-wave stimulation**, intensity level 1. The control group received standard care without sham acupuncture.

All patients received standardized general anesthesia and **IV morphine 0.1 mg/kg intraoperatively**. Postoperatively, both groups received **morphine PCA with 1-mg IV boluses, a 5-min lockout, and no background infusion**. Additional rescue morphine was included in the total-dose outcome.

This is one of the strongest studies in the dataset for the review's **24-hour postoperative opioid-sparing endpoint**. Total 24-h morphine consumption was:
- **EA: 21.38 ± 14.38 mg**
- **Control: 33.94 ± 20.24 mg**
- mean difference **-12.56 mg (95% CI -21.34 to -3.79)**
- **P=0.006**
- calculated relative reduction ≈ **37%**

PCA demand was also markedly lower:
- **27.28 ± 21.61 vs 55.25 ± 46.85 presses**
- **P=0.003**

Pain was significantly lower with EA at:
- **30 min: 2.75 ± 2.34 vs 4.50 ± 2.37**
- **2 h: 2.25 ± 1.80 vs 3.88 ± 2.21**

The antiemetic effect was strongest early: nausea at 30 min was **15.6% vs 46.9%**, favoring EA.

For your meta-analysis, this study is **directly usable for continuous 24-h IV morphine consumption** and should be prioritized as a high-quality opioid-consumption datapoint.
