# 19. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **All 90 randomized patients appear to have been analyzed**, with no reported attrition.
2. Randomization used a random-number table, but **allocation concealment is not described**.
3. The placebo procedure for Groups I-II is relatively strong and includes nonpenetrating needles plus a visually active but electrically inactive stimulator.
4. Blinding was assessed and appeared reasonably successful between placebo and preoperative EA.
5. The acupuncturist and anaesthetists were **not blinded**.
6. Group III was awake for postoperative EA and therefore was not participant-blinded in the same way as Groups I-II.
7. The Methods define Group II as **45-min preoperative EA**, but the Discussion later says some EA continued under GA until the end of surgery. Exact exposure duration requires clarification.
8. The study used **two frequencies simultaneously at different sites**: 2 Hz at ST36/PC6 and 100 Hz along the incision.
9. The placebo-controlled intraoperative alfentanil difference (Group II vs I) was **not significant**.
10. The significant intraoperative comparison was Group II vs Group III, but Group III had not yet received its EA intervention during surgery.
11. The postoperative morphine-sparing effect was strongest and statistically significant only during **6-12 h**.
12. The 24-h cumulative morphine consumption was lower with preoperative EA but **not statistically significant**.
13. Morphine is reported as **mg/kg**, not absolute mg, limiting direct MME conversion from aggregate data.
14. Mean 24-h VAS was not lower in the preoperative EA group than placebo.
15. PONV, drowsiness, and pruritus were numerically lower with preop EA but not significantly different.
16. The study was powered for a **30% reduction in 24-h morphine**, but the observed placebo-controlled mean reduction was approximately **23.5%**.
17. Single-center study with 30 participants per arm.

# 21. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Sim et al. 2002 (Study #952)** randomized **90 women undergoing total abdominal hysterectomy with or without bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy** into three groups of 30:
- placebo EA before anesthesia,
- preoperative EA,
- postoperative EA.

EA used invasive needling at **bilateral ST36 and PC6**, plus subcutaneous needles along the surgical incision. Electrical stimulation was **2 Hz at ST36/PC6** and **100 Hz along the incision**. The formal protocol describes **45 min of preoperative EA**, although the Discussion later suggests some stimulation may have continued intraoperatively.

The placebo procedure was comparatively rigorous: nonpenetrating taped needles, sensory tapping with a guide tube, and a flashing mock stimulator with **no current**.

For intraoperative opioid use, alfentanil requirement was:
- placebo: **0.51 ± 0.21 µg/kg/min**
- preop EA: **0.44 ± 0.15 µg/kg/min**
- postop EA: **0.58 ± 0.22 µg/kg/min**

Preoperative EA was significantly lower than postoperative EA (**P=0.024**) but **not significantly lower than placebo EA (P=0.472)**.

For postoperative opioid use, cumulative 24-h IV morphine was:
- placebo: **0.68 ± 0.38 mg/kg**
- preop EA: **0.52 ± 0.19 mg/kg**
- postop EA: **0.58 ± 0.27 mg/kg**

The preop-EA group used approximately **23.5% less morphine than placebo**, but the **24-h cumulative difference was not statistically significant**. The statistically significant opioid-sparing effect occurred specifically during the **6-12-h interval**, where preop EA used **0.03 (0.05) mg/kg** versus **0.10 (0.11) mg/kg** with placebo (**P=0.015**) and **0.08 (0.10) mg/kg** with postoperative EA (**P=0.010**).

The study therefore supports a **short-term early postoperative morphine-sparing effect**, but it should **not be coded as demonstrating a statistically significant 24-h cumulative opioid reduction**.
