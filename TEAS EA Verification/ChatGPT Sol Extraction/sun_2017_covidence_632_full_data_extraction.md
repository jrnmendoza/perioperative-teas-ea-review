# 18. Pain interpretation

The study's key pain finding is:

- **Single preoperative TEAS (TSS) was not effective.**
- **Two-session TEAS protocols (TTS and TST) reduced activity-evoked pain.**
- **Resting pain was not significantly affected.**
- The **preoperative + postoperative** protocol (TST) showed the most persistent benefit through 48 h.

This is an important protocol-characteristic finding for the review because **timing/repetition modified the clinical effect**.

# 24. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **380 randomized but 361 analyzed**, with no full ITT outcome analysis.
2. Post-randomization withdrawals included conversion to open surgery, prolonged surgery, and intraoperative complications.
3. Allocation concealment is reasonably described using **sealed opaque envelopes**.
4. Patients, anesthesiologists, and evaluators were blinded; TEAS operators were not.
5. The sham procedure was **not completely inert**: it delivered 5 min of perceptible 2/100-Hz electrical stimulation.
6. The authors explicitly acknowledge that sham TEAS may have had some analgesic effect.
7. The study's central finding is **protocol/timing dependent**:
   - preoperative TEAS alone did not improve postoperative pain,
   - repeated TEAS including intraoperative or postoperative treatment did.
8. Benefits occurred primarily in **activity-evoked pain**, not resting pain.
9. There was **no intraoperative anesthetic or opioid-sparing effect**.
10. The “intraoperative morphine” variable is based on the study's own sufentanil/remifentanil-to-morphine conversion and should not be assumed equivalent to a standard MME method.
11. The postoperative rescue analgesic was **flurbiprofen**, not an opioid.
12. Therefore the lower supplemental analgesic requirement in TST **must not be coded as postoperative opioid sparing**.
13. No quantitative postoperative opioid-consumption endpoint is available.
14. Mixed surgical population may introduce heterogeneity.
15. The registry wording is internally inconsistent: the article says “ClinicalTrials.gov” but provides **ChiCTR-IOR-15006032**.
16. Single-center Chinese study with **82.3% female participants**.
17. Exact numerical resting-pain data are graph-only.
18. The exact intraoperative and postoperative clock timing of the 30-min TEAS sessions is not fully specified.

# 26. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Sun et al. 2017 (Study #632)** randomized **380 patients undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy or gynecological surgery** into four timing protocols of TEAS, with **361 analyzed**. The study directly tested whether the timing and repetition of TEAS influenced postoperative analgesia. fileciteturn41file0

All treatment groups used **bilateral LI4 and PC6**, a **HANS-200A**, and alternating **2/100-Hz stimulation every 3 seconds**, with 30-min treatment periods. The four groups were:
- **SSS:** sham preop + sham intraop + sham postop
- **TSS:** true preop only
- **TTS:** true preop + true intraop
- **TST:** true preop + true postop

The key finding was that **preoperative TEAS alone was ineffective**, whereas **repeated TEAS was effective**. At 6 h, activity VAS was:
- SSS **5.0 (4.0-6.0)**
- TSS **5.0 (5.0-6.0)**
- TTS **3.0 (3.0-4.0)**
- TST **3.0 (2.5-5.0)**

At 24 h, the most favorable result was in TST:
- **3.0 (3.0-4.0)** versus **4.0 (3.8-5.0)** in SSS and **4.0 (4.0-5.0)** in TSS.

There was **no significant reduction in intraoperative propofol or opioid consumption**. The paper's “intraoperative morphine” outcome is a study-specific morphine-equivalent conversion of sufentanil and remifentanil, with values of approximately **1.5-1.6 mg/kg across groups (P=0.303)**.

Postoperative rescue analgesia consisted of **flurbiprofen axetil 50 mg IV**, not an opioid. Supplemental analgesic use within 48 h was:
- SSS **23.3%**
- TSS **11.0%**
- TTS **15.4%**
- TST **7.9%**

TST was significantly lower than SSS (**P=0.020**). However, because the rescue drug was an NSAID and no quantitative postoperative opioid consumption was reported, this study **cannot contribute a continuous 24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint**.

The main consensus-audit points are the **380-to-361 complete-case analysis**, the biologically non-inert 5-min sham stimulation, the study-specific opioid-equivalence method, and the need to code the trial primarily as evidence that **repeated perioperative TEAS—rather than one preoperative session—improves activity-evoked postoperative pain**.
