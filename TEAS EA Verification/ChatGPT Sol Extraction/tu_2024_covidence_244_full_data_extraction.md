# 8. Baseline statistical inconsistencies

Several Table 2 t-statistics appear incompatible with the printed P-values.

Examples:
- anesthesia time: **t = -0.713** paired with **P=0.958**
- operation time: **t = -0.563** paired with **P=0.801**
- sufentanil dose: **t = 0.368** paired with **P=0.350**

These combinations are not mathematically consistent under a conventional two-sample t test.

### Extraction decision
Use the **raw group means/SDs** as the primary numerical data and treat the printed inferential statistics cautiously until clarified.

# 23. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **120 randomized but 115 analyzed**; no full ITT analysis.
2. Post-randomization exclusions included clinically relevant events such as persistent coma and intracranial-hypertension-related vomiting.
3. Allocation concealment was reasonably strong: **sealed opaque sequential envelopes**.
4. Participants, assessors, data collectors, and statisticians were blinded.
5. Sham TEAS was methodologically credible because the device appeared and sounded active while internal wires prevented current delivery.
6. The trial used only **one bilateral acupoint pair: P6/PC6**, which is useful for isolating the effect of this antiemetic point.
7. TEAS was **postoperative only**, so it should not be pooled indiscriminately with preoperative/intraoperative protocols.
8. The significant vomiting effect was limited to **2-6 h**.
9. The Results prose prints **P=0.47**, while the abstract and Table 3 print **P=0.047**.
10. The inclusion criterion stating **no history of motion sickness** conflicts directly with Table 2, which reports motion-sickness history in about 28% of both groups.
11. Several Table 2 t-statistic/P-value pairs appear internally inconsistent.
12. The study showed a large pain-score reduction at **6-24 h**.
13. Rescue tramadol use was numerically lower but **not statistically significant**.
14. Exact continuous 24-h postoperative opioid consumption is unavailable.
15. Therefore the study cannot contribute a continuous 24-h opioid/MME endpoint from the main article.
16. Single-center design.
17. Recruitment occurred from 2014-2018, while publication was not until 2023/2024.

# 25. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Tu et al. 2024 (online 2023; Study #244)** randomized **120 craniotomy patients** to bilateral **P6 TEAS** or a credible no-current sham. Five participants were excluded after randomization, leaving **57 TEAS and 58 sham patients** for analysis. fileciteturn42file0

TEAS was delivered **postoperatively only**, once the patient regained consciousness in the anesthesia recovery area. Bilateral P6 electrodes were connected to an **SDZ-V** stimulator for **30 min** using a **2/100-Hz dilatational wave**, with intensity increased until a continuous de qi sensation was perceived. Sham patients had the same electrodes, device display, and sound, but specially severed internal wires prevented electrical current.

All patients received **ondansetron 8 mg IV** 30 min before the end of surgery.

The main significant antiemetic result was at **2-6 h**:
- TEAS: **8/57 (14.0%)**
- Sham: **17/58 (29.3%)**
- **P=0.047**

The Results prose contains an apparent typo of **P=0.47**, while the abstract and Table 3 give **0.047**.

Pain also favored TEAS at **6-24 h**:
- TEAS: **2 (0.5-3)**
- Sham: **4 (3-4)**
- **P=0.001**

For the opioid-sparing review, however, the study is **not quantitatively usable for a continuous 24-h opioid/MME endpoint**. Rescue analgesia was **tramadol 10 mg IM for VAS >6**, used by **3/57 TEAS vs 6/58 sham patients (P=0.315)**, but the article does not report the number of doses per patient or cumulative tramadol consumption.

The major audit concerns are the **120-to-115 complete-case analysis**, the contradiction between the stated exclusion of patients with motion-sickness history and Table 2 reporting such a history in ~28% of both groups, several inconsistent t-statistic/P-value pairs in Table 2, and the decimal-place error in the 2-6 h vomiting P-value.
