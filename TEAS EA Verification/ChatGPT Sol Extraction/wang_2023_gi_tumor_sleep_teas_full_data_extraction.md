# 19. Proposed mechanistic interpretation

The authors propose that perioperative TEAS may:
- activate the **Nrf2/ARE antioxidant pathway**,
- increase Nrf2, SOD and GPX3,
- reduce cortisol/stress response,
- reduce IL-6-mediated inflammation,
- improve postoperative sleep and activity-related pain.

### Caution
These biomarker associations support a mechanistic hypothesis but do not independently establish mediation/causality.

# 22. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **88 randomized but only 83 analyzed**; the abstract incorrectly states that 83 patients were allocated.
2. Five randomized patients were excluded for **protocol breach**, with no full ITT analysis.
3. Randomization and allocation concealment were otherwise reasonably described.
4. The trial is genuinely **multicenter (2 centers)**.
5. The article nevertheless states in its limitations that future “multicenter” research is needed, despite already including two centers.
6. Participants, surgeons, OR nurses, data collectors and the statistician were blinded; TEAS providers were not.
7. The sham delivered **no current**, so repeated awake nighttime stimulation could potentially reveal allocation through sensation.
8. The **stimulation frequency is internally inconsistent**:
   - 2/100 Hz in the abstract
   - 2/10 Hz in the detailed Methods.
9. Exact sleep and pain values are **figure-only**.
10. TEAS improved **activity pain but not resting pain**, an important outcome-specific effect.
11. The study reports significantly fewer rescue-analgesia doses, but **does not identify the analgesic**.
12. Therefore the analgesic-sparing result cannot be assumed to represent opioid sparing.
13. No quantitative 24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint is available.
14. Group-specific intraoperative opioid totals are not reported.
15. The trial used repeated perioperative treatment focused around **nighttime/sleep periods**, which is materially different from single preoperative or intraoperative TEAS protocols.
16. PONV was substantially reduced over 72 h.
17. First flatus and hospital stay were not significantly improved.
18. The Results prose incorrectly implies cortisol was higher with TEAS, while Table 4 and the Discussion show **lower cortisol**.
19. The trial excluded patients with pre-existing substantial sleep disturbance, limiting generalizability to patients with insomnia.
20. Sample size is modest despite the two-center design.

# 24. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Wang et al. 2023** was a two-center prospective randomized sham-controlled trial evaluating repeated perioperative TEAS in patients undergoing **laparoscopic gastric or colorectal cancer surgery**. The CONSORT diagram shows **88 randomized (43 TEAS, 45 sham)** and **83 analyzed (40 TEAS, 43 sham)** after five protocol breaches.

TEAS was delivered at **bilateral HT7, PC6 and ST36** for **30 min on three occasions**:
1. the night before surgery,
2. the night of surgery,
3. the first night after surgery.

The detailed Methods report **dense-disperse 2/10-Hz stimulation at 6-15 mA**, whereas the abstract reports **2/100 Hz**. This frequency discrepancy should be resolved before final consensus coding.

TEAS significantly improved postoperative sleep quality on D1 and D3 and reduced **activity-evoked pain at 24, 48 and 72 h**, while resting pain was not significantly different.

The study also reported fewer rescue-analgesia doses over 72 h:
- **TEAS: 0.53 ± 0.55**
- **Sham: 0.98 ± 0.96**
- **P=0.011**
