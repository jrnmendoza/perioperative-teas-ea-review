# 2. Study structure and randomized contrasts

Patients were first assigned to one of two **non-randomized risk strata** based on history of nausea and vomiting during pregnancy:

- **SNVP:** severe nausea and vomiting during pregnancy
- **MNVP:** mild-moderate nausea and vomiting during pregnancy

Each stratum was then randomized to:

- **OD:** ondansetron + dexamethasone
- **ODT:** ondansetron + dexamethasone + TEAS

| Stratum / subgroup | Meaning | Randomized? | Final analyzed n |
|---|---|---|---:|
| SNVP-OD | Severe NVP history + ondansetron/dexamethasone | **Yes, within SNVP stratum** | **35** |
| SNVP-ODT | Severe NVP history + ondansetron/dexamethasone + TEAS | **Yes, within SNVP stratum** | **33** |
| MNVP-OD | Mild-moderate NVP history + ondansetron/dexamethasone | **Yes, within MNVP stratum** | **35** |
| MNVP-ODT | Mild-moderate NVP history + ondansetron/dexamethasone + TEAS | **Yes, within MNVP stratum** | **35** |

### Correct treatment-effect comparisons
The randomized TEAS comparisons are:

1. **SNVP-ODT vs SNVP-OD**
2. **MNVP-ODT vs MNVP-OD**

The overall **SNVP vs MNVP** comparison is **observational**, because pregnancy-NVP history was not randomized.

# 28. Major methodological / audit issues

1. The study is **not a simple four-arm fully randomized trial**: SNVP vs MNVP status was observational, while OD vs ODT was randomized within each stratum.
2. **140 entered the stratified randomized design but 138 were analyzed** after 2 SNVP-ODT patients were excluded for postoperative fever.
3. No full ITT analysis is reported.
4. Randomization and allocation concealment appear reasonably strong, using computer-generated allocation and sequentially numbered envelopes.
5. Sham TEAS is relatively credible because wires were cut internally while device sounds/indicator remained active.
6. The intervention provider was unblinded, but patients, anesthesiologists, surgeons, ward staff, and outcome assessors were blinded.
7. There were important **surgical-procedure imbalances**:
   - SNVP-OD had more radical mastectomy + axillary dissection.
   - MNVP-ODT had more segmental mastectomy + axillary dissection.
8. The authors acknowledge these imbalances.
9. TEAS showed **time-specific PONV benefits**, but total PONV over 36 h was not significantly different within either randomized stratum.
10. In SNVP patients, the strongest effect was during **6-24 h** and on early vomiting.
11. In MNVP patients, benefit was concentrated in the **first 6 h**.
12. No postoperative opioid-consumption endpoint is provided.
13. Postoperative rescue analgesia was non-opioid **flurbiprofen**, so the study is not usable for 24-h postoperative MME.
14. Intraoperative sufentanil consumption was similar with and without TEAS.
15. The study title/conclusion uses genetic language for GDF-15, but the study measured **serum protein concentration**, not genotype.
16. Table 3 contains an obvious percentage error for MNVP-ODT asymptomatic patients at 12-24 h.
17. Single-center study.
18. SNVP/MNVP classification relied on retrospective pregnancy-history reporting.

# 30. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Wang et al. 2024 (Study #212)** evaluated TEAS as an adjunct to standard antiemetic prophylaxis in **women undergoing breast cancer surgery**. The study first classified patients by history of pregnancy-related nausea/vomiting into a high-risk **SNVP** group and a lower-risk **MNVP** group, then randomized patients within each stratum to **ondansetron + dexamethasone (OD)** or **OD + TEAS (ODT)**. A total of 140 patients entered the design and **138 were analyzed** after two SNVP-ODT patients were excluded because of postoperative fever. fileciteturn43file0

TEAS was delivered **preoperatively for 30 min before induction** at **bilateral PC6 and LI4**, using a **HANS-200A**, **2/100-Hz stimulation**, and **10 mA**. Sham patients had the same electrodes and active-looking/sounding device, but the output wires were internally cut so no current was delivered.

All patients received **dexamethasone 5 mg** and **ondansetron 4 mg** as pharmacologic prophylaxis.

In the randomized high-risk SNVP comparison, TEAS significantly reduced PONV:
- **6-12 h:** 21.2% vs 45.7%, P=0.033
- **12-24 h:** 6.1% vs 31.4%, P=0.008

Early vomiting within 6 h was also lower:
- **7/33 vs 19/35, P=0.005**

In the MNVP stratum, TEAS significantly reduced PONV during:
- **0-2 h:** 5.7% vs 25.7%, P=0.022
- **2-6 h:** 5.7% vs 22.9%, P=0.040

However, total PONV over the full 36-h period was not significantly lower in either randomized stratum.

For the opioid-sparing review, this study is **not usable for a continuous postoperative 24-h opioid/MME endpoint**. Postoperative rescue analgesia was **flurbiprofen axetil 100 mg**, not an opioid, and no postoperative opioid consumption was reported. Intraoperative sufentanil was similar between OD and ODT subgroups.

The paper also reports that higher **preoperative circulating GDF-15** was associated with both SNVP history and PONV, but this should be coded as a **serum biomarker association**, not as direct evidence of a susceptibility gene/genotype.

The major consensus-audit issues are the **partially randomized/stratified design**, exclusion of two randomized TEAS patients, surgical-procedure imbalance within treatment strata, the lack of postoperative opioid data, and an obvious Table 3 percentage error.
