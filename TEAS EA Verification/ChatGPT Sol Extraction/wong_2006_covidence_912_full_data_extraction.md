# 13. Critical interpretation of PCA timing

The paper states that PCA was used for the **first 3 postoperative days**, and Table 2 labels consumption as:
- Day 0
- Day 1
- Day 2
- Total

However, the article does **not define these rows as exact consecutive 0-24 h, 24-48 h, and 48-72 h intervals measured from the end of surgery**.

Data collection occurred:
- 1 h after surgery on Day 0,
- 08:00, 14:00, and 20:00 on Days 1-3.

### Extraction decision
Do **not** automatically interpret:
- Day 1 as exact 0-24-h consumption, or
- Day 2 as exact 24-48-h consumption.

The most defensible source-derived statement is:
- **calendar/postoperative-day-specific PCA morphine use was significantly lower on POD2**
- **total PCA use across the first 3 postoperative days was not significantly different**.

# 21. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **Pilot study with very small sample:** 27 randomized and only 25 analyzed.
2. Two post-randomization exclusions occurred **only in the sham group**.
3. The excluded events were serious postoperative complications: respiratory failure and GI bleeding.
4. No full ITT analysis is presented.
5. Randomization was computer-generated, but the envelopes are described as sealed rather than explicitly **opaque**.
6. Participant, surgeon, and outcome-assessor blinding was good in concept.
7. The sham acupuncture design is a methodological strength: blunt nonpenetrating needle, opaque fixator, same true acupoints, inactive electrical setup.
8. The EA protocol was **postoperative only** and unusually intensive: twice daily for 7 days.
9. EA used **60 Hz**, which the authors themselves hypothesize may explain the delayed effect.
10. The statistically significant morphine benefit emerged only on **POD2**.
11. Total PCA morphine use over the first 3 days was **not significantly reduced**.
12. The study does not provide an unambiguous **exact first-24-h morphine endpoint**.
13. Scheduled **dextropropoxyphene** means PCA morphine is not the total postoperative opioid exposure.
14. The anesthesia Methods print **fentanyl 2 mg/kg**, an unusually high dose expression that should be verified.
15. The Methods/Results contain a screening-number discrepancy: **32 eligible consecutive patients vs 153 assessed**.
16. VAS is described as 0-100 mm but reported numerically on an apparent 0-10 scale.
17. The POD2 VAS P-value of **0.92** appears inconsistent with the printed means/SDs and should be verified.
18. One demographic percentage is incorrect: **9/13 males = 69.2%, not 62.9%**.
19. Single-center trial conducted by one thoracic surgeon.
20. The study is primarily hypothesis-generating rather than definitive.

# 23. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Wong et al. 2006 (Study #912)** was a small randomized double-blind sham-controlled pilot trial of postoperative electroacupuncture after **open thoracotomy for non-small cell lung cancer**. Twenty-seven patients were randomized (**13 EA, 14 sham**), but two sham patients developed serious postoperative complications before receiving the intervention and were excluded, leaving **13 EA and 12 sham patients analyzed**.

EA began **immediately after return to the ward** and was given **twice daily for 30 min over the first 7 postoperative days** at ipsilateral **LI4, GB34, GB36 and TE8**, using invasive acupuncture, de qi and **60-Hz electrical stimulation**. The sham was methodologically credible: a blunt nonpenetrating needle was fixed at the same acupoints beneath an opaque device and connected to an incorrect stimulator output so no current flowed.

All patients received **IV morphine PCA (1-mg bolus, 5-min lockout) for the first 3 postoperative days**, plus scheduled oral **acetaminophen 640 mg + dextropropoxyphene 65 mg four times daily**.

PCA morphine use was:
- **POD0:** 8.3 ± 4.9 vs 7.8 ± 6.4 mg, P=0.84
- **POD1:** 18.0 ± 8.8 vs 18.8 ± 9.8 mg, P=0.83
- **POD2:** 7.5 ± 5.0 vs 15.7 ± 12.0 mg, **P=0.035**
- **Total first-three-day PCA:** 33.9 ± 12.8 vs 42.3 ± 21.3 mg, **P=0.25**

Thus the paper demonstrates a **delayed, POD2-specific morphine-sparing effect**, with approximately **52% lower PCA morphine on POD2**, but **no significant reduction in total PCA morphine over the full first 3 postoperative days**.

For a review requiring an exact **24-hour postoperative opioid/MME endpoint**, this study is problematic because the calendar “Day 0/Day 1/Day 2” PCA rows are not explicitly defined as exact 24-h windows from surgery. It should therefore **not automatically be coded as providing a clean 24-h MME endpoint** without clarification.

The major consensus-audit issues are the small pilot sample, sham-only post-randomization attrition, unclear exact 24-h opioid timing, scheduled background dextropropoxyphene, the unusually printed **fentanyl 2 mg/kg** induction dose, the **32-vs-153 screening discrepancy**, and the apparent VAS scale/P-value inconsistencies.
