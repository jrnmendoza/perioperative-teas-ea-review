# 6. pTEAS protocol - overall structure

This study used a **multiphase perioperative TEAS protocol**:

1. **30 min before anesthesia induction**
2. **Continuous stimulation throughout surgery**

# 30. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **90 randomized but only 84 analyzed**; no full ITT analysis.
2. Attrition was **asymmetric**: 5 exclusions in control vs 1 in pTEAS.
3. Post-randomization exclusions included massive hemorrhage and a serious postoperative complication.
4. The trial was **retrospectively registered on 25 January 2018**, after recruitment ended in February 2017.
5. The article says it was registered at **clinicaltrials.gov**, despite providing a **ChiCTR** identifier.
6. Allocation concealment is not described.
7. The trial is described as double-blind, but participants received **maximum-tolerated active stimulation while awake preoperatively and postoperatively**, while controls received no current.
8. Therefore participant blinding is questionable and subjective VAS outcomes are vulnerable to expectation/performance bias.
9. The TEAS protocol is unusually intensive and **multiphase**, combining preoperative, intraoperative, and four postoperative sessions.
10. Acupoints change across treatment phases:
    - ST36/SP6 pre/intraoperatively
    - LR3/PC6 postoperatively.
11. The study provides a valid quantitative **intraoperative remifentanil-sparing outcome**.
12. Remifentanil was approximately **15.5% lower** in the pTEAS group.
13. There is **no quantitative continuous postoperative 24-h opioid endpoint**.
14. All patients received standardized end-of-surgery **tramadol 2 mg/kg + fentanyl 1 µg/kg**.
15. Pain was significantly lower only on POD1 and POD2, not POD3-POD5.
16. PONV benefit was delayed, becoming significant only on **POD5**.
17. Table 4 shows significant dizziness reductions on **POD1, POD4 and POD5**, while the abstract mentions only POD1 and POD5.
18. Table 4 contains denominator/percentage inconsistencies in the pTEAS “PPI and G” rows.
19. pTEAS was also associated with significantly less blood loss, but this secondary result should be interpreted cautiously in a modest single-center sample.
20. Sample-size justification is incompletely reported.

# 32. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Wu et al. 2022** randomized **90 adults undergoing major posterior lumbar decompression, fusion, and internal fixation surgery** to perioperative TEAS or control. Six participants were excluded after randomization, leaving **44 pTEAS and 40 control patients** for analysis.

The pTEAS protocol was intensive and phase-specific:
- **30 min preoperatively:** bilateral **ST36 + SP6**, **2/15 Hz**
- **throughout surgery:** bilateral **ST36 + SP6**, **2/100 Hz**
- **POD1-POD4:** bilateral **LR3 + PC6**, **2/15 Hz for 30 min once daily**

A **HANS LH-202H** delivered alternating dense-disperse stimulation at maximum tolerated intensity. Controls had identical electrode placement but **no electrical output**.

The strongest opioid-related finding was intraoperative remifentanil:
- pTEAS: **1383 ± 494 µg**
- control: **1637 ± 630 µg**
- **P=0.042**
- calculated relative reduction: **15.5%**

The normalized remifentanil index was also lower:
- **0.084 ± 0.018 vs 0.114 ± 0.090 µg/min/kg, P=0.045**

Postoperative pain was lower with pTEAS on:
- **POD1:** 1.77 ± 0.91 vs 2.25 ± 0.71, P=0.009
- **POD2:** 1.55 ± 0.59 vs 2.00 ± 0.91, P=0.007

No significant pain difference remained on POD3-POD5.

For the review's strict **24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint**, this study is **not usable from the main article**. Both groups received standardized **tramadol 2 mg/kg + fentanyl 1 µg/kg at the end of surgery**, but the paper does not report cumulative postoperative opioid consumption during the first 24 h.

Other notable findings:
- blood loss: **379 ± 202 vs 497 ± 283 mL, P=0.030**
- POD5 PONV: **0 vs 6/40 (15%), P=0.009**
- dizziness lower with pTEAS on **POD1, POD4 and POD5**

The major consensus-audit concerns are the **90-to-84 complete-case analysis, asymmetric control-group attrition, retrospective trial registration, questionable participant blinding despite the “double-blind” label, absence of a 24-h postoperative opioid endpoint, and internal inconsistencies in Table 4 medication percentages and the abstract's dizziness summary**.
