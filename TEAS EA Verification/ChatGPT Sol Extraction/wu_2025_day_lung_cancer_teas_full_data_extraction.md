# 25. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **100 randomized and all 100 retained in the stated ITT analysis**, despite 3 patients lacking 24-h direct follow-up.
2. The exact method used to handle those 3 missing 24-h values is **not explained**.
3. Randomization was computer-generated by an independent party, but the precise allocation-concealment mechanism is incompletely described.
4. Active TEAS was delivered during residual anesthesia to improve blinding, but the authors acknowledge that active electrical sensations could still reveal allocation.
5. Sham TEAS delivered **no current**, so sensory equivalence was incomplete.
6. The study used a strong co-intervention: **thoracic paravertebral block**, which reduces early opioid need and affects generalizability.
7. The paper shows a very large effect on subjective pain outcomes, including **12% vs 74% moderate-to-severe pain**, which should be interpreted with the blinding limitation in mind.
8. The study demonstrates substantial reduction in **non-opioid rescue analgesic use**, especially parecoxib and compound paracetamol.
9. It does **not demonstrate reduced oxycodone/acetaminophen use**: 0.3 vs 0.3, P=0.864.
10. The exact oxycodone strength is not reported, so **24-h MME cannot be calculated**.
11. There was no significant intraoperative sufentanil or remifentanil reduction.
12. Acupoint reporting is internally inconsistent:
    - LI4 in the abstract
    - LI14 in Methods
    - PC6 + ST36 in Discussion.
13. The protocol itself most consistently supports **LI4 + PC6**.
14. The article uses “PONV” broadly, but the quantitative table reports **vomiting** rather than clearly separated nausea and vomiting outcomes.
15. No significant vomiting or rescue-antiemetic reduction was demonstrated.
16. QoR-15 improvement was large (**+14.9 points calculated**).
17. Length of stay did not improve despite the large pain/QoR effect.
18. Single-center trial with short follow-up and day-surgery population.
19. The article itself notes that the short 30-min intervention and ≤24-h efficacy window limit long-term conclusions.
20. Main article refers readers to **Supplementary Material 1** for further anesthetic/analgesic details, so rescue-drug formulations may be recoverable from supplement rather than author contact.

# 27. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Wu et al. 2025** randomized **100 adults undergoing ambulatory VATS pulmonary wedge resection for lung cancer** to one 30-min session of active TEAS or sham TEAS immediately after arrival in PACU. All patients received standardized general anesthesia and an **ultrasound-guided thoracic paravertebral block**, and routine postoperative PCA was not used.

The intervention is most consistently described as **bilateral LI4 + PC6**, using a **HANS-200A**, **2/10-Hz stimulation**, and **12-20 mA for 30 min** during residual anesthesia. The article contains important acupoint-reporting errors: LI4 is printed as “LI14” in the Methods, and the Discussion later incorrectly/inconsistently states that PC6 + ST36 were selected.

TEAS produced large reductions in postoperative pain:
- **1 h:** 0.9 ± 1.2 vs 1.8 ± 1.6
- **12 h:** 2.0 ± 1.1 vs 5.4 ± 1.6
- **24 h:** 1.7 ± 0.9 vs 4.0 ± 1.4
- **VAS >4 within 12 h:** 12% vs 74%

Rescue analgesic use within 24 h was:
- compound paracetamol: **0.3 ± 0.5 vs 0.8 ± 0.9**, P=0.001
- oxycodone + acetaminophen: **0.3 ± 0.5 vs 0.3 ± 0.6**, P=0.864
- parecoxib: **4.8 ± 13.1 vs 11.2 ± 18.1 mg**, P=0.046

For the opioid-sparing review, this study **does not provide a usable continuous 24-h opioid/MME endpoint**. Oxycodone-containing rescue medication was used, but the paper reports only tablet/dose counts and does not state the oxycodone strength per tablet. Importantly, **oxycodone/acetaminophen use itself was not reduced**.

Intraoperative opioids were also not significantly different:
- sufentanil: **24.2 ± 9.5 vs 24.3 ± 10.9 µg**
- remifentanil: **489.4 ± 226.0 vs 533.8 ± 263.6 µg**

QoR-15 at 24 h strongly favored TEAS:
- **130.8 ± 9.0 vs 115.9 ± 12.2**

Vomiting, rescue metoclopramide use, length of stay, and hospitalization costs were not significantly different.

The major consensus-audit issues are the **unexplained handling of 3 missing 24-h observations in the ITT analysis, incomplete sensory blinding, strong TPVB co-intervention, internally inconsistent acupoint reporting, unspecified oxycodone-tablet strength, and absence of a calculable 24-h MME endpoint**.
