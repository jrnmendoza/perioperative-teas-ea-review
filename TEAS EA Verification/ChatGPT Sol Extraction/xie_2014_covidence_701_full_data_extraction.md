# 16. Important internal arithmetic concern in sufentanil regimen

If the abstract's basal infusion of **2.25 µg/h** were administered continuously for 48 h:
- 7.5 µg loading + 2.25 × 48 h = **115.5 µg**

This is strikingly close to the EAS group's reported total mean of **115 µg**, even before adding any successful patient-controlled boluses.

At the same time, Table 2 reports approximately **16 patient demands** in EAS.

This creates uncertainty about:
- whether “patient demand” means attempts rather than delivered doses,
- whether the total dose includes all programmed basal and bolus doses,
- whether the observation window was shorter/different,
- or whether one of the infusion-rate/time descriptions is inaccurate.

### Extraction decision
Do not reconstruct dose from the PCA settings. Use the **directly published total sufentanil value** and flag the regimen arithmetic for author clarification.

# 29. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **All 60 randomized patients were analyzed**, with no reported attrition.
2. Randomization used computer-generated numbers, but **allocation concealment is not reported**.
3. Outcome/data-analysis personnel were blinded.
4. Participant blinding is unclear, especially because active EAS produces an electrical sensation while sham involves needles without current.
5. Sham EA is better than no-treatment control but does not fully reproduce the electrical sensory component.
6. The trial specifically targets **remifentanil-associated breakthrough pain/hyperalgesia** after thoracic esophagectomy.
7. There was **no intraoperative remifentanil reduction**.
8. There was a clear **postoperative sufentanil-sparing effect**.
9. PCA demand frequency and dezocine rescue use also strongly favored EAS.
10. The exact time window for “total sufentanil dose” is **not clearly defined in Table 2**.
11. The Methods say infusion continued to **POD3**, while the Discussion emphasizes reduced sufentanil through **48 h**.
12. The basal infusion is internally inconsistent: **2.25 µg/h in the abstract vs 2.5 µg/h in Methods**.
13. The PCA-regimen arithmetic does not cleanly reconcile with the reported total dose and demand counts.
14. Therefore the published total sufentanil should be extracted directly, but **not relabeled as exact 24-h consumption**.
15. Exact VAS means/SDs are graph-only.
16. The induction TCI target units appear unusual and may be typographical.
17. No trial registration is reported.
18. Sample-size calculation is not reported.
19. Small single-study sample: 20 participants per arm.
20. Fisher LSD post hoc testing provides relatively limited multiplicity control.

# 31. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Xie et al. 2014 (Study #701)** randomized **60 ASA III patients undergoing radical thoracic esophagectomy** to general anesthesia alone, sham EA, or active electroacupuncture stimulation, with **20 patients per group and no reported attrition**. fileciteturn49file0

Active EAS used **ipsilateral PC4 (Ximen) and PC6 (Neiguan)**, invasive needles, a **HANS-200**, and **2/20-Hz disperse-dense stimulation**. Treatment began **30 min before induction** and continued **throughout surgery**. Sham patients received needles at the same points but no electrical stimulation.

Intraoperative remifentanil consumption was virtually identical:
- control: **1.90 ± 0.65 mg**
- sham: **1.90 ± 0.59 mg**
- EAS: **1.89 ± 0.63 mg**

The main opioid-sparing effect occurred postoperatively. Total sufentanil was:
- control: **134 ± 5.9 µg**
- sham: **133 ± 7.0 µg**
- EAS: **115 ± 6.0 µg**

Thus EAS reduced reported total sufentanil by about **13.5% vs sham** and **14.2% vs control**.

PCA demand was also markedly lower:
- **42 ± 5**
- **43 ± 3**
- **16 ± 3**

and dezocine rescue was:
- **35%**
- **30%**
- **5%**

respectively.

Pain VAS at **2, 12, 24 and 48 h** was significantly lower with EAS, although exact means/SDs are shown only graphically.

For the review's strict **24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint**, caution is required. The paper does **not clearly state that the Table 2 total sufentanil represents exactly the first 24 h**. The Methods state that infusion continued to POD3, while the Discussion discusses opioid use through 48 h. There is also an internal infusion-rate discrepancy (**2.25 vs 2.5 µg/h**) and the PCA-demand counts do not readily reconcile with the reported total dose.

Therefore this study should be coded as showing a **strong postoperative sufentanil-sparing signal**, but **not as a clean exact 24-h opioid/MME datapoint without clarification**.
