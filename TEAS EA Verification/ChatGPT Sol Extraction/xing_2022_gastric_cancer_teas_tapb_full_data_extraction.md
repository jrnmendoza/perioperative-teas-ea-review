# 32. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **90 randomized, 87 analyzed**; no full ITT analysis is reported.
2. The Randomization section incorrectly states **1:1:1:1** despite only three groups.
3. Allocation concealment is reasonably described via sealed envelopes and an independent nurse.
4. The study is labeled double-blind, but the authors acknowledge that pre-induction active TEAS may have compromised participant blinding.
5. The design is useful because **NTG vs NG isolates the incremental effect of TEAS on top of TAPB**.
6. Group G also underwent a sham saline TAP block, which improves procedural comparability.
7. The trial demonstrates a strong **intraoperative remifentanil-sparing effect**, including a statistically significant additional reduction with TEAS beyond TAPB.
8. Intraoperative propofol was not significantly reduced.
9. Postoperative PCIA press counts were reduced, but **actual delivered sufentanil dose was not reported**.
10. Therefore press counts should **not be converted directly into opioid consumption** without confirming whether they represent successful deliveries.
11. The article's PCIA wording about **no background vs continuous infusion** is internally awkward/contradictory.
12. Exact 24-h postoperative opioid consumption and MME are unavailable.
13. Early pain improvement through 24 h was strongly driven by TAPB; the clear incremental TEAS-vs-TAPB pain difference appears at **48 h**.
14. PONV and first-flatus outcomes favored TEAS + TAPB over TAPB alone.
15. QoR-15 improvements with NTG over NG on POD3/POD7 exceeded the study's prespecified 8-point clinically meaningful threshold descriptively.
16. Immune T-cell outcomes consistently favored NTG on POD1/POD3.
17. The hemodynamic Results prose contains an internal contradiction regarding significance at **T1**.
18. Single-center trial with modest sample size.
19. Elderly gastric-cancer population limits generalizability to younger patients and other surgeries.
20. The study evaluates a **combined TEAS + regional block strategy**, so results should not be attributed to TEAS alone when comparing NTG with G.

# 34. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Xing et al. 2022** randomized **90 elderly patients undergoing laparoscopic gastric cancer surgery** into:
- general anesthesia + sham saline TAPB + sham/no-current TEAS,
- active TAPB + sham/no-current TEAS,
- active TAPB + active TEAS.

Three patients were lost/excluded after randomization, leaving **29 per group (87 total)** for analysis. fileciteturn50file0

Active TEAS was delivered at **bilateral LI4, PC6 and ST36** using an **SDZ-V** with **2/100-Hz stimulation**, starting **30 min before anesthesia and continuing until the end of surgery**. Both NG and NTG received bilateral TAPB with **40 mL 0.375% ropivacaine**; group G received saline TAPB.

The strongest opioid-related result was intraoperative remifentanil:
- G: **1619.13 ± 328.98 µg**
- NG: **1415.41 ± 295.67 µg**
- NTG: **1182.61 ± 253.61 µg**

Thus active TEAS added to TAPB reduced remifentanil by approximately **16.4% vs TAPB alone** and **27.0% vs general anesthesia/sham block**.

All patients received postoperative **sufentanil PCIA for 2 days** using 0.5 µg/mL solution, 2-mL boluses and a 15-min lockout. PCIA press numbers were:
- G: **12.45 ± 4.21**
- NG: **9.97 ± 3.80**
- NTG: **6.55 ± 3.19**

However, the paper does **not report the actual delivered sufentanil dose**, and a pump press may not equal a successful bolus. Therefore the study **cannot provide a clean continuous 24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint** from the main article.

At 24 h, VAS was:
- rest: **1.83 ± 0.55 vs 1.40 ± 0.52 vs 1.18 ± 0.42**
- movement: **2.84 ± 0.56 vs 2.15 ± 0.50 vs 1.89 ± 0.52**

Both TAPB groups were lower than G, but the table does not show a significant NTG-vs-NG difference at 24 h. At **48 h**, NTG was significantly lower than both G and NG for rest and movement pain, suggesting a more prolonged incremental TEAS effect.

Other benefits of TEAS + TAPB included:
- PONV: **17.24% vs 37.93% with TAPB alone vs 44.82% with G**
- first flatus: **32.57 ± 6.94 vs 36.83 ± 6.19 vs 43.03 ± 8.13 h**
- POD3 QoR-15: **116.38 ± 10.82 vs 100.55 ± 10.06 vs 96.55 ± 10.51**
- POD7 QoR-15: **130.66 ± 10.20 vs 116.97 ± 11.01 vs 112.62 ± 10.67**

The main consensus-audit issues are the **1:1:1:1 randomization typo, 90-to-87 complete-case analysis, imperfect participant blinding, ambiguous PCIA basal-infusion wording, lack of actual postoperative sufentanil consumption, and contradictory hemodynamic significance statements at T1**.
