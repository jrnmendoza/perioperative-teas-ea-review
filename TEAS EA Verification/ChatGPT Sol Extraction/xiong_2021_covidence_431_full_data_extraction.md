# 24. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **All 62 randomized participants were analyzed**, with no post-randomization attrition.
2. Randomization used a computerized generator and sealed envelopes.
3. Participants and postoperative assessors were blinded, but anesthetists and the acupuncturist were not.
4. The no-current sham was visually masked, but active postoperative TEAS could produce tingling and potentially reveal treatment allocation.
5. The authors acknowledge this blinding limitation.
6. The trial enrolled a highly selected **young, female, obese, high-PONV-risk** population undergoing one bariatric procedure.
7. TEAS was a **repeated perioperative protocol**, not a single preoperative session.
8. The principal benefit was antiemetic, not analgesic.
9. There was **no significant difference in intraoperative opioid-equivalent consumption**.
10. There was **no significant difference in reported postoperative opioid-equivalent consumption**.
11. The postoperative opioid variable is useful but its **time window is not clearly defined**, preventing confident classification as a 24-h endpoint.
12. Exact 24-h MME therefore cannot be assigned from the main publication.
13. PCIA included a fixed **sufentanil background infusion**, which reduces interpretability of patient-driven opioid demand as a treatment effect.
14. Pain scores did not differ significantly, despite markedly lower PONV.
15. The published RR column appears internally inconsistent with some crude event ratios; raw event counts should be prioritized.
16. The Methods' ASA eligibility wording conflicts with the actual ASA II/III/IV enrollment.
17. One TEAS patient had transient electrode-site itching.
18. The study used dual pharmacologic antiemetic prophylaxis in both groups, so TEAS represents an **adjunctive effect** beyond dexamethasone + tropisetron.

# 26. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Xiong et al. 2021 (Study #431)** randomized **62 female patients undergoing laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy** to perioperative TEAS plus dexamethasone/tropisetron or dexamethasone/tropisetron alone, with **31 patients per group and no attrition**. fileciteturn51file0

TEAS was delivered at **bilateral PC6 and ST36** using a **Hwato SDZ-V** with **2/10-Hz dense-disperse stimulation**, beginning about **30 min before induction**, continuing intraoperatively, and repeated at **2 h and 6 h postoperatively**.

All patients received:
- **dexamethasone 10 mg IV**
- **tropisetron 4 mg IV**
- parecoxib and local ropivacaine
- postoperative **sufentanil patient-controlled analgesia** with **1.5 µg/h background infusion, 1-µg bolus and 20-min lockout**.

The trial showed a strong antiemetic effect:
- **0-24 h PONV:** 38.7% vs 77.4%, P=0.002
- **0-48 h PONV:** 41.9% vs 77.4%, P=0.004
- **0-48 h vomiting:** 35.5% vs 67.7%, P=0.011
- **clinically important PONV:** 16.1% vs 48.4%, P=0.007
- **rescue antiemetic use:** 29.0% vs 58.1%, P=0.021

TEAS also accelerated first flatus:
- **23.6 ± 7.7 vs 32.3 ± 13.2 h, P=0.003**

However, there was **no opioid-sparing effect** in the published opioid-equivalent measures:
- intraoperative: **1.8 ± 0.4 vs 1.8 ± 0.4 mg morphine equivalent/kg, P=0.795**
- postoperative: **0.6 ± 0.2 vs 0.6 ± 0.1 mg morphine equivalent/kg, P=0.908**
