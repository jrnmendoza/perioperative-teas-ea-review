# 27. Major methodological / audit issues

1. This was explicitly a **feasibility study**, not a fully powered efficacy trial.
2. **59 patients were randomized but only 57 analyzed**.
3. There was **no intention-to-treat analysis** retaining all randomized participants.
4. One randomized EA patient was excluded because PVB was not administered.
5. One randomized UC participant withdrew before surgery.
6. Randomization used an SPSS-generated sequence and sealed envelope, but the concealment process is not described in the same detail as later Yang et al. trials.
7. Patients and acupuncturists were **not blinded**.
8. No sham-acupuncture control was used.
9. Assessors, anesthetists, and statisticians were blinded.
10. The main efficacy signal was gastrointestinal:
    - faster flatus
    - faster defecation.
11. There was **no significant effect on PONV or pain**.
12. The study included a strong multimodal analgesic background:
    - paravertebral block
    - morphine/ketamine PCA
    - celecoxib.
13. The paper does **not report actual morphine consumption**, despite describing the PCA regimen.
14. Therefore the study cannot contribute a continuous 24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint.
15. The study's later 2024 definitive RCT used essentially the same EA concept but a larger sample; this feasibility paper should be treated as a distinct earlier trial unless overlap is demonstrated.
16. The EA protocol is repeated but **not intraoperative**.
17. The Methods print a potentially unusual **midazolam 0.5 mg/kg** induction dose.
18. The Methods report a relatively shallow **3-5 mm acupuncture depth**.
19. The out-of-bed activity P-value appears potentially inconsistent with the printed group means/SDs.
20. Exact adverse-event counts are not provided.

# 29. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Yang et al. 2020 (Study #464)** was a **single-center randomized open-label feasibility trial** in patients undergoing VATS lobectomy or segmentectomy. Sixty patients were screened, 59 randomized (**30 EA, 29 usual care**), and **57 analyzed (29 EA, 28 control)**. fileciteturn53file0

EA was delivered at **PC6, ST36, and ST37**, using invasive needles, **15-Hz stimulation**, and **3-5 mA** for **30 min** per session. Treatment was given:
1. **24 h before surgery**
2. **4 h after surgery**
3. **24 h after surgery**

All patients received standardized multimodal analgesia including:
- thoracic paravertebral block with **20 mL 0.375% ropivacaine**
- morphine/ketamine PCA
- oral celecoxib.

The main positive findings were gastrointestinal:
- time to first flatus: **20.8 ± 4.6 vs 24.1 ± 6.2 h, P=0.026**
- time to first defecation: **53.9 ± 6.0 vs 57.5 ± 7.2 h, P=0.046**

There was **no significant difference** in:
- abdominal distension
- PONV
- static or dynamic pain
- time to out-of-bed activity
- hospital stay.

For the opioid-sparing review, this study **does not provide a usable 24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint**. Although the PCA protocol contained **morphine 1 mg/kg in a 100-mL reservoir with a 2-mL/h background infusion and 1-mL bolus**, the article does not report actual morphine delivered or cumulative consumption by group.

The major audit issues are the **59-to-57 complete-case analysis, absence of sham EA, open-label participant treatment, no direct postoperative opioid-consumption endpoint, unusual printed midazolam dose, and potentially inconsistent out-of-bed activity P-value**.
