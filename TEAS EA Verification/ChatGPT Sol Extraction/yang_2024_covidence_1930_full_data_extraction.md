# 28. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **180 randomized and all 180 retained in the ITT analysis**, a major strength.
2. Three participants did not fully complete the assigned protocol, but were appropriately retained:
   - one stopped EA,
   - one lacked PVB,
   - one converted to open surgery.
3. Randomization and allocation concealment were reasonably strong: computer-generated codes + sequentially numbered opaque envelopes.
4. The study was **open-label for participants and acupuncturists**.
5. There was **no sham-EA control**, so placebo/expectancy effects cannot be excluded for subjective gastrointestinal and symptom outcomes.
6. Anesthetists, assessors, data collectors, and statisticians were blinded.
7. The intervention consisted of **three separated perioperative sessions**, not continuous intraoperative EA.
8. The 4-h postoperative session occurred within the main first-24-h efficacy window.
9. All patients received a strong multimodal analgesic regimen including:
   - paravertebral block,
   - celecoxib,
   - parecoxib,
   - morphine/ketamine PCA.
10. This likely reduced the ability to demonstrate incremental analgesic/opioid-sparing effects.
11. Importantly, the study provides a **direct exact first-24-h morphine consumption endpoint**.
12. The 24-h morphine result is essentially identical between groups and should be coded as a **null opioid-sparing result**.
13. The 72-h morphine result is likewise null.
14. Pain scores were low in both groups and showed no significant treatment effect.
15. Despite no opioid or pain reduction, EA significantly improved:
   - first flatus,
   - first defecation,
   - abdominal distension incidence,
   - first-24-h nausea,
   - first-24-h vomiting.
16. Therefore gastrointestinal/PONV benefit cannot be explained simply by lower morphine consumption in this trial.
17. Rescue-medication identity in Table 2 is not explicitly stated.
18. One EA patient discontinued because of discomfort.
19. Single-center study.
20. Short 72-h postoperative follow-up limits conclusions about chronic constipation or chronic pain.

# 30. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Yang et al. 2024 (Study #1930)** randomized **180 adults undergoing VATS lobectomy or segmentectomy** to perioperative electroacupuncture plus usual care or usual care alone, with **90 participants per group**. Three patients had protocol deviations, but **all 180 were retained in the intention-to-treat analysis**.

EA used invasive needling at **bilateral PC6, ST36, and ST37**, with **15-Hz stimulation** for **30 min per session**, delivered:
1. **24 h before surgery**
2. **4 h after surgery**
3. **24 h after surgery**

The comparator received usual care without sham acupuncture. All patients received multimodal analgesia including a **paravertebral block, parecoxib, celecoxib, and IV PCA containing morphine + ketamine**.

This study provides a particularly clean negative datapoint for the review's **24-hour postoperative opioid-sparing endpoint**:

- **EA: 43.7 ± 4.7 mg morphine**
- **UC: 44.0 ± 4.9 mg morphine**
- mean difference **-0.3 mg**
- **P=0.733**
- relative reduction only approximately **0.7%**

The Methods explicitly state that postoperative opioid use for the **first 24 h** was extracted from the PCA device records. Therefore, unlike studies reporting only pump presses or unclear postoperative time windows, this endpoint is **directly usable for continuous 24-h postoperative morphine consumption meta-analysis**.

The 72-h cumulative morphine result was also null:
- **127.0 ± 12.0 vs 127.5 ± 12.5 mg, P=0.785**

Likewise, postoperative pain did not differ significantly at rest or with movement.

The principal effects of EA were instead gastrointestinal and antiemetic:
- first flatus: **15.4 ± 3.2 vs 17.0 ± 3.7 h, P=0.004**
- first defecation: **75.9 ± 7.9 vs 79.7 ± 8.1 h, P=0.002**
- abdominal distension: **34.4% vs 51.1%, P=0.024**
- 0-24 h nausea: **26.7% vs 44.4%, P=0.013**
- 0-24 h vomiting: **13.3% vs 27.8%, P=0.016**

The key interpretation for your review is that **EA improved gastrointestinal recovery and PONV despite essentially identical 24-h and 72-h morphine consumption**. This makes the study particularly informative when examining whether EA benefits are mediated by opioid sparing versus other mechanisms.
