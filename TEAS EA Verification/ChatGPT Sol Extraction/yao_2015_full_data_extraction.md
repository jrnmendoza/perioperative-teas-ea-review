Although every rescue dose was weight-based at 0.05 µg/kg, the study provides:
- group mean weight,

# 24. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **74 randomized but only 71 analyzed** because of 3 protocol breaches.
2. No full ITT analysis is reported.
3. Randomization and allocation concealment were otherwise strong: computer-generated sequence + sequentially numbered opaque envelopes.
4. The study is described as double-blind and lists patients, anesthesiologists, surgeons, nurses, collectors, and the statistician as blinded.
5. However, active TEAS caused perceptible stimulation and slight muscle twitching while control received no current; formal blinding success was not tested/reported.
6. The intervention was **one preoperative 30-min session only**.
7. The study provides a clear opioid-sparing signal in terms of **fewer rescue sufentanil administrations**.
8. It does **not provide total sufentanil mass or a continuous 24-h opioid dose**.
9. Therefore the rescue-count outcome should not be converted to MME using group mean weight and median rescue counts.
10. Pain values are graph-only and should not be entered as exact values without digitization.
11. The global QoR-40 difference was **11.7 points**, exceeding the prespecified 10-point clinically relevant threshold descriptively.
12. TEAS also reduced nausea, vomiting, dizziness, and PACU stay despite routine tropisetron prophylaxis.
13. The trial is single-center and restricted to relatively young ASA I-II women undergoing short gynecologic laparoscopic procedures.
14. Previous acupuncture experience was excluded, which may affect generalizability.
15. Device-specific TEAS safety events are not clearly reported.

# 26. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Yao et al. 2015** randomized **74 women undergoing elective gynecological laparoscopic surgery** to one 30-min session of preoperative TEAS or no-current sham control. Three patients were excluded because of protocol breaches, leaving **35 TEAS and 36 control patients** for analysis.

TEAS was delivered at **bilateral LI4, PC6, ST36, and SP6** using a **HANS-100B**, **2/10-Hz dense-disperse stimulation**, and **6-9 mA**, beginning **30 min before anesthesia induction**. The control group was connected to the same apparatus but received no electrical current.

The main 24-h recovery endpoint strongly favored TEAS:
- **QoR-40: 176.5 ± 10.2 vs 164.8 ± 14.7, P<0.001**
- calculated mean difference: **+11.7 points**

Postoperative analgesia also favored TEAS. Rescue analgesia consisted of **IV sufentanil 0.05 µg/kg whenever VAS ≥4**. During the first 24 h:
- time to first rescue: **59 (31-1440) vs 47 (13-196) min, P=0.039**
- cumulative rescue administrations: **1 (1-3) vs 3.5 (2-7.8), P=0.004**

This is a genuine **opioid-sparing signal**, but the paper reports **number of rescue administrations rather than total sufentanil dose**. Because the rescue dose was weight-based and the count outcome is reported as median/IQR, a valid continuous 24-h opioid dose or MME **cannot be reconstructed from the aggregate data**.

Other 24-h outcomes favored TEAS:
- nausea: **48.6% vs 72.2%, P=0.041**
- vomiting: **20.0% vs 52.8%, P=0.004**
- dizziness: **40.0% vs 77.8%, P=0.001**
- PACU stay: **27.8 ± 8.3 vs 35.7 ± 7.2 min, P<0.001**
- satisfaction score ≥8: **51.4% vs 16.7%, P=0.002**

For your review, this study should therefore be coded as showing **reduced postoperative rescue-opioid frequency**, but **not as providing a continuous 24-h opioid/MME endpoint** unless the authors can supply total sufentanil consumption.
