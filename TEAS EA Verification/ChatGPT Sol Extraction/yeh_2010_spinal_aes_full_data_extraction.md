# 25. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **99 randomized/entered but only 94 analyzed**; five patients were excluded because of unstable conditions.
2. The article does not report the post-randomization exclusions by treatment arm.
3. Full ITT analysis was not performed.
4. Random sequence generation is not described in enough detail.
5. Only one researcher knew treatment allocation; patients, nurses and physicians were blinded.
6. The sham comparator is methodologically strong because it appears to reproduce **electrical stimulation at nonacupoint locations**.
7. The abstract's phrase “sham acupoint manually” conflicts with the detailed Methods, which describe the same electrical procedure at sham points.
8. The opioid endpoint is a **direct exact 24-h morphine dose**.
9. However, the PCA route is reported as **epidural**, creating a major conversion/interpretation issue.
10. True AES reduced 24-h morphine significantly versus no-treatment control, but **not clearly versus sham**.
11. The more rigorous acupoint-specific contrast is therefore **true AES vs electrical sham**, not true AES vs untreated control.
12. The study had a significant baseline imbalance in **marital status**.
13. The authors did not find significant PONV benefit.
14. The study is single-center.
15. The most important review-level issue is **probable duplicate/overlapping publication with Yeh et al. 2011 / Study #828**.
16. The near-identical morphine data make double-counting especially hazardous.
17. The differing stimulation timings and PCA routes between the two reports require clarification rather than assuming they are independent studies.

# 27. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Yeh et al. 2010** evaluated acupoint electrical stimulation after lumbar spinal surgery using a three-arm randomized design. Ninety-nine participants entered the study; five did not complete because of unstable conditions, leaving **94 analyzed: 33 true AES, 30 sham AES, and 31 no-intervention controls**.

True AES was delivered bilaterally at **BL40, GB34, HT7, and PC6** using **2/100-Hz dense-disperse stimulation**, **0.25-ms pulses**, and **4-7 mA** for **20 min per session**. The reported schedule was:
1. **1 h before surgery**
2. **1 h after return to the ward**
3. **2 h after return to the ward**

The detailed Methods state that sham participants received the **same electrical procedure at points 2 cm away from the true acupoints and outside meridians**, making this a useful sensory-matched sham comparison.

The study reports a direct exact **first-24-h morphine endpoint**:
- true AES: **18.6 ± 9.7 mg**
- sham: **21.6 ± 13.1 mg**
- control: **27.2 ± 12.5 mg**
- overall **P=0.02**

True AES reduced morphine by:
- **31.6% vs no-treatment control**, significant by Scheffé post hoc (**P=0.02**)
- **13.9% vs sham**, with no significant pairwise difference reported.

PCA button pushes were also lower:
- **25.3 ± 28.5**
- **47.4 ± 39.7**
- **74.7 ± 85.5**

but time to first PCA request did not differ.

Pain outcomes favored true AES over both sham and control, while **PONV did not differ significantly**.

For your systematic review, the most important issue is that this publication appears **highly likely to overlap with Yeh et al. 2011 / Study #828**. The two papers have nearly identical authors, population, acupoints, stimulation parameters, three-arm design, and 24-h morphine results, but report different sample sizes, stimulation timing, and PCA route. **Do not include both as independent studies until the overlap is adjudicated.**

A second major issue is that this 2010 paper describes the morphine PCA as **epidural**. Therefore, although the 24-h morphine amount is exact, I would **not convert it to standard systemic MME until the PCA route is confirmed**.
