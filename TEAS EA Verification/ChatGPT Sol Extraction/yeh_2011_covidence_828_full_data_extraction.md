# 21. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **90 randomized and all 90 analyzed**, with no attrition.
2. Computer-generated randomization was used.
3. Allocation concealment was weaker: the article explicitly states assignments were sequential **without sealed envelopes**.
4. The trial is described as **single-blind**, yet it also states both participants and medical staff did not know group allocation.
5. Researchers performed both AES interventions and data collection, which may introduce some risk despite stated blinding of patients/staff.
6. The sham comparator is a major strength: it delivered **active electrical stimulation at nonacupoints**, controlling for sensory stimulation.
7. Therefore **AES vs sham** is the preferred contrast for isolating acupoint-specific efficacy.
8. The study provides an **exact direct first-24-h morphine endpoint** suitable for continuous opioid meta-analysis.
9. True AES significantly reduced morphine versus **no-treatment control**, but the paper does not report significant AES-vs-sham opioid reduction.
10. In contrast, true AES significantly reduced 24-h worst/average pain versus both sham and control.
11. The no-treatment control exaggerates the total treatment effect relative to a sensory-matched sham comparison.
12. The table appears to have **body weight and body height labels reversed**.
13. The printed **fentanyl 4.3 mg/kg** anesthesia dose is highly unusual and likely requires verification.
14. The printed **0.25 min pulse duration** is also unusual terminology and may represent a typographical or unit error.
15. PCA button pushes greatly exceeded delivered morphine dose in some patients/groups, which is plausible because unsuccessful presses during lockout may be counted.
16. No biochemical mechanistic outcomes were collected.
17. Single-center study.
18. Follow-up was limited to the first 24 h for the main opioid and BPI outcomes.

# 23. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Yeh et al. 2011 (Study #828)** randomized **90 adults undergoing lumbar spine surgery** to:
- true acupoint electrical stimulation,
- sensory-matched sham electrical stimulation at nonacupoints,
- no AES control,

with **30 participants per arm and no attrition**.

True AES was delivered at **BL40, GB34, HT7, and PC6** using noninvasive electrical stimulation with a **2/100-Hz dense-disperse waveform** and **4-7 mA**, for **20 min in the third postoperative hour and another 20 min in the fourth postoperative hour**.

The sham group received the **same type of electrical stimulation 2 cm away from the true acupoints**, making this a strong placebo/sensory-control design.

All patients received IV morphine PCA:
- **1-mg bolus**
- **10-min lockout**
- first-24-h opioid use directly measured.

The exact 24-h morphine endpoint was:
- **AES: 19.3 ± 9.7 mg**
- **Sham: 21.6 ± 13.1 mg**
- **Control: 28.0 ± 12.1 mg**
- overall **P=0.017**

Post hoc testing showed:
- **AES vs control: P=0.023**
- approximately **31.1% lower morphine use**
- the article does **not report a significant AES-vs-sham morphine difference**.

PCA button pushes were:
- **24.9 ± 28.5**
- **47.4 ± 39.7**
- **76.9 ± 86.1**
respectively, with overall **P=0.004**.

At 24 h, true AES also reduced:
- worst pain: **5.4 ± 1.6 vs 6.5 ± 1.6 vs 6.5 ± 1.7**
- average pain: **2.1 ± 1.3 vs 3.0 ± 1.4 vs 3.0 ± 1.4**

For your opioid-sparing review, this is a **directly usable exact 24-h morphine-consumption study**. However, if your primary contrast is meant to isolate the specific therapeutic effect beyond placebo/sensory stimulation, the most rigorous comparison is **AES vs sham**, where the morphine reduction was only about **10.6%** and was **not reported as statistically significant**. The larger **31% reduction vs no-treatment control** includes both specific and nonspecific treatment effects.
