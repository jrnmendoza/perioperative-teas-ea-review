# 7. Major protocol discrepancy — Figure 3 vs Methods

The **study-procedure diagram on page 5** gives a materially different TEAS protocol from the abstract and detailed Methods.

## Figure 3 states:
- Acupoints: **GV20, ST36, EX-HN3, PC6, KI3**
- Frequency: **2/10 Hz**
- Intensity: **6-9 mA**
- Duration: **30 min**

## Abstract / detailed Methods state:
- Acupoints: **GV20, EX-HN3, ST36, PC6**
- Frequency: **2/100 Hz**
- Intensity: **12-15 mA**
- Duration: **30 min**

### Extraction decision
For primary protocol coding, the **detailed Methods and abstract support GV20 + EX-HN3 + ST36 + PC6, 2/100 Hz, 12-15 mA**.

However, the discrepancy is substantial enough that these fields should be marked **“requires verification”** before final consensus:
1. whether **KI3** was actually stimulated,
2. whether frequency was **2/100 Hz or 2/10 Hz**,
3. whether intensity was **12-15 mA or 6-9 mA**.

Do **not** silently merge the two protocols.

# 18. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **All 60 randomized patients were analyzed**, with no attrition.
2. Computer-generated randomization was used.
3. Allocation concealment is incompletely described; a nurse knew allocation and informed the acupuncturist immediately before treatment.
4. Participants, anesthesiologists, surgeons, PACU physicians, and the data collector were described as blinded.
5. The acupuncturist was necessarily unblinded.
6. The no-current sham may have imperfect sensory equivalence because active TEAS produced muscle twitching.
7. **The trial was retrospectively registered** on 2 December 2015, after recruitment ended in November 2014.
8. The intervention protocol contains **major internal contradictions**:
   - Methods/abstract: 4 points, 2/100 Hz, 12-15 mA
   - Figure 3: adds KI3, 2/10 Hz, 6-9 mA.
9. Therefore the stimulation protocol should be marked for **author/registry verification before final consensus**.
10. The Results prose incorrectly says postoperative MMSE was lower with TEAS; the published numbers and conclusion show it was higher.
11. The POD2 VAS result is internally inconsistent:
   - prose **P=0.26**
   - table/abstract **P<0.05**
   - TEAS SD **0.98 in table vs 0.88 in abstract**.
12. The study shows substantially fewer patients requiring postoperative rescue sufentanil.
13. However, **no dose of sufentanil and no cumulative opioid amount is reported**.
14. Therefore it is not usable for a continuous 24-h postoperative opioid/MME analysis.
15. Group-specific intraoperative remifentanil consumption is also not reported.
16. The study provides strong binary PONV and rescue-antiemetic effects.
17. The authors explicitly acknowledge an unusual data-quality limitation: **no external monitoring or auditing was planned because of economic restrictions**, and they state that they cannot prove the data are error-free.
18. The article itself recommends reproduction in a future trial with stronger planning, monitoring, auditing, and reporting.
19. Sample size is small (**30/group**) and single-center.
20. Exact gynecological procedure types are not reported in the main article.

# 19. Data-integrity / monitoring statement

The Discussion contains an unusually explicit limitation:

- external monitoring and auditing were **not planned because of economic restrictions**
- the authors state that they **cannot prove that the data are free from errors**

This should be retained as a **study-level audit concern**. It does not by itself prove that the results are invalid, but it materially increases the importance of verifying the internal inconsistencies before using protocol-sensitive variables in meta-regression.

# 22. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Yu et al. 2020** randomized **60 ASA I-II patients undergoing elective gynecological laparoscopic surgery** to a single 30-min preoperative TEAS session or a no-current electrode control, with **30 patients per arm and no attrition**. fileciteturn57file0

The detailed Methods describe TEAS at **GV20, EX-HN3, ST36 and PC6** using a **Hwato SDZ-V**, dense-disperse **2/100-Hz stimulation at 12-15 mA for 30 min before anesthesia**. However, **Figure 3 on page 5 gives a conflicting protocol**: it adds **KI3**, gives **2/10 Hz**, and gives **6-9 mA**. These stimulation variables should therefore be treated as unresolved until checked against the trial registry or authors.

The study does **not** provide a continuous 24-h postoperative opioid dose. It reports only whether patients required rescue sufentanil during the first 24 h:
- control: **24/30 (80.0%)**
- TEAS: **13/30 (43.3%)**
- **P<0.01**

This corresponds to a calculated:
- RR ≈ **0.54**
- absolute risk reduction ≈ **36.7%**
- NNT ≈ **2.7**

Thus there is a clear **binary opioid-rescue reduction**, but the sufentanil dose, number of administrations, cumulative dose, and 24-h MME are not reported.

PONV and rescue antiemetic therapy also favored TEAS:
- PONV: **23.3% vs 56.7%, P<0.01**
- rescue tropisetron: **23.3% vs 53.3%, P<0.02**

QoR-40 and MMSE were higher with TEAS on postoperative days 1 and 2. Resting pain on POD1 was lower with TEAS, while the POD2 pain result is internally inconsistent: the Results prose gives **P=0.26**, whereas Table 2 and the abstract mark it significant.

The other major audit issues are the **retrospective trial registration**, the erroneous Results statement that MMSE was lower with TEAS despite the numeric values showing the opposite, and the authors' explicit acknowledgment that **no external monitoring/auditing was performed and that they could not guarantee the data were free from errors**.

For your opioid-sparing review, code this study as:
- **binary 24-h postoperative rescue-opioid reduction: YES**
- **continuous 24-h opioid/MME endpoint: NO**
- **protocol characteristics: requires adjudication because of Figure 3 vs Methods discrepancies**
