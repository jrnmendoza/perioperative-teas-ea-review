# 9. Comparator / sham structure

There was **no sham TEAS arm**.

The two comparison groups were:
- TAP + PCIA
- PCIA alone

### Risk-of-bias implication
Because patients receiving TEAS were not given a sensory-matched sham comparator, subjective outcomes such as VAS pain may be influenced by:
- expectation,
- additional staff contact,
- nonspecific stimulation effects.

The **TAP + TEAS vs TAP** comparison remains randomized, but not placebo-controlled for TEAS.

# 25. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **90 randomized and all 90 analyzed**, with no attrition.
2. Computer-generated randomization was used.
3. Allocation concealment is incompletely described.
4. The paper is called **single blind**, but the exact identity of the blinded party is not completely clear.
5. Treating-anesthetist blinding is described in a way that is difficult to reconcile with actually delivering TAP and TEAS.
6. There is **no sham TEAS group**.
7. Therefore subjective pain outcomes may include nonspecific treatment effects.
8. The most appropriate TEAS-specific contrast is **TAP + TEAS vs TAP**, because both receive the same regional block and PCIA.
9. TEAS was **postoperative only**, with sessions before PACU discharge and at 24 h.
10. TAP + TEAS was superior to TAP alone for pain at **24 h and 48 h**, each P=0.03.
11. The pain table uses unusual notation that is not clearly defined.
12. All groups received sufentanil PCIA for 48 h.
13. The study **does not report actual opioid consumption**, even though the Discussion claims TEAS may reduce narcotic use.
14. Theoretical programmed basal sufentanil exposure should **not** be used as observed consumption.
15. Therefore the trial cannot contribute a continuous 24-h opioid/MME endpoint.
16. TAP + TEAS consistently reduced postoperative dizziness and nausea/vomiting compared with usual care and, for several time points, compared with TAP alone.
17. TAP-block ropivacaine volume is ambiguously worded.
18. Sample is modest: **30 participants/arm**.
19. Surgeries are heterogeneous across gastrectomy and colectomy.
20. Exact opioid-demand and rescue-analgesia data would materially improve interpretation of the analgesic mechanism.

# 27. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Zhan & Tian 2020 / Study #1389** randomized **90 adults undergoing open abdominal surgery** to:
- bilateral TAP block + PCIA,
- bilateral TAP block + postoperative TEAS + PCIA,
- PCIA-only usual care,

with **30 participants per group and no post-randomization attrition**. fileciteturn61file0

TEAS was applied at **ST36 (Zusanli) and PC6 (Neiguan)** using a **KD2B** stimulator with **2-100-Hz sparse-dense stimulation**, titrated to a **strong but comfortable level**. Each session lasted **30 min**, with treatment given:
1. **before leaving PACU**, after the TAP block
2. **24 h after surgery**

Thus this is a **postoperative-only TEAS protocol**.

For the most rigorous TEAS-specific comparison, use **TAP + TEAS vs TAP**, because both groups received the same regional block and PCIA.

Pain outcomes favored TEAS at the later time points:
- **24 h: TAP + TEAS vs TAP, P=0.03**
- **48 h: TAP + TEAS vs TAP, P=0.03**

The analgesic advantage persisted at 48 h even though TAP alone was no longer significantly better than usual care, supporting a prolonged incremental TEAS effect.

All groups received IV PCIA containing:
- **100 µg sufentanil + 6 mg tropisetron in 100 mL**
- basal infusion **2 mL/h**
- duration **48 h**.

However, the paper does **not report actual sufentanil consumption, bolus settings, or PCA demand counts**. Therefore this trial **cannot provide a valid continuous 24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint**. The theoretical basal exposure must not be treated as measured consumption.

Postoperative adverse events strongly favored TAP + TEAS. At 24 h:
- dizziness: **6.7% vs 33.3% TAP vs 46.7% usual care**
- nausea/vomiting: **10.0% vs 26.7% vs 43.3%**
- total adverse reactions: **13.3% vs 40.0% vs 60.0%**

The main audit issues are the **absence of a sham-TEAS comparator, unclear blinding description, ambiguous TAP ropivacaine volume, unusual VAS table notation, and lack of any measured opioid-consumption endpoint**.
