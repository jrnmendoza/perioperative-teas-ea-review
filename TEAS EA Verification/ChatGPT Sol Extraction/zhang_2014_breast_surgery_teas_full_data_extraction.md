# 3. Major participant-flow discrepancy

The paper contains an important inconsistency:

### Abstract
States that:
- **72 women were randomly allocated** to TEAS or sham.

### Results and Figure 2 flowchart on page 4
Show:
- **72 assessed for eligibility**
- **7 excluded before randomization**
  - 4 did not meet inclusion criteria
  - 3 declined participation
- **65 randomized**
  - TEAS: **33**
  - sham: **32**
- all 65 completed the study and were analyzed.

### Extraction decision
Use the **flowchart denominator of 65 randomized / 65 analyzed**, because it provides the detailed allocation numbers and matches Tables 1-5.

# 24. Major methodological / audit issues

1. The **abstract says 72 women were randomized**, whereas the detailed **flowchart shows only 65 randomized** after 7 pre-randomization exclusions.
2. Use **65 randomized / 65 analyzed** for extraction unless external trial-registry data establish otherwise.
3. No post-randomization attrition is shown among the 65 randomized participants.
4. Random-number-table sequence generation was used.
5. Allocation lists were concealed from anesthesiologists and statisticians, but the exact concealment procedure before allocation is not fully described.
6. Participant and recovery-room staff blinding were intended.
7. The acupuncturist was not blinded, but did not participate in data acquisition or analysis.
8. The sham used the same electrodes at the same points but **no electrical current**, so sensory equivalence may be imperfect.
9. The TEAS protocol itself is clearly reported and internally consistent: **LI4 + PC6 + ST36, 2/10 Hz, 6-9 mA, 30 min preoperatively**.
10. The study demonstrates a strong **intraoperative remifentanil-sparing effect** of approximately one third.
11. Propofol consumption did not differ.
12. Postoperative pain was lower through 24 h.
13. However, **no postoperative opioid-consumption endpoint is reported**.
14. Therefore postoperative pain benefit cannot be translated into 24-h MME from this article.
15. Nausea, vomiting, dizziness, and pruritus were all significantly reduced.
16. No stress-hormone differences were found, supporting comparable surgical stress despite lower remifentanil.
17. Single-center study restricted to relatively young women having cosmetic ambulatory breast procedures.
18. The primary outcome was recovery-room stay, not opioid consumption.
19. Pain data are figure-only and should not be entered as exact continuous values without digitization.
20. TEAS-specific skin/device adverse events were not separately reported.

# 26. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Zhang et al. 2014** evaluated a single **30-min preoperative TEAS** treatment in women undergoing ambulatory cosmetic breast surgery. The detailed flowchart shows **72 patients assessed, 7 excluded before randomization, and 65 randomized/analyzed: 33 TEAS and 32 sham**, although the abstract incorrectly or inconsistently states that all 72 were randomized. fileciteturn58file0

TEAS was delivered at **bilateral LI4, PC6, and ST36** using a **Hwato SDZ-V**, with **2/10-Hz dense-disperse stimulation at 6-9 mA**, titrated to slight muscle twitching. The sham group had the same electrode placement but received no current.

The most important opioid result was **intraoperative remifentanil consumption**:
- TEAS: **0.06 ± 0.02 µg/kg/min**
- sham: **0.09 ± 0.03 µg/kg/min**
- **P=0.01**
- calculated relative reduction: approximately **33.3%**

The authors describe this as an almost 35% reduction. Propofol consumption was unchanged.

Clinical recovery also favored TEAS:
- recovery-room stay: **35.6 ± 12.9 vs 48.3 ± 16.3 min**
- LMA removal: **10.2 ± 2.5 vs 17.8 ± 4.4 min**
- reorientation: **14.6 ± 3.2 vs 26.5 ± 5.0 min**
- all **P=0.01**

Pain was significantly lower with TEAS at all displayed times through 24 h, but the exact values are graph-only.

Postoperative side effects were also lower:
- dizziness: **18.2% vs 53.1%**
- nausea: **21.2% vs 46.9%**
- vomiting: **12.1% vs 34.4%**
- pruritus: **6.1% vs 25.0%**

For your opioid-sparing review, this study is **useful for intraoperative remifentanil sparing**, but **not for a continuous 24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint**, because no postoperative opioid or rescue-analgesic consumption is reported.
