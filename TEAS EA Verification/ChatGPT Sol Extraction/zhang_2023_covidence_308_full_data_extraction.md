# 5. TEAS intervention

The protocol is **phase-specific**, clarified by **Figure 1 on page 3**.

## Operating-room phase
- **Bilateral BL32 (Ciliao) + bilateral SP6 (Sanyinjiao)**
- started **30 min before induction**
- continued **through the operation until surgery ended**

## PACU phase
- **CV3 (Zhongji) + CV4 (Guanyuan)**
- stimulation continued in PACU for **at least 45 min**

## Electrical parameters
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Device | **Hwato SDZ-IV** |
| Manufacturer | Suzhou Medical Appliances |
| Frequency | **2 Hz** |
| Waveform | **Sparse wave** |
| Intensity | Increased to strongest tolerable **needle-like tingling** |
| Exact mA | **NR** |
| Practitioner | Registered TCM practitioner, **8 years' experience** |

### Review classification
**Preoperative + intraoperative + immediate postoperative TEAS**, with acupoints changing by phase.

# 6. Sham comparator

- Same acupoints/apparatus.
- Output wires were **cut**, so no current reached the patient.
- Power indicator and device sound remained active.
- Patients were told they might or might not feel tingling.
- Formal blinding-success testing: **NR**.

This is a visually/acoustically credible sham, though active TEAS produced a distinctive tingling sensation.

# 22. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **1,948 randomized but only 1,838 analyzed**; no full ITT analysis.
2. Randomization/concealment were strong.
3. Blinding was strong except for the acupuncturist.
4. Active tingling may still have compromised sensory blinding.
5. TEAS was a **multiphase perioperative intervention**, not a single-session protocol.
6. Acupoints changed between OR and PACU.
7. Only **2-Hz sparse-wave** stimulation was used.
8. Main benefit was urinary recovery, not opioid sparing.
9. Intraoperative opioid-equivalent medians were identical.
10. 48-h postoperative opioid-equivalent medians were identical.
11. Exact 24-h opioid consumption is unavailable.
12. Opioids were converted using **ClinCalc**, which may not match the systematic review's prespecified conversion method.
13. The abstract reports sham first-void time as **7.0 h** vs **7.1 h** in Table 2.
14. Single-center design.
15. PCIA dose/programming details are incompletely reported.

# 24. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Zhang et al. 2023 / Study #308** randomized **1,948 patients undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy** to active or sham TEAS; **1,838 were analyzed (922 vs 916)**.

TEAS was phase-specific:
- bilateral **BL32 + SP6** from 30 min before induction through the operation,
- **CV3 + CV4** in PACU for at least 45 min,
- **Hwato SDZ-IV**, **2-Hz sparse wave**, intensity to maximum tolerated tingling.

For opioid sparing:

- intraoperative opioid exposure: **30 (25-35) vs 30 (25-35) mg IV morphine equivalents**
- postoperative opioid exposure within 48 h: **110 (80-110) vs 110 (90-110) mg IV morphine equivalents**

Therefore, the paper shows **no clear opioid-sparing signal**. It does **not** provide a separate exact first-24-h opioid endpoint, so it should not enter a strict 24-h continuous opioid meta-analysis. It can potentially contribute a **48-h null opioid-equivalent outcome** if that time point is analyzed.

The primary urinary outcomes favored TEAS:
- first spontaneous void: **5.6 vs 7.1 h, P<0.001**
- delayed urinary recovery: **11.2% vs 18.9%, P<0.001**
- POUR: **8.1% vs 11.4%, P=0.02**
- voiding difficulty: **28.3% vs 45.1%, P<0.001**

Pain was modestly lower (**4 vs 5, P=0.007**) despite essentially unchanged 48-h opioid exposure.
