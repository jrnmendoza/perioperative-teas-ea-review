# 34. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **88 randomized but 85 analyzed** using mITT rather than full ITT.
2. Two TEAS patients and one control patient were removed after randomization because of post-randomization protocol criteria.
3. Random sequence generation and allocation concealment were clearly described.
4. Participants, assessors, and data analysts were blinded.
5. Intervention providers were unblinded.
6. The source is inconsistent about whether the provider was a licensed acupuncturist or an anesthesiologist.
7. Sham involved identical electrodes with **no current**, so active muscle twitching could potentially reveal allocation.
8. Both groups received bilateral TAP blocks, allowing a clean incremental TEAS comparison on top of regional analgesia.
9. The study provides a strong **direct intraoperative remifentanil-sparing endpoint**: approximately **19.5% lower**.
10. Postoperative PCIA total and effective demand counts were also lower with TEAS.
11. However, **actual postoperative sufentanil dose cannot be calculated**, because PCIA concentration/final volume is missing.
12. Therefore this trial cannot contribute a continuous 24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint from the main publication.
13. The 24-h NRS P-value is internally inconsistent: **0.042 in prose vs 0.422 in Table 4**.
14. The TEAS myomectomy percentage is mathematically inconsistent with the numerator.
15. The text incorrectly says better postoperative sleep was associated with **higher PSQI**, while the scale and data show **lower PSQI is better**.
16. The paper repeatedly refers to **QoR-30** in the Discussion/Conclusion despite clearly using **QoR-40**.
17. The 48-h QoR-40 difference was statistically significant but **below the stated MCID**.
18. PONV reduction was clinically substantial and was concentrated primarily in the first 2 postoperative hours.
19. Gastrointestinal recovery was significantly faster.
20. Single-center, relatively small mITT sample.

# 36. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Zheng et al. 2025** randomized **88 patients undergoing gynecological laparoscopic surgery** to active or sham TEAS; **85 were included in the modified ITT analysis (42 TEAS, 43 control)**.

Active TEAS used **bilateral PC6, ST36 and LI4** with a **Huatuo SDZ-V**, **2/100-Hz sparse-dense stimulation**, starting at **6 mA** and titrated to maximum tolerable intensity up to **15 mA**. Stimulation began **30 min before induction and continued until the end of anesthesia**. Sham participants had identical electrodes but no current.

Importantly, **both groups also received bilateral TAP blocks with 15 mL 0.375% ropivacaine per side**, so the randomized contrast estimates the incremental benefit of TEAS on top of regional analgesia.

The strongest opioid result was intraoperative remifentanil:
- **TEAS: 233.1 ± 29.6 µg**
- **Control: 289.5 ± 37.9 µg**
- **P=0.031**
- calculated relative reduction: approximately **19.5%**

Postoperatively, TEAS also reduced PCIA demand:
- total PCIA presses: **6.3 ± 2.2 vs 10.7 ± 3.9, P=0.004**
- effective PCIA presses: **3.8 ± 1.0 vs 4.8 ± 1.6, P=0.039**

However, the PCIA contained **sufentanil 2 µg/kg in an unspecified final saline volume**, with a 2-mL/h basal rate, 0.5-mL bolus and 15-min lockout. Because the final concentration is not reported, **actual postoperative sufentanil dose and 24-h MME cannot be calculated**. This study therefore provides a strong postoperative opioid-demand signal but **not a continuous 24-h opioid-consumption endpoint**.

The primary PONV endpoint also favored TEAS:
- **42.9% vs 67.4%, P=0.023**
- calculated RR ≈ **0.64**
- ARR **24.5 percentage points**
- NNT ≈ **4.1**

The benefit was concentrated in early postoperative nausea:
- 0-2 h nausea: **23.8% vs 48.8%, P=0.013**

Other benefits included:
- first flatus: **24.2 ± 2.5 vs 31.6 ± 4.4 h, P=0.003**
- first borborygmus: **19.2 ± 3.3 vs 27.6 ± 2.7 h, P=0.035**
- 48-h QoR-40: **184 vs 181, P<0.001**, although the improvement was below the stated MCID.

The major audit issues are:
- **88 randomized / 85 mITT analyzed**
- missing PCIA concentration and actual postoperative sufentanil consumption
- **24-h NRS P=0.042 in prose vs P=0.422 in Table 4**
- incorrect myomectomy percentage in the TEAS baseline table
- “higher PSQI” wording despite lower scores being better
- repeated erroneous **QoR-30** terminology despite use of QoR-40
- inconsistent description of whether TEAS was delivered by an acupuncturist or anesthesiologist.
