# 15. Opioid-equivalence conversion method

The Methods state:

**pethidine : pentazocine : dezocine : morphine  
= 100 mg : 30-60 mg : 10 mg : 10 mg**

Table 3 footnote instead uses:

**100 mg : 45 mg : 10 mg : 10 mg**

### Extraction interpretation
The table appears to use **45 mg pentazocine** as an intermediate value within the 30-60 mg range described in the Methods.

### Major review issue
The authors explicitly acknowledge that:
- pentazocine equivalence ranges from **30-60 mg**
- a clear equivalent dose for their population was not established.

Therefore the reported morphine-equivalent variable has **conversion-method uncertainty**.

### Review coding recommendation
Preserve the authors' native morphine-equivalent values, but do not assume they are directly interchangeable with a prespecified external MME conversion algorithm without sensitivity analysis.

# 28. Major methodological / audit issues

1. The trial was **prospectively registered** before recruitment.
2. **82 randomized and 81 analyzed**; one control patient withdrew after pulmonary infection.
3. Random sequence generation is reported, but **allocation concealment is not clearly described**.
4. The trial was explicitly **unblinded**.
5. There was **no sham TEAS**, making subjective pain and comfort outcomes vulnerable to expectation/performance bias.
6. TEAS was **postoperative only**, unlike many perioperative/preoperative protocols.
7. Treatment was intensive: **6 sessions over POD1-3**.
8. The study provides a strong reduction in the **proportion of patients receiving any opioid**.
9. The reported morphine-equivalent mean was ~30% lower, but the comparison was **not statistically significant**.
10. The exact time window for morphine-equivalent consumption is **not stated**, so it cannot be treated as a strict 24-h endpoint.
11. The authors' morphine-equivalence conversion is uncertain, especially for **pentazocine**.
12. Methods give pentazocine equivalence as **30-60 mg**, while Table 3 uses **45 mg**.
13. The paper itself acknowledges uncertainty in pentazocine conversion.
14. The abstract reverses the direction of multiple mechanistic biomarkers compared with Table 5 and Results.
15. Table/Results therefore should be prioritized over the abstract for biomarkers.
16. The Discussion says average pain decreased by at least 20%, but the published average values imply about **17.7%**.
17. The Discussion describes the ~3.9-mg morphine-equivalent reduction as “nearly half,” whereas the calculated relative reduction is about **30%**.
18. Patients requiring PCA were excluded, limiting generalizability to opioid-heavy postoperative analgesic protocols.
19. Patients developing severe postoperative complications could be withdrawn, potentially biasing recovery outcomes toward uncomplicated cases.
20. Single-center sample with only **81 analyzed patients**.

# 30. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Zhou et al. 2021** randomized **82 patients undergoing laparoscopic or robotic radical gastrectomy for gastric cancer** to postoperative TEAS or usual ERAS care; **81 were analyzed (41 TEAS, 40 control)** after one control patient withdrew because of pulmonary infection.

TEAS was delivered at **LI4, PC6, BL21, BL27, ST36, and ST37** using a **Huatuo SDZ-II** with **20/100-Hz alternating stimulation**. Treatment lasted **30 min twice daily at 08:00 and 16:00 on POD1-POD3**, for **six postoperative sessions in total**. There was no sham treatment and the trial was unblinded.

For opioid sparing, the strongest result was the proportion of patients requiring opioids:
- **TEAS: 18/41 (43.9%)**
- **Control: 30/40 (75.0%)**
- **P=0.004**
- calculated RR ≈ **0.59**
- ARR ≈ **31.1 percentage points**
- NNT ≈ **3.2**

The study also reports:
- TEAS: **8.935 ± 3.660 mg morphine equivalents**
- Control: **12.83 ± 8.097 mg**
- **P=0.182**
- calculated reduction ≈ **3.90 mg / 30.4%**

However, the exact postoperative time window for this morphine-equivalent total is **not clearly stated**, so it should **not be coded as a strict 24-h opioid/MME endpoint** without clarification. In addition, the morphine-equivalent conversion is uncertain because the Methods give pentazocine equivalence as a range of **30-60 mg**, while Table 3 uses **45 mg**.

Pain and gastrointestinal recovery favored TEAS:
- average POD1-POD5 pain: **2.55 ± 0.21 vs 3.10 ± 0.42, P<0.001**
- first flatus: **55.63 ± 16.74 vs 72.60 ± 20.92 h, P<0.001**
- first defecation: **72.20 ± 16.24 vs 95.78 ± 17.75 h, P<0.001**
- nausea frequency: **1.88 ± 1.09 vs 2.58 ± 0.77, P=0.029**

The most important source-quality issue is that the **abstract reverses the direction of the biomarker results**. Table 5 and the Results show **higher β-endorphin, IL-2 and motilin and lower 5-HT, IL-6 and TNF-α with TEAS**, while the abstract states the opposite.
