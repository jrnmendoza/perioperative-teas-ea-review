# 7. Important intensity-determination timing nuance

The intervention section states that:

> before anesthesia induction, the optimal intensity was determined by gradually increasing current until the patient experienced a strong but comfortable De Qi sensation.

The actual therapeutic TEAS session, however, was delivered only **after arrival in PACU**.

### Extraction decision
Code:
- **therapeutic intervention timing: PACU/post-anesthesia only**
- **preoperative intensity calibration/test: yes, according to the Methods**
- **preoperative therapeutic TEAS: no**

This distinction matters if protocol meta-regression separates *any preoperative electrical exposure* from the actual therapeutic treatment window.

# 25. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **100 randomized and 97 analyzed**, with only 3 post-randomization withdrawals.
2. Randomization and allocation concealment were clearly described.
3. Participants, anesthesiologists, surgeons, and data collectors were blinded.
4. The acupuncturist was unblinded but used a standardized interaction protocol.
5. Sham stimulation used identical devices/electrodes but **no current**.
6. Because active TEAS delivered **10-15 mA and De Qi**, sensory blinding may still have been imperfect.
7. The therapeutic TEAS treatment itself occurred **only during PACU recovery**, making this protocol distinct from preoperative/intraoperative TEAS studies.
8. However, the Methods state that stimulation intensity was **determined before anesthesia induction**, creating a protocol-classification nuance.
9. The primary outcome was recovery quality, not opioid consumption.
10. The study shows better POD1 QoR-15, lower pain, less POD1 PONV, and faster flatus/ambulation.
11. Rescue analgesia numerically favored TEAS but was **not statistically significant**.
12. Rescue analgesia was reported only over the **first 3 postoperative days**, not specifically at 24 h.
13. No cumulative tramadol dose is reported.
14. No group-specific intraoperative opioid consumption is reported.
15. Therefore the study cannot contribute a continuous first-24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint.
16. All patients received substantial multimodal analgesia:
    - flurbiprofen
    - sufentanil near end of surgery
    - incision-site ropivacaine.
17. The trial is single-center with a modest final sample of 97.
18. Follow-up was limited to POD3.
19. Patient expectations/beliefs regarding TEAS were not formally measured.
20. No local skin irritation occurred, supporting short-term procedural safety.

# 27. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Zhou et al. 2025** randomized **100 patients undergoing elective gynecological laparoscopic surgery** to active or sham TEAS; **97 completed follow-up and were analyzed (48 TEAS, 49 sham)**. The trial was prospectively registered and used computer randomization with sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes.

The therapeutic TEAS intervention was unusual in being delivered **only during the post-anesthesia recovery period in PACU**:
- bilateral **LI4, PC6, ST36, SP6**
- **Hwato SDZ-II**
- **2/10-Hz dense-disperse stimulation**
- **10-15 mA**
- strong but comfortable **De Qi**
- **30 min**
- one session.

The Methods state that each patient's optimal current intensity was determined **before anesthesia induction**, but the therapeutic session itself occurred in PACU. This should be retained as a protocol-classification nuance rather than automatically coding the study as preoperative TEAS.

The primary recovery outcome favored TEAS on POD1:
- global QoR-15: **112.00 ± 10.67 vs 107.62 ± 10.27, P=0.042**
- mean difference: **+4.38 points**

Pain also favored TEAS:
- POD1 resting VAS: **3 (3-4) vs 4 (4-5), P<0.001**
- POD1 exercise VAS: **4 (4-5) vs 5 (4-6), P=0.008**
- POD2 exercise VAS: **2 (2-3) vs 4 (3-4), P=0.036**

POD1 PONV was lower:
- **20.8% vs 38.7%, P=0.047**

Rescue antiemetic use over the first 3 days was also lower:
- **20.8% vs 40.8%, P=0.033**

For the opioid-sparing review, postoperative rescue analgesia consisted of **IV tramadol 50-100 mg every 6 h as needed**. The study reports only whether patients required rescue analgesia during the first 3 postoperative days:
- **6/48 (12.5%) TEAS**
- **13/49 (26.5%) sham**
- **P=0.124**

No cumulative tramadol dose, no POD1-specific rescue use, and no exact 0-24-h opioid dose are reported. Therefore this study **does not provide a continuous 24-h postoperative opioid/MME endpoint**.

The study is still useful for:
- postoperative pain outcomes,
- binary 3-day rescue-analgesic use,
- PONV,
- QoR-15,
- gastrointestinal/functional recovery.

The main audit issues are the absence of a strict 24-h opioid endpoint, lack of total rescue-tramadol dosing, no group-specific intraoperative opioid totals, and the unusual preoperative current-calibration wording despite a PACU-only therapeutic intervention.
