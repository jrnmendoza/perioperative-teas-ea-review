# 24. Major methodological / audit issues

1. **413 randomized, 400 per-protocol analyzed**.
2. The article additionally reports an **ITT PON/POV analysis in 405 patients who did not convert to open surgery**.
3. Randomization and sealed-envelope concealment were clearly described.
4. Anesthetists, surgeons, and outcome assessors were blinded.
5. There was **no sham EA comparator**, so participants were not blinded to active treatment versus usual care.
6. The study is strongest for **timing and PONV**, not opioid sparing.
7. All groups received modern multimodal antiemetic prophylaxis with **dexamethasone + tropisetron**.
8. The EA protocol used only **PC6 + ST36**, 20-100 Hz, 30 min.
9. No evidence that two EA sessions were superior to one.
10. The primary movement-pain outcome at 6-24 h was **not significant**.
11. Early 0-6 h pain was statistically lower with EA, but authors considered the difference **not clinically relevant** because pain was mild.
12. Intraoperative sufentanil and remifentanil exposure did **not differ** among groups.
13. Postoperative analgesic use was rare.
14. The postoperative rescue analgesic was **flurbiprofen, not an opioid**.
15. Therefore this study should **not be coded as having a postoperative opioid-consumption endpoint**.
16. Exact postoperative analgesic dosage consumed was not recorded; the authors explicitly note this as a limitation.
17. First-flatus time significantly favored EA.
18. QoR-15 did not differ.
19. Three Combined-group patients developed mild needling-site pain/itching; two declined the second EA session.
20. Table 1 contains an apparent surgery-duration typographical problem in Group 30 and Group Comb (**6.7 and 6.9 min as printed**).
21. The trial is single-center and limited to relatively short gynecological laparoscopic procedures.
22. The study was not powered to determine equivalence among EA timing schedules.

# 27. Concise Covidence-ready summary

**Zhu et al. 2022 / Study #381** randomized **413 women undergoing laparoscopic gynecological surgery** to four groups:

1. EA **the day before surgery**
2. EA **30 min before surgery**
3. EA at **both time points**
4. usual care without EA.

The final per-protocol analysis included **400 participants**:
- Pre **101**
- 30 min **98**
- Combined **100**
- Usual care **101**.

EA was delivered bilaterally at **PC6 and ST36** with **0.30 × 40-mm needles**, eliciting **de qi**, followed by **30 min of 20-100-Hz dense-disperse electrical stimulation** using an **XS-998B** device.

The main benefit was prevention of PONV despite multimodal antiemetic prophylaxis. At **6-24 h**:

### Nausea
- Pre: **11/101**
- 30 min: **18/98**
- Combined: **11/100**
- Usual: **27/101**
- **P=0.003**

### Vomiting
- Pre: **5/101**
- 30 min: **8/98**
- Combined: **9/100**
- Usual: **22/101**
- **P<0.001**

The primary movement-pain outcome at 6-24 h was **not significantly different**:
- **1.15 ± 1.02 / 1.12 ± 1.03 / 1.17 ± 1.17 / 1.44 ± 1.30**
- **P=0.179**

Early 0-6 h pain was lower overall with EA (**P=0.024**), but the authors judged the magnitude clinically unimportant because pain was mild.

For the opioid-sparing review, this trial **does not provide a postoperative opioid endpoint**. Intraoperative sufentanil and remifentanil were similar across groups, and postoperative rescue analgesia was **flurbiprofen 100 mg**, an NSAID. Only **2, 3, 1, and 3 patients**, respectively, required postoperative analgesia over 0-48 h, and no actual analgesic dose totals were recorded.

Therefore code:
- **intraoperative opioid sparing: NO**
- **postoperative opioid use endpoint: NO**
- **strict 24-h opioid/MME endpoint: NO**
- **postoperative non-opioid rescue analgesia: available only as binary counts**

EA also accelerated first flatus:
- **18.38 ± 6.11**
- **18.47 ± 6.47**
- **18.82 ± 7.50**
- **21.56 ± 8.07 h**
- **P=0.003**

The main audit issues are the lack of sham EA, the 413/405/400 denominator structure, no postoperative opioid-consumption data, and apparent surgery-duration typos in Table 1.
