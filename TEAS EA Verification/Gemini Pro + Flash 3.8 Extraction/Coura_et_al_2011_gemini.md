## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Not explicitly stated [cite: 11].
* **Country:** Brazil (Joinville, SC) [cite: 11].
* **Journal:** Acupuncture in Medicine (Acupunct Med) [cite: 11].
* **DOI:** 10.1136/aim.2010.003251 [cite: 11].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** Not reported [cite: 11].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Prospective, randomised and controlled study [cite: 11].
* **Randomization method:** Randomised into permutation groups (randomised in block) [cite: 11].
* **Blinding:** The anaesthesiologist, surgeon, patient, intensivist, and nursing staff did not know which group patients belonged to [cite: 11].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** Group 1 (treatment) and Group 2 (control) [cite: 11].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 32 patients were randomised, but 10 were excluded, leaving 22 included in the study (13 in the treatment group and 9 in the control group) [cite: 11].
* **Age:** Treatment group: 56.2 ± 11.8 years; Control group: 62.5 ± 10.8 years [cite: 11].
* **Sex distribution:** Treatment group: 10 Male / 3 Female; Control group: 4 Male / 5 Female [cite: 11].
* **BMI:** Not reported [cite: 11].
* **ASA classification:** Not reported [cite: 11].
* **Comorbidities:** Patients were excluded if they had significant haemodynamic instability, need of prolonged endotracheal intubation, significant changes in consciousness, or preoperative heart attack [cite: 11].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Elective conventional cardiac surgery, specifically myocardial revascularisation and valve replacement [cite: 11].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** General anesthesia was used, though specific procedures were not standardised and were under the anaesthesiologist's responsibility [cite: 11].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Preoperative electroacupuncture (EA) [cite: 11].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 13 patients analyzed [cite: 11].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Bilateral points LI4-LI11, LR3-ST36, and PC6-TE5 [cite: 11].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** 4 s alternating frequencies of 3 and 15 Hz using a typical biphasic waveform without a galvanic component [cite: 11].
* **Treatment intensity:** Power was determined according to individual maximum tolerance [cite: 11].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** 12-18 hours prior to the surgery (the day before) [cite: 11].
* **Duration of each treatment:** 30 minutes [cite: 11].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** Single session [cite: 11].
* **Total treatment period:** Single day (preoperative) [cite: 11].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** Postoperative patient-controlled analgesia (PCA) [cite: 11].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Sham transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS) [cite: 11].
* **Number of participants in control group:** 9 patients analyzed [cite: 11].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** Electrocardiogram electrode patches were applied to points LI4-LI11 and LR3-ST36 [cite: 11]. Electrical stimulation was applied with a 300 Hz frequency for 5 seconds, after which the device was turned off but remained connected for 30 minutes [cite: 11].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** PCA pump with continuous injection of 0.4 μg/kg/h fentanyl [cite: 11].
* **Dosage parameters:** Option of a 0.3 μg/kg bolus with a minimum 15-minute interval between boluses, up to a total limit of 30 ml in a 4-hour period [cite: 11].
* **Duration:** Measured and recorded for 24 hours from the start of fentanyl infusion [cite: 11].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Verbal pain scale (0 = no pain, 10 = worst possible) [cite: 11].
* **Pain assessment time points:** Every 2 hours post-extubation [cite: 11].
* **Mean/median pain score for each group:** Mean pain scale was 2.5 ± 1.1 in the treatment group and 4.0 ± 2.0 in the control group [cite: 11].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** Pain intensity evaluation levels differed significantly between the groups (p < 0.04) [cite: 11].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Fentanyl [cite: 11].
* **Route of administration:** Intravenous via PCA [cite: 11].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention group:** The average total dose of fentanyl was 13.1 ± 2.2 μg/kg, and the effective bolus dosage was 4.1 ± 2.0 μg/kg [cite: 11].
* **Opioid consumption for control group:** The average total dose of fentanyl was 16.3 ± 1.6 μg/kg, and the effective bolus dosage was 6.9 ± 1.7 μg/kg [cite: 11].
* **Measurement time point(s):** First 24 hours after operation [cite: 11].
* **Between-group difference:** There was a significant difference in both total fentanyl dosage (p < 0.002) and bolus dosage (p < 0.003) [cite: 11].
* **Percentage/relative reduction:** The average total dose of fentanyl was 20% lower in the treatment group, and the effective bolus self-administered was 41% lower in the treatment group [cite: 11].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** Measured via the number of effective boluses self-administered [cite: 11]. The number of boluses issued was 13.9 ± 7.0 in the treatment group and 24.8 ± 7.0 in the control group (p < 0.002) [cite: 11].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting:** Not specifically measured or reported [cite: 11].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** One patient in the control group was sufficiently drowsy for the anaesthetists to reduce the rate of PCA infusion; no side effects were reported in the treatment group [cite: 11].
* **Length of stay:** The average hospitalisation time was 8.5 ± 5.7 days in the treatment group and 10.7 ± 11.2 days in the control group (p = 0.55) [cite: 11].
