## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Not explicitly stated [cite: 13].
* **Country:** United Kingdom (Scunthorpe & Goole Hospitals, North Lincolnshire) [cite: 13].
* **Journal:** Acupuncture in Medicine (Acupunct Med) [cite: 13].
* **DOI:** 10.1136/aim.2008.000075 [cite: 13].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** Not reported [cite: 13].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Randomised, double-blind, comparative study (randomised controlled trial) [cite: 13].
* **Randomization method:** Computer-generated randomisation list using Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) [cite: 13].
* **Blinding:** Double-blind; adhesive dressings were placed over all acupuncture sites in all patients (both groups) so neither patients nor assessing staff knew the allocation [cite: 13]. The anaesthetist who delivered the acupuncture was not involved in the assessment [cite: 13].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** PCA + acupuncture group and PCA alone (control) group [cite: 13].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 107 randomised, 102 completed and were analysed [cite: 13]. Note: The text reports 58 analysed in PCA and 44 in PCA + acupuncture, while Table 1 reports 56 in PCA and 46 in PCA + acupuncture [cite: 13].
* **Age:** Not reported (baseline data was lost due to the statistician becoming ill) [cite: 13].
* **Sex distribution:** 92 females, 10 males [cite: 13].
* **BMI:** Not reported [cite: 13].
* **ASA classification:** ASA I or II [cite: 13].
* **Comorbidities:** Patients were excluded if pregnant, diagnosed with a coagulopathy, over the age of 70 years, or undergoing surgery additional to the intended operation [cite: 13].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy or abdominal hysterectomy [cite: 13].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** General anaesthesia [cite: 13].
* **Induction:** Propofol [cite: 13].
* **Maintenance:** Nitrous oxide 50-60%, oxygen 40-50%, and isoflurane 1-2%; tracheal intubation facilitated by a muscle relaxant (atracurium, vecuronium, rocuronium, or cisatracurium) [cite: 13].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Intraoperative electroacupuncture [cite: 13].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 46 (based on Table 1) [cite: 13].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Hysterectomy: GV2, GV4 (midline), BL32, BL23, LI4, PC6 (bilaterally) [cite: 13]. Laparoscopic cholecystectomy: LR3, SP6, LI4, PC6 (bilaterally) [cite: 13].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** 10 Hz [cite: 13].
* **Treatment intensity:** 7/10 on the intensity scale of the electroacupuncture unit (occasional muscle twitches were noticed) [cite: 13].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** Needles inserted after induction of anaesthesia and removed before the patient was transferred to the recovery room [cite: 13].
* **Duration of each treatment:** Applied for the duration of the operation (mean operation time 108.2 ± 65.8 minutes) [cite: 13].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** One single intraoperative session [cite: 13].
* **Total treatment period:** Single day (intraoperative) [cite: 13].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** Patient-controlled analgesia (PCA) using morphine sulphate in the postoperative period [cite: 13]. Intraoperative morphine (100 μg/kg) given just before skin incision [cite: 13].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** No additional treatment (PCA alone) with adhesive dressings placed to mimic the acupuncture group for blinding [cite: 13].
* **Number of participants in control group:** 56 (based on Table 1) [cite: 13].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** No needles were inserted; adhesive dressings were placed over the acupuncture points to ensure blinding [cite: 13].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** PCA pump with morphine sulphate [cite: 13].
* **Dosage parameters:** Concentration of 1 mg/ml, lock-out time of 5 minutes [cite: 13].
* **Duration:** During the first 24 h of postoperative care [cite: 13].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Four-point rating scale (0 = no pain, 1 = mild pain, 2 = moderate pain, 3 = severe pain) [cite: 13].
* **Pain assessment time points:** 1 h intervals for the first 4 h, 2 h intervals for the next 12 h, and 4 h intervals for the remaining 8 h [cite: 13].
* **Mean/median pain score for each group:** In the PCA + acupuncture group, 31 scored 0, 15 scored 1, 0 scored 2, and 0 scored 3 [cite: 13]. In the PCA group, 32 scored 0, 18 scored 1, 5 scored 2, and 1 scored 3 [cite: 13].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** The difference in pain scores was not statistically significant (p = 0.147) [cite: 13].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Morphine sulphate [cite: 13].
* **Route of administration:** Intravenous via PCA [cite: 13].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention group:** Total analgesia in first 4 h = 13.4 ± 7.5 mg; Total analgesia in 24 h = 35.3 ± 18.0 mg [cite: 13].
* **Opioid consumption for control group:** Total analgesia in first 4 h = 13.2 ± 8.6 mg; Total analgesia in 24 h = 36.9 ± 18.0 mg [cite: 13].
* **Measurement time point(s):** 4 h and 24 h [cite: 13].
* **Between-group difference:** There were no significant differences between the groups for the use of PCA (p = 0.675 at 4 h; p = 0.961 at 24 h) [cite: 13].
* **Percentage/relative reduction:** None [cite: 13].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** Addressed via the total mg of PCA morphine used; no significant difference in consumption was observed between groups [cite: 13].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting:** 89.1% of the PCA + acupuncture group had no PONV symptoms compared with 87.5% in the PCA alone group; the difference was not significant (p = 0.065) [cite: 13]. Standard droperidol (1 mg) was given intraoperatively as a baseline antiemetic [cite: 13].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** Sedation scores were not significantly different between the two groups (p = 0.574) [cite: 13]. Total time in recovery was 59.6 ± 16.8 (units listed as 'h' in Table 2, but likely minutes) for the intervention group and 59.0 ± 13.8 for the control group (p = 0.983) [cite: 13]. Time PCA in use was 60.1 ± 66.6 h in the acupuncture group and 47.0 ± 13.3 h in the control group (p = 0.459) [cite: 13].
* **Length of stay:** Not reported [cite: 13].
* **Other postoperative complications:** None explicitly reported [cite: 13].
