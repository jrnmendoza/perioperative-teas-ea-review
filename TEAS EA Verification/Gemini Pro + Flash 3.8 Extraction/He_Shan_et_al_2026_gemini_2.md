## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Funded by National Natural Science Foundation of China (No. 82293644, 82371476), and Institutional supporting project (2023LC2352, XJZT24LY15) [cite: 25].
* **Country:** China (Xi'an, Zhengzhou, Xining, Changsha) [cite: 25].
* **Journal:** Journal of Investigative Surgery [cite: 25].
* **DOI:** 10.1080/08941939.2026.2653912 [cite: 25].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** Clinical Trial.gov (NCT05396716) [cite: 25]. Approved by the Ethics Committee of the Xijing hospital (Ethics approval number: KY20222195-F-1) [cite: 28].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Multicenter, prospective, double-blind randomized clinical trial [cite: 25].
* **Randomization method:** Randomly allocated (1:1 ratio) through a secure web-based randomization system using permuted blocks, stratified by treatment center [cite: 25, 28].
* **Blinding:** Double-blinded; an independent investigator accessed the system to reveal group assignments and applied the intervention or sham [cite: 25]. The stimulator was placed inside an opaque box, preventing operating room personnel from identifying treatment allocation [cite: 25]. The patients, anesthesiologists, outcome assessors, and ward/statistical staff remained unaware of treatment assignments [cite: 25, 28].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** TEAS group and Control group [cite: 25].
* **Analysis sets:** The modified intention-to-treat (mITT) set included all randomly assigned participants who initiated the study intervention and did not withdraw consent [cite: 28]. The per-protocol (PP) set included patients who received randomization, consequent operation therapy, anesthetic agents, and completed the follow-up according to the study protocol [cite: 28].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 161 randomized; 159 included in the modified intention-to-treat (mITT) analysis (TEAS: n=80; Control: n=79) [cite: 25]. Per-protocol (PP) analysis included 76 patients in TEAS and 77 in Control [cite: 25, 29].
* **Age:** mITT: TEAS group: 52.3 ± 9.1 years; Control group: 54.3 ± 10.9 years [cite: 25]. PP: TEAS group: 52.1 ± 9.2 years; Control group: 54.3 ± 10.8 years [cite: 29].
* **Sex distribution:** mITT: TEAS group: 52 Male / 28 Female; Control group: 53 Male / 26 Female [cite: 25]. PP: TEAS group: 49 Male / 27 Female; Control group: 51 Male / 26 Female [cite: 29].
* **BMI:** mITT: TEAS group: 23.4 ± 3.3 kg/m²; Control group: 23.7 ± 4.9 kg/m² [cite: 25]. PP: TEAS group: 23.5 ± 3.4 kg/m²; Control group: 23.6 ± 4.9 kg/m² [cite: 29].
* **ASA classification:** The study enrolled patients with ASA I or II, and a small number of ASA III in the PP set (TEAS: n=3; Control: n=5) [cite: 25, 29]. Patients with ASA status > III were excluded [cite: 25].
* **Comorbidities:** Baseline diagnoses included liver cancer, bile duct cancer, hemangioma, bile duct stones, and liver echinococcosis [cite: 25]. Patients were excluded for drug or alcohol abuse, history of neurological or psychiatric disorders, or if they were pregnant/lactating [cite: 25].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Elective hepatectomy [cite: 25].
* **Surgical approach:** Included laparoscopic surgery (mITT TEAS: 39; mITT Control: 35) and open abdominal surgery (mITT TEAS: 41; mITT Control: 44) [cite: 25].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** General anesthesia [cite: 25].
* **Induction:** Midazolam (0.03-0.05 mg/kg), sufentanil (0.3-0.5 μg/kg), propofol (1.5-2.5 mg/kg), and rocuronium (0.6 mg/kg) [cite: 25].
* **Maintenance:** Sevoflurane (1-1.5 age-adjusted MAC), remifentanil infusion (0.1-0.3 μg/kg/min), and dexmedetomidine (0.4 μg/kg/h), with supplemental rocuronium boluses as required [cite: 25].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Intraoperative transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) [cite: 25].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 80 analyzed in mITT [cite: 25].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Bilateral PC6 acupoints [cite: 25].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** Alternating "disperse-dense" wave frequencies (2Hz and 10 Hz) in two cycles [cite: 25].
* **Treatment intensity:** Individually titrated to each patient's maximum tolerable threshold while maintaining the characteristic "De Qi" sensation [cite: 25].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** Administered from immediately after anesthesia induction until surgery completion [cite: 25].
* **Duration of each treatment:** Maintained continuously throughout the surgical procedure (mean length of surgery was 221.1 ± 97.6 min in the TEAS group) [cite: 25].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** One continuous intraoperative session [cite: 25].
* **Total treatment period:** Single day (intraoperative) [cite: 25].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** Preemptive analgesia with parecoxib sodium (40 mg IV) and dexamethasone (10 mg IV) prior to surgical incision; antiemetic prophylaxis with palonosetron (0.25 mg IV); and oxycodone (5 mg IV) prior to surgical completion [cite: 25]. Postoperative patient-controlled analgesia (PCA) was also provided [cite: 25].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Inactive stimulator [cite: 25].
* **Number of participants in control group:** 79 analyzed in mITT [cite: 25].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** Electrodes were placed similarly but remained inactive [cite: 25]. The intervention provider went through the motions of adjusting the stimulator settings but did not switch the device on [cite: 25].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** Intravenous PCA system programmed to deliver a solution containing sufentanil (100 μg) and oxycodone (20 mg) in normal saline [cite: 25].
* **Dosage parameters:** Background infusion rate of 0.015 ml/kg/h, bolus doses of 0.015 ml/kg, and a 10-minute lockout interval [cite: 25].
* **Duration:** Administered postoperatively (assessed up to 48h) [cite: 25].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Visual analogue scale (VAS) score (0-10 cm) [cite: 25, 28].
* **Pain at rest / movement:** Did not explicitly specify rest vs. movement for pain VAS [cite: 25].
* **Pain assessment time points:** During PACU stay, by 24h, and by 48h postoperatively [cite: 25].
* **Median pain score for each group (median [range]):**
    * PACU stay: TEAS = 1 (6); Control = 1 (7) [cite: 25].
    * 24h after surgery: TEAS = 3 (8); Control = 3 (9) [cite: 25].
    * 48h after surgery: TEAS = 2 (8); Control = 3 (8) [cite: 25].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** Analysis of pain VAS scores revealed no statistically significant intergroup differences at any postoperative assessment time point (P = 0.39, 0.34, 0.17 respectively) [cite: 25].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Sufentanil and remifentanil (intraoperatively), sufentanil and oxycodone (postoperatively via PCA) [cite: 25].
* **Route of administration:** Intravenous [cite: 25].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention group:** Intraoperative sufentanil = 32.1 ± 8.2 μg; Intraoperative remifentanil = 2.5 ± 1.4 mg [cite: 25]. Total postoperative opioid consumption in morphine equivalents (mITT): 20.0 ± 2.5 mg at 24h; 39.5 ± 4.0 mg at 48h [cite: 29].
* **Opioid consumption for control group:** Intraoperative sufentanil = 32.3 ± 7.2 μg; Intraoperative remifentanil = 2.8 ± 1.9 mg [cite: 25]. Total postoperative opioid consumption in morphine equivalents (mITT): 20.6 ± 4.5 mg at 24h; 40.4 ± 7.1 mg at 48h [cite: 29].
* **Between-group difference:** There was no significant difference in intraoperative opioid consumption [cite: 25]. Total opioid consumption on morphine equivalent by 24h (P = 0.30) and 48h (P = 0.32) after surgery were not significantly different between the two groups [cite: 29].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** Addressed via the number of PCA attempts and deliveries [cite: 25].
* **PCA attempts (24h):** TEAS group showed fewer total PCA attempts (2.7 ± 3.3) compared to the Control group (3.5 ± 6.1) (P = 0.02) [cite: 25]. 
* **Effective PCA deliveries (24h):** TEAS group showed fewer effective deliveries (2.6 ± 3.3) compared to the Control group (3.5 ± 6.0) (P = 0.02) [cite: 25].
* **PCA attempts/deliveries at 48h:** Not significantly different between the two groups [cite: 25].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting (Primary Outcome):** The incidence of PONV by 24h postoperatively was 23.8% in the TEAS group and 26.6% in the control group, showing no statistically significant difference (OR = 0.86, 95% CI: 0.42-1.76; p = 0.68) [cite: 25]. Nausea was assessed on a 4-point scale (0=no nausea to 3=persistent nausea at rest worsening with activity) and vomiting on a 4-point scale (0=no vomiting to 3=severe vomiting not relieved by intervention) [cite: 28]. For nausea VAS score during PACU stay, a statistically significant difference was detected favoring TEAS (median 0 vs 0; P = 0.046), but this lacked clinical relevance [cite: 25].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** The 15-item quality of recovery (QoR-15) score showed no statistically significant differences between the treatment arms by 24h and 48h postoperatively [cite: 25]. Time to flatus, time to oral intake, and time to ambulation after surgery identified no statistically significant differences between the two groups [cite: 25].
* **Length of stay:** Median time to discharge was 9 days for both the TEAS and control groups (Log-rank P = 0.35) [cite: 25].
* **Other postoperative complications:** Mortality at 6 months postoperatively was 2 (2.5%) in the TEAS group and 0 (0%) in the control group; the difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.1) [cite: 25].
