## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Supported by the Beijing Administration of Traditional Chinese Medicine, PR China (Grant No. JJ-2020-62) [cite: 41].
* **Country:** China [cite: 41].
* **Journal:** Journal of Integrative Medicine [cite: 41].
* **DOI:** 10.1016/j.joim.2021.01.005 [cite: 41].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** Chinese Clinical Trial Registry, ChiCTR1900023263 [cite: 41].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Single-blinded randomized controlled trial [cite: 41].
* **Randomization method:** Blocked randomization approach with a block length of four; computer-generated random numbers were used to determine the allocation of blocks [cite: 41].
* **Blinding:** Single-blinded; observers responsible for postoperative follow-up and participants were blinded to the grouping information [cite: 41]. The technicians who carried out acupuncture therapy and anesthesia knew the grouping information but did not participate in outcome measurements [cite: 41].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** TEAS group (group T) and sham group (group S) [cite: 41].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 311 assessed for eligibility; 306 randomized; 280 finally completed the entire trial and were analyzed (group T: n=140; group S: n=140) [cite: 41].
* **Age:** Group T: 60 ± 13 years; Group S: 62 ± 12 years (mean ± SD) [cite: 41].
* **Sex distribution:** Group T: 85 Male / 55 Female; Group S: 96 Male / 44 Female [cite: 41].
* **BMI:** Weight: Group T = 67 ± 12 kg; Group S = 66 ± 10 kg [cite: 41]. BMI inclusion criteria: between 18 kg/m² and 30 kg/m² [cite: 41].
* **ASA classification:** ASA Physical Status Grades I-III [cite: 41].
* **Comorbidities:** Hypertension (Group T: 39.3%; Group S: 34.3%), Anemia (Group T: 9.3%; Group S: 7.1%), and Previous abdominal surgery (Group T: 7.1%; Group S: 4.3%) [cite: 41].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Laparoscopic abdominal tumor resection (stomach, colon or rectum) [cite: 41]. Data stratified by gastric surgery (group T: n=50; group S: n=50) and colorectal surgery (group T: n=90; group S: n=90) [cite: 41].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** General anesthesia [cite: 41].
* **Induction:** Not explicitly detailed [cite: 41].
* **Maintenance:** Not explicitly detailed [cite: 41].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) [cite: 41].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 140 analyzed [cite: 41].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Bilateral Hegu (LI4), Neiguan (PC6), Zusanli (ST36), and Shangjuxu (ST37) [cite: 41].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** Disperse-dense wave, 2 and 100 Hz in frequency, alternating every 3 s (wave widths were 2 Hz for 0.6 ms and 100 Hz for 0.2 ms) [cite: 41].
* **Treatment intensity:** Adjusted in accordance with the maximal level tolerated by each patient [cite: 41].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** From 30 min before surgery until the end of the operation; and at 9:00 am on the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd days after surgery [cite: 41].
* **Duration of each treatment:** 30 min pre-induction + intraoperative period (surgery duration: 186 ± 87 min), and 30 min per session postoperatively [cite: 41].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** 4 sessions (1 perioperative, and 1 session daily on postoperative days 1-3) [cite: 41].
* **Total treatment period:** Up to 3 days postoperatively [cite: 41].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** Intravenous analgesic pump postoperatively [cite: 41].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Sham TEAS [cite: 41].
* **Number of participants in control group:** 140 analyzed [cite: 41].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** Treated bilaterally at four sham acupoints (pseudo-acupoints located near HT7 and BL60) [cite: 41]. Electrodes were attached to a HANS stimulator, but no stimulation was delivered [cite: 41]. Followed the same schedule as the intervention group [cite: 41].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** Intravenous analgesic pump that dispensed 250 μg sufentanil in 250 mL saline [cite: 41]. Flurbiprofen (50 mg intravenously guttae) was given if VAS score was > 4 points [cite: 41].
* **Dosage parameters:** 250 μg sufentanil in 250 mL saline (pump settings not specified) [cite: 41].
* **Duration:** Measured postoperatively [cite: 41].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Visual Analog Scale (VAS) (0-10) [cite: 41].
* **Pain at rest / movement:** Not specifically distinguished [cite: 41].
* **Pain assessment time points:** 6 and 12 h after surgery, and at 9:00 am and 3:00 pm on postoperative day 1 and day 2 [cite: 41].
* **Mean/median pain score for each group:** Categorized as VAS > 4:
    * 6 h post-OP: Group T = 50 (35.7%); Group S = 54 (38.6%) (P = 0.008) [cite: 41].
    * 12 h post-OP: Group T = 30 (21.4%); Group S = 45 (32.1%) (P = 0.261) [cite: 41].
    * Note: The text in results Section 3.5 states: "The VAS score in group T at 6 h after surgery was much lower than in group S (VAS > 4; 21.4% vs. 35.7%; P < 0.01)". This text contradicts Table 5 which shows the values for 12 h.
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** The rate of VAS > 4 in group T at 6 h (or 12 h depending on text vs table interpretation) was much lower than in group S [cite: 41]. The difference in VAS score between the two groups did not reach statistical significance at the other five postoperative periods (P > 0.05) [cite: 41].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Sufentanil [cite: 41].
* **Route of administration:** Intravenous via pump [cite: 41].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention/control groups:** Sufentanil dosage 48 h after operation was included in the logistic regression, but mean/median consumption values were not explicitly reported [cite: 41].
* **Measurement time point(s):** Postoperative [cite: 41].
* **Between-group difference:** Not reported [cite: 41].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** Flurbiprofen given if VAS > 4, but the proportion requiring rescue analgesia was not explicitly reported [cite: 41].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **Gastrointestinal recovery (Primary Outcome):** 
    * Time to first bowel motion (median [IQR]): Group T = 31 (18.8-45.3) h; Group S = 42 (22.1-52.4) h (P < 0.01) [cite: 41].
    * Time to first flatus (median [IQR]): Group T = 44 (26.5-63.9) h; Group S = 64 (30.0-70.6) h (P < 0.01) [cite: 41].
    * Incidence of postoperative gastrointestinal dysfunction (PGD) (defined as absence of bowel sounds for >48h): Group T = 26 (18.6%); Group S = 44 (31.4%) (P < 0.05) [cite: 41].
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting:** PONV occurrence was significantly lower in group T than group S at 6 h, 12 h, and 3:00 pm on day 1 (P < 0.01) [cite: 41].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** Time to first ambulation (median [IQR]): Group T = 44 (24.5-70.7) h; Group S = 67 (42.6-72.9) h (P < 0.01) [cite: 41].
* **Length of stay:** Length of postoperative hospital stay: Group T = 6.2 ± 3.3 days; Group S = 8.1 ± 3.6 days (P < 0.01) [cite: 41].
* **Hospitalization costs:** Group T = 64,967.5 ± 27,937.2 RMB; Group S = 73,281.1 ± 23,850.7 RMB (P < 0.01) [cite: 41].
* **Other relevant physiological/biochemical outcomes (Biomarkers):** In patients undergoing colorectal surgery, the level of Substance P (SP) was lower in group T than in group S at postoperative day 3 (P < 0.05) [cite: 41].
