## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Funded by a research grant from the Postgraduate Research & Practice Innovation Program of Jiangsu Province (grant numbers SJCX21_0783), China [cite: 47].
* **Country:** China (Nanjing, Jiangsu) [cite: 47].
* **Journal:** Journal of Pain Research [cite: 47].
* **DOI:** 10.2147/JPR.S365587 [cite: 47].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2100050235) [cite: 47]. Approved by the Human Research Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing University of Traditional Chinese Medicine (2021NL-198-02) [cite: 47].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Prospective, single-center, randomized, double-blind controlled trial [cite: 47].
* **Randomization method:** Randomization was performed at a 1:1 ratio using a computer-generated list, with patients assigned using a sealed envelope [cite: 47].
* **Blinding:** Double-blinded; all study personnel, including patients, the anesthetist, the investigator, the surgeons, and the recovery ward nurses who collected the data, were blinded to the group assignments [cite: 47].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** Electroacupuncture (EA) group and sham EA (SEA) group [cite: 47].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 83 assessed; 80 randomized (EA: n=40; SEA: n=40); 79 completed and were analyzed (EA: n=39; SEA: n=40) [cite: 47].
* **Age:** EA group: 35.2 (6.1) years; SEA group: 34.4 (9.1) years (mean (SD)) [cite: 47].
* **Sex distribution:** EA group: 9 Male / 30 Female; SEA group: 10 Male / 30 Female [cite: 47].
* **BMI:** EA group: 23.1 (2.9) kg/m²; SEA group: 23.2 (2.7) kg/m² [cite: 47].
* **ASA classification:** ASA physical status class I or II [cite: 47].
* **Comorbidities:** Patients were excluded if they had a history of operative treatment, history of severe pain, were pregnant/breastfeeding, had mental/neurological disorders or intellectual disabilities, implanted cardiac devices, or rash/local infection on the stimulated skin area [cite: 47].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Elective thyroidectomy (total or partial) [cite: 47]. (EA: 12 total / 27 partial; SEA: 14 total / 26 partial) [cite: 47].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** General anesthesia [cite: 47].
* **Induction:** Lidocaine 1 mg/kg i.v., midazolam 0.05 mg/kg i.v., propofol 2-3 mg/kg i.v., sufentanil 0.2-0.4 μg/kg i.v., followed by vecuronium 0.1 mg/kg i.v [cite: 47].
* **Maintenance:** Steady intravenous injection of propofol (3-5 mg/kg/h), vecuronium, and remifentanil at a constant rate of 0.3 μg/kg/min [cite: 47]. Bispectral (BIS) index maintained at 40-60 [cite: 47].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Preoperative electroacupuncture (EA) [cite: 47].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 39 analyzed [cite: 47].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Bilateral Zusanli (ST36) and Neiguan (PC6) [cite: 47].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** Dense-disperse stimulation mode (20-100 Hz) [cite: 47].
* **Treatment intensity:** High intensity, but safe and comfortable [cite: 47].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** Within 24 h before surgery [cite: 47].
* **Duration of each treatment:** 30 minutes [cite: 47].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** One single preoperative session [cite: 47].
* **Total treatment period:** Single day (preoperative) [cite: 47].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** Granisetron 3 mg i.v. following induction to avoid PONV [cite: 47].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Sham EA (SEA) [cite: 47].
* **Number of participants in control group:** 40 analyzed [cite: 47].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** Non-invasive SEA method pressing an empty plastic guiding tube onto a non-acupoint close to the real point [cite: 47]. Bent needles with adhesive bandages were placed on the skin and attached to a stimulator that displayed a flashing light but provided no electrical stimulation [cite: 47]. The acupuncturist repositioned the stimulator and needles to simulate the procedure [cite: 47].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** Flurbiprofen [cite: 47].
* **Dosage parameters:** 50 mg flurbiprofen i.v. as a last resort for postoperative pain relief [cite: 47].
* **Duration:** Administered as needed postoperatively (monitored up to 48 hours) [cite: 47].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Numerical rating scale (NRS) for pain intensity; Electronic von Frey (EvF) device for mechanical pain thresholds [cite: 47].
* **Pain at rest / movement:** QST (EvF) measured mechanical pain thresholds surrounding the skin incision and on the forearm [cite: 47].
* **Pain assessment time points:** 30 min, 6, 24, and 48 h after surgery [cite: 47].
* **Median pain score for each group (NRS, median [IQR]):**
    * 30 min: EA = 2.4 (2.0-3.0); SEA = 2.7 (2.0-3.0) (P = 0.005) [cite: 47].
    * 6 h: EA = 1.9 (1.3-3.0); SEA = 2.2 (2.0-3.0) (P = 0.006) [cite: 47].
    * 24 h: EA = 1.5 (1.0-3.0); SEA = 1.6 (1.0-3.0) (P = 0.223) [cite: 47].
    * 48 h: EA = 1.5 (1.0-3.0); SEA = 1.5 (1.0-3.0) (P = 0.736) [cite: 47].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** The EA group had significantly lower NRS pain scores at 30 min and 6 h after surgery compared to the SEA group [cite: 47]. At 30 min and 6 h after surgery, the mechanical pain thresholds around the skin incision were significantly higher in the EA group than in the SEA group (P < 0.0001 and P = 0.0098, respectively) [cite: 47].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Sufentanil (induction) and Remifentanil (maintenance) [cite: 47].
* **Route of administration:** Intravenous [cite: 47].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention group:** Intraoperative Sufentanil = 41.6 (2.3) μg; Intraoperative Remifentanil = 2009 (143.2) μg [cite: 47].
* **Opioid consumption for control group:** Intraoperative Sufentanil = 41.5 (2.6) μg; Intraoperative Remifentanil = 1989 (158.7) μg [cite: 47].
* **Measurement time point(s):** Intraoperative [cite: 47].
* **Between-group difference:** There was no significant difference in intraoperative anesthetic drug consumption [cite: 47].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** Analgesics 0-48 h after surgery (n [%]): EA = 0 (0); SEA = 0 (0) [cite: 47]. No rescue analgesia (50 mg flurbiprofen i.v.) was needed in either group [cite: 47].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **Remifentanil-induced post-infusion hyperalgesia (RPH) (Primary Outcome):** 
    * Incidence of hyperalgesia (skin incision) at 30 min: EA = 12.8%; SEA = 47.5% (P = 0.001) [cite: 47].
    * Incidence of hyperalgesia (skin incision) at 6 h: EA = 10.3%; SEA = 42.5% (P = 0.001) [cite: 47].
    * The EA group showed a lower incidence of postoperative hyperalgesia surrounding the surgical site than the SEA group at 30 min and 6 h after surgery [cite: 47].
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting:** Not specifically detailed in the results section, but granisetron 3 mg i.v. was administered preventatively following induction [cite: 47].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** Not reported. Extubation time was similar (EA: 9.4 ± 2.9 min; SEA: 9.7 ± 3.1 min) [cite: 47].
* **Length of stay:** Hospital stay (mean (SD)): EA = 6.7 (0.6) days; SEA = 6.7 (0.7) days (P = 0.597) [cite: 47].
* **Other postoperative complications:** Two patients (one per group) complained of mild pain at the needle sites that did not require medical attention [cite: 47].
