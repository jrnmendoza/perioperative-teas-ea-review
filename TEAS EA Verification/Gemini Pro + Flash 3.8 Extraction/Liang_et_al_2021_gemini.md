## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Supported from Wenzhou Science and Technology Bureau of China (Y20190515) [cite: 43].
* **Country:** China [cite: 43].
* **Journal:** Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine [cite: 43].
* **DOI:** 10.1155/2021/6691459 [cite: 43].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (Registration number: ChiCTR1800019951) [cite: 43].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Prospective, randomized, controlled, double-blind study [cite: 43].
* **Randomization method:** Randomly distributed into two groups with the help of a computer-generated table of random numbers by an independent statistician [cite: 43].
* **Blinding:** Double-blind [cite: 43]. Identical sealed envelopes contained the assignment [cite: 43]. The anesthesiologist performing general anesthesia and recording intraoperative data, the investigator in charge of all postoperative assessments, and the investigators performing data recording and analysis were all blinded to group identity [cite: 43]. The acupuncturist opened the envelope and performed the intervention but was not involved in patient assessments [cite: 43].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** TEAS group (T group) and control group (C group) [cite: 43].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 80 male patients enrolled; 75 randomized [cite: 43]. 70 completed the study and were analyzed (TEAS group: n=35; Control group: n=35) [cite: 43].
* **Age:** TEAS group: 70.8 (6.5) years; Control group: 69.1 (6.1) years (mean (SD)) [cite: 43].
* **Sex distribution:** 100% Male (patients undergoing transurethral resection of the prostate) [cite: 43].
* **BMI:** TEAS group: 23.6 (2.5) kg/m²; Control group: 23.1 (2.6) kg/m² (mean (SD)) [cite: 43].
* **ASA classification:** American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) status II or III [cite: 43].
* **Comorbidities:** Patients with clinical diagnosis of benign prostatic hyperplasia [cite: 43]. Patients were excluded if they had severe cardiopulmonary disease, bleeding disorder, hepatic dysfunction, renal impairment, giant/multiple bladder stones, peptic ulcer, diabetes mellitus, severe cognitive impairment, prostate tumors, or a history of urethral stricture/open prostatectomy/acupuncture in the urinary tract [cite: 43].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Transurethral resection of the prostate (TURP) requiring intraoperative urinary catheterization [cite: 43].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** Laryngeal mask general anesthesia [cite: 43].
* **Induction:** Propofol 2 mg/kg, sufentanil 0.3-0.5 μg/kg, and intubation with Cisatracurium 2 mg/kg [cite: 43].
* **Maintenance:** Target-controlled infusion (TCI) of propofol and remifentanil [cite: 43]. The bispectrum index (BIS) was maintained within the range of 45-55 [cite: 43].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) [cite: 43].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 35 analyzed [cite: 43].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Yinjiao (RN7), Qihai (RN6), Shimen (RN5), Guan Yuan (RN4), Zhongji point (RN3), and bilateral ciliao (BL32), zhongliao (BL33), and xialiao (BL34) [cite: 43].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** 2/100 Hz, dense-disperse [cite: 43].
* **Treatment intensity:** Highest tolerable level that caused no discomfort to the patient [cite: 43].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** 30 minutes before general anesthesia [cite: 43].
* **Duration of each treatment:** 30 minutes [cite: 43].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** One single preoperative session [cite: 43].
* **Total treatment period:** Single day (preoperative) [cite: 43].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** Sufentanil 0.1 mg/kg per 30 minutes during the surgery to provide analgesia; Intravenous prophylactic ondansetron (8 mg) to prevent PONV [cite: 43].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** No stimulation [cite: 43].
* **Number of participants in control group:** 35 analyzed [cite: 43].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** The patients received no stimulation [cite: 43]. (The text specifies they "received allocated intervention (no TEAS stimulation)") [cite: 43].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** The number of patients who required extra analgesia was recorded postoperatively [cite: 43]. Intraoperative analgesia was provided by sufentanil 0.1 mg/kg per 30 minutes [cite: 43].
* **Dosage parameters:** Not specifically detailed for postoperative rescue [cite: 43].
* **Duration:** Extra analgesia requirement was monitored postoperatively [cite: 43].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Verbal rating scales (VRS), scored as 0 = no pain, 1 = mild pain, 2 = moderate pain, 3 = severe pain, 4 = very severe pain, and 5 = intolerable pain [cite: 43].
* **Pain at rest / movement:** Not specifically distinguished [cite: 43].
* **Pain assessment time points:** At the end of surgery (T5), at the time out of PACU (T9), postoperative 24 hours (T11), and postoperative 48 hours (T12) [cite: 43].
* **Mean/median pain score for each group:** Table 4 reports the proportion of patients with pain [cite: 43].
    * T5: TEAS = 2 (5.7%); Control = 3 (8.6%) [cite: 43].
    * T9: TEAS = 7 (20%); Control = 1 (2.9%) [cite: 43].
    * T11: TEAS = 8 (22.9%); Control = 6 (17.1%) [cite: 43].
    * T12: TEAS = 8 (19.6%) [Note: The table lists 19.6% but the numerator is 8; 8/35 is ~22.8%]; Control = 7 (20%) [cite: 43].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** There was no significant difference in the incidence of pain in patients over time between the two groups [cite: 43].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Sufentanil and Remifentanil [cite: 43].
* **Route of administration:** Intravenous [cite: 43].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention group:** Intraoperative Sufentanil consumption = 21.4 (3.1) μg; Intraoperative Remifentanil consumption = 521.5 (206.8) μg [cite: 43].
* **Opioid consumption for control group:** Intraoperative Sufentanil consumption = 20.0 (2.9) μg; Intraoperative Remifentanil consumption = 464.7 (156.0) μg [cite: 43].
* **Between-group difference:** There was no significant difference between the two groups for intraoperative sufentanil, propofol, and remifentanil consumption [cite: 43].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** "Extra analgesia" value in Table 4: TEAS group = 3.8 (1.9); Control group = 5.0 (2.9) [cite: 43].
* **Between-group difference:** The amount of extra analgesia required in the control group was significantly higher than that in the TEAS group (P = 0.045) [cite: 43].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **Catheter-related bladder discomfort (CRBD) (Primary Outcome):** 
    * Incidence of CRBD at T5 (end of surgery): TEAS = 9 (26%); Control = 28 (80%) (P < 0.001) [cite: 43].
    * Incidence of CRBD at T9 (out of PACU): TEAS = 20 (57%); Control = 28 (80%) (P = 0.039) [cite: 43].
    * Incidence of CRBD at T11 (24h): TEAS = 7 (20%); Control = 31 (89%) (P < 0.001) [cite: 43].
    * Incidence of CRBD at T12 (48h): TEAS = 4 (11%); Control = 19 (54%) [Note: text in abstract says 7(20%) for control at T12, but Table 2 says 19 (54%)] (P = 0.003) [cite: 43].
    * Incidence of moderate to severe CRBD was significantly lower in the TEAS group at T5 (0 vs 29%, P < 0.001), T9 (6% vs 29%, P = 0.011), and T11 (0 vs 26%, P = 0.002) [cite: 43].
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting:** Assessed via nausea verbal scale description (NVSD). There was no significant difference in the incidence of PONV over time between the two groups [cite: 43]. (e.g., at T9: TEAS = 2 (5.7%); Control = 2 (5.7%)) [cite: 43].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** 
    * QoR-40 total scores at T11 (191.7 ± 4.4 vs 189.1 ± 4.3, P = 0.007) and T12 (195.3 ± 1.9 vs 193.3 ± 3.0, P < 0.001) were significantly higher in the TEAS group than in the control group [cite: 43]. 
    * MMSE scores showed no significant difference between the two groups [cite: 43].
* **Length of stay:** Not reported [cite: 43].
* **Other relevant physiological/biochemical outcomes (Biomarkers):** Blood glucose and lactic acid levels, as well as serum concentrations of IL-1, TNF-α, and β-EP, showed no significant differences between the two groups from T0 to T10 [cite: 43].
