## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Grant from National Natural Science Foundation of China (to Shuangshuang Li, No. 82201375) [cite: 45].
* **Country:** China (Shanghai) [cite: 45].
* **Journal:** BMC Complementary Medicine and Therapies [cite: 45].
* **DOI:** 10.1186/s12906-026-05357-8 [cite: 45].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** Registered at [Anonymized] in text; Ethics approval by the Ethics Committee of the Eye & ENT Hospital of Fudan University (Approval number: 2024041) [cite: 45].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Prospective, single-blind, randomized, parallel-group trial [cite: 45].
* **Randomization method:** Computer-generated randomization sequence in a 1:1 ratio, with allocation concealed in sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes [cite: 45].
* **Blinding:** Single-blind [cite: 45]. Patients, postoperative outcome assessors, ward nurses, and statisticians remained blinded to group allocation [cite: 45]. Attending anesthesiologists performing the intervention could not be blinded [cite: 45].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** TEAS group and Sham-TEAS group [cite: 45].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 286 assessed for eligibility; 277 enrolled and randomized; 277 analyzed (TEAS: n=138; Sham-TEAS: n=139) [cite: 45].
* **Age:** TEAS group: 45.18 ± 11.93 years; Sham-TEAS group: 46.29 ± 11.86 years (mean (SD)) [cite: 45].
* **Sex distribution:** TEAS group: 47 Male / 91 Female; Sham-TEAS group: 59 Male / 80 Female [cite: 45].
* **BMI:** TEAS group: 22.71 ± 3.34 kg/m²; Sham-TEAS group: 23.24 ± 3.72 kg/m² [cite: 45].
* **ASA classification:** ASA physical status I-II [cite: 45]. TEAS: 88 ASA I / 50 ASA II; Sham-TEAS: 80 ASA I / 59 ASA II [cite: 45].
* **Comorbidities:** Included history of motion sickness or PONV (TEAS: 59; Sham-TEAS: 48) [cite: 45]. Excluded if presence of nausea/vomiting or antiemetic use within 24 h preoperatively, hepatic/renal dysfunction, diabetes, peripheral neuropathy, history of substance/alcohol abuse, or uncontrolled cardiovascular disease [cite: 45].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Tympanoplasty [cite: 45].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** General anesthesia [cite: 45].
* **Induction:** Intravenous sufentanil (0.2 μg/kg), propofol (2-4 mg/kg), and rocuronium (0.6 mg/kg) [cite: 45]. Intravenous dexamethasone (8 mg) was administered immediately post-induction [cite: 45].
* **Maintenance:** Sevoflurane (0.8-1.0 MAC) and a continuous remifentanil infusion [cite: 45]. Depth of anesthesia was monitored via BIS, maintained between 40 and 60 [cite: 45].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) [cite: 45].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 138 analyzed [cite: 45].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Bilateral Pericardium 6 (PC6) [cite: 45].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** 2 Hz [cite: 45].
* **Treatment intensity:** Initiated at 1 mA and gradually increased (up to 2-20 mA) until the patient experienced a tolerable tingling sensation [cite: 45].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** Applied prior to anesthetic induction; electrical stimulation was maintained continuously from induction of anesthesia until the end of surgery [cite: 45].
* **Duration of each treatment:** Maintained throughout surgery (mean duration of anesthesia in TEAS group was 86.34 ± 30.85 min) [cite: 45].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** One single continuous intraoperative session [cite: 45].
* **Total treatment period:** Single day (intraoperative) [cite: 45].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** Intravenous dexamethasone (8 mg) post-induction; palonosetron (0.075 mg IV) administered at wound closure for antiemetic prophylaxis [cite: 45].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Sham TEAS [cite: 45].
* **Number of participants in control group:** 139 analyzed [cite: 45].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** Identical electrode placement (bilateral PC6) and procedures were followed, but the stimulator was turned off, and no electrical current was delivered [cite: 45]. Received the same pharmacological antiemetic prophylaxis (dexamethasone + palonosetron) [cite: 45].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** Intravenous oxycodone (0.7 mg/kg) postoperatively [cite: 45]. Rescue acetaminophen (0.3 g IV) was administered for NRS scores > 4 [cite: 45].
* **Dosage parameters:** Oxycodone 0.7 mg/kg IV; Acetaminophen 0.3 g IV [cite: 45].
* **Duration:** Tracked up to 48 hours [cite: 45].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) (0-10 assumed) [cite: 45].
* **Pain at rest / movement:** Not specifically distinguished [cite: 45].
* **Pain assessment time points:** 2 h, 6 h, 24 h, and 48 h postoperatively [cite: 45].
* **Mean/median pain score for each group (Mean (SD)):**
    * Postoperative 2 h: TEAS = 1.67 (0.82); Sham-TEAS = 1.66 (0.93) (P = 0.91) [cite: 45].
    * Postoperative 6 h: TEAS = 1.55 (0.85); Sham-TEAS = 1.86 (0.97) (P = 0.01) [cite: 45].
    * Postoperative 24 h: TEAS = 1.30 (0.92); Sham-TEAS = 1.65 (0.95) (P < 0.01) [cite: 45].
    * Postoperative 48 h: TEAS = 0.82 (0.75); Sham-TEAS = 1.09 (0.75) (P < 0.01) [cite: 45].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** The TEAS group demonstrated significantly lower NRS pain scores at 6 h, 24 h, and 48 h postoperatively compared with the Sham-TEAS group [cite: 45]. No significant intergroup difference was observed at the 2-hour time point [cite: 45].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Sufentanil (induction), Remifentanil (maintenance), Oxycodone (postoperative) [cite: 45].
* **Route of administration:** Intravenous [cite: 45].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention group:** Intraoperative Sufentanil equivalents = 15.86 ± 5.11 mg [cite: 45].
* **Opioid consumption for control group:** Intraoperative Sufentanil equivalents = 15.18 ± 4.95 mg [cite: 45].
* **Measurement time point(s):** Intraoperative [cite: 45].
* **Between-group difference:** There was no significant difference in intraoperative opioid consumption (sufentanil equivalents) between the groups (P = 0.27) [cite: 45].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** Five patients in each group required acetaminophen for NRS scores > 4 [cite: 45].
* **Between-group difference:** No significant difference in total analgesic consumption or rescue analgesia requirement [cite: 45].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting (Primary Outcome):** 
    * Incidence of Nausea (0-48h): TEAS = 24 (17.39%); Sham-TEAS = 53 (38.13%) (P < 0.01) [cite: 45].
    * Incidence of Vomiting (0-48h): TEAS = 9 (6.52%); Sham-TEAS = 27 (19.42%) (P < 0.01) [cite: 45].
    * Complete response (absence of nausea and vomiting without rescue antiemetics) within 48h: TEAS = 112 (81.16%); Sham-TEAS = 83 (59.71%) (P < 0.01) [cite: 45].
    * Severity of vomiting was significantly lower in the TEAS group during the 0-2, 2-6, and 6-24 h intervals (all P < 0.05) [cite: 45].
    * Consumption of rescue antiemetics (droperidol) within 48h was low and comparable between groups (e.g., 0-2h: TEAS = 7 (5.07%), Sham = 5 (3.60%), P = 0.55) [cite: 45].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** Extubation time (TEAS: 18.86 ± 4.21 min; Sham: 19.03 ± 4.75 min, P=0.75) and length of PACU stay (TEAS: 34.98 ± 4.06 min; Sham: 34.19 ± 3.94 min, P=0.10) showed no significant intergroup differences [cite: 45].
* **Length of stay:** Not reported [cite: 45].
* **Other postoperative complications:** No instances of severe headache, dizziness, or drowsiness (commonly associated with palonosetron) were recorded in either group [cite: 45]. No adverse cutaneous reactions were observed at the electrode application sites [cite: 45].
