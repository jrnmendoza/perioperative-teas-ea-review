# Data Extraction: covidence_1970_ng_2013.pdf (Ng et al., 2013)

## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Supported by the Health and Health Services Research Fund (reference number 06070371), Food and Health Bureau, Hong Kong SAR Government [cite: 53].
* **Country:** Hong Kong SAR (China) [cite: 53].
* **Journal:** Gastroenterology [cite: 53].
* **DOI:** 10.1053/j.gastro.2012.10.050 [cite: 53].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** ClinicalTrials.gov, number NCT00464425 [cite: 53]. Approved by the Joint Chinese University of Hong Kong-New Territories East Cluster Clinical Research Ethics Committee [cite: 53].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Prospective randomized sham-controlled study [cite: 53].
* **Randomization method:** Simple randomization using a computer-generated random sequence [cite: 53]. A sealed nonopaque envelope was opened to determine the limb of entry [cite: 53].
* **Blinding:** The acupuncturist was aware of the allocation [cite: 53]. Patients randomized to the EA and SA groups and the outcome assessor were blinded to the treatment allocation [cite: 53]. It was not possible to blind the patients in the NA group [cite: 53].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** Electroacupuncture (EA) group, sham acupuncture (SA) group, and no acupuncture (NA) group [cite: 53].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 208 assessed for eligibility; 165 enrolled, randomized, and analyzed (EA: n=55; SA: n=55; NA: n=55) [cite: 53]. There was no withdrawal or drop-out [cite: 53].
* **Age:** EA group: 67.4 ± 9.7 years; SA group: 37.4 ± 10.7 years; NA group: 38.5 ± 10.6 years (mean ± SD) [cite: 53].
* **Sex distribution:** EA group: 35 Male / 20 Female; SA group: 33 Male / 22 Female; NA group: 31 Male / 24 Female [cite: 53].
* **BMI:** EA group: 22.8 ± 2.9 kg/m²; SA group: 22.9 ± 3.4 kg/m²; NA group: 23.4 ± 3.1 kg/m² [cite: 53].
* **ASA classification:** ASA grading I-III [cite: 53]. EA: 17/36/2; SA: 14/30/11; NA: 11/32/12 [cite: 53].
* **Comorbidities:** Included patients undergoing elective laparoscopic resection of colonic and upper rectal cancer [cite: 53]. Number of patients with comorbidities: EA: 29 (52.7%); SA: 34 (61.8%); NA: 37 (67.3%) [cite: 53]. Excluded patients who underwent midrectal/low rectal cancer resection, total/proctocolectomy, required conversion to open surgery, complex laparoscopic procedures, stoma creation, intraoperative complications, epidural anesthesia/analgesia, or those with cardiac pacemakers or previous acupuncture [cite: 53].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Elective laparoscopic resection of colonic and upper rectal cancer [cite: 53].
* **Specific procedures:** Right hemicolectomy (RH), left hemicolectomy (LH), sigmoid colectomy (SIG), and anterior resection (AR) [cite: 53]. EA: 15/12/7/21; SA: 12/12/7/24; NA: 17/10/8/20 [cite: 53].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** General anesthesia [cite: 53].
* **Induction:** Not explicitly detailed [cite: 53].
* **Maintenance:** Not explicitly detailed [cite: 53].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Electroacupuncture (EA) [cite: 53].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 55 analyzed [cite: 53].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Zusanli (ST-36), Sanyinjiao (SP-6), Hegu (LI-4), and Zhigou (TE-6) [cite: 53].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** 100 Hz frequency [cite: 53].
* **Treatment intensity:** Needles were inserted to a depth of about 20 mm [cite: 53]. The achievement of a radiating sensation with paresthesia (de qi) was indicative of effective needling [cite: 53].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** Postoperative days 1 until day 4 (or until the time when the primary outcome had occurred) [cite: 53].
* **Duration of each treatment:** 20 minutes per session [cite: 53].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** One session daily from postoperative day 1 to day 4 (up to 4 sessions) [cite: 53].
* **Total treatment period:** Up to 4 days postoperatively [cite: 53].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** Standardized postoperative management, including early ambulation, early oral feeding, and on-demand pethidine (1 mg/kg every 4 hours) [cite: 53].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Sham acupuncture (SA) and No acupuncture (NA) [cite: 53].
* **Number of participants in control group:** SA: 55 analyzed; NA: 55 analyzed [cite: 53].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** 
    * **SA group:** Shorter needles were placed 15 mm away from the acupoints at a shallower depth (2 mm) to avoid de qi [cite: 53]. Pseudostimulation was provided by deliberately connecting the needle to the incorrect output socket of the device (no electric current flow) [cite: 53]. The output light flashed, and patients were told the frequency was not perceivable [cite: 53].
    * **NA group:** No acupuncture was performed [cite: 53].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** Pethidine [cite: 53].
* **Dosage parameters:** 1 mg/kg every 4 hours on demand [cite: 53].
* **Duration:** Monitored over the postoperative period [cite: 53].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Visual analogue scale (VAS) (from 0 = no pain, to 10 = worst pain) [cite: 53].
* **Pain at rest / movement:** Not specifically distinguished [cite: 53].
* **Pain assessment time points:** First 3 postoperative days (Day 1, Day 2, Day 3) [cite: 53].
* **Mean/median pain score for each group (mean ± SD):**
    * VAS pain score on day 1: EA = 5.6 ± 2.0; SA = 5.8 ± 1.9; NA = 5.5 ± 2.3 [cite: 53].
    * VAS pain score on day 2: EA = 3.2 ± 1.6; SA = 4.6 ± 2.0; NA = 4.2 ± 2.0 [cite: 53].
    * VAS pain score on day 3: EA = 2.1 ± 1.2; SA = 3.4 ± 2.2; NA = 3.2 ± 1.8 [cite: 53].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** The EA group had significantly lower pain scores on day 2 and day 3 compared to both the NA group (P = .004 on day 2; P < .001 on day 3) and the SA group (P < .001 on both days) [cite: 53]. No significant differences were seen on day 1 [cite: 53].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Pethidine [cite: 53].
* **Route of administration:** Intramuscular or intravenous (not explicitly stated, but "on demand" every 4 hours generally implies IM or IV; dose is "number of injections of 50-mg pethidine") [cite: 53].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention/control groups:** Addressed via the number of injections of 50-mg pethidine [cite: 53].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** Postoperative analgesic requirement (number of injections of 50-mg pethidine): EA = 2.7 ± 2.3; SA = 5.2 ± 4.7; NA = 5.0 ± 4.5 [cite: 53].
* **Between-group difference:** The EA group required significantly less analgesic compared to both the NA group (P = .001) and the SA group (P = .001) [cite: 53].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **Gastrointestinal recovery (Primary Outcome: Time to defecation):** 
    * Time to first bowel motion (defecation) (h): EA = 85.9 ± 36.1; SA = 107.5 ± 46.2; NA = 122.1 ± 53.5 [cite: 53].
    * The EA group had a significantly shorter time to defecation than both the NA group (P < .001) and the SA group (P = .007) [cite: 53].
    * Time to first passing flatus (days): EA = 2.0 ± 0.9; SA = 2.3 ± 1.1; NA = 2.6 ± 1.1 [cite: 53]. EA was significantly shorter than NA (P = .003) but not SA (P = .095) [cite: 53].
    * Time to resume normal diet (days): EA = 4.0 ± 1.1; SA = 4.1 ± 0.8; NA = 4.8 ± 2.0 [cite: 53]. EA was significantly shorter than NA (P = .010) but not SA (P = .695) [cite: 53].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** Time to walk independently (days): EA = 2.8 ± 1.5; SA = 3.3 ± 1.1; NA = 3.8 ± 1.8 [cite: 53]. EA was significantly shorter than NA (P = .001) and SA (P = .028) [cite: 53].
* **Length of stay:** Hospital stay (days): EA = 6.5 ± 2.2; SA = 6.8 ± 1.7; NA = 8.5 ± 4.8 [cite: 53]. EA was significantly shorter than NA (P = .007), but similar to SA (P = .491) [cite: 53].
* **Other postoperative complications:** The overall complication rates were not significantly different among the groups (EA: 6 (10.9%); SA: 5 (9.1%); NA: 10 (18.2%); P = .318) [cite: 53]. Prolonged ileus occurred in 3 (EA), 1 (SA), and 6 (NA) patients [cite: 53].
