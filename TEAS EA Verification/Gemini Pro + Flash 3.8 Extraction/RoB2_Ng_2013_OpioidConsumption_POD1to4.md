# Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 (RoB 2) Assessment

## Study & Result Information
- **Study Citation:** Ng SSM, Leung WW, Mak TWC, Hon SSF, Li JCM, Wong CYN, Tsoi KKF, Lee JFY. Electroacupuncture Reduces Duration of Postoperative Ileus After Laparoscopic Surgery for Colorectal Cancer. *Gastroenterology*. 2013;144(2):307–313.
- **Source Document in Google Drive:** `covidence_1970_ng_2013.pdf`
- **Trial Registration:** Not reported / omitted in text
- **Population:** 165 patients undergoing elective laparoscopic resection for colonic and upper rectal cancer (Prince of Wales Hospital, Hong Kong; Oct 2008 – Oct 2010).
- **Intervention:** Electroacupuncture (EA) at Zusanli (ST-36), Sanyinjiao (SP-6), Hegu (LI-4), and Zhigou (TE-6), 25 mm needles inserted 20 mm with *de qi*, connected to Ito ES-160 stimulator at 100 Hz for 20 minutes once daily on postoperative days 1 to 4 (n = 55).
- **Comparator 1 (Primary Control):** Sham acupuncture (SA) using 13 mm needles inserted 15 mm away from true acupoints to a shallow depth of 2 mm without *de qi*, connected to dummy output sockets (light flashing, no electric current, told frequency is imperceptible; n = 55).
- **Comparator 2:** No acupuncture (NA) receiving conventional postoperative care alone (n = 55).
- **Assessed Outcome:** Postoperative Analgesic Requirement (Cumulative number of on-demand injections of 50-mg pethidine)
- **Assessed Timepoint:** Cumulative through Postoperative Days 1 to 4
- **Numerical Result:**
  - EA group (n = 55): Mean 2.7 ± 2.3 injections
  - SA group (n = 55): Mean 5.2 ± 4.7 injections ($P = 0.001$ vs. EA; Table 4)
  - NA group (n = 55): Mean 5.0 ± 4.5 injections ($P = 0.001$ vs. EA; Table 3)

---

### Step 1: Study Design Verification
- **Experimental Design:** 3-arm prospective, randomized, sham-controlled, parallel-group trial (1:1:1 allocation ratio).
- **Unit of Randomization:** Individual patient.

---

### Step 2: Signaling Questions & Domain Evaluations

#### Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process
- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random?**
  - **Response:** Yes (Y)
  - **Evidence:** "Patients were randomized (using simple randomization) to receive either EA, sham acupuncture (SA), or no acupuncture (NA) in a 1:1:1 ratio... produced according to the computer-generated random sequence" (`covidence_1970_ng_2013.pdf`, Page 308, col. 1).
- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**
  - **Response:** Probably No (PN)
  - **Evidence:** "A sealed nonopaque envelope (produced according to the computer-generated random sequence) was opened to determine the limb of entry." (`covidence_1970_ng_2013.pdf`, Page 308, col. 1).
  - **Reasoning:** The text explicitly specifies a **"nonopaque envelope"** (translucent / see-through). Furthermore, sequential numbering is not stated. Under strict Cochrane RoB 2 rules, explicit description of nonopaque envelopes cannot be assumed to provide robust allocation concealment.
- **1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
  - **Evidence:** Table 1 demonstrates balanced age (65.9 ± 10.9 vs. 66.8 ± 11.2 vs. 65.6 ± 12.0 yr), sex, ASA grade, tumor site, operating time, and blood loss across the three groups ($P > 0.05$; Page 309).
- **Domain 1 Judgment:** **Some Concerns** (due to explicit report of "nonopaque envelope").

---

#### Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions (Effect of Assignment)
- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial?**
  - **Response:** 
    - For EA vs. SA: Probably No (PN). "In the SA group, pseudostimulation was provided by deliberately connecting the needle to the incorrect output socket... Patients could see the output light flashing but no current was transmitted... Patients were told that the stimulation frequency selected was not perceivable by human beings." (Page 308, col. 2).
    - For EA vs. NA: Yes (Y). "It was not possible to blind the patients in the NA group." (Page 308, col. 1).
- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants' assigned intervention during the trial?**
  - **Response:** Probably No (PN) for EA vs. SA.
  - **Evidence:** "The acupuncturist was the only individual who was aware of the treatment allocation; the patients randomized to the EA/SA groups and the outcome assessor were blinded to the treatment allocation... The postoperative management of all patients was standardized. Pethidine 1 mg/kg as postoperative analgesia was given every 4 hours on demand." (Page 308, col. 1).
  - **Reasoning:** Ward nurses administering pethidine on demand were unaware of allocation between EA and SA.
- **2.3 Were there deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
  - **Evidence:** Standardized postoperative clinical pathway and analgesia dosing guidelines applied across all wards.
- **2.5 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention?**
  - **Response:** Yes (Y)
  - **Evidence:** "The data were analyzed according to the intention-to-treat principle." Flowchart (Figure 2, p. 309) confirms all 55 randomized per arm were analyzed in their assigned group (100% ITT).
- **Domain 2 Judgment:** **Low Risk of Bias** (for EA vs. SA sham comparison); **Some Concerns** (for EA vs. NA open comparison). Overall for the primary sham comparison: **Low Risk of Bias**.

---

#### Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data
- **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?**
  - **Response:** Yes (Y)
  - **Evidence:** 165 of 165 participants (100.0%) had complete outcome data recorded for postoperative pethidine injections (Table 3 & Table 4, Page 310).
- **Domain 3 Judgment:** **Low Risk of Bias**

---

#### Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome
- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
  - **Evidence:** Pethidine administration logs (number of 50-mg injections administered) recorded in hospital medication administration charts.
- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
  - **Evidence:** "The data were recorded and measured by an independent research assistant... the outcome assessor were blinded to the treatment allocation" (`covidence_1970_ng_2013.pdf`, Page 308, col. 1–2).
- **4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
- **Domain 4 Judgment:** **Low Risk of Bias**

---

#### Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result
- **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analyzed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan that was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available?**
  - **Response:** No Information (NI)
  - **Evidence:** The published paper does not cite a prospective clinical trial registry number (e.g., ClinicalTrials.gov, ChiCTR, or HKCTR) or an accessible pre-published protocol.
  - **Reasoning:** In the absence of verifiable trial registration or a pre-specified analysis plan, selection bias cannot be fully ruled out.
- **5.2 Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected from multiple outcome measurements within the outcome domain?**
  - **Response:** Probably No (PN)
  - **Evidence:** Analgesic requirement is reported as cumulative pethidine doses across the full trial period.
- **5.3 Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected from multiple analyses of the data?**
  - **Response:** Probably No (PN)
- **Domain 5 Judgment:** **Some Concerns** (due to NI on pre-specified trial registration / protocol).

### Step 3: Overall Judgment & Summary Table

| Domain | Judgment | Key Supporting Rationale |
|---|:---:|---|
| **Domain 1: Randomization Process** | **Some concerns** | Computer-generated sequence and balanced baseline, but envelopes explicitly described as "nonopaque" and sequential numbering omitted. |
| **Domain 2: Deviations from Intended Interventions** | **Low** | Sham control with flashing inactive output and sensory deception; ward nurses blinded to EA/SA; 100% ITT analysis. |

| **Domain 4: Measurement of the Outcome** | **Low** | Independent blinded research assistant extracting nursing injection records. |

| **OVERALL RISK OF BIAS** | **SOME CONCERNS** | Raised by Domain 1 (envelope opacity text) and Domain 5 (unregistered analysis plan). |
