# Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 (RoB 2) Assessment

## Study & Result Information
- **Study Citation:** Xiong Q, Min S, Wei K, Yang Y, Ma J, Liu D, Zeng M, Zou L. Transcutaneous Electrical Acupoint Stimulation Combined with Dexamethasone and Tropisetron Prevents Postoperative Nausea and Vomiting in Female Patients Undergoing Laparoscopic Sleeve Gastrectomy: a Prospective, Randomized Controlled Trial. *Obesity Surgery*. 2021;31(5):1912–1920.
- **Source Document in Google Drive:** `covidence_431_full_article.pdf`
- **Trial Registration:** Chinese Clinical Trial Registry ChiCTR2000037785 (Registered retrospectively in 2020; study enrollment ran May 2018 – February 2020)
- **Population:** 62 high-risk female patients (Apfel score $\ge 3$) undergoing elective laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy (LSG) under general anesthesia.
- **Intervention:** TEAS at bilateral PC6 (Neiguan) and ST36 (Zusanli) (dense-disperse 2/10 Hz, Hwato SDZ-V) initiated 30 minutes pre-induction, maintained intraoperatively, and re-administered at 2 h and 6 h postoperatively (twice daily, 30 min each) + IV dexamethasone (8 mg) + tropisetron (2 mg) (n = 31 analyzed).
- **Comparator:** Control group: Identical gel electrodes applied at PC6 and ST36 connected to instrument without electrical stimulation + IV dexamethasone (8 mg) + tropisetron (2 mg) (n = 31 analyzed).
- **Assessed Outcome:** Incidence of Postoperative Nausea and Vomiting (PONV)
- **Assessed Timepoint:** 0 to 48 hours postoperatively
- **Numerical Result:**
  - TEAS group (n = 31): 13 / 31 (41.9%)
  - Control group (n = 31): 24 / 31 (77.4%)
  - Difference: Pearson $\chi^2$, $P = 0.004$; Relative Risk = 0.39 (95% CI: 0.19 to 0.77); Absolute Risk Reduction = 35.5% (Table 2)

---

### Step 1: Study Design Verification
- **Experimental Design:** Prospective, randomized, controlled parallel-group trial (1:1 allocation ratio).
- **Unit of Randomization:** Individual patient.

---

### Step 2: Signaling Questions & Domain Evaluations

#### Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process
- **1.1 Was the allocation sequence random?**
  - **Response:** Yes (Y)
  - **Evidence:** "patients were randomly assigned to the TEAS or control groups using a computerized random number generator." (`covidence_431_full_article.pdf`, Section "Randomization and Blinding", Page 1913).
- **1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**
  - **Response:** Probably Yes (PY)
  - **Evidence:** "Group assignment was exposed from a sealed envelope only by an acupuncturist when patients arrived at the operating room." (`covidence_431_full_article.pdf`, Page 1913).
  - **Reasoning:** Envelope allocation by an independent therapist upon OR arrival provides concealment, though sequential numbering and envelope opacity are not explicitly affirmed.
- **1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
  - **Evidence:** Table 1 shows age (31.7 ± 9.3 vs. 32.1 ± 10.3 yr), BMI (34.9 ± 5.9 vs. 35.1 ± 6.2 $\text{kg/m}^2$), ASA physical status, Apfel score, anesthesia duration (125.5 ± 25.1 vs. 129.4 ± 24.3 min), surgery duration, intraoperative opioid consumption, and fluid volume balanced ($P > 0.05$; Page 1914).
- **Domain 1 Judgment:** **Low Risk of Bias**

---

#### Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from the intended interventions (Effect of Assignment)
- **2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial?**
  - **Response:** Probably Yes (PY)
  - **Evidence:** "The patients were blinded to the group assignment and were told that they might or might not feel a sensation when the acupoint stimulation was working... The stimulation intensity was adjusted according to individual requirements, starting at 1 mA and increasing gradually to obtain the maximum tolerance for a slight twitching of local muscles... In contrast, the patients in the control group had gel electrodes applied and connected to an acupuncture instrument without stimulation." (`covidence_431_full_article.pdf`, Section "Study Protocol" & "Randomization and Blinding", Page 1913).
  - **Reasoning:** Stimulation was adjusted to produce visible/tactile local muscle twitching at maximum tolerance 30 min before anesthesia while awake, and repeated at 2 h and 6 h postoperatively while fully conscious. Control patients received zero current and felt no muscle contraction. Awake muscle twitching provides tactile awareness of active therapy.
- **2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants' assigned intervention during the trial?**
  - **Response:** Probably No (PN)
  - **Evidence:** "The anesthetists were not blind to the processing because they were responsible for intraoperative management but they were not involved in the postoperative assessment. An anesthetic resident who was not involved in the anesthesia routine and who was blinded to the group assignments performed the follow-up and data collection." Postoperative ward care followed standard pathways.
- **2.3 Were there deviations from the intended intervention that arose because of the trial context?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
  - **Evidence:** Standardized intraoperative general anesthesia and postoperative PCIA sufentanil (1.5 $\mu$g/h background infusion, 1 $\mu$g bolus, 20 min lockout). Standardized rescue tropisetron 2 mg IV for PONV score > 1.
- **2.5 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention?**
  - **Response:** Yes (Y)
  - **Evidence:** All 62 randomized participants (31 per group) completed the trial and were analyzed in their assigned groups (Fig. 1, Page 1915).
- **Domain 2 Judgment:** **Some Concerns** (due to noticeable sensory and motor muscle twitching in conscious patients during pre-induction and postoperative sessions).

---

#### Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data
- **3.1 Were data for this outcome available for all, or nearly all, participants randomized?**
  - **Response:** Yes (Y)
  - **Evidence:** 62 of 62 randomized patients (100.0%) had complete 48-h outcome data (`covidence_431_full_article.pdf`, Fig. 1 & Table 2).
- **Domain 3 Judgment:** **Low Risk of Bias**

---

#### Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome
- **4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
  - **Evidence:** Standard validated scale for PONV incidence (nausea, retching, vomiting) and rescue antiemetic documentation (`covidence_431_full_article.pdf`, Page 1913–1914).
- **4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
- **4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received by study participants?**
  - **Response:** No (N)
  - **Evidence:** "An anesthetic resident who was not involved in the anesthesia routine and who was blinded to the group assignments performed the follow-up and data collection." (`covidence_431_full_article.pdf`, Page 1913).
- **Domain 4 Judgment:** **Low Risk of Bias**

---

#### Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result
- **5.1 Were the data that produced this result analyzed in accordance with a pre-specified analysis plan that was finalized before unblinded outcome data were available?**
  - **Response:** Probably No (PN)
  - **Evidence:** "The trial was registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2000037785)" (`covidence_431_full_article.pdf`, Page 1913). Enrollment took place "between May 2018 and February 2020" (Page 1913), whereas ChiCTR registration occurred in 2020 (ChiCTR2000037785 prefix 20 indicates registration year 2020, at or near study closure).
  - **Reasoning:** Retrospective registration precludes verification that the analysis plan was finalized prior to unblinded data availability.
- **5.2 Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected from multiple outcome measurements within the outcome domain?**
  - **Response:** Probably No (PN)
  - **Evidence:** 48-hour cumulative PONV incidence was the primary endpoint calculated in the sample size section (Page 1913).
- **5.3 Is the numerical result being assessed likely to have been selected from multiple analyses of the data?**
  - **Response:** Probably No (PN)
- **Domain 5 Judgment:** **Some Concerns** (due to retrospective trial registration).
