# Data Extraction: covidence_952_full_article.pdf (Sim et al., 2002)

## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Funded by an NMRC grant (0354/1999) [cite: 41].
* **Country:** Singapore [cite: 41].
* **Journal:** Acupuncture in Medicine [cite: 41].
* **DOI / Citation:** 2002;20(2-3):56-65 [cite: 41].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** Not reported. Ethics Committee approval and written informed consent were obtained [cite: 41].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Randomized controlled trial [cite: 41].
* **Randomization method:** Patients were randomised by the use of a table of random numbers into three groups [cite: 41].
* **Blinding:** Patients in Group I and II were blinded to the types of acupuncture administered [cite: 41]. An independent observer blinded to the group assignments carried out the postoperative assessment [cite: 41]. The acupuncturist and anaesthetists administering the procedure were aware of group assignments [cite: 41].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** Group I (Control / placebo EA before induction), Group II (Preoperative EA before induction), and Group III (Postoperative EA on arrival to recovery area) [cite: 41].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 90 female patients recruited and analyzed (30 patients per group) [cite: 41].
* **Age:** Group I: 47 ± 6 years; Group II: 46 ± 5 years; Group III: 45 ± 4 years (mean ± SD) [cite: 41].
* **Sex distribution:** 100% Female [cite: 41].
* **BMI:** Weight: Group I = 59 ± 9 kg; Group II = 60 ± 10 kg; Group III = 57 ± 9 kg [cite: 41]. Body mass index greater than 30 was excluded [cite: 41].
* **ASA classification:** ASA physical status I or II [cite: 41].
* **Comorbidities:** Patients on β-blockers, with a psychiatric history, or who received opioids in the preceding month were excluded [cite: 41].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Gynaecologic lower abdominal surgery (total abdominal hysterectomy with or without bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy) [cite: 41].
* **Specific procedures:** Incision types included Pfannenstiel or midline [cite: 41].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** Balanced general anaesthesia (GA) with endotracheal intubation [cite: 41].
* **Induction:** Midazolam 7.5 mg an hour before arrival, thiopentone sodium 4–5 mg/kg, atracurium infusion for muscle relaxation, and alfentanil [cite: 41].
* **Maintenance:** Isoflurane titrated to achieve an adequate level of anaesthesia (Bispectral Index [BIS] index 45–55) [cite: 41]. Alfentanil was titrated to maintain blood pressure and pulse rate within ±15% of basal values [cite: 41].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Electroacupuncture (EA) [cite: 41].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** Group II (Preoperative EA): 30; Group III (Postoperative EA): 30 [cite: 41].
* **Acupuncture points used:** ST36 and PC6 bilaterally and subcutaneously along the skin incision [cite: 41].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** 2 Hz at ST36 and PC6, and 100 Hz at the skin incision [cite: 41].
* **Treatment intensity:** Delivered via a battery-operated stimulator (model WQ1002K) [cite: 41].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** 
    * Group II: 45 minutes of EA before induction of GA [cite: 41].
    * Group III: 45 minutes of EA on arrival to the recovery area (extubated and awake) [cite: 41].
* **Duration of each treatment:** 45 minutes [cite: 41].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** One single session (preoperative or postoperative) [cite: 41].
* **Total treatment period:** Single day (perioperative) [cite: 41].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** Intravenous morphine via patient-controlled analgesia (PCA) device postoperatively [cite: 41].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Group I (Control group receiving placebo EA for 45 minutes before induction of GA) [cite: 41].
* **Number of participants in control group:** 30 analyzed [cite: 41].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** Tapping a plastic needle tube on the bony area next to each acupuncture point to produce sensation, with the acupuncture needle taped to the skin without skin penetration. Electrodes from a mock electrical stimulator were attached with a blinking indicator light, but no electrical current was applied [cite: 41].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** Intravenous morphine via a patient-controlled analgesia (PCA) device [cite: 41].
* **Dosage parameters:** 1 mg of morphine i.v. with a lock-out time of five minutes [cite: 41].
* **Duration:** Assessed over 24 hours postoperatively [cite: 41].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** 0–10 visual-analogue scale (VAS) [cite: 41].
* **Pain at rest / movement:** At rest [cite: 41].
* **Pain assessment time points:** Every six hours, up to 24 hours postoperatively [cite: 41].
* **Mean pain score for each group:** 
    * Mean VAS over 24 hours: Group I = 4.5 ± 2.1; Group II = 4.7 ± 1.8; Group III = 3.9 ± 1.6 [cite: 41].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** The VAS for pain assessed every 6 hours was not significantly different among the three groups [cite: 41].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Alfentanil (intraoperative) and Morphine (postoperative) [cite: 41].
* **Route of administration:** Intravenous [cite: 41].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention group (Group II - Preop EA):** 
    * Intraoperative Alfentanil = 0.44 ± 0.15 μg/kg/min [cite: 41].
    * 24-hour cumulative Morphine consumption = 0.52 ± 0.19 mg/kg [cite: 41].
* **Opioid consumption for control / comparison groups:** 
    * Intraoperative Alfentanil: Group I (Control) = 0.51 ± 0.21 μg/kg/min; Group III (Postop EA) = 0.58 ± 0.22 μg/kg/min [cite: 41].
    * 24-hour cumulative Morphine consumption: Group I = 0.68 ± 0.38 mg/kg; Group III = 0.58 ± 0.27 mg/kg [cite: 41].
* **Measurement time point(s):** Intraoperative and postoperative periods (0-6h, 6-12h, 12-18h, 18-24h, and total 24h) [cite: 41].
* **Between-group difference:** 
    * Patients in Group II received significantly less alfentanil than those in Group III (p = 0.024), but not significantly less than Group I (p = 0.472) [cite: 41].
    * Postoperative morphine consumption was statistically significantly lower during the 6–12 hour period between Group II [0.03 (0.05) mg/kg] and Group I [0.10 (0.11) mg/kg] (p = 0.015), and Group II and Group III [0.08 (0.10) mg/kg] (p = 0.010) [cite: 41]. Total 24-hour morphine consumption was lower in Group II, but the difference did not reach statistical significance [cite: 41].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Rescue analgesia details:** Postoperative pain managed by PCA morphine; intermittent i.v. morphine 1–2 mg given in the recovery room until VAS ≤ 3 before PCA use [cite: 41].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting:** 
    * Incidence of PONV over 24 hours: Group I = 46.6%; Group II = 43.3%; Group III = 56.7% (differences not statistically significant) [cite: 41].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** 
    * Drowsiness: Group I = 30.0%; Group II = 23.3%; Group III = 33.3% [cite: 41].
    * Pruritus: Group I = 16.7%; Group II = 13.3%; Group III = 16.7% [cite: 41].
    * Duration of recovery room stay: Group I = 86 ± 23 min; Group II = 80 ± 36 min; Group III = 83 ± 24 min (p = 0.73) [cite: 41].
* **Length of stay:** Not reported [cite: 41].
* **Other postoperative complications:** No complications related to the EA procedures [cite: 41]. 65% of patients who received EA stated they would not mind receiving EA for future surgery [cite: 41].
