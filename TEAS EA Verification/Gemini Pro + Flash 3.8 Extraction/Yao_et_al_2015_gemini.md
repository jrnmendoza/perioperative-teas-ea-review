# Data Extraction: 039_yao_2015.pdf (Yao et al., 2015)

## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Supported by Social Development of Key Projects in Fujian Province (2012Y0012) [cite: 41].
* **Country:** China [cite: 41].
* **Journal:** Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine [cite: 41].
* **DOI:** 10.1155/2015/324360 [cite: 41].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** Not explicitly reported, but approved by the Institutional Review Board of Fujian Provincial Hospital (Ref. K2014-05-008) [cite: 41].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Single-center, prospective, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial [cite: 41].
* **Randomization method:** Assigned by a table of computer-generated random numbers with a 1:1 allocation ratio [cite: 41]. Group assignments were sealed in sequentially numbered opaque envelopes [cite: 41].
* **Blinding:** Double-blind; patients, attending anesthesiologist, surgeons, recovery ward nurses, data collectors, and the person who performed the final statistical analysis were blinded to group assignment [cite: 41].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** TEAS group and Control group [cite: 41].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 103 assessed for eligibility; 74 randomized (TEAS group: n=37; Control group: n=37); 71 analyzed (TEAS group: n=35; Control group: n=36) [cite: 41].
* **Age:** TEAS group: 34.2 (7.2) years; Control group: 35.6 (8.7) years (mean (SD)) [cite: 41].
* **Sex distribution:** 100% Female (undergoing gynecological laparoscopic surgery) [cite: 41].
* **BMI:** Weight: TEAS group = 53.5 (6.7) kg; Control group = 55.8 (8.2) kg [cite: 41]. Excluded if BMI > 30 kg/m² [cite: 41].
* **ASA classification:** American Society of Anesthesiologists physical status I or II [cite: 41]. TEAS group: ASA I=34, ASA II=1; Control group: ASA I=34, ASA II=2 [cite: 41].
* **Comorbidities:** Included patients undergoing general anesthesia for elective gynecological laparoscopic surgery [cite: 41]. Excluded potentially difficult airway, sore throat, history of chronic pain, drug/alcohol abuse, mental disorder, obesity, analgesic drug intake within 48 h before surgery, and previous experience with acupuncture treatment [cite: 41].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Gynecological laparoscopic surgery [cite: 41].
* **Specific procedures:** Ovarian cystectomy (TEAS: 30, Control: 29) and Myomectomy surgery (TEAS: 5, Control: 7) [cite: 41].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** General anesthesia [cite: 41].
* **Induction:** IV sufentanil 0.5 μg/kg and propofol 2.0 mg/kg, facilitated with cisatracurium 0.15 mg/kg [cite: 41].
* **Maintenance:** Sevoflurane 2%-3% according to both hemodynamic parameters and bispectral index (BIS) of 40-60 [cite: 41].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Transcutaneous electric acupoint stimulation (TEAS) [cite: 41].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 35 analyzed [cite: 41].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Bilateral Hegu (LI4), Neiguan (PC6), Zusanli (ST36), and Sanyinjiao (SP6) [cite: 41].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** Dense-disperse frequency of 2/10 Hz [cite: 41].
* **Treatment intensity:** 6-9 mA (optimal intensity adjusted to maintain a slight twitching of the regional muscle according to individual maximum tolerance) [cite: 41].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** Preoperative (in the holding area before the induction of anesthesia) [cite: 41].
* **Duration of each treatment:** 30 minutes [cite: 41].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** One single preoperative session [cite: 41].
* **Total treatment period:** Single day (preoperative) [cite: 41].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** IV tropisetron 5 mg 30 min before the end of surgery for all patients [cite: 41].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Placebo/sham control [cite: 41].
* **Number of participants in control group:** 36 analyzed [cite: 41].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** The patients were connected to the apparatus, but electronic stimulation was not applied [cite: 41].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** IV sufentanil as rescue analgesia [cite: 41].
* **Dosage parameters:** IV sufentanil 0.05 μg/kg [cite: 41].
* **Duration:** In 24 h after surgery [cite: 41].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) [cite: 41].
* **Pain at rest / movement:** Not specifically distinguished [cite: 41].
* **Pain assessment time points:** 0.5 h, 1.0 h, 2.0 h, 4.0 h, 8.0 h, and 24.0 h after surgery [cite: 41].
* **Mean/median pain score for each group:** Visualized in Figure 3 as median and interquartile range (IQR) [cite: 41].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** Postoperative pain scores were significantly reduced in the TEAS group compared with the control group at 2h, 4h, 8h, and 24h after surgery (P < 0.05) [cite: 41].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Sufentanil [cite: 41].
* **Route of administration:** Intravenous [cite: 41].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention group:** The cumulative number of opioids administered was reported as a median (IQR) cumulative number of rescue analgesia: 1 (1-3) [cite: 41].
* **Opioid consumption for control group:** Cumulative number of rescue analgesia: 3.5 (2-7.8) [cite: 41].
* **Measurement time point(s):** In 24 h after surgery [cite: 41].
* **Between-group difference:** The cumulative number of opioids administered (rescue analgesia) was significantly lower in the TEAS group (P = 0.004) [cite: 41].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** All patients who had a VAS score ≥ 4 received rescue analgesia [cite: 41].
* **Between-group difference:** Time to first rescue analgesia (min) was significantly longer in the TEAS group: 59 (31-1440) vs 47 (13-196) (P = 0.039) [cite: 41]. The cumulative number of rescue analgesia was significantly lower in the TEAS group (P = 0.004) [cite: 41].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting:** Incidence of nausea: TEAS group = 17 (48.6%); Control group = 26 (72.2%) (P = 0.041) [cite: 41]. Incidence of vomiting: TEAS group = 7 (20.0%); Control group = 19 (52.8%) (P = 0.004) [cite: 41].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** 
    * Global QoR-40 scores at 24 h after surgery (Primary Outcome): TEAS group = 176.5 (10.2); Control group = 164.8 (14.7) (P < 0.001) [cite: 41].
    * Incidence of dizziness: TEAS group = 14 (40.0%); Control group = 28 (77.8%) (P = 0.001) [cite: 41].
    * Patient's satisfaction score (median (IQR)): TEAS group = 8 (6-8); Control group = 6 (5-7) (P = 0.002) [cite: 41].
* **Length of stay:** Duration of PACU stay (min): TEAS group = 27.8 (8.3); Control group = 35.7 (7.2) (P < 0.001) [cite: 41]. Total length of hospital stay not explicitly reported [cite: 41].
* **Other postoperative complications:** Not explicitly reported, apart from nausea, vomiting, and dizziness [cite: 41].
