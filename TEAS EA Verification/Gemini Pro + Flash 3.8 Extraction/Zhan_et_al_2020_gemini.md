# Data Extraction: covidence_1389_full_article.pdf (Zhan & Tian, 2020)

## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Supported by Project of National Clinical Research Base of Traditional Chinese Medicine in Jiangsu Province, China [cite: 41].
* **Country:** China (Nanjing) [cite: 41].
* **Journal:** European Journal of Integrative Medicine [cite: 41].
* **DOI:** 10.1016/j.eujim.2020.101087 [cite: 41].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ref: Chi CTR-INR-17011598) [cite: 41]. Ethical approval obtained from the Affiliated Hospital of Nanjing University of Chinese Medicine (Approval letter No. 2015NL-130-02) [cite: 41].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Single-blind randomized control trial [cite: 41].
* **Randomization method:** Computer derived randomized serial numbers generated a 1:1:1 allocation [cite: 41]. An independent research assistant informed treating anesthetists based on results [cite: 41].
* **Blinding:** Single-blind [cite: 41]. The assessor, treating anesthetists (performing TAP/TEAS vs those managing PCIA), and statistical analysis were blinded from group allocation [cite: 41]. Patients were implicitly not blinded to the interventions [cite: 41].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** TAP group, TAP + TEAS group, and usual care group [cite: 41].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 91 included initially; 90 completed the study and were analyzed (TAP: n=30, TAP + TEAS: n=30, Usual care: n=30) [cite: 41].
* **Age:** TAP group: 58.4 ± 13.8 years; TAP + TEAS group: 64.6 ± 14.6 years; Usual care group: 59.6 ± 12.2 years (mean ± SD) [cite: 41].
* **Sex distribution:** TAP group: 15 Male / 15 Female; TAP + TEAS group: 15 Male / 15 Female; Usual care group: 18 Male / 12 Female [cite: 41].
* **BMI:** TAP group: 22.9 ± 4.0 kg/m²; TAP + TEAS group: 23.1 ± 2.9 kg/m²; Usual care group: 21.6 ± 3.4 kg/m² [cite: 41].
* **ASA classification:** ASA physical status I-II [cite: 41].
* **Comorbidities:** Included patients aged 18-85 years, weight 45-85 kg, undergoing elective open abdominal surgery (total gastrectomy, partial gastrectomy, radical colectomy) < 5 hours [cite: 41]. Excluded patients with heart failure, severe hypertension, liver and renal failure, hypoalbuminemia, respiratory dysfunction, diabetes mellitus, mental and neurological disorders, alcohol/drug abuse, hearing/communication disorders, allergy to local anesthetics, intraoperative/postoperative complications, and rash/infection over TEAS skin area [cite: 41].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Elective open abdominal surgery [cite: 41].
* **Specific procedures:** Total gastrectomy (TAP: 8, TAP+TEAS: 6, Usual care: 8); Partial gastrectomy (TAP: 12, TAP+TEAS: 13, Usual care: 9); Radical colectomy (TAP: 10, TAP+TEAS: 11, Usual care: 13) [cite: 41].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** General anesthesia [cite: 41].
* **Induction:** Intravascular injection of midazolam 0.05 mg/kg, propofol 2 mg/kg, sufentanil 0.4 μg/kg, vecuronium bromide 0.15 mg/kg [cite: 41].
* **Maintenance:** Inhalation of sevoflurane (1.0%-1.5%) and intravenous infusion of propofol 4-5 mg/kg/h, vecuronium bromide 1-2 μg/kg/min, and remifentanil 0.1-0.2 μg/kg/min [cite: 41].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Transcutaneous electric acupoint stimulation (TEAS) combined with Transverse abdominis plane (TAP) block [cite: 41].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 30 analyzed (TAP + TEAS group) [cite: 41].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Zusanli (ST36) and Neiguan (PC6) [cite: 41].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** Sparse-dense wave stimulation, frequency of 2-100 Hz [cite: 41].
* **Treatment intensity:** Increased gradually from zero to a strong but comfortable level [cite: 41].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** Before leaving PACU and at 24 h after surgery [cite: 41]. Administrated after TAP was done [cite: 41].
* **Duration of each treatment:** 30 minutes [cite: 41].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** Two sessions (before leaving PACU and at 24 h post-surgery) [cite: 41].
* **Total treatment period:** Up to 24 hours postoperatively [cite: 41].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** Bilateral TAP block performed at the end of surgery under ultrasonography guidance using 20 ml of 0.375% ropivacaine infiltrated at both sides [cite: 41]. Patient-controlled intravenous analgesia (PCIA) [cite: 41]. 100 mg flurbiprofen axetil before the end of surgery [cite: 41].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** TAP block alone (TAP group) and Usual care alone (Usual care group) [cite: 41].
* **Number of participants in control group:** TAP group: 30 analyzed; Usual care group: 30 analyzed [cite: 41].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** 
    * TAP group received bilateral TAP blocks at the end of surgery and PCIA, but no TEAS [cite: 41].
    * Usual care group received only PCIA for postoperative analgesia (no TAP, no TEAS) [cite: 41].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** Patient-controlled intravenous analgesia (PCIA) [cite: 41].
* **Dosage parameters:** 100 μg sufentanil and 6 mg tropisetron hydrochloride per 100 ml of solution [cite: 41]. Basal infusion rate was 2 ml per hour [cite: 41].
* **Duration:** Continuously administered for 48 h [cite: 41].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Visual analogue scale (VAS) (0-100 mm scale reported, but median values in Table 2 are 2-3, suggesting a 0-10 scale was used for data representation) [cite: 41].
* **Pain at rest / movement:** Not specifically distinguished [cite: 41].
* **Pain assessment time points:** 2h, 24h, and 48h after surgery [cite: 41].
* **Median pain score for each group (Median (mean ± SD)):**
    * 2h: TAP = 2 (1.7 ± 3.0); TAP+TEAS = 2 (2.0 ± 3.0); Usual care = 2 (2.0 ± 3.0) (P = 0.82) [cite: 41].
    * 24h: TAP = 2 (2.0 ± 3.0); TAP+TEAS = 2 (1.7 ± 2.2); Usual care = 3 (2.0 ± 4.0) (P < 0.0001) [cite: 41].
    * 48h: TAP = 2 (2.0 ± 3.0); TAP+TEAS = 2 (1.7 ± 2.0); Usual care = 3 (2.0 ± 3.2) (P = 0.007) [cite: 41].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** Compared with the usual care group, VAS at 24 h in the TAP and TAP+TEAS groups were significantly decreased [cite: 41]. VAS at 48 h in the TAP+TEAS group was lower than the usual care group (P = 0.004) [cite: 41]. The TAP+TEAS group had lower VAS scores at both 24 and 48h compared with the TAP group (P = 0.03, P = 0.03) [cite: 41].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Sufentanil (via PCIA) [cite: 41].
* **Route of administration:** Intravenous [cite: 41].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention group:** Not reported separately (PCIA usage amounts were not detailed) [cite: 41].
* **Opioid consumption for control group:** Not reported separately [cite: 41].
* **Measurement time point(s):** N/A.
* **Between-group difference:** Not reported [cite: 41].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** Not reported [cite: 41]. Postoperatively, patients were discouraged from receiving any analgesic treatments except for PCIA, TAP, and TEAS [cite: 41].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting and other side effects:**
    * Dizziness (Total across 72h): TAP+TEAS = 4 (13.3%); TAP = 12 (40.0%); Usual care = 18 (60.0%) (P = 0.001) [cite: 41].
    * Nausea/Vomiting (Total across 72h): TAP+TEAS = 1 (3.3%); TAP = 8 (26.7%); Usual care = 13 (43.3%) (P = 0.001) [cite: 41].
    * Compared with usual care, the TAP+TEAS group showed significantly less anesthesia-related adverse reactions at 24, 48, and 72 h (P < 0.0001) [cite: 41]. Incidence in the TAP+TEAS group was also significantly lower than the TAP group (P = 0.02, P = 0.026) [cite: 41].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** Hemodynamic stability: SBP and HR were significantly elevated before leaving PACU in the usual care group (P = 0.04), while there were no significant changes in the TAP and TAP+TEAS groups [cite: 41]. Hemodynamics in the TAP+TEAS group were more stable than in the usual care group before leaving PACU (P = 0.02) [cite: 41].
* **Length of stay:** Not reported [cite: 41].
* **Other postoperative complications:** Not reported [cite: 41].
