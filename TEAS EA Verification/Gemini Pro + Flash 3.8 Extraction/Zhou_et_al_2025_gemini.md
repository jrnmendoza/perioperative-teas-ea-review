# Data Extraction: 105119.pdf (Zhou et al., 2025)

## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Supported by the Guangxi Medical and Health Appropriate Technology Development and Promotion Application Project (S2020108); Self-financed scientific research projects of the Administration of Traditional Chinese Medicine of the Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region (GXZYZ20210390); Middle-aged Teachers in Colleges and Universities in Guangxi (2020KY12023) [cite: 39].
* **Country:** China (Guilin, Guangxi) [cite: 39].
* **Journal:** Therapeutics and Clinical Risk Management [cite: 39].
* **DOI:** Not explicitly stated with standard prefix in the text, but the URL https://doi.org/10.2147/TCRM.S507856 is present in the document footer [cite: 39].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2200055338) [cite: 39]. Approved by the Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Guilin Medical University (NO. ZYYJ_2021001) [cite: 39].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Single-center, randomized, double-blinded, sham-controlled trial [cite: 39].
* **Randomization method:** Computer-generated randomization sequence [cite: 39]. Allocation was concealed using sequentially numbered, sealed, opaque envelopes [cite: 39].
* **Blinding:** Double-blinded [cite: 39]. Patients, anesthesiologists, surgeons, and data collectors remained blinded to group allocation [cite: 39]. The acupuncturist administering TEAS could not be blinded [cite: 39].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** TEAS group and Sham group [cite: 39].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 127 assessed for eligibility; 100 randomized; 97 completed and analyzed (TEAS group: n=48; Sham group: n=49) [cite: 39].
* **Age:** TEAS group: 43.92 ± 8.84 years; Sham group: 40.98 ± 7.90 years (mean ± SD) [cite: 39].
* **Sex distribution:** 100% Female (undergoing gynecological laparoscopic surgery) [cite: 39].
* **BMI:** Not explicitly reported (Height and weight reported: Height (cm) TEAS = 157.30 ± 5.32, Sham = 156.10 ± 6.85; Weight (kg) TEAS = 55.78 ± 7.52, Sham = 55.98 ± 11.04) [cite: 39].
* **ASA classification:** ASA physical status I-III [cite: 39]. TEAS group (I/II/III): 19/26/3; Sham group (I/II/III): 15/32/2 [cite: 39].
* **Comorbidities:** Included patients 18-65 years old undergoing elective gynecological laparoscopic surgery [cite: 39]. Excluded patients with severe cardiovascular, respiratory, hepatic, or renal dysfunction; history of neurological or psychiatric disorders or long-term use of sedative medications; severe cognitive/visual/hearing impairment or motor disorders; and skin infection/damage/scarring at acupoint sites [cite: 39].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Gynecological laparoscopic surgery [cite: 39].
* **Specific procedures:** Ovarian and Tubal Surgery (TEAS: 19, Sham: 20); Uterine Surgery (TEAS: 17, Sham: 18); Gynecologic Oncologic Surgery (TEAS: 8, Sham: 7); Other Surgeries (TEAS: 4, Sham: 4) [cite: 39].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** General anesthesia with endotracheal intubation [cite: 39].
* **Induction:** Midazolam (0.05 mg/kg), etomidate (0.2 mg/kg), cisatracurium (0.2 mg/kg), and sufentanil (0.5 μg/kg) [cite: 39].
* **Maintenance:** Continuous infusion of propofol (4-10 mg/kg/h) and remifentanil (0.1-0.4 μg/kg/min), supplemented with intermittent sevoflurane inhalation (1.0-2.0%) [cite: 39]. Adjusted to keep BIS values between 40 and 60 [cite: 39].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Transcutaneous Electrical Acupoint Stimulation (TEAS) [cite: 39].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 48 analyzed [cite: 39].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Bilateral Hegu (LI4), Neiguan (PC6), Zusanli (ST36), and Sanyinjiao (SP6) [cite: 39].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** Alternating dense-disperse wave mode at 2/10 Hz [cite: 39].
* **Treatment intensity:** 10-15 mA [cite: 39]. The optimal intensity for each patient was determined prior to anesthesia induction by gradually increasing current until a strong but comfortable "De Qi" sensation was experienced, which was then applied in the PACU [cite: 39].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** During the post-anesthesia recovery period in the post-anesthesia care unit (PACU) [cite: 39].
* **Duration of each treatment:** 30 minutes [cite: 39].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** One single postoperative session [cite: 39].
* **Total treatment period:** Single day (postoperative in PACU) [cite: 39].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** Flurbiprofen axetil (50 mg), sufentanil (0.1 μg/kg), and tropisetron (5 mg) given 30 minutes prior to the conclusion of surgery [cite: 39]. Ropivacaine (0.5%, 10 mL) infiltrated at the abdominal incision site [cite: 39].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Sham TEAS [cite: 39].
* **Number of participants in control group:** 49 analyzed [cite: 39].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** Sham TEAS performed using the same protocol as real TEAS, but without electrical current output from the stimulator [cite: 39].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** Intravenous tramadol as needed [cite: 39]. Local ropivacaine infiltration and intraoperative flurbiprofen/sufentanil as described above [cite: 39]. No PCA was mentioned [cite: 39].
* **Dosage parameters:** Intravenous tramadol (50-100 mg) every 6 hours as needed [cite: 39].
* **Duration:** Postoperative period [cite: 39].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Visual Analog Scale (VAS) (0-10 reported as median) [cite: 39].
* **Pain at rest / movement:** Measured at rest and during exercise (coughing and movement) [cite: 39].
* **Pain assessment time points:** Preoperatively and on POD 1, 2, and 3 [cite: 39].
* **Mean/median pain score for each group (Median (25th-75th percentile)):**
    * Resting VAS POD 1: TEAS = 3 (3-4); Sham = 4 (4-5) (P < 0.001) [cite: 39].
    * Resting VAS POD 2: TEAS = 2 (1-2); Sham = 2 (2-2) (P = 0.675) [cite: 39].
    * Exercise VAS POD 1: TEAS = 4 (4-5); Sham = 5 (4-6) (P = 0.008) [cite: 39].
    * Exercise VAS POD 2: TEAS = 2 (2-3); Sham = 4 (3-4) (P = 0.036) [cite: 39].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** The TEAS group had significantly lower resting VAS scores on POD 1 and lower exercise VAS scores on POD 1 and 2 compared to the Sham group [cite: 39].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Sufentanil and remifentanil used intraoperatively [cite: 39]. Tramadol used postoperatively [cite: 39].
* **Route of administration:** Intravenous [cite: 39].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention group:** Not reported separately (total mg/μg values not provided) [cite: 39].
* **Opioid consumption for control group:** Not reported separately [cite: 39].
* **Measurement time point(s):** N/A.
* **Between-group difference:** Not reported [cite: 39].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** Rescue analgesia (within the first three postoperative days): TEAS = 6 (12.5%); Sham = 13 (26.5%) [cite: 39].
* **Between-group difference:** The TEAS group exhibited a lower proportion of patients requiring rescue analgesia, although this difference did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.124) [cite: 39].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting:** 
    * PONV incidence on POD 1: TEAS = 10 (20.8%); Sham = 19 (38.7%) (P = 0.047) [cite: 39]. No significant differences on POD 2 or 3 [cite: 39].
    * Rescue antiemetics required (within first 3 days): TEAS = 10 (20.8%); Sham = 20 (40.8%) (P = 0.033) [cite: 39].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes (Primary Outcome):** 
    * QoR-15 Global score on POD 1: TEAS = 112.00 ± 10.67; Sham = 107.62 ± 10.27 (P = 0.042) [cite: 39].
    * QoR-15 on POD 1 showed significant improvements in emotional state, physical comfort, and pain dimensions in the TEAS group [cite: 39].
    * Time to first flatus (h): TEAS = 14.10 ± 3.19; Sham = 15.88 ± 3.78 (P = 0.014) [cite: 39].
    * Time to first ambulation (h): TEAS = 17.50 ± 3.40; Sham = 19.42 ± 4.03 (P = 0.013) [cite: 39].
    * Time to awakening and extubation did not differ significantly [cite: 39].
* **Length of stay:** Not reported [cite: 39].
* **Other postoperative complications:** Adverse events (dizziness, headache, hypertension, hypotension, hypoxemia) were comparable between groups with no statistically significant differences. No local skin irritation was noted in the TEAS group [cite: 39].
