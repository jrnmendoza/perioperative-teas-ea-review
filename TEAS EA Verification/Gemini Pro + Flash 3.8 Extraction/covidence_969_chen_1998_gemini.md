## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** Supported in part by funds from the Ambulatory Anesthesia Research Foundation of Texas [cite: 9].
* **Country:** United States (Dallas, Texas and Los Angeles, California) [cite: 9].
* **Journal:** Anesthesia & Analgesia (Anesth Analg) [cite: 9].
* **DOI:** Not explicitly reported (published 1998) [cite: 9].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** Not reported [cite: 9].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Randomized, single-blind, sham-controlled study [cite: 9].
* **Randomization method:** Randomly assigned to one of four treatment groups (method of generation not specified) [cite: 9].
* **Blinding:** Single-blind; a blind observer who was not aware of the TENS treatment group recorded PCA demands and doses [cite: 9].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** Group I = sham-TENS (no electrical current) at Zusanli acupoints; Group II = nonacupoint-TENS at the shoulders; Group III = dermatomal-TENS at the level of the surgical incision; Group IV = acupoint-TENS at the Zusanli acupoints [cite: 9].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 100 enrolled patients, with 25 randomly assigned to each of the four groups [cite: 9].
* **Age:** Group I: 45 ± 12 years; Group II: 44 ± 13 years; Group III: 44 ± 13 years; Group IV: 43 ± 13 years [cite: 9].
* **Sex distribution:** 100% Female [cite: 9].
* **BMI:** Weight was reported as Group I: 68 ± 15 kg; Group II: 72 ± 14 kg; Group III: 69 ± 16 kg; Group IV: 70 ± 12 kg (BMI not reported) [cite: 9].
* **ASA classification:** ASA physical status I or II [cite: 9].
* **Comorbidities:** Patients with a history of narcotic abuse, extreme sensitivity to opioid-related side effects, previous experience with acupuncture-like therapies, or clinically significant cardiovascular, pulmonary, renal, hepatic, and neurologic diseases were excluded [cite: 9].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Elective abdominal hysterectomy or myomectomy procedures [cite: 9].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** Standardized general anesthesia [cite: 9].
* **Induction:** Fentanyl 1-2 μg/kg IV and thiopental 4 mg/kg IV [cite: 9].
* **Maintenance:** Desflurane 3%-5% and nitrous oxide 67% in oxygen [cite: 9].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS) [cite: 9].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** Group III (dermatomal) n=25; Group IV (acupoint) n=25 [cite: 9].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Zusanli (ST36) acupoints bilaterally for Group IV, and adjacent dermatomal levels across the surgical incision for Group III [cite: 9].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** Standard dense-and-disperse (D-D) mode at a frequency setting of 2/100 Hz (alternating 2 Hz and 100 Hz every 3 s) [cite: 9].
* **Treatment intensity:** Set at 9-12 mA depending on patient tolerance for Groups II, III, and IV [cite: 9].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** Initiated when the patient arrived in the postanesthesia care unit (PACU) [cite: 9].
* **Duration of each treatment:** 30 minutes per session [cite: 9].
* **Number/frequency of treatment sessions:** Instructed to use the TENS device every 2-3 hours while awake, at bedtime, and on waking in the morning [cite: 9].
* **Total treatment period:** TENS therapy was discontinued when patients were able to control their pain with oral analgesic medication (mean duration of TENS therapy ranged from 31 ± 7 to 36 ± 10 hours across groups) [cite: 9].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** Patient-controlled analgesia (PCA) [cite: 9].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Sham-TENS and Nonacupoint-TENS [cite: 9].
* **Number of participants in control group:** Group I (sham) n=25; Group II (nonacupoint) n=25 [cite: 9].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** Group I received TENS pads at Zusanli points with an intensity of 0 mA (no electrical current) but functional indicator lights [cite: 9]. Group II received functional TENS at a nonacupoint location (bilaterally at the deltoid region of the shoulder) at 9-12 mA [cite: 9].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** PCA device containing a standardized solution of hydromorphone (HM; 1 mg/mL) [cite: 9]. 
* **Dosage parameters:** Bolus doses of HM 0.2-0.4 mg IV on demand, minimal lockout interval of 10 min, and maximal hourly dose of 6.0 mg [cite: 9].
* **Duration:** PCA therapy was discontinued when the patient no longer required IV therapy and pain was controllable with oral analgesic drugs [cite: 9].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Standard 100-mm visual analog scales (VAS) [cite: 9].
* **Pain at rest / movement:** VAS assessed adverse feelings associated with the surgical incision [cite: 9].
* **Pain assessment time points:** Baseline (before midazolam premedication) and at 24-h, 48-h, and 72-h intervals after the operation [cite: 9].
* **Mean/median pain score for each group:** VAS scores for pain are not numerically tabulated, but global adequacy of pain relief via a posttherapy questionnaire indicated that 88%-96% of patients reported their pain was "adequately" treated across all groups [cite: 9].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** The number of patients who felt TENS decreased their postoperative pain was significantly larger in Groups III and IV (10 and 8, respectively) compared with Groups I and II (2 and 2, respectively) (P < 0.05) [cite: 9].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Hydromorphone (HM) [cite: 9].
* **Route of administration:** Intravenous via PCA [cite: 9].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention group:** Total HM in 24 h for Group III was 6.8 ± 4.2 mg; Total HM in 24 h for Group IV was 6.5 ± 3.5 mg [cite: 9].
* **Opioid consumption for control group:** Total HM in 24 h for Group I (sham) was 10.7 ± 5.0 mg; Total HM in 24 h for Group II (nonacupoint) was 10.5 ± 6.2 mg [cite: 9].
* **Measurement time point(s):** In PACU, 0-24 h, and 24-48 h [cite: 9].
* **Between-group difference:** The total dosage of HM administered in the first 24 h was significantly decreased in Groups III and IV compared with Groups I and II (P < 0.05) [cite: 9].
* **Percentage/relative reduction:** In the first 24 h postoperatively, opioid requirements in Groups III and IV were decreased by 37% and 39%, respectively, compared with the control (sham) group, and 35% and 38%, respectively, compared with Group II [cite: 9].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** Addressed primarily via PCA demands [cite: 9]. Supplemental bolus doses of HM 0.5-1 mg IM were administered if patients were unable to achieve adequate pain relief via PCA [cite: 9]. There were no differences among the four groups with respect to the number of patients requiring supplemental injections of opioid or nonopioid oral analgesics [cite: 9].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting:** Incidence of nausea (0-24 h) was 88% in Group I, 88% in Group II, 60% in Group III, and 64% in Group IV [cite: 9]. Incidence of nausea was significantly decreased in Groups III and IV compared with Groups I and II (P < 0.05) [cite: 9].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** VAS sedation score at 24 h was significantly smaller in Groups III (35 ± 25) and IV (31 ± 25) compared with Groups I (56 ± 29) and II (53 ± 31) [cite: 9]. The duration of PCA use was significantly shorter in Groups III (30 ± 8 h) and IV (31 ± 10 h) than in Groups I (38 ± 9 h) and II (37 ± 9 h) [cite: 9].
* **Other postoperative complications:** Incidence of dizziness (0-24 h) was significantly reduced in Groups III (44%) and IV (48%) compared with Groups I (80%) and II (76%) (P < 0.05) [cite: 9]. 
* **Length of stay:** Length of hospitalization was 4 ± 2 days in Group I, 3 ± 2 days in Group II, 3 ± 1 days in Group III, and 3 ± 2 days in Group IV (no significant difference) [cite: 9].
