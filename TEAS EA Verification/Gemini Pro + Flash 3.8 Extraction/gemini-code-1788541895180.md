# Data Extraction: Thoracic Cancer - 2020 - Chen - Efficacy of transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation combined with general anesthesia.pdf

## Study details
* **Sponsorship/funding source:** National Basic Research Development Program (973 Program) of China (2013CB531905), National Key R&D Program of China (2017YFC0907504), Beijing Municipal Administration of Hospitals Special Fund for Digestive System Multi-disciplinary Synergetic Development (XXT18), and Beijing Municipal Administration of Hospitals Youth Program Grant (QML20171106)[cite: 7].
* **Country:** China[cite: 7].
* **Journal:** Thoracic Cancer[cite: 7].
* **DOI:** 10.1111/1759-7714.13343[cite: 7].
* **Clinical trial registration/registration number:** Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (http://www.chictr.org.cn), accession number ChiCTR1800016067[cite: 7].

## Methodology
* **Study design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study with a pre- and a post-test design[cite: 7].
* **Randomization method:** Computer-based sample randomization with allocation code generated by an independent statistician[cite: 7].
* **Blinding:** Double-blind; anesthesiologists, surgeons, physicians in the post-anesthesia care unit, and patients were unaware of the allocation[cite: 7].
* **Group allocation / study groups:** TEAS group (acupuncture-drug compound general anesthesia) and sham-TEAS group[cite: 7].

## Study population
* **Number of participants:** 80 patients completed the study and were analyzed (TEAS: n=40; Sham-TEAS: n=40)[cite: 7].
* **Age:** TEAS group: 56.0 ± 3.7 years; Sham-TEAS group: 55.8 ± 3.2 years[cite: 7].
* **Sex distribution:** TEAS group: 17 Female / 23 Male; Sham-TEAS group: 18 Female / 22 Male[cite: 7].
* **BMI:** TEAS group: 23.5 ± 0.9 kg/m²; Sham-TEAS group: 23.4 ± 1.0 kg/m²[cite: 7].
* **ASA classification:** ASA status I or II[cite: 7]. Patients with ASA status III, IV, or V were excluded[cite: 7].
* **Comorbidities:** Patients were excluded if they had severe comorbidities (pre-existing coagulopathy, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, diabetes), history of chronic pain, or history of opioid or alcohol abuse[cite: 7].

## Type of surgery / surgical method
* **Type of surgery:** Video-assisted thoracic surgery (VATS) pulmonary resection (single or bilobectomy) for early-stage lung cancer[cite: 7].

## Type of anesthesia
* **Type of anesthesia:** General anesthesia[cite: 7].
* **Induction:** Pre-induction intravenous drip of midazolam (0.04-0.06 mg/kg); induction with 0.3-0.5 μg/kg sufentanil, 2 mg/kg propofol, and 0.6-1 mg/kg rocuronium bromide[cite: 7].
* **Maintenance:** Target-controlled infusion of propofol (adjusted to maintain BIS 40-60) and sufentanil (adjusted based on hemodynamics), with intermittent rocuronium bromide[cite: 7].

## Intervention
* **Type of acupuncture/intervention:** Transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS)[cite: 7].
* **Number of participants in intervention group:** 40[cite: 7].
* **Acupuncture points used:** Bilateral Hegu (LI 4), Neiguan (PC 6), Houxi (SI 3), and Zhigou (TE 6)[cite: 7].
* **Electrical stimulation frequency/parameters:** 2/100 Hz[cite: 7].
* **Treatment intensity:** 10-15 mA for pre- and postoperative stimulations; 30 mA for intraoperative stimulation[cite: 7].
* **Timing relative to surgery:** 30 minutes before anesthetic induction, continuously throughout the surgical procedure, and intermittently at 6, 24, and 48 hours after surgery[cite: 7].
* **Duration of each treatment:** 30 minutes for pre-induction; continuously during surgery; 30 minutes per session postoperatively[cite: 7].
* **Total treatment period:** Preoperative up to 48 hours postoperative[cite: 7].
* **Additional/co-interventions:** Patient-controlled intravenous analgesia (PCIA) connected 30 minutes before the end of the procedure[cite: 7].

## Comparator/control
* **Type of control:** Sham TEAS[cite: 7].
* **Number of participants in control group:** 40[cite: 7].
* **Details of sham/control procedure:** 4 mA stimulation for 30 minutes before and after surgery (at 6, 24, and 48 hours); no electrical stimulation was applied during the surgery[cite: 7].

## Analgesic regimen/common perioperative treatment
* **Analgesic regimen:** PCIA pump with sufentanil (1.5 μg/mL)[cite: 7]. 30 minutes before surgery end, 10 μg sufentanil was administered for transition[cite: 7].
* **PCIA parameters:** Priming volume: 0, PCIA dose: 3 mL, background dose: 2 mL/h, interval: 15 minutes[cite: 7].
* **Duration:** Maintained for 2 days postoperatively[cite: 7].

## Postoperative pain
* **Pain measurement instrument:** Visual analogue scale (VAS)[cite: 7].
* **Pain assessment time points:** 6, 24, 48 hours, and 1 month after surgery[cite: 7].
* **Mean pain score for each group:** 
    * 6 hours: TEAS = 3.21 ± 0.47; Sham-TEAS = 5.78 ± 0.80[cite: 7].
    * 24 hours: TEAS = 2.91 ± 0.2; Sham-TEAS = 5.97 ± 0.56[cite: 7].
    * 48 hours: TEAS = 1.69 ± 0.66; Sham-TEAS = 2.92 ± 0.38[cite: 7].
    * 1 month: TEAS = 1.49 ± 0.41; Sham-TEAS = 4.48 ± 0.86[cite: 7].
* **Between-group difference/statistical significance:** The VAS scores in the TEAS group were significantly lower than those in the sham-TEAS group at all time points (P < 0.001)[cite: 7].

## Opioid/analgesic consumption
* **Opioid used:** Sufentanil[cite: 7].
* **Route of administration:** Intravenous via PCIA[cite: 7].
* **Opioid consumption for intervention group:** 
    * 6 hours: 46.85 ± 5.65 μg[cite: 7].
    * 24 hours: 72.43 ± 4.78 μg[cite: 7].
    * 48 hours: 118.52 ± 9.77 μg[cite: 7].
* **Opioid consumption for control group:** 
    * 6 hours: 60.08 ± 6.91 μg[cite: 7].
    * 24 hours: 100.62 ± 10.20 μg[cite: 7].
    * 48 hours: 140.15 ± 7.87 μg[cite: 7].
* **Measurement time point(s):** Cumulative at 6, 24, and 48 hours[cite: 7].
* **Between-group difference:** Sufentanil consumption during PCIA was significantly lower in the TEAS group at 6, 24, and 48 hours (P < 0.001)[cite: 7].
* **Percentage/relative reduction:** The study reported that TEAS reduced postoperative sufentanil consumption by almost 16%[cite: 7].

## Rescue analgesia requirement
* **Rescue analgesia requirement:** Measured by the number of total and effective PCIA pump attempts[cite: 7].
* **Number/proportion requiring rescue analgesia:** 
    * Mean total attempts: TEAS = 4.83 ± 1.06 vs. Sham-TEAS = 14.05 ± 2.01 (P < 0.001)[cite: 7].
    * Mean effective attempts: TEAS = 4.58 ± 0.93 vs. Sham-TEAS = 7.48 ± 1.18 (P < 0.001)[cite: 7].

## Other postoperative outcomes
* **PONV/nausea/vomiting:** 
    * Incidence of nausea was significantly lower in the TEAS group at 0, 6, 24, and 48 hours (all P < 0.001)[cite: 7]. 
    * Incidence of vomiting was significantly lower in the TEAS group at 0 hours (0% vs 12.5%, P = 0.03), 6 hours (15.0% vs 35.0%, P = 0.04), and 24 hours (2.5% vs 27.5%, P < 0.001); no significant difference at 48 hours[cite: 7].
* **Recovery/quality-of-recovery outcomes:** Postoperative Observer's Assessment of Alertness/Sedation (OAAS) scores were similar between the groups at 6, 24, and 48 hours[cite: 7].
* **Other relevant physiological/biochemical outcomes:** Pre-induction BIS scores at 10, 20, and 30 minutes after starting TEAS were significantly lower in the TEAS group than in the sham-TEAS group (P < 0.001), indicating a sedative effect[cite: 7].
