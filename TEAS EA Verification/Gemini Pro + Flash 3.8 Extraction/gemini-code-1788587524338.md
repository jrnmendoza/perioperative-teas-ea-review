## STEP 2 — COMPLETE ALL RoB 2 SIGNALING QUESTIONS

### DOMAIN 1 — Risk of bias arising from the randomization process

**1.1 Was the allocation sequence random?**
* **Response:** Y
* **Evidence:** "Eligible participants were randomly assigned to one of the following four groups using a computer-generated randomization sequence and secure code"[cite: 21].
* **Reasoning:** Computer-generated sequence is valid.

**1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**
* **Response:** Y
* **Evidence:** "Each number was printed and stored in sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes. Eligible participants chose one envelope, which was opened by the acupuncturist who recorded the treatment allocation..."[cite: 21].
* **Reasoning:** Sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes provide adequate concealment.

**1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**
* **Response:** N
* **Evidence:** No imbalances reported; the randomization procedure appears robust.
* **Reasoning:** Proper randomization and concealment usually ensure balance.

**Domain 1 judgment:** LOW RISK
**Domain 1 rationale:** Valid sequence generation and robust allocation concealment methods were utilized.

---

### DOMAIN 2 — Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions

**2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial?**
* **Response:** Y
* **Evidence:** "Participants were instructed not to disclose their treatment."[cite: 21]. Group Usual received "usual care" without a sham EA procedure, meaning patients knew if they received acupuncture or not.
* **Reasoning:** The lack of a sham control means participants were unblinded.

**2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants' assigned intervention?**
* **Response:** Y
* **Evidence:** The acupuncturist delivered the treatment[cite: 21].
* **Reasoning:** Care providers were unblinded.

**2.3 If participants or carers were aware of intervention assignment, were there deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the experimental context?**
* **Response:** NI
* **Evidence:** Insufficient information / not reported in the available sources.

**2.4 Were these deviations likely to have affected the outcome?**
* **Response:** NA

**2.5 Were these deviations balanced between groups?**
* **Response:** NA

**2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention?**
* **Response:** Y
* **Evidence:** The denominator for nausea/vomiting incidence remains 400[cite: 21], suggesting ITT analysis without major exclusions.

**2.7 If an inappropriate analysis was used, was there potential for a substantial impact on the result?**
* **Response:** NA

**Domain 2 judgment:** SOME CONCERNS
**Domain 2 rationale:** The trial was unblinded (usual care control), raising some concerns for subjective outcomes, though no obvious deviations are reported.

---

### DOMAIN 4 — Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?**
* **Response:** N
* **Evidence:** Incidence of nausea reported[cite: 21].
* **Reasoning:** Standard outcome measurement.

**4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?**
* **Response:** N
* **Evidence:** Same parameters measured across all groups.

**4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received?**
* **Response:** Y
* **Evidence:** Nausea is patient-reported, and patients were aware they did not receive sham acupuncture (Group Usual received usual care)[cite: 21].
* **Reasoning:** For patient-reported subjective outcomes, unblinded patients mean the assessor is unblinded.

**4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**
* **Response:** PY
* **Evidence:** Nausea is highly subjective.
* **Reasoning:** Knowing one received an active acupuncture treatment versus usual care could introduce a placebo effect, influencing self-reported nausea.

**4.5 Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**
* **Response:** PY
* **Evidence:** Subjective nature of nausea.
* **Reasoning:** Without a sham control, placebo effects are likely in acupuncture trials for subjective symptoms like nausea.

**Domain 4 judgment:** HIGH RISK
**Domain 4 rationale:** The outcome (nausea) is highly subjective, and participants (who act as assessors) were unblinded because the control group received usual care rather than a sham intervention. This creates a high risk of measurement bias (placebo effect).
