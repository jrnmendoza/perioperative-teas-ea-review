### DOMAIN 1 — Risk of bias arising from the randomization process
**1.1 Was the allocation sequence random?**
* **Response:** Y
* **Evidence:** "A randomization sequence was generated by Statistical Product and Service Solutions (SPSS 25.0) software"[cite: 8].
* **Reasoning:** A computer-generated sequence is valid.
**1.2 Was the allocation sequence concealed until participants were enrolled and assigned to interventions?**
* **Response:** NI
* **Evidence:** "the allocation code was performed by an independent technician"[cite: 8].
* **Reasoning:** The physical/logistical mechanism of concealment at enrollment is not specified.
**1.3 Did baseline differences between intervention groups suggest a problem with the randomization process?**
* **Response:** N
* **Evidence:** Baseline characteristics (Age, Race, Gender, BMI, MMSE, MoCA, etc.) showed no significant differences (P > 0.05)[cite: 8].
**Domain 1 judgment:** SOME CONCERNS
**Domain 1 rationale:** Sequence generation was adequate and groups were balanced, but allocation concealment methods are not explicitly described.

### DOMAIN 2 — Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions
**2.1 Were participants aware of their assigned intervention during the trial?**
* **Response:** PN
* **Evidence:** A sham-TEAS group was used[cite: 8].
* **Reasoning:** Use of a sham device generally implies participants were blinded, though efficacy is not explicitly confirmed in the snippet.
**2.2 Were carers and people delivering the interventions aware of participants' assigned intervention?**
* **Response:** NI
* **Evidence:** Insufficient information in the snippet[cite: 8].
**2.3 If participants or carers were aware of intervention assignment, were there deviations from intended intervention that arose because of the experimental context?**
* **Response:** NI
**2.6 Was an appropriate analysis used to estimate the effect of assignment to intervention?**
* **Response:** N
* **Evidence:** 128 enrolled, but only 110 analyzed. Data from 18 dropouts were "excluded from the analyses"[cite: 8].
* **Reasoning:** Not a strict intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis.
**2.7 If an inappropriate analysis was used, was there potential for a substantial impact on the result?**
* **Response:** PN
* **Evidence:** The analyzed groups were perfectly balanced (55 vs 55)[cite: 8].
**Domain 2 judgment:** LOW RISK
**Domain 2 rationale:** Exclusions were balanced, and a sham was used to blind participants, mitigating substantial bias.

### DOMAIN 4 — Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome
