### DOMAIN 4 — Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome

**4.1 Was the method of measuring the outcome inappropriate?**
* **Response:** N
* **Evidence:** Incidence of abdominal distension[cite: 16].
* **Reasoning:** Abdominal distension is a standard, appropriate clinical metric for postoperative gastrointestinal recovery.

**4.2 Could measurement or ascertainment of the outcome have differed between intervention groups?**
* **Response:** N
* **Evidence:** The outcome is a standard milestone assessed postoperatively for both groups[cite: 16].

**4.3 Were outcome assessors aware of the intervention received?**
* **Response:** Y
* **Evidence:** The comparator was "usual care"[cite: 16]. 
* **Reasoning:** Abdominal distension relies heavily on patient-reported symptomology. Because the trial lacked a sham group, the patients (the primary assessors of this symptom) were aware of their intervention.

**4.4 Could assessment of the outcome have been influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**
* **Response:** PY
* **Evidence:** Patients knew they were receiving electroacupuncture aimed at improving recovery[cite: 16].
* **Reasoning:** Knowledge of receiving an active, complementary treatment versus usual care can introduce a placebo effect, making patients hyper-vigilant or biased in reporting subjective symptoms like distension.

**4.5 Is it likely that assessment of the outcome was influenced by knowledge of intervention received?**
* **Response:** PY
* **Evidence:** The trial lacked blinding entirely[cite: 16].
* **Reasoning:** In an open-label trial with a usual care control, subjective, patient-reported outcomes like distension are highly susceptible to performance and measurement biases.

**Domain 4 judgment:** HIGH RISK
**Domain 4 rationale:** The outcome relies heavily on patient reporting. Because patients were completely unblinded (usual care control), their knowledge of the intervention likely influenced the assessment of this subjective symptom.
