KEYWORDS            Postoperative nausea and vomiting; transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation; hepatectomy; clinical trial

Postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) prophylaxis is a key component of enhanced recovery
after surgery (ERAS). However, despite the widespread use of double or triple antiemetic therapy, the
incidence of PONV remains high in specific patient populations [                                                                   1]. Epidemiological data from                               The
Lancet reveal that China carries the highest global burden of hepatitis B virus (HBV), accounting for
nearly one-third of the world’s 240 million chronic HBV infections [1]. Without intervention, 20%–30%
of chronic hepatitis B cases progress along the clinical spectrum to cirrhosis and/or hepatocellular
carcinoma, frequently requiring surgical management [                                                        2]. In patients undergoing hepatic resection,
PONV may exacerbate hemorrhagic complications and extend hospital stays [1]. Notably, a prospective
cohort study reported a PONV incidence of 53.3% following hepatectomy [                                                                               3].
     Recent studies suggest that acupuncture-related therapies, particularly transcutaneous electrical
acupoint stimulation (TEAS), may offer perioperative benefits, including PONV mitigation and pres                                                                                                    -
ervation of gastrointestinal function [                                   4,5]. However, there are research gaps in TEAS being used for

                                                            METHODS
Study Design and Patients
This multicenter, prospective, randomized clinical trial was conducted in 3 tertiary hospitals and has
been approved by the institutional review board of each center (Xijing Hospital, the First Affiliated
Hospital of Henan University of Chinese Medicine, Qinghai Provincial People’s Hospital). The study
was registered at ClinicalTrial.gov (NCT 05396716). Written informed consent was obtained from all
participants.
   Eligible patients were those who were scheduled for elective hepatectomy under general anesthesia.
Exclusion criteria included age younger than 18                                                     years old, body mass index < 18                                                                                                     kg/m2 or >30                                                          kg/m2,
American society of anesthesiologists (ASA) status         >       III, contraindications to TEAS (skin damage or
infection at the stimulation site, implanted electronic devices), drug or alcohol abuse, history of
neurological or psychiatric disorders, or pregnant/lactating women.


Randomization and Blinding
Participants were randomly allocated (1:1 ratio) to either the TEAS group or the control group
through a secure web-based randomization system (Randomiser for Clinical Trial Lite, MEDSHARING).
The randomization sequence was generated using permuted blocks, stratified by treatment center.
Upon arrival in the operating room, an independent investigator (uninvolved in anesthesia adminis                                      -
tration or outcome assessment) accessed the randomization system to reveal group assignments. The
TEAS intervention commenced immediately after anesthesia induction and continued throughout the
surgical procedure. Electrodes were attached to acupoints and connected to a stimulator in both
groups. The intervention provider determined the maximum current intensity each patient could
tolerate, after which the stimulator was turned off. Immediately following anesthesia induction, the
intervention provider adjusted the stimulator settings for patients in the TEAS group and initiated
stimulation. For patients in the control group, the intervention provider went through the motions
of adjusting the stimulator settings but did not switch the device on. The stimulator was then placed
inside an opaque box, preventing operating room personnel from identifying treatment allocation.
The patients, anesthesiologists, outcome assessors, and ward staff remained unaware of treatment
assignments throughout the study period.


Inter vention
The transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) procedure strictly adhered to the Standards
for Reporting of Controlled Trials in Acupuncture [                  10]. Surface electrodes were positioned bilaterally
at the PC6 acupoints and connected to the stimulator (Hwato SDZ-V; Suzhou Medical Appliances
Co., Ltd., China). The device delivered alternating “disperse-dense” wave frequencies (2                                                                                     Hz and 10                                                   Hz)

in two cycles. Stimulation intensity was individually titrated to each patient’s maximum tolerable
threshold while maintaining the characteristic “De Qi” sensation (heaviness, numbness, and distension
at the stimulation site). In the TEAS group, bilateral acupoint stimulation was administered from
immediately after anesthesia induction until surgery completion. Immediately after anesthesia induction
was defined as loss of consciousness after administration of propofol and sufentanil. For control group
participants, electrodes were similarly placed but remained inactive.


Procedures
Standard intraoperative monitoring was implemented, including continuous electrocardiography, pulse
oximetry, invasive arterial blood pressure monitoring, and Narcotrend index measurement for depth
of anesthesia assessment. Anesthesia induction was achieved using midazolam (0.03–0.05                                                                                                                           mg/kg),
sufentanil (0.3–0.5                                                                                  μg/kg), and propofol (1.5–2.5                                                                                     mg/kg), with rocuronium (0.6                                                                      mg/kg) administered
to facilitate endotracheal intubation. Anesthesia maintenance consisted of sevoflurane (1–1.5 age-adjusted
MAC), remifentanil infusion (0.1–0.3                                                                                           μg/kg/min), and dexmedetomidine (0.4                                                      μg/kg/h), with supple-
mental rocuronium boluses as required. Preemptive analgesia was provided with parecoxib sodium
(40                    mg IV) and dexamethasone (10                                           mg IV) prior to surgical incision. The Narcotrend index was
maintained between 40 and 60 throughout the procedure. Antiemetic prophylaxis included palono                                                                         -
setron (0.25                                                                      mg IV), with oxycodone (5                                       mg IV) administered for postoperative pain control prior to
surgical completion. Following extubation, patients were transferred to the post-anesthesia care unit
(PACU) for recovery. Postoperative analgesia was maintained using an intravenous patient-controlled
analgesia (PCA) system programmed to deliver a solution containing sufentanil (100                                                  μg) and oxyco-
done (20                                        mg) in normal saline, with a background infusion rate of 0.015                                                              ml/kg/h, bolus doses of
0.015                                                 ml/kg, and a 10-minute lockout interval.


Outcomes
The primary outcome was the incidence of PONV by 24                                            h postoperatively. Secondary outcomes
included visual analogue scale (VAS) score of PONV, nausea and vomiting during PACU stay, by
24                      h and 48                                                 h postoperatively; incidence of PONV during PACU stay and by 48                                                         h postoperatively; the
duration between the end of surgery and the onset of nausea and vomiting; the VAS of pain during
PACU stay, by 24                                       h and 48                                        h postoperatively; the number of total PCA attempts and effective PCA
deliveries by 24                                              h and 48                                               h after surgery; the 15-item quality of recovery (QoR-15) score by 24                                                       h and
48                 h postoperatively; length of hospital stay after surgery; time to ambulation, to flatus and to oral
intake after surgery; major complications during hospitalization; and mortality at 6                               months
postoperatively.


Statistical Analysis
The primary endpoint was the incidence of PONV by 24                                                h postoperatively. According to a previous
report [11], we anticipated that the incidence of PONV in patients undergoing hepatectomy was 55%.
Assuming a reduction of incidence of PONV to 33% after TEAS based on our pilot study, to detect
this reduction with an α of 0.05 (two-sided) and 80% power (                                              β  =       0.2), we required 72 patients per
group. Accounting for a potential 10% attrition rate, we enrolled a total of 158 participants (79 per
group) to ensure adequate statistical power.
    Data analyses were primarily performed in a modified intention-to-treat population, which included
all randomly assigned participants who initiated the study intervention and did not withdraw consent.
The primary endpoint was compared with an unadjusted χ                                            2 test and results are presented as relative
risks (odds ratio, OR) with 95% confidence intervals. Results for the primary outcome are additionally
reported as absolute risk difference (RD) with 95% confidence intervals. Pre-defined subgroup analyses
were done based on gender, history of smoking, motion sickness, chemotherapy and type of surgery
(laparoscopic surgery or open surgery).

   For secondary endpoints, OR and RD together with a 95% CI were calculated using a general
linear model for binomial outcomes. Scores were compared between groups with ordered logistic
regression. Time-to-event results (time to flatus, to oral intake of water, to ambulation and to dis                                       -
charge) were analyzed with Kaplan–Meier survival analyses with log-rank tests. Univariable Cox
proportional hazards models were used to calculate the hazard ratio and 95% CI.
   Given that fewer than 5% of data points were missing, no imputation methods were applied. We
considered secondary outcomes as exploratory and thus did not include any multiplicity adjustment.
   Post-hoc sensitivity analyses included analysis of the primary outcome in the per-protocol (PP)
population, analysis on the incidence of nausea and vomiting respectively. Mixed effects logistic
regression analysis by dose of opioid and type of disease as fixed covariates and trial center as a
random effect were performed as well [                  12].
   Statistical significance was defined as a two-tailed                   p value <0.05. The statistical analysis was con                  -
ducted using SPSS 25.0 software package (IBM SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) and R Studio 4.3.0 (R
Studio, Boston, MA, USA). Details of the statistical analysis plan are shown in Supplemental Material 1.


                                                               RESULTS
Patient Characteristics
Between June 5, 2022, and May 17, 2023, a total of 201 patients were screened, of whom 161 were
randomly assigned to group TEAS and group Control. The last follow-up was November 17, 2023.
One patient in group TEAS had surgery canceled and one patient in group Control withdraw the
informed consent, both were excluded from the mITT analysis. Four patients in group TEAS and
two patients in group Control were excluded from PP analysis due to a variation to the protocol.
Thus, the PP set included 76 patients in group TEAS and 77 patients in group Control (                                        Figure 1).
The mean (SD) age of participants was 53.3 (9.2) years; 54 patients were women (34.0%). Race and
ethnicity data were not collected. The two groups were well balanced at baseline (                                 Table 1). Baseline
characteristics of the PP population are shown in                      Supplemental Material 2 (eTable 2).

Primary Outcomes
For the mITT analysis, the incidence of PONV by 24                                                  h after surgery was 23.8% in the TEAS group
and 26.6% in the control group (OR = 0.86, 95% CI, 0.42–1.76;                              p=        0.68). Based on the significance
level of .05, there was no statistical evidence of a difference between the two groups. Findings were
consistent for the per-protocol population (OR = 0.89, 95% CI, 0.43–1.83;                                p=0.75)                      (Table 2).

Secondary Outcomes
Using mITT analysis, no statistically significant difference was detected for the scores of PONV
assessed with PONV scale and VAS score by 24h and 48h after surgery (                                   Table 3). For nausea VAS
score during PACU stay, a statistically significant difference was detected in favor of TEAS (median,
range: 0, 2 vs 0, 3;       p=        0.046). Analysis of pain VAS scores revealed no statistically significant inter                       -
group differences at any postoperative assessment time point (                         Table 3). However, compared with the
Control group, the TEAS group showed less total PCA attempts (2.7                                                                 ±       3.3 vs 3.5                                                                                             ±       6.1, p=0.02) and
less effective deliveries (2.6                                                                          ±        3.3 vs 3.5                                                                                                 ±        6.0, p=        0.02) by 24                                          h after surgery. Total PCA attempts and
effective deliveries were not different between the two groups by 48h after surgery. Moreover, the
total opioid consumption on morphine equivalent by 24h and 48h after surgery were not different
between the two groups (           Supplemental material 2, eTable 1). No statistically significant difference in
QoR-15 was detected between treatment arms (                      Table 3).
   Analysis of time to flatus, to oral intake and to ambulation after surgery identified no statistically
significant differences between the two groups (                  Figure 2). Although two patients in the TEAS group
died within 6                                                        months after surgery compared with none in the control group, the difference in mor-
tality rates between groups was not statistically significant (                     p=0.1,    Table 2).

protocol. Comparative studies demonstrate that transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS)
enhances PONV prophylaxis when combined with antiemetics, though its efficacy depends on
baseline PONV risk. In laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy [                         13], TEAS added to dexamethasone/tro                    -
pisetron reduced PONV incidence from 77.4% to 41.9% (45.9% relative reduction), while in gyne                                            -
cologic laparoscopy [        14], TEAS plus dexamethasone achieved 24-h PONV rates of 28% versus 50%
with dexamethasone alone (OR 0.39, 95% CI 0.17–0.89), comparable to tropisetron plus dexameth                                            -
asone (26%, OR 0.36, 95% CI 0.16–0.82). However, when using dexamethasone/palonosetron (current
study), the PONV incidence of group Control was only 26.6%, suggesting that TEAS’s additive
effect may be obscured in low-risk settings where modern antiemetics already achieve substantial
PONV reduction. Notably, TEAS showed non-inferiority to antiemetics (                                  p=0.857                         vs.                         tropisetron)
and has been successfully implemented in breast surgery [                             15], supporting its role as a viable
non-pharmacologic adjunct, particularly in moderate-to-high-risk populations where baseline PONV
rates exceed 50%.
   A particular feature of this trial was the beginning of the intervention after anesthesia induction,
which ascertained the blinding of the participants to the group allocation. The issue of PONV man                                        -
agement by TEAS has been debated over the past years, a significant component of the controversy
being the unblinding of participants during stimulation. Previous studies have described themselves
as double-blind, but it can be observed that the blinding effect on subjects in their study designs
was not actually achieved. For example, Jin’s study [16] reported being double-blind, yet the inter                                      -
vention was administered postoperatively in the ward, making it unlikely for subjects to remain

unaware of their group assignment. Moreover, such questionable blinding methods are remarkably
common in perioperative acupoint stimulation research. In clinical trials investigating the sedative
and analgesic effects of acupoint stimulation, the interventions are frequently administered while
patients remain conscious [         15,17,18], making it impossible to guarantee subjects’ unawareness of their
group allocation. Only interventions initiated after anesthesia induction can truly ensure proper
blinding of study participants.
   The acupoints stimulated in our study are bilateral PC6. PC6 is the most frequently chosen acu                                      -
point for the prevention and treatment of PONV [                    19]. However, compared with PC6 alone, concom                      -
itant stimulation of the PC6 and the L14 acupuncture points may induce a more prophylactic antiemetic
effect after general anesthesia [         20]. Furthermore, to achieve other therapeutic effects, the selection of
different acupoints may be necessary. For instance, the Zusanli acupoint (ST36) is typically chosen
for preventing postoperative ileus [4,8,21–23]. This specificity in acupoint selection might also partially
explain why our study failed to observe TEAS effects on time to first flatus, time to oral intake, and
time to ambulation.
   We observed less boluses of PCA, which is in accordance with the previous studies [                                   23,24]. The
beneficial analgesic effect of TEAS might depend on the stimulation of PC6 which is frequently used
in studies on postoperative analgesia [              25]. However, the total opioid consumption of PCA was not
different between the groups, indicating that the analgesic effect of TEAS is relatively weak.


Limitations
This study is not without limitations. First, we acknowledge that the lower-than-anticipated PONV
incidence (26.6% in controls vs. 40% assumed in sample size calculation) may have reduced the
study’s power. A post-hoc power analysis revealed that with the observed rates (23.8% vs. 26.6%)
and   N  =     160, the power to detect a 40% relative risk reduction was only 27.7% (using                                   α=                                                      0.05,
two-sided test), indicating insufficient statistical power. The non-significant result should therefore
be interpreted as inconclusive rather than definitive evidence of no effect. Future trials with larger
samples, tailored to the actual PONV incidence, are needed to confirm or refute our findings. Second,
the study population predominantly consisted of male participants, while female gender is an inde                                      -
pendent risk factor for PONV. However, our predefined subgroup analysis demonstrated no interaction
effect between gender and intervention groups, indicating that gender did not influence TEAS efficacy.
Third, given that our study employed a single-session TEAS protocol confined to the period between
anesthesia induction and surgical conclusion, both the stimulation duration and cumulative dose were
inherently limited. This restricted application may account for the observed ineffectiveness of TEAS
in preventing PONV, as the intervention might not have reached its full therapeutic potential under
these constrained conditions. Additionally, although we observed a statistically significant difference
in nausea scores between the TEAS and control groups in the PACU, the actual difference (0 vs 0)
lacked clinical significance.


                                                          CONCLUSION
In summary, our study demonstrates that additional intraoperative TEAS administered at PC6 acu                                         -
points does not further reduce the incidence of PONV in patients undergoing hepatectomy when
added to the current antiemetic regimen. However, this TEAS protocol may potentially decrease
postoperative analgesic requirements.
