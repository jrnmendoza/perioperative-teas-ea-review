# Abstract

**Background:** Transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS) and
electroacupuncture (EA) are used as opioid-sparing adjuncts, but prior syntheses
pooled heterogeneous modalities, pooled sham with usual-care comparisons, and
combined opioid quantities that are not the same estimand. Whether either
produces opioid sparing that matters is unresolved.

**Methods:** We systematically reviewed randomised trials of perioperative TEAS
or EA in adults having surgery under general anaesthesia. The primary outcome
was all delivered systemic opioid from end of surgery to 24 hours in milligrams
of intravenous morphine equivalents (IV MME), with ~24-hour pain as co-primary.
Clinical importance required a joint criterion: ≥10 mg sparing plus paired pain
evidence whose upper 95% confidence limit lay below +1 point on 0–10. TEAS and EA
were analysed separately and never pooled, as were sham, usual-care and active
comparators; candidate opioid results were classified against the registered
construct before synthesis. Random-effects models used REML with safeguarded
Hartung–Knapp intervals; single-study contrasts used within-study normal
intervals. Certainty used GRADE.

**Results:** Seventy reports representing 69 trial families were included
(12,103 randomised, operational count). **No evidence body established the
registered joint criterion.** TEAS versus sham was a single-study contrast (k=1,
N=48): −7.70 mg IV MME (95% CI −10.62 to −4.78), low certainty; TEAS versus
usual care, same trial third arm (k=1, N=47), −8.00 mg (−10.92 to −5.08),
low certainty. **No eligible sham-controlled EA evidence existed**; EA versus
usual care (k=2, N=159) gave −6.83 mg (−76.39 to 62.73), very low certainty. For
rest pain, TEAS versus sham (k=3, N=218) showed little or no difference, −0.213
points (−0.754 to 0.327) — the only moderate-certainty body. Composite nausea
and vomiting favoured TEAS (k=10, RR 0.640, 0.517 to 0.793, low certainty),
though components crossed the null. Quality-of-recovery syntheses were
of very low certainty throughout.

**Conclusions:** Current evidence does not establish clinically meaningful
opioid sparing for perioperative TEAS or EA. The principal comparison rests on
one small trial, no eligible sham-controlled EA evidence exists, and the most robust
finding is an absence of difference in 24-hour rest pain. Adequately powered
sham-controlled trials reporting all delivered systemic opioid at a fixed window
are needed.

**Systematic review registration:** PROSPERO CRD420261452908.
