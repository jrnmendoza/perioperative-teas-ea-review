## 1. Full eligibility criteria (PICOS)

### 1.1 Population

*[registered]* **Included**

- Adults aged 18 years or older undergoing an operative surgical procedure under
  general anaesthesia.
- General anaesthesia alone or general anaesthesia combined with regional or
  neuraxial anaesthesia or analgesia.
- Any surgical specialty and elective or urgent surgery, provided postoperative
  opioid consumption or another eligible outcome is reported.
- Mixed-age studies when adult data can be extracted separately or when at least
  90% of participants are aged 18 years or older.

*[registered]* **Excluded**

- Studies involving only children or adolescents.
- Procedures conducted exclusively under local, regional, or neuraxial
  anaesthesia without general anaesthesia.
- Non-surgical procedures, dental procedures without general anaesthesia,
  chronic pain populations without an acute perioperative context, and
  interventions delivered only after recovery from the acute postoperative
  period.

**Implementation note.** General anaesthesia was verified from the source
report's methods rather than from a study-level label, a patient-controlled
analgesia regimen, or an indexing term. Where the source did not establish
general anaesthesia, the report was held and contributed to no model.

### 1.2 Interventions

*[registered]* **Included**

1. **Transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation (TEAS):** non-invasive
   electrical stimulation delivered through surface electrodes positioned over
   recognised or explicitly defined body acupuncture points, without needle
   penetration.
2. **Electroacupuncture (EA):** electrical stimulation delivered through one or
   more pairs of acupuncture needles inserted at recognised or explicitly defined
   body acupuncture points.

Eligible terminology includes transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation,
transcutaneous electric acupoint stimulation, transcutaneous acupoint electrical
stimulation, electrical acupoint stimulation, electroacupoint stimulation,
acupuncture-like TENS delivered at acupuncture points, electroacupuncture,
electric acupuncture, and electrically stimulated acupuncture, **when the
intervention clearly meets the definitions above**.

At least one eligible stimulation session must occur before the end of the first
24 postoperative hours. Interventions may be preoperative, intraoperative,
postoperative, or delivered across more than one perioperative phase, and may be
used alone or as an adjunct to balanced usual multimodal perioperative care.

*[registered]* **Excluded**

- Manual acupuncture without electrical stimulation.
- Dry needling, trigger-point needling, acupuncture-point injection, laser
  acupuncture, moxibustion, cupping, massage, or herbal interventions.
- Ordinary TENS or percutaneous electrical nerve stimulation delivered only at
  non-acupuncture locations.
- Auricular acupuncture, battlefield acupuncture, auricular acupressure,
  electroauriculotherapy, press-tack or intradermal needle interventions,
  wrist-ankle acupuncture, and buccal acupuncture.
- Electrical stimulation used only for neuromuscular monitoring, nerve
  localisation, or physiological measurement.
- Mixed interventions in which the independent effect of TEAS or EA cannot be
  separated.
- Studies in which TEAS and EA are combined within the same intervention arm and
  their individual contribution cannot be separated.

**Implementation notes.** Classification followed the physical criterion, not the
trial's own terminology. Two consequences arose:

| Report | Issue | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Huang 2017 | Titled and labelled as electroacupuncture, but describes surface electrodes and a non-invasive surface stimulator | Reclassified **EA → TEAS**. Contributes to no model. |
| Long 2025 | Intervention described as "needle or patch", which does not establish needle penetration | Held as **modality-unclear**. Contributes to no model. |

### 1.3 Comparators

*[registered]* **Included — principal (sham-controlled)**

1. TEAS versus inactive or credible sham TEAS, including identical electrode
   placement with no current, sub-sensory or minimally active current, or
   non-acupoint sham electrical stimulation.
2. EA versus sham EA, including non-penetrating sham needles, superficial or
   non-acupoint needling, needles connected to an inactive device, or another
   credible sham procedure designed to preserve participant blinding.

*[registered]* **Included — analysed separately**

Usual perioperative care, standard multimodal analgesia, no TEAS or EA,
waiting-list control, or attention control are eligible but analysed separately
as **supportive** comparisons. Active electrical comparators, such as
non-acupoint TENS, may be included and reported **separately** when the contrast
is clinically interpretable.

Anaesthetic techniques, regional or neuraxial analgesia, non-opioid analgesics,
antiemetics and postoperative care pathways must be balanced between groups or
sufficiently described to assess their potential influence.

*[registered]* **Excluded**

- Comparators containing an active acupuncture-point intervention that prevents
  estimation of the independent effect of TEAS or EA.
- Groups receiving substantially different perioperative or analgesic
  co-interventions without adequate adjustment.
- Comparators insufficiently described to classify as sham, usual care, no
  treatment, or active comparator.

**Implementation note — why comparator class is assigned per result.** The
registered record lists non-acupoint electrical stimulation *both* as a form of
credible sham (item 1 above) *and* as a possible active comparator. A
study-level label therefore cannot resolve a trial's stratum. Class was assigned
from quoted source text at the level of the individual result. Four
reclassifications followed:

| Trial | Source basis | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Pan 2023 | "Patients in Group C did not undergo TEAS." No sham procedure described. | sham → **usual care** |
| Liang 2021 | "in control group, the patients received no stimulation." No electrode placement described. | sham → **usual care** |
| Xiong 2021 | "gel electrodes applied and connected to an acupuncture instrument without stimulation" | usual care → **sham** |
| Song 2020 | Matched active non-acupoint electrical stimulation | retained as a **separate active-comparator** class, not merged into inert sham; a broad-sham analysis is provided as a diagnostic |

### 1.4 Study designs

*[registered]* **Included**

- Individually randomised parallel-group controlled trials.
- Cluster-randomised trials when appropriate design-adjusted results are
  available or can be calculated.
- Crossover trials only when an eligible perioperative treatment period can be
  validly separated and carryover is implausible.

*[registered]* **Excluded**

- Quasi-randomised studies, non-randomised controlled studies, observational
  studies, case series, case reports, qualitative studies, reviews, and
  protocols without results.
- Conference abstracts without sufficient information for eligibility,
  risk-of-bias assessment and outcome extraction. Such records may be listed as
  awaiting classification and investigators may be contacted.

### 1.5 Context, setting and language

*[registered]* Inpatient, day-surgery, ambulatory surgical and other
perioperative hospital settings in any country or healthcare system are
eligible. Surgical specialties may include orthopaedic, abdominal,
gynaecological, urological, thoracic, cardiac, neurological, breast, head and
neck, and other operative fields. No restrictions apply on the basis of sex,
ethnicity, socioeconomic status or geographic region.

*[registered]* Only studies for which the full-text report is published in
English are eligible. No language restriction is applied at the database-search
stage; reports published in languages other than English that are identified by
the unrestricted searches are excluded during study selection on the basis of
publication language.

## 2. Protocol-characteristic extraction: STRICTA-compatible domains

*[registered]* Protocol characteristics were extracted against the following
domains: **modality; timing; number of sessions; session duration; cumulative
stimulation duration before 24 hours; acupoints; frequency; waveform; pulse
width; intensity strategy; current amplitude; laterality; device; practitioner;
and treatment fidelity.**

*[registered]* Trials remain eligible when one or more protocol characteristics
are incompletely reported; however, they contribute only to analyses for which
the relevant intervention information is sufficiently available.

## 3. Full registered outcome list

### 3.1 Main outcomes *[registered]*

1. Cumulative systemic postoperative opioid consumption from the end of surgery
   to 24 hours, converted to IV MME using prespecified conversion rules.
   Programmed but undelivered patient-controlled analgesia doses will not be
   counted as consumed opioid. The principal effect measure will be mean
   difference in IV MME.
2. Postoperative pain at approximately 24 hours, assessed separately at rest and
   during movement where reported. Pain scores will be converted to a 0–10 scale
   when valid; otherwise standardised mean differences will be used in
   supportive analyses.

### 3.2 Clinical interpretation *[registered]*

> "a pooled opioid-sparing effect will be considered clinically meaningful when
> the point estimate indicates a reduction of at least 10 mg IV MME during 0–24
> hours and the corresponding pain evidence does not permit clinically important
> worsening. Clinically important pain worsening is prespecified as an increase
> of 1.0 point or more on a 0–10 scale; the upper 95% confidence limit will be
> examined against this margin. Sensitivity interpretations will use an 8 mg IV
> MME threshold and, when calculable, a 30% relative opioid reduction."

### 3.3 Additional outcomes *[registered]*

- Cumulative postoperative opioid consumption during 0–48 hours, 0–72 hours, and
  the longest reported acute postoperative interval.
- Intraoperative opioid consumption, converted to an appropriate common
  opioid-equivalent metric when valid.
- Pain at rest and during movement at approximately 6, 24, 48 and 72 hours and
  at the longest reported acute time point.
- Postoperative nausea, vomiting, or composite PONV during 0–24 hours, 0–48
  hours, and the longest reported interval.
- Need for rescue analgesia and time to first rescue analgesia.
- Quality of recovery measured using validated instruments such as QoR-15,
  QoR-40 or QoR-9.
- Patient satisfaction, gastrointestinal recovery, functional recovery, time to
  mobilisation, and length of hospital stay.
- Persistent postoperative opioid use beyond 30 days, when reported.
- Serious and non-serious adverse events plausibly related to TEAS or EA,
  including skin reactions, bleeding, infection, electrical discomfort,
  needle-related adverse effects, and device-related events.

## 4. Opioid conversion rules

### 4.1 Applied factors

| Opioid | Route | Source unit | Factor (mg IV morphine) | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Morphine | IV | mg | 1 | Identity |
| Hydromorphone | IV | mg | 5 | Conventional approximation |
| Fentanyl | IV | µg | 0.1 | Conventional approximation |
| Sufentanil | IV | µg | **0.5** | **Central analytical factor** |
| Sufentanil | IV | µg | 0.25 | Mandatory lower sensitivity bound |
| Sufentanil | IV | µg | 1.0 | Mandatory upper sensitivity bound |

The sufentanil central factor is the geometric midpoint of a published broad
parenteral equivalence range, selected symmetrically on the ratio scale before
the final model fits. It is **not** a validated constant for acute postoperative
patient-controlled analgesia. No principal contributor requires a sufentanil
conversion; the factor therefore affects only explicitly labelled expanded and
uncertain-exposure analyses. The source range and its contextual limitations are
recorded in the conversion register (Additional file 5).

### 4.2 The five prohibited conversions

Each requires a quantity that is not available at the individual level, and each
would silently manufacture precision:

1. **Multiplying a group mean weight** to reconcile a weight-normalised dose
   (mg/kg) with an absolute dose (mg). The within-group weight distribution is
   not recoverable, and the product's variance is not the reported variance.
2. **Multiplying an infusion duration** to turn a dose *rate* into a cumulative
   dose. Rate is not constant over the window and the duration is rarely
   reported per participant.
3. **Converting pump volume to mass without a verified drug concentration.** A
   reported millilitre volume of a mixed patient-controlled analgesia solution
   does not determine a delivered opioid mass.
4. **Treating programmed doses or button presses as delivered dose.** The
   registered record excludes programmed but undelivered doses from the primary
   outcome.
5. **Summing separately reported rescue components into a total standard
   deviation.** The individual-level covariance between components is not
   published, so the total's variance cannot be reconstructed.

### 4.3 Opioids analysed in native units

Tramadol, pethidine, alfentanil, remifentanil, butorphanol, dezocine, oxycodone
and bucinnazine were **not** assigned universal postoperative IV MME factors.
Where matching routes, units and windows existed, they were analysed in native
units as distinct estimands. In particular, no remifentanil-to-morphine factor
was applied to intraoperative dose; micrograms of remifentanil is a separate
estimand. Where a report gave only a rescue quantity of an unconverted drug, it
was described but not converted.

One trial's author-reported "morphine equivalent" figure, whose oral or
intravenous basis could not be established from the report or its registered
supplement, was retained at factor 1 only within a labelled *assuming author MME
equals IV MME* sensitivity analysis. No component reconstruction of a mixed pump
was attempted.

Canonical result rows retain the raw unit, the applied factor, the route
uncertainty and the conversion decision, so an unconverted row is never silently
treated as IV MME. The full register, including opioids for which no conversion
was adopted, is Additional file 5.

## 5. Protocol moderators: registered definitions and classification schemes

*[registered]* The prespecified protocol moderators are:

| Moderator | Registered classification |
|---|---|
| Intervention timing | preoperative only · intraoperative-inclusive · postoperative only · spanning multiple perioperative phases |
| Repetition | single versus repeated treatment before the 24-hour outcome |
| Cumulative active stimulation duration before 24 hours | analysed **per 30-minute increment** |
| Electrical frequency | low · intermediate · high · alternating/mixed |
| Stimulation intensity | sub-sensory or fixed low current · sensory-level · motor-twitch threshold · maximum tolerated · insufficiently reported |

*[registered]* Actual current amplitude will be analysed only when reporting is
comparable. Meta-regression will be conducted separately for TEAS and EA,
without causal claims of superiority. Surgery type, regional or neuraxial
analgesia, comparator design, background multimodal analgesia, geographic
setting, publication year and risk of bias will inform adjustment, sensitivity
analysis or interpretation.

*[registered]* **Minimum requirements.** "Normally, at least 10 independent
estimates per predictor and four studies per categorical level will be required.
Univariable models will be prioritised. Multivariable models will be considered
only with at least 20 studies and 10 studies per predictor."

*[registered]* **Fallback.** "If meta-regression is unjustified, protocol
characteristics will be synthesised descriptively using structured tables and
effect-direction plots."

**Implementation outcome.** The minimum requirements were not met for any
moderator within any modality–comparator stratum. No meta-regression is
reported. The thresholds were not relaxed, and an earlier mixed-modality
regression pooling TEAS with EA was not carried forward, because such pooling is
prohibited by the registered design. The registered fallback was applied.

### 6.1 Separate exclusion on temporal grounds

**Wu 2025 intraoperative remifentanil.** Randomisation and the start of
treatment occurred on arrival in the post-anaesthesia care unit — *after* the
intraoperative dose had been administered. The contrast therefore cannot
estimate a treatment effect. It is retained solely as a pre-intervention
negative-control diagnostic and is excluded from the causal intraoperative body.

### 7.1 The post-hoc broadened opioid estimand (E2)

**Construct.** Cumulative postoperative opioid reported as consumed or delivered
in the first 24 postoperative hours, in mg IV morphine equivalents. Totals that
capture patient-controlled analgesia only, and totals where additional rescue
opioid was given but not quantified, are admitted. Unquantified components are
never imputed or added.

**Admission rules.**

- **Consumed, not requested.** Quantities reported as demands, button presses or
  programmed doses are excluded.
- **Window.** A stated 24-hour window: from the end of surgery, or from recovery-
  unit arrival or PCA connection within the first postoperative hour, to 24 hours.
  Postoperative-day-1 totals are taken as the first 24 hours (E2.1). Unstated,
  "perioperative", calendar-day and longer windows do not qualify.
- **Convertible drug.** The reported total must be in a drug with a registered
  factor (Section 4.1), or be the authors' own morphine equivalents, which are
  admitted as reported with a sensitivity analysis excluding them. A result whose
  only quantified opioid lacks a registered factor is excluded. An unfactored drug
  given only as unquantified rescue alongside a convertible total does not exclude
  the result.
- **Absolute dose with dispersion.** Arm-level values printed only in a figure may
  be digitised (E2.1).
- **Eligibility.** General anaesthesia and a systemic route.

**Unchanged from the registered analysis:** the five prohibited conversions
(Section 4.2), separation of modality and comparator classes, holds for
unresolved source questions, the joint criterion for clinical importance, and
the statistical methods.

**Timeline.**

1. E2 was defined and committed.
2. The 32 of the 70 included reports without an extracted opioid result, but with text indicating a
   possible opioid quantity, were classified under it.
3. E2.1 was made and committed.
4. All results were classified and committed.
5. The E2 models were run.
6. The registered outcome was retained as primary.

The dated decision record, hashes and commit identifiers are in the project's
decision register. Results for E2 are in the main text and Additional file 9.

## 8. Harms and patient-reported recovery: coverage assessment

The coverage assessment described in the Methods searched the available full
texts of all 70 included reports for validated recovery instruments,
satisfaction and acceptability measures, local-reaction and general
adverse-event statements, and long-term opioid terminology.

**What was located.** Sixteen reports using validated global recovery
instruments (eight QoR-40, seven QoR-15, one QoR-9); nine reports assessing
satisfaction or willingness to repeat treatment; two further health-related
quality-of-life reports (SF-12, EORTC-QLQ-C30); two acceptability reports kept
distinct from satisfaction with analgesia; and 31 safety-relevant report
entries. These are report counts, not independent-trial counts, and not mutually
exclusive categories.

**Persistent opioid use.** No eligible result beyond 30 days was located. Later
*pain* outcomes, 30-day complication counts, a trial's twelve-month study period
and background references to persistent use are none of them measures of
persistent opioid use. This is a reporting gap, not evidence of zero persistent
users.

**Why harms are not pooled.** Definitions, ascertainment windows and
denominators differ; several reports name event types without counts; many lack
explicit treatment-harm reporting; and double-zero reports contribute
descriptive information without identifying a comparative risk ratio. Specific
constraints recorded during the assessment include: all-cause adverse-event
counts that include unrelated events and must not be relabelled as
device-related; withdrawal denominators that are the randomised rather than the
analysed arm sizes; separately tabulated event types that cannot be summed into
unique affected patients without overlap information; and one report of two
deaths at six months in which the article does not establish intervention
causation — which must neither be presented as intervention-caused mortality nor
erased by a blanket claim of no serious events.

**Why recovery outcomes are reported by instrument and time.** Reported
statistics are not interchangeable across reports: some give medians with
interquartile ranges or with minimum and maximum, one gives graph-only mean and
standard error for a subgroup, one labels parentheses explicitly as *range*, one
does not specify the assessment time, and one has a printed *P* value that
disagrees with its own nearly identical means and standard deviations. Medians
were not converted to means, ranges were not treated as standard deviations,
domain standard deviations were not summed, and shared control groups were not
reused as independent groups.

**Incomplete at the time of this draft.** Several fixed-time comparisons contain
compatible numerical data and are candidates for additional outcome-specific
synthesis. These candidate input populations have **not** been converted into
pooled estimates, and no exact-result risk-of-bias assessment or certainty
rating has been produced for them. The manuscript must not claim that recovery
evidence is absent, nor that synthesis was impossible, nor that synthesis is
complete.

**Deliverables.** `report_coverage.csv` (all 70 reports, positive and
not-located findings), `recovery_evidence.csv`, `safety_evidence.csv`,
`coverage_summary.json`, and page-anchor and hash verification records.
