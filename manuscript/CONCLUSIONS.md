# Conclusions

> **Draft status.** Written 20 September 2026. The previous file was a four-word
> stub ("See Discussion for Conclusions."); BMC Anesthesiology requires
> Conclusions as its own section.

Current evidence does not establish clinically meaningful opioid sparing from
perioperative transcutaneous electrical acupoint stimulation or
electroacupuncture. No evidence body met the registered joint criterion of at
least 10 mg intravenous morphine equivalent sparing over the first 24
postoperative hours together with paired pain evidence excluding clinically
important worsening.

Three findings should govern how this is read. The principal sham-controlled
TEAS comparison rests on **a single trial of 48 analysed participants** and is a
single-study contrast rather than a pooled estimate. **No eligible
sham-controlled electroacupuncture evidence exists** for this outcome, so that
comparison could not be evaluated at all. And the review's only
moderate-certainty result is an **absence of difference** in 24-hour rest pain
between TEAS and sham, which supports a statement about average pain but is
neither paired opioid/pain evidence nor a non-inferiority finding.

Evidence for quality of recovery, gastrointestinal recovery, harms and
satisfaction is of low or very low certainty, or is reported descriptively
because the underlying data cannot bear synthesis. Harms were inconsistently
reported; no pooled safety estimate was computed, and that absence is not
evidence of safety. Persistent opioid use beyond 30 days was not reported by any
included trial, so this review cannot address it.

These conclusions rest on decisions made after the results were known, on
risk-of-bias and certainty judgements conducted with AI assistance under the
authors' delegation rather than by two independent human assessors, and on an
evidence base whose completeness is limited by an early outcome-focused
screening rule and by restriction to English full texts. They should be read as
bounded accordingly.

The registered question of whether protocol characteristics explain variation in
effect could not be answered, because no moderator met the registered minimum
number of independent estimates. Answering it will require trials that report
all delivered systemic opioid, including rescue, in absolute units at a fixed
24-hour window, report pain at that same window, and characterise stimulation
parameters to STRICTA standards. Sham-controlled electroacupuncture trials that
capture all delivered systemic opioid over 24 hours are the single clearest gap
in the current literature.
